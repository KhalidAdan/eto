Israel braces for an election each side warns could bring ruin

Israelis head to the polls for one of the most consequential elections in the country's history, with rival camps warning that victory for the other would put Israel's future in jeopardy. The elections serve as a referendum on Benjamin Netanyahu's leadership and his handling of the security failures that led to the Hamas-led October 7 attacks. Netanyahu's campaign focused heavily on the war with Iran, claiming Iran is moving closer to a nuclear bomb. He posted an AI-generated image of Tel Aviv being destroyed by an Iranian nuclear bomb, implying that's what will happen if he loses. Netanyahu sought support from President Trump to strike Iran ahead of the elections, but Trump did not go along. Israeli officials say Netanyahu tried to order the Israel Defense Forces to take steps toward a potential new military strike, but IDF chief of staff General Eyal Zamir refused, warning of the risk of escalation. The opposition, led by former IDF Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot and former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett, criticized Netanyahu for the October 7 failure and called for accountability. Bennett accused Netanyahu of extreme government dysfunction, while Eisenkot called for a national commission of inquiry and stronger military readiness.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Netanyahu tried to order the IDF to take steps toward a potential new military strike, but IDF chief of staff General Eyal Zamir refused, warning of the risk of escalation. The Guardian adds that Netanyahu reportedly pressured the military to attack Iran in the run-up to the election, with a clash reported between Netanyahu and Zamir over a directive that could "potentially spark an immediate conflagration with Iran." FOX News mentions that Bennett sought to build economic partnerships with Arab nations and rebuild Israel's international relationships, while The Nation highlights that Bennett's campaign focused on regional diplomacy and rebuilding Israel's strained international relationships, particularly with the United States. The Guardian and Axios both note that Netanyahu denied press reports of pressuring the military, while The Nation and Straight Arrow News mention that Netanyahu is on trial in Israel on corruption charges and faces an ICC war crimes warrant.

Justice department charges ex-FBI director James Comey for third time

The Justice Department charged former FBI director James Comey on Monday with two counts of leaking information related to national defense. The charges, filed in federal court in Washington DC, are the third time the Trump administration has filed criminal charges against Comey. The government alleges that Comey leaked the information in 2016 and conspired with an unnamed person to give the information to a national news outlet. Comey’s attorney, Patrick Fitzgerald, stated that the charges are related to a New York Times article published on October 5, 2016, which had already been investigated by prosecutors. The case was filed through a criminal maneuver known as an “information,” which does not require approval by a grand jury. Comey’s lawyers argue that the statute of limitations has expired and that the law requires an indictment, not an information, within the 10-year statute of limitations.

The charges mark the third attempt by the Trump administration to prosecute Comey, following previous cases that were dismissed or closed without charges. The latest allegations concern the October 2016 New York Times article about a secret court order requiring Yahoo to scan emails for messages potentially linked to a foreign terrorist organization. The Justice Department alleges that Comey sought to correct misinformation by directing his staff to speak with a Times reporter about the accuracy of a Reuters article published the day before. The case has been ongoing under seal since early October, and a federal judge granted Comey’s request to unseal the complaint.

Where the accounts differ

THE GUARDIAN reports that the charges were filed through an “information” and that the case was not approved by a grand jury. NPR and CBS News also mention the use of an “information” to extend the statute of limitations. THE HILL and AXIOS both state that the case has been under seal since October 5, 2026, while THE GUARDIAN and CBS News mention that the case was filed in Washington DC. JUST THE NEWS states that a grand jury has not indicted Comey on the latest charges, but the case has been playing out under seal since October 5, 2026. AXIOS and CBS News note that the charges are related to a New York Times article published on October 5, 2016, while THE GUARDIAN and THE HILL specify that the article was about a secret court order requiring Yahoo to scan emails for messages potentially linked to a foreign terrorist organization.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · CBS News · Axios · The Hill · Just The News

Trump dials up Islamophobia against Abdul El-Sayed at Michigan Senate rally

President Donald Trump intensified his anti-Muslim rhetoric against Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed during a rally in Port Huron, Michigan, on October 25, 2026. Trump accused El-Sayed of being a “jihadist” and claimed that if El-Sayed won, Christmas would be canceled. He also suggested El-Sayed would need to pass through a metal detector to enter the Senate. The rally was part of Trump’s campaign to support Republican candidates in Michigan ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. El-Sayed, a US-born citizen and former public health official, faces Republican candidate Mike Rogers in the race for Michigan’s open Senate seat. The race is considered crucial for Republicans to retain control of the Senate, which currently holds a 53-seat majority.

Trump’s remarks focused heavily on El-Sayed’s Muslim identity, falsely accusing him of wanting to impose Islamic law in the US. He also referenced the 9/11 attacks, comparing El-Sayed’s potential victory to the actions of Todd Beamer, a passenger who helped regain control of United Flight 93. El-Sayed has previously dismissed the anti-Muslim rhetoric as a distraction from domestic issues like healthcare and the cost of living.

Judge rules Trump DOJ 'unlawfully' fired Maurene Comey

A federal judge in New York ruled that the Trump administration unlawfully fired Maurene Comey from the Manhattan U.S. attorney's office. Judge Jesse Furman found the termination violated civil service rules and the separation of powers. Comey, who led the prosecution of Sean "Diddy" Combs, was ordered reinstated with back pay. The DOJ stated it disagrees with the decision and will appeal. The judge rejected the administration's argument that the firing was lawful under Article II of the Constitution.

Furman's ruling outlined that Comey's reinstatement applies from the date of her termination in July 2025 until December 2025, when she resigned by accepting a new job. The judge criticized the DOJ's claim that the firing was lawful under federal statute and that the Civil Service Reform Act (CSRA) was an unconstitutional intrusion into executive authority. The ruling emphasized that the CSRA limits the Attorney General's power to remove assistant U.S. attorneys.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and The Guardian both state that Comey was fired in July 2025 and that her reinstatement applies until December 2025. The Daily Wire and FOX News also mention the same dates. The Guardian and BBC note that Comey's lawyers claimed she was fired due to her father's political opposition to Trump and her perceived political affiliations. The New York Post adds that Comey's attorneys accused the White House of firing her out of retribution for her father's actions and that right-wing influencer Laura Loomer publicly claimed credit for the move. The Daily Wire and FOX News mention that the DOJ conceded Comey was not fired for cause or performance and that she received no advance notice or opportunity to respond. The Guardian and BBC do not explicitly state this concession.

Sources ABC News · The Daily Wire · The Guardian · BBC · FOX News · New York Post

According to a new analysis by Civiqs, President Donald Trump's net approval rating is negative in 39 states and all major battlegrounds one week before the midterms. The data, based on 138,839 responses collected between January 20, 2025, and October 26, 2026, shows a national net approval rating of -26. Trump's strongest states are North Dakota at +17 and Wyoming at +15. His approval has declined significantly in several states, including Kentucky, which fell 34 points from +23 to -11. US appeals court sceptical of Trump’s lawsuit over California voter rolls

A federal appeals court has expressed skepticism about the Trump administration’s lawsuit against California, which seeks to block the counting of mail-in ballots linked to 20,000 people the government claims are noncitizens. The case is part of a broader effort by the Trump administration to challenge election procedures ahead of the November midterms. The court is considering whether to issue a decision quickly, following a rare weekend hearing. Circuit Judge Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee, questioned the legality of the administration’s request, citing the Elections Clause, which grants states authority over election administration.

California officials have disputed the federal government’s claims, arguing that the list of 20,000 names is unverified and includes eligible voters. The state has emphasized its safeguards against noncitizen voting and criticized the administration for not providing data to support its allegations. The case highlights tensions between the Trump administration and California, which has long been a target of the administration’s election misinformation efforts.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and The Guardian both report that the Trump administration claims the list includes 20,000 names it believes are noncitizens, while The Hill and FOX News also mention the same number. However, The Guardian and The Hill note that California officials have analyzed a sample of 200 names from the list and found a “high percentage” were born in the United States. Al Jazeera and FOX News do not mention this sample analysis. The Guardian and The Hill also state that California’s secretary of state, Shirley Weber, called the DOJ’s claims “vague and unsubstantiated,” a detail not included in Al Jazeera or FOX News. The Hill and Straight Arrow News mention that the DOJ wants a ruling before a Tuesday hearing, while Al Jazeera and The Guardian do not specify the timing of the court’s decision.

Judge continues to block Trump from cutting down trees at D.C. golf course

A federal judge has extended a court order blocking the Trump administration from removing additional trees at East Potomac Golf Links in Washington, D.C. The order, issued by U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes, prohibits the government from removing or damaging any trees on the course while a lawsuit challenges the renovation plan. The judge also requires 10 days' notice before any "irreparable modification" to the course. Trump has criticized the ruling as unconstitutional and stated the administration will appeal. The D.C. Preservation League sued the government, arguing the project violated the National Environmental Policy Act by not assessing its environmental impacts as a whole. Reyes found the plaintiffs likely to succeed in their claim that the government's approach to the project was legally flawed.

Trump has repeatedly claimed the course is "extremely dangerous to play" and that its current design leads to "a constant barrage of people being hit with wayward shots." He announced the course would close on November 15 for renovations, though he later said work would begin "IMMEDIATELY." The judge criticized the government for cutting down hundreds of trees without notice, calling the action "inexcusable" and expressing frustration with the administration's handling of the case.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that the judge required 10 days' notice before any "irreparable modification" to the course, while Newsweek states the order requires 10 business days' notice in writing. CBS News and ABC News both mention the judge's reference to the Dr. Seuss character "The Lorax," while Washington Examiner does not. Newsweek includes a timeline of the legal battle, which the other accounts omit. CBS News and ABC News both note that the judge found the D.C. Preservation League likely to succeed in its claim, while Washington Examiner states the judge determined the administration likely violated federal environmental law.

Teen Accused Of Unleashing Hail Of Bullets Into Crowd Near College Homecoming Game

A 15-year-old, Anthony Rivers III, has been arrested in connection with a shooting near Florida A&M University that left 12 people injured. The incident occurred near Bragg Memorial Stadium following the university’s homecoming football game. Authorities reported that 11 individuals sustained gunshot wounds, while one person injured their ankle while fleeing. Six of the victims were minors, and at least three suffered serious injuries. The shooting happened around 8 p.m. Saturday near Perry and Manatee streets. Police believe the shooting stemmed from a dispute between individuals who knew each other, and none of the victims appear to have been the intended targets. The university stated the incident occurred near campus, not on campus, and that the campus remains safe.

Tallahassee Police Chief Lawrence Revell described the event as a "night of fear and violence" for the victims and their families. He emphasized that while the arrest marks a significant step, the investigation is ongoing, and additional arrests are possible. The police credited tips and digital evidence from the public for advancing the case. Officers provided medical aid to victims before securing the scene, and videos from social media showed people fleeing the area as gunfire erupted.
