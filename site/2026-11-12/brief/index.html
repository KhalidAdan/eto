House Republicans have chosen Jim Jordan, a hardline member of the House Freedom Caucus, as their new leader following a contentious vote. Jordan defeated longtime leader Steve Scalise in a 106–73 race, signaling a shift toward a more confrontational approach. Jordan has positioned himself as a fighter capable of defending President Trump against Democratic investigations and reclaiming congressional power. Some Republicans, like Rep. Randy Fine and Dan Meuser, have endorsed Jordan's aggressive stance, arguing that the party needs a strong leader after a significant midterm defeat. Others, including Rep. Carlos Gimenez, have questioned whether the party's losses were due to policy failures, particularly regarding immigration, and whether Jordan is the right choice to lead the charge back to power.

Separately, Rep. Thomas Massie introduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, accusing him of waging war on Iran without congressional authorization and of directing extrajudicial killings. Mississippi, Once the Epicenter of Jim Crow, Elects Its First Black Senator Since Reconstruction

Scott Colom, a Mississippi Democratic Senate candidate, won a narrow victory in the state's Senate race, becoming the first Black U.S. senator from Mississippi since Reconstruction. Colom, a longtime district attorney, defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith by less than 5,000 votes. The race was marked by a razor-thin margin, with the final count showing Colom with approximately 49.2% of the vote compared to Hyde-Smith’s 48.7%. The outcome was confirmed by multiple outlets after a week of vote counting. Colom’s victory is seen as a historic moment for Mississippi, which has a long history of racial violence and segregation, including the lynching of Emmett Till and the assassination of Medgar Evers.

Colom ran on a platform focused on healthcare access, rural hospital preservation, and affordability. His campaign emphasized bipartisanship, noting that many Mississippians support policies that benefit all residents regardless of political affiliation. The race was also influenced by national political developments, including the Supreme Court’s decision in Louisiana v. Callais, which weakened the Voting Rights Act and spurred increased voter turnout among Black communities. Mississippi Republican Gov. Tate Reeves had previously called for legislative action to remove Rep. Bennie Thompson, the only Black member of Congress from the state, but Colom’s victory has sparked widespread celebration and reflection on the state’s civil rights history.

UK probes alleged Iran-backed plot against base hosting US forces

Federal prosecutors in New York have charged three men with terrorism offenses over what they describe as an Iran-backed campaign of nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks across Europe and the United States. The charges include a September plot against a British air base where American forces were stationed. The indictment names Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood Al-Saadi, Mohsen Mohajeri, and Mohammed Ali Al-Hassani, who are alleged to have acted on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and the Iraqi militia Kata'ib Hezbollah. Prosecutors allege the men orchestrated attacks targeting Jewish communities and a British base hosting American troops, including an attempted attack on a London synagogue. The U.S. has designated both groups as foreign terrorist organizations.

The United Kingdom seems to have been a central target of the plot, with as many as seven attacks taking place there. The indictment describes an operation directed by the suspects from thousands of miles away, including real-time coordination via FaceTime. Al-Hassani allegedly coached an attacker through igniting and hurling incendiary devices and later directed another man to "light it up like a Christmas tree" during an attack on a London synagogue. The men allegedly prepared for an attack on RAF Fairford by acquiring vans, fuel, and chemical accelerants, with the vans disguised as fuel delivery and pool supply vehicles. British authorities seized the vans near the base and arrested those inside.

Israeli strike on a Gaza hospital kills 6 people, health authorities say

An Israeli airstrike targeting the Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza killed six people, including a general surgeon, and wounded at least 28 more, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. The strike occurred early Thursday, as reported by the Israeli military, which claimed it struck a Hamas commander and other militants without providing evidence. The hospital's surgical wing was hit, with parts of several floors severely damaged or destroyed. British surgeon Goher Rahbour, volunteering at the hospital, described the strike as occurring just after midnight, shaking the entire building and reducing the recovery area on the fourth floor to rubble. He noted that he had been operating alongside the deceased doctor, Rizk Abu Shaira, just hours before the strike.

The Israeli military accused Hamas of using the hospital as a command center for military and terrorist activities, stating that the commander had turned it into a headquarters. The hospital is one of the few functioning medical facilities in Gaza, whose healthcare infrastructure has been heavily damaged by years of conflict. Israeli forces have previously attacked Nasser Hospital, including a strike in 2025 that killed five journalists, including an Associated Press contributor.
