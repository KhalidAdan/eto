Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Admits to Affair with Woman Linked to Chinese Spy Allegations

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with a woman linked to a Chinese spy scandal, according to multiple outlets. The affair, which Huang acknowledged in a letter to employees, has sparked an internal investigation by Nvidia's board. The woman, identified as Alice Huang, is a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a company that has been under scrutiny for potential violations of U.S. export controls. The New York Times reported that U.S. officials believe Alice Huang was recruited by Chinese intelligence to gather information about Nvidia and potentially influence its chip sales to China. Nvidia's stock price dropped by over 4% following the revelations. The controversy has also led to a shareholder lawsuit alleging that Nvidia and Huang misled investors about the affair and its national security implications.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire reports that Huang's management style and his emphasis on resilience and character are central to Nvidia's success, while the other accounts focus on the affair and its national security implications. The Dispatch and Al Jazeera both mention the affair and the alleged spy connection, but Al Jazeera provides more detail about the investigation and the stock market reaction. The Guardian adds the legal aspect of the shareholder lawsuit and mentions Trump's comments about the FBI investigation. Axios provides specific details about Alice Huang's background and her company's ties to Nvidia, including financial transactions and corporate history. The BBC highlights the broader implications for the U.S.-China AI competition and the potential impact on national security.

Russell Brand Sentenced to Over 10 Years for Rape and Sexual Assault

Russell Brand, the British comedian and actor, was sentenced to 10 and a half years in prison for rape and sexual assault following a trial in Southwark Crown Court. The conviction came after a four-week trial where a jury found him guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. The charges involved incidents dating back to 2004 and 2009. Brand was acquitted on two other rape charges and one count of attempted indecent assault. The judge, Joel Bennathan, described Brand as a "sexual predator" and noted his "staggering arrogance." The sentence includes nine years for rape and 18 months for sexual assaults, with Brand expected to serve at least seven years in prison and the remainder under restrictions. Six women made allegations against Brand, spanning from 1999 to 2009. Victim impact statements were read in court, detailing the trauma and long-term effects of the incidents.

Where the accounts differ

The Free Press reports that Brand was found guilty of rape and sexual assault but does not specify the number of charges or the exact sentence. CBS News and NPR provide detailed information on the charges, including the specific incidents and the length of the sentence. Slate mentions the Channel 4 documentary and the Sunday Times reporting that led to the trial, which are not referenced in the other accounts. The Daily Caller notes that Brand was acquitted on two rape charges and one attempted indecent assault charge, a detail not included in the Free Press or CBS News accounts. The BBC focuses on Jonathan Ross's reaction to the conviction and separate allegations against David Walliams, which are not part of the other accounts.

Sources The Free Press · CBS News · Slate · NPR · BBC · The Daily Caller

Kennedy family joins US lawsuits to block Trump’s ‘unlawful’ arch plan

The Kennedy family has filed a legal motion to halt the construction of President Donald Trump’s 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington, DC, arguing it would obscure the graves of their ancestors and the JFK Eternal Flame at Arlington National Cemetery. The motion was filed by veteran Washington lawyer Norm Eisen and the Democracy Defenders Fund, a centre-left legal advocacy group. The plaintiffs, including children and grandchildren of former Senators Robert F Kennedy and Edward M Kennedy, claim the arch would “fundamentally alter the experience of visiting the gravesites” and block the sightline between the Lincoln Memorial and the cemetery’s Arlington House. The lawsuit adds to existing legal challenges against the project, including from the governments of D.C., Virginia, and Arlington County, as well as Vietnam War veterans and a historian.

The project is part of Trump’s broader effort to reshape Washington, DC, including plans for a historic golf course renovation and threats to demolish the Kennedy Center. The National Capital Planning Commission, which is stacked with Trump allies, approved the arch’s design last week, but U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan issued a temporary restraining order, halting construction for 14 days. The White House has defended the project as a tribute to 250 years of American independence, though opponents argue it lacks proper legal authorization.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and The New York Post both mention that Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a Trump appointee, did not join the litigation. CBS News and The Hill do not mention this detail. Al Jazeera and The New York Post also note that the Kennedy family members are represented by Norm Eisen and the Democracy Defenders Fund, while CBS News and The Hill do not specify the legal representatives. The Hill and CBS News refer to the National Capital Planning Commission’s vote, while Al Jazeera and The New York Post do not mention this.

California Voters Reject Billionaire Wealth Tax Proposition 40

California voters rejected Proposition 40, a one-time 5% tax on billionaires with a net worth exceeding $1 billion, by a margin of 52.8% to 47.2%. The measure, backed by healthcare unions and progressive politicians, aimed to fund healthcare, education, and food assistance. The Associated Press called the race late Monday night with 83% of votes counted. Opponents, including billionaires and some Democrats, argued the tax would harm the economy and drive away wealthy residents. Governor Gavin Newsom, a key opponent, warned it could undermine California’s economic competitiveness. The defeat of Prop 40 may slow nationwide momentum for wealth taxes, though supporters argue the measure’s existence represents progress.

Proposition 40 was sponsored by the healthcare union SEIU-UHW and supported by progressive figures like Rep. Ro Khanna and Sen. Bernie Sanders. Critics, including Newsom and tech billionaires, claimed the tax would disproportionately harm the state’s economy and tax base. The opposition campaign, led by billionaires like Sergey Brin, spent over $425 million, making it the most expensive ballot measure campaign in California history. Prop 42, which would ban future wealth taxes, remained ahead by 3.2 percentage points.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Guardian both report that Prop 40 was a one-time 5% tax on billionaires with a net worth over $1 billion, but only The Guardian specifies that the tax would have applied to individuals with a net worth of more than $1bn as of January 1, 2026. Mother Jones does not mention the exact date. The Guardian also states that Prop 40 was sponsored by SEIU-UHW, while Mother Jones refers to it as being sponsored by the healthcare workers union. The Daily Wire and Fox Business both mention that the tax would have been funded primarily for healthcare, but only The Daily Wire specifies that 90% of the money would have gone to healthcare. Mother Jones and The Guardian do not provide this detail. The Guardian and The Daily Wire both attribute the claim that the tax would have driven billionaires out of the state to critics, while Mother Jones attributes it to Rep. Khanna.

Sources Mother Jones · The Guardian · Washington Examiner · New York Post · Fox Business · The Daily Wire

US reportedly evacuates aircraft from RAF Mildenhall over Iran threat

The US military evacuated aircraft from RAF Mildenhall in eastern England in response to a threat from Iran, according to multiple reports. The move involved refueling tankers and transport aircraft being relocated to bases in Germany, Spain, and Scotland, as well as Osprey transport planes sent to nearby RAF Lakenheath. This follows a similar redeployment of bombers from RAF Fairford in October after a security incident linked to Iran. The US has raised the security threat level at RAF Mildenhall and RAF Lakenheath to Force Protection Condition Charlie, the second-highest defensive alert level. UK officials have stated that British bases remain secure, though the precise nature of the threat remains under discussion among US officials.

The incident comes amid heightened concerns about the security implications of the US-Israel war on Iran. Iranian officials have previously threatened to target UK bases used by the US military for strikes against Iran. The US has also faced criticism over the legality of its operations in the region, with former Prime Minister Keir Starmer initially opposing the use of UK bases for strikes against Iran.

Where the accounts differ

France Blames Iran For Grocery Store Hack That Spoiled Food And Sparked Fires

French authorities accused Iranian hackers of targeting refrigeration systems at 700 grocery stores, causing food spoilage and fires. The Paris prosecutor’s office opened a judicial investigation, citing the attack as the first large-scale cyberattack aimed at sabotage on French territory. The hackers, linked to Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), disabled refrigeration alarms and triggered endless defrost cycles, raising temperatures to 122 degrees Fahrenheit. The attack affected major chains like Intermarché and Coopérative U, with losses estimated in the tens of millions of euros. The French government warned of potential European sanctions against those responsible.

The FBI and other U.S. agencies issued a joint warning about similar risks to American grocery stores, urging operators to secure their systems. French officials traced the attack to IP addresses in Iran and noted the use of Farsi in the operation. The French government has summoned Iran’s ambassador to protest the attack, though Iran has not yet responded. The incident is part of a broader trend of attacks on refrigeration systems across Europe and the U.S., with some experts linking the attacks to Iranian hacking groups.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and CBS News both state that the attack caused roughly 20 fires, while Axios reports at least 15 fires. BBC mentions 20 fires but does not specify the exact number. NPR does not mention the number of fires at all. The Daily Wire and CBS News also note that the attack affected 700 stores, while BBC and Axios report 700 stores as well, but BBC adds that the attacks were followed by similar incidents in other European countries. NPR and Axios mention additional attacks on hydroelectric plants and blood banks, which are not detailed in the Daily Wire or CBS News. The Daily Wire and CBS News attribute the attack to the IRGC and Shahid Kaveh, while BBC and Axios also mention the same groups but note that Iran has not officially responded. NPR and Axios provide more context about the broader trend of attacks on refrigeration systems globally.

Sources The Daily Wire · BBC · NPR · CBS News · Axios

Tennessee’s lethal drug supplier fears retaliation after Christa Pike’s botched execution: court docs

Tennessee prison officials are now seeking to keep the identities of their lethal drug suppliers confidential, citing fears of retaliation. A Tennessee Department of Corrections employee filed court documents warning that suppliers may stop providing pentobarbital if their identities are exposed, which could halt executions in the state. The court documents were filed in response to a judge’s order to preserve evidence from the execution, including drug vials and needles.

Tennessee officials are urging a judge to keep the identities of their lethal injection drug suppliers secret, warning that exposure could lead to retaliation and the loss of pentobarbital suppliers.
