Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO After Board Reversal

Nvidia's board of directors reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO effective immediately following a reversal of its earlier decision to remove him. Huang had resigned earlier this month amid reports of extramarital affairs, including one involving Alice Huang, a woman identified by U.S. intelligence as a possible Chinese government spy. The board cited satisfaction with Huang's candor and leadership ability, and stated it received input from senior U.S. government officials that confirmed no national security risk. Nvidia stock surged over 30% following the announcement, with broader markets also seeing significant gains. The decision has drawn mixed reactions, with some praising it as necessary for the company's future and others expressing concerns about national security implications.

Where the accounts differ

Fox Business reports that the board received input from senior U.S. government officials and that the decision was influenced by the U.S. government's assessment of Huang's national security risk. Mother Jones states that the board received input from senior U.S. government officials and that President Trump spoke directly with the board, reassuring them about Huang's safety. Axios and The New York Post note that Trump and Vice President JD Vance worked to reinstate Huang and that the administration viewed Huang's support for open-source AI as a backstop against major AI companies accruing uncontested capabilities. The Daily Wire characterizes the reinstatement as a business move that supports open-source AI models, while Fox News and The New York Post highlight Trump's direct involvement and the administration's security briefings.

Sources Fox Business · Mother Jones · Axios · FOX News · The Daily Wire · New York Post

Trump Blocks Army’s Toxic “Lead Bomb”—And Blames Rob Sand For the Fallout

The U.S. Army planned to detonate 68 barrels of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, a hazardous material that has degraded over 20 years. The detonation, initially scheduled for Friday, was postponed multiple times due to public concern and legal challenges. President Trump announced on Truth Social that he would not proceed with the detonation without approval from the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals and incoming Iowa Governor-elect Rob Sand. The Army stated it would proceed with the detonation on Wednesday to allow for optimal weather conditions and mitigation measures. The Department of Justice warned of an "immediate risk" that the lead azide could explode if temperatures drop below 30 degrees. Residents of Middletown, Iowa, were given just a month’s notice of the plan, with other nearby communities learning of it only five days before the initial planned date.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Guardian both state that the Army postponed the detonation to Wednesday, while FOX News and CBS News mention the Army had delayed the plan to Monday before the court stepped in. The Hill reports that the Army postponed the detonation to Wednesday after a court pause, while The Guardian notes the Army changed the date three times. The Daily Wire and The Guardian attribute the delay to weather conditions, while CBS News and FOX News mention the Army’s need to allow for mitigation measures. The Hill and The Guardian note that the Army warned of an "immediate risk" of an uncontrolled explosion, while FOX News and CBS News cite the Department of Justice as the source of that warning. The Daily Wire and The Guardian attribute the existence of the lead azide to the Obama administration, while CBS News and The Guardian do not specify the origin of the material.

Sources Mother Jones · The Hill · FOX News · The Daily Wire · The Guardian · CBS News

Russian warship sinks in Benghazi after suspected Ukrainian drone attack

A Russian warship docked in the Libyan port city of Benghazi was struck in the early hours of Sunday in a suspected Ukrainian sea drone attack, causing a dramatic night-time explosion and the sinking of the vessel. Libyan officials from the country’s eastern government confirmed the strike severely damaged the Merkury, a Russian navy corvette that had stopped to refuel and take on supplies. The attack, which took place in the Mediterranean, more than 1,000 miles from south Ukraine, marked a notable geographic expansion of the now near five-year war. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy claimed responsibility for the attack, stating that seven domestically made sea drones were used to sink the ship. The UN mission in Libya expressed “serious concern” over the drone attacks and called for “an immediate, independent and transparent investigation.”

Hours later, a Russia-linked cargo ship said to be carrying military supplies, the Ascalon, was hit by a sea drone off the coast of Benghazi. Video showed the drone heading directly towards one end of the vessel, again causing a huge explosion. Libyan naval forces rescued the vessel’s crew, with some members in stable condition with minor injuries. The number of injured and their nationalities are not known. The eastern-based Libyan authority said port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe and that normal operations would resume once the fire was brought under control and the site secured.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and BBC both report that the attack occurred in Benghazi, with the Merkury being the Russian warship struck. The Guardian states that at least four drones were intercepted and two hit the ship, while the BBC notes that the health ministry said it had received injured people from a commercial ship that was targeted off the coast of the port, but it is unclear if this is the same vessel as the one Ukraine has claimed responsibility for. CBS News and Al Jazeera both mention that the Merkury was one of Russia’s newest vessels, commissioned in 2023, while the Guardian and BBC do not specify the vessel’s commission date. FOX News does not mention the Merkury by name and instead refers to it as the corvette Mercury, and it focuses more on the political implications of the attack rather than the event itself.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · Al Jazeera · CBS News · BBC · FOX News

Trump announces federal death penalty directive as he blasts Newsom’s clemency plan

President Donald Trump announced Monday that he would direct the Justice Department to seek the death penalty in federal child murder cases and in cases in which rapists or human traffickers kill their victims. He used the speech at the DOJ headquarters to condemn outgoing Gov. Gavin Newsom’s (D-CA) plan to commute 548 California death sentences. Trump’s directive would tell federal prosecutors to seek the death penalty in child murder cases and in cases involving rape or human trafficking in which the offender kills the victim. The move would have no effect on Newsom’s plan to replace 548 state death sentences with life without parole, which Trump condemned before an audience that included victims’ families.

Trump said he would sign a presidential directive that would also order federal prosecutors to seek the death penalty for rapists and human traffickers who kill their victims. "Once juries have spoken, capital punishment will be swift and certain," he said. Newsom announced Nov. 5 that he intended to replace all 548 death sentences with life in prison without parole, arguing that capital punishment is incompatible with the state’s values. Trump and several speakers described the commutations as already granted, but the plan has yet to take effect. Under California’s Constitution, 382 of the proposed commutations require a recommendation from the state Supreme Court because the inmates were convicted of more than one felony. Newsom’s administration has said it will wait for the court to act before proceeding with any of the commutations.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and FOX News both report that Trump condemned Newsom’s plan to commute 548 death sentences, but the Examiner notes that Trump’s directive would have no effect on Newsom’s plan, while FOX News states that Trump described the commutations as already granted. The Guardian and Just The News both mention that Newsom’s plan does not release any prisoners, overturn convictions, or remove restitution obligations, but the Guardian adds that most of the commutations require approval from the California Supreme Court. The New York Post and The Daily Wire both include quotes from Marc Klaas, but the Daily Wire attributes the quote "a shiv in my back" to Klaas, while the New York Post attributes it to Klaas as well, but the Guardian and FOX News do not include this specific quote.

Sources Washington Examiner · FOX News · The Daily Wire · Just The News · The Guardian · New York Post

Federal court releases Rep. Ilhan Omar D.C. home raid documents

A federal court released documents related to the search warrant and raid of Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington, D.C., home. The documents, obtained by multiple outlets, include exchanges between federal prosecutors and a magistrate judge regarding probable cause, constitutional privilege, and the warrant's execution. The search, conducted by Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) on October 12, targeted electronic devices and records, including computers, cellphones, and external hard drives. The documents also mention that the warrant was initially sealed due to concerns about evidence tampering and witness intimidation.

The documents reveal that federal officials applied for a search warrant on October 2, seeking records related to alleged violations involving Ilhan Nur Said Elmi a/k/a Ilhan Abdullahi Omar, spanning from 1980 to the present. Prosecutors argued that the investigation predated Omar's time in Congress, and that the United States believes she has committed "REDACTED" crimes over decades. The search warrant was signed by a judge on October 9, and the warrant was sealed three days before the raid. The U.S. Attorney's Office requested that the files remain confidential for potential use in a future trial.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News states that the U.S. Attorney's Office, Jeanine Pirro, requested that the files not be released, while The Daily Wire and FOX News note that the documents were unsealed following a lawsuit by 15 media outlets and Judicial Watch. The New York Post and Washington Examiner mention that the documents were released under a court order, with the latter noting that the search warrant records were unsealed after a judge's ruling. The Daily Wire and FOX News attribute the claim that the United States believes Omar committed crimes over decades to the federal government's filings, while CBS News and The New York Post also include this claim but attribute it to the government's filings. The Daily Wire and FOX News cite Fox News as the source for the claim about the ex parte brief, while CBS News and The New York Post do not attribute the claim to a specific outlet.

Dems Unveil 2028 Primary Debate Plan And Draw Their First Red Line

The Democratic National Committee (DNC) announced plans to hold 12 presidential primary debates in the 2028 cycle, with five debates scheduled for 2027 and seven for 2028. Candidates must agree to denounce dark money spending and avoid joint fundraising agreements with party committees to qualify for the debate stage. The DNC emphasized the need for a fair and transparent process to ensure unity within the party and strengthen its eventual nominee. The first two debates are set for June and July 2027, with specific criteria for the early debates to be announced in January 2027. The DNC also mentioned that early debates may involve up to 20 candidates, potentially held over two consecutive nights.

The DNC's plan includes a "Fair Campaign Practices Pledge" requiring candidates to denounce dark money spending and coordinate party spending during the primary. The pledge does not apply to the general election. The DNC will use polling and grassroots fundraising to determine eligibility for the debate stage, with the specific criteria for the first two debates to be announced in January 2027. The DNC also highlighted the importance of maintaining party unity and positioning Democrats to take back the White House.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and ABC News both mention that five debates will be held in 2027, with the first two in June and July 2027. However, ABC News specifies that six of the 2028 debates will be in states with early primaries: South Carolina, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Michigan, and Virginia. The Guardian does not mention specific states for the 2028 debates. The Daily Wire lists potential candidates including Kamala Harris, Gavin Newsom, Jon Ossoff, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, Pete Buttigieg, Josh Shapiro, and Mark Kelly, while the Guardian includes JB Pritzker, Andy Beshear, and mentions early polling showing support for younger and more progressive candidates. The Guardian also notes that the DNC will incorporate "creators and influencers" in the process, which the other accounts do not mention.

Sources The Daily Wire · ABC News · Axios · The Guardian · FOX News · Just The News

Video of ICE detaining father and daughter while boarding flight sparks protests in Seattle

A video of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers detaining a father and daughter while they boarded an Alaska Airlines flight at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport has sparked widespread protests. The incident, captured on camera by fellow passengers, went viral on social media and drew condemnation from Washington state officials, including Governor Bob Ferguson and Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson. Protesters gathered at the airport, chanting slogans such as “ICE out of Washington” and “Abolish ICE.” The father, Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, and daughter, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, were reportedly traveling to a family baptism ceremony in California. Their lawyer, Olia Catala, stated they had no criminal record and were seeking asylum in the U.S., where they had lived since 2015. ICE claimed Mendoza overstayed his visa and that Mejia hurled hot coffee at officers, potentially leading to charges of assaulting a federal officer. Alaska Airlines noted the operation occurred during a busy travel weekend without prior notice.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News both report that Mejia was carrying a cup of coffee when the confrontation occurred, and that it was dropped during the commotion. FOX News and Newsweek describe the incident as involving an alleged assault by Mejia, with FOX News citing DHS claims that she threw hot coffee at officers. The Guardian and CBS News attribute the characterization of the coffee incident to the attorney, Olia Catala, while FOX News and Newsweek attribute it to DHS. The Guardian and CBS News also note that Mejia had married a U.S. citizen and was in the process of adjusting her status, a detail not included in the FOX News account. The BBC and CBS News mention that the Port of Seattle was “outraged” by the incident, while FOX News and Newsweek do not include this detail.

Sources The Guardian · BBC · FOX News · CBS News · Newsweek

Environmental crimes: Israel blamed for toxic pollution in 99% of Gaza, endangering health and safety of thousands.

Israeli attacks have turned Gaza into 'toxic wasteland', Oxfam says

Oxfam and the Conflict and Environment Observatory (CEOBS) report that Israeli military actions have caused widespread environmental contamination across Gaza, with heavy metals, explosive residues, asbestos, and sewage posing long-term health risks. The report, based on satellite imagery, remote sensing, and open-source data, highlights the severe impact of three years of conflict on Gaza’s environment. Oxfam’s executive director, Amitabh Behar, called the situation an "environmental war crime" and part of an "ongoing genocide in Gaza," which breaches Israel’s legal obligations under international law. The report also notes that the destruction of sanitation systems has led to raw sewage leaking into the ground and sea, increasing the risk of disease. The UN Environment Programme estimates that Israel’s military operations have generated over 61 million tonnes of debris, turning much of Gaza into a hazardous environment.

The report emphasizes the urgent need for an independent environmental assessment to identify contamination risks and guide cleanup efforts. It also highlights the challenges of testing contamination due to destroyed laboratories and Israeli restrictions on scientific equipment. Oxfam’s report author, Lama Abdul Samad, warned of long-term health impacts, including respiratory diseases, cancers, and birth defects, for Gazans now and in the future.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Al Jazeera both report that Oxfam and CEOBS found environmental contamination in Gaza, with Al Jazeera specifying that 99% of the territory shows indicators of contamination. The BBC does not mention a specific percentage but states that more than 99% of Gaza shows indicators of potential environmental contamination. The Guardian and BBC both cite Oxfam’s report as stating that over 74,000 Palestinians have been killed and at least 175,000 wounded, while Al Jazeera states that more than 1,500 Palestinians have been killed since the so-called “ceasefire” in October 2025. The Guardian and BBC mention the UN Environment Programme’s estimate of 61 million tonnes of debris, while Al Jazeera includes this figure as well. The BBC notes that the Israeli embassy in the UK criticized the report for falling short of professional standards, while the other accounts do not mention this.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · BBC
