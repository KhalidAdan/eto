Trump-Mamdani Meeting Takes A Turn Liberal Media Didn’t Expect

President Donald Trump and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani held a press conference following their meeting, during which Trump praised Mamdani’s efforts to improve affordable housing in New York City. Trump stated he wanted Mamdani to be a “great mayor” and emphasized the importance of affordable housing in any development. Mamdani, a Democrat, said he would “meet with anyone, work with anyone who could deliver for New Yorkers,” and that the two had agreed to set up a new level of conversation between their housing teams. The meeting marked their third in-person encounter, with discussions focusing on Sunnyside Yard, a $21 billion development project in Queens. Trump noted that the project would require “a lot of federal funding and probably federal approvals.”

During the press conference, Mamdani revealed that Trump had previously invited him to Gracie Mansion, which Trump described as one of his favorite places. Trump also addressed their disagreements over immigration and the administration’s decision to bar several news organizations from White House grounds. Mamdani stated he would ensure all press members were present on the lawn, while Trump claimed the press would “never boycott” him.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire reports that Trump and Mamdani discussed the release of a Columbia University student from ICE custody, which Mamdani attributed to their last meeting. This detail is not included in the BBC or The Hill accounts. The Daily Caller and The Free Press do not mention the meeting with Mamdani at all, focusing instead on the media ban and subsequent lawsuit. The Daily Wire and The Hill both mention the meeting, but The Daily Wire includes specific details about the Sunnyside Yard project and Trump’s comments on federal funding, which are not present in The Hill’s account. The BBC and The Free Press do not mention the meeting with Mamdani, focusing instead on the legal action against the media ban.

Sources The Daily Wire · Newsweek · BBC · The Free Press · The Daily Caller · The Hill

Washington has also pressed Beijing over supplies of rare-earth magnets and other critical minerals used in industries ranging from cars to advanced semiconductors. Bessent and Greer announced no new progress on that issue after Sunday’s meeting.

World leaders descend on New York for UNGA — but who really has America’s ear?

More than 100 heads of state and government will attend the 81st United Nations General Assembly in New York, with the event focusing on global issues such as the Iran war, Russia’s war in Ukraine, and climate change. President Donald Trump is expected to address the assembly, with meetings planned with key leaders including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, French President Emmanuel Macron, and Venezuelan interim leader Delcy Rodríguez. The U.N. General Assembly will also feature speeches from Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, and others. The event is expected to be marked by high-level discussions and bilateral meetings, with the U.S. administration emphasizing the importance of global cooperation and security.

The U.N. General Assembly will open with a theme of "Restoring trust, managing transformation: A United Nations that delivers for all." Topics expected to be discussed include freedom of navigation in key shipping routes, the impact of the Iran war on global oil prices, and efforts to deescalate the Russia-Ukraine conflict. U.S. officials have also highlighted the importance of addressing climate change, with California Governor Gavin Newsom participating in Climate Week events in New York.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that the U.N. expects 73 heads of state and 45 heads of government, while ABC News states that nearly 130 leaders are expected to attend. ABC also mentions that the U.N. released a report criticizing the U.S. for alleged civilian casualties in Iran, which the State Department rejected. BBC and NPR do not mention this report. Additionally, ABC notes that Trump will meet with Venezuela’s Delcy Rodríguez, while FOX and NPR do not specify this meeting. NPR also states that the U.N. secretary-general is pushing for de-escalation in the Middle East and freedom of navigation, which is not mentioned in FOX or ABC.

Sources FOX News · ABC News · BBC · Axios · New York Post · NPR

Paramount May Not Leave California After All

Paramount Skydance has reached a settlement with a coalition of 12 U.S. states that had sued to block its $110 billion merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. The agreement includes commitments to increase domestic film production, produce at least 30 films annually, and establish independent editorial boards for CBS and CNN. California Attorney General Rob Bonta stated the settlement is not a vote of support for the merger but a resolution to antitrust concerns. The deal clears a major hurdle for the merger, which was previously blocked by state lawsuits and the Writers Guild of America. If the parties do not reach an agreement by October 1, Paramount will face daily penalties of $7 million.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News both report that the settlement includes a commitment to produce 30 films annually, with specific requirements for wide-release films and penalties for non-compliance. The Daily Wire and Fox Business mention a $1.5 billion investment in California production, while the Guardian and CBS News state the investment is $1.5 billion over five years. The BBC and CBS News note that the agreement includes a requirement for 20% of film production to be in the U.S. for the first two years, rising to 30% over the next three years. The Daily Wire and Straight Arrow News mention that some Paramount staffers are privately enthusiastic about relocation, while the Guardian and CBS News do not include this detail. The Guardian and CBS News report that the settlement includes a financial penalty of $30 million per film missed, while the Daily Wire and Fox Business mention a $30 million penalty per film and the possibility of selling Miramax.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Guardian · Fox Business · CBS News · BBC · Straight Arrow News

Airport operations resume after telecom outage disrupts flights across Northeast

A telecom outage caused by a severed fiber-optic cable disrupted flights at multiple major airports in the Northeast on Monday. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) paused flights at Newark Liberty International Airport, John F. Kennedy International Airport, LaGuardia Airport, Philadelphia International Airport, and Teterboro Airport. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed that telecom lines had been repaired and airport operations were resuming, though delays were expected. The FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford explained that the primary circuit in Philadelphia TRACON failed, and when switching to the backup system, a broken fiber-optic cable was discovered. Some airlines, including United Airlines, allowed customers to change flights without fees.

The incident occurred due to a construction activity in New Jersey. Amtrak initially claimed an Amtrak construction crew accidentally cut into a Verizon fiber line, but later disputed this, stating the incident happened on NJ Transit property involving a NJ Transit contractor. NJ Transit confirmed that a construction crew working on the Delco Lead project severed a fiber-optic cable, which was a backup to a primary circuit system that had failed. Verizon stated it was not responsible for the cable.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Guardian attribute the fiber cut to an Amtrak construction crew, while Fox Business, CBS News, and BBC report that NJ Transit was involved. The Guardian and The Daily Wire mention the FAA’s replacement circuit also failed, which Fox Business and CBS News do not. The Guardian and The Daily Wire include details about the GAO report on FAA cybersecurity vulnerabilities, which are absent in the other accounts. The Guardian and The Daily Wire also note that the FAA moved the Philadelphia TRACON to a new fiber-optic link in 2025, a detail not included in the other accounts.

Sources The Daily Wire · Fox Business · The Guardian · CBS News · BBC · Just The News

18 suspects accused in the 2021 killing of Haiti's president being extradited to U.S.

Eighteen suspects arrested in the July 2021 killing of Haitian President Jovenel Moïse were being extradited to the U.S. on Sunday, according to U.S. Attorney Jason Reding Quiñones. The suspects will face trial in Florida, with Quiñones stating that the extradition marks the next phase in the pursuit of accountability for the assassination. The suspects include 17 Colombian soldiers and Joseph Badio, a former Haitian Ministry of Justice employee. Some of the suspects were arrested in Haiti in 2023 after being on the run for over two years. The Haitian government stated it surrendered the suspects to allow for their trial, noting that the judicial process in Haiti continues to face significant difficulties.

The FBI and other agencies remain committed to holding all individuals who allegedly profited from the assassination accountable. FBI Special Agent in Charge Brett Skiles confirmed multiple arrests in the U.S. and abroad on Sunday. The suspects were transported in a military plane from Haiti to Florida. The wife of one of the suspects, Diana Marcela Arbeláez Llano, expressed surprise at the sudden extradition, noting the poor conditions in Haitian prisons and the health concerns of the detained Colombians.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and Al Jazeera both mention that the suspects include 17 Colombian soldiers and Joseph Badio, while FOX News adds that the suspects were "operatives from Haiti and Colombia" and that some allegedly posed as DEA agents. The Guardian and Al Jazeera state that the assassination was carried out by "military-trained Colombian mercenaries," while FOX News specifies that the suspects "were making their first appearance in federal court in Miami" and that the plot was "planned, financed, conspired, here in Miami." The Guardian also notes that the U.S. has jurisdiction over plots hatched on American soil, while FOX News claims the conspiracy was "largely organized and financed in South Florida" and that the defendants used fraudulent PPP loans to fund the mission.

Sources NPR · Washington Examiner · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · FOX News

Appeals court ruling tees up Supreme Court battle for GOP-friendly Missouri map

A federal appeals court in Missouri ruled Monday that the state’s 2025 congressional map must be used in the November elections, setting the issue for another hearing before the Supreme Court. The map, which favors Republicans, was passed by the legislature in 2025 and used in the state primaries last month. Critics argue it should be subject to a statewide referendum before use and that the 2022 map should be used instead. The 8th Circuit Court of Appeals stated that reverting to the 2022 map would cause confusion for voters who have already used the 2025 map in primaries.

The ruling comes after the Supreme Court twice blocked the use of the 2025 map, but the 8th Circuit judges interpreted the latest decision as a call to action. The appeals court delayed the effect of its order for a week to allow time for an appeal. The Supreme Court had previously allowed a decision from the Missouri Supreme Court to take effect, which ordered the use of the 2022 map. However, the 8th Circuit judges argued that using the 2022 map would violate the Constitution.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner states that the appeals court ruled the 2025 map must be used in November, while The Guardian notes that the ruling has no immediate impact because the federal appellate panel delayed the effect of its order for a week. The Newsweek account adds that the appeals court's decision was made by three judges, including those nominated by Trump, and that the Supreme Court had granted an emergency appeal blocking the new map. The Daily Caller mentions that the 8th Circuit Court of Appeals ruled the map must be used despite the Supreme Court's earlier blocking, and that the decision was supported by two judges, with a third dissenting on standing. Just The News reports that the new map, which was used in the state primaries, favors Republicans in a 7-1 split by erasing Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver’s district, a detail not included in the Washington Examiner or The Guardian accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · Just The News · The Guardian · Newsweek · The Daily Caller

Friedrich Merz vows to stay in office after CDU ‘disaster’ in German state elections

In two state elections held on Sunday, Germany’s Christian Democratic Union (CDU) suffered its worst-ever results, with the party failing to meet the 5% threshold needed to enter the state parliament in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania. The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) emerged as the strongest party in both Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Berlin, with the AfD securing 38.3% in the former and the far-left Die Linke taking 25% in Berlin. Merz, the CDU leader and German chancellor, called the results a “disaster” but vowed to continue his reform agenda, including tax and welfare changes, which he described as the antidote to authoritarianism. The AfD’s co-leader, Alice Weidel, criticized Merz for dragging the CDU down and suggested his party would face consequences for its losses.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Axios both report that the CDU received 4.9% in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, below the 5% threshold, while the BBC reports the CDU fell short of the threshold but does not specify the exact percentage. The Guardian and NPR both state that the AfD received 38.2% in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, while Axios reports 38%, and the BBC does not specify the exact percentage. The Guardian and BBC mention that the AfD is unlikely to form a government due to a “firewall” of non-cooperation, while the NPR and Axios accounts do not explicitly mention this firewall. The Guardian and BBC attribute the claim that the AfD is “unlikely to find coalition partners” to the BBC, while the other accounts do not attribute this claim to a specific outlet.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · BBC · Al Jazeera · Axios

Massachusetts Governor Proposes Postpartum Home Visits and Mental Health Support

Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey (D) has proposed expanding the state’s Welcome Family program to include home visits for new parents within days or weeks of childbirth. The initiative aims to provide healthcare providers with an opportunity to assess the need for additional support and resources. Healey also proposed an additional $250,000 to the Massachusetts Child Psychiatry Access Program for Moms, which offers mental health support to providers caring for pregnant and postpartum patients. The program is described as voluntary, though some critics argue it may become mandatory. The proposal has reignited debates over postpartum mental health following the Lindsay Clancy case, where Clancy was accused of killing her three children in January 2023. A mistrial was declared in September 2026 after the jury failed to reach a verdict.

The initiative includes screenings for depression and other mental health concerns to identify families in need of extra support. Healey emphasized that postpartum mental health conditions are real medical issues and that no mother should feel ashamed to seek help. The program is part of a broader effort to expand postpartum care and mental health support across the state, with proposed funding of $2 million to provide universal access to the Welcome Family program.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Caller reports that the proposed funding includes $2 million for universal access to the Welcome Family program, while The Daily Wire and FOX News mention the $250,000 addition to the Massachusetts Child Psychiatry Access Program for Moms. The Daily Caller states that the Welcome Family program currently serves 3,000 births annually, a detail not included in the other accounts. FOX News highlights the online backlash against the proposal, including quotes from conservative figures like Rep. Nancy Mace and Sen. Tim Sheehy, which are absent in the other accounts. The Daily Wire notes that the program is voluntary, a point also made by FOX News, but The Daily Caller does not specify the program’s voluntariness.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller · FOX News
