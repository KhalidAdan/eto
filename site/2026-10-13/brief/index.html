Trump privately expresses doubts about livestreaming execution

President Trump has privately shared misgivings about livestreaming the firing-squad execution of former Army Maj. Nidal Hasan, according to four people who have spoken with the president. Trump has been receiving "negative input" about the idea from a bipartisan chorus of lawmakers and advisers, a senior administration official said. A livestream of Hasan's execution on Dec. 3 "might not happen," Trump told one confidant, who doesn't work in the administration and shared the comment with Axios. "He didn't sound particularly enthusiastic" about webcasting the execution, the source said. "That's why he's calling around, doing the Trump thing, asking people what they think." Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the plan on Real America's Voice. "We'll make sure that people are able to watch it — that it's public — because people need to understand that there's serious consequences for these types of things," Hegseth said. Trump later publicly shifted his position, initially supporting the idea, then expressing nuance, and finally stating he would make the final decision.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Trump has been receiving "negative input" from a bipartisan chorus of lawmakers and advisers, while The Free Press and Al Jazeera do not mention this input. The Daily Wire and The Dispatch do not mention Trump's private doubts, instead focusing on the moral and public implications of the execution. The Free Press and Al Jazeera both describe Hasan's attack as a mass shooting that killed 14 people, while Axios and The Daily Wire state 13 people were killed. The Free Press and Al Jazeera also note that Hasan was paralyzed from the waist down during the attack, a detail not included in Axios or The Daily Wire.

Trump faces growing backlash over plans to import diesel from Russia

President Donald Trump announced a deal with Russia to supply diesel to the U.S. and global markets, including 300,000 tonnes immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November, and 1 million tonnes afterward. The agreement comes amid rising fuel prices and ahead of the midterm elections. Some lawmakers, including seven senators, criticized the deal as violating U.S. laws that prohibit Russian energy imports. Russia's Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak confirmed the export volume could reach three million tonnes per month in the future. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called the deal a "gift to Putin" that funds Russia's war against Ukraine. The U.S. Treasury is suspending sanctions on Russian diesel exports until 7 April as part of the deal, but other assets remain frozen.

Republican Thomas Massie supported the deal, despite his adversarial relationship with Trump, while others like Thom Tillis argued the agreement undermines efforts to impose sanctions on Russia. The deal has also drawn criticism from a former NATO commander, who warned it could provide Putin with funds to continue the war in Ukraine. Analysts question whether Russia can deliver the promised quantities of diesel and whether it will significantly lower prices for American consumers.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that the U.S. uses approximately 3.6 million barrels of diesel a day and that even if Russia delivers its promised 300,000 tonnes, it would only cover half of daily consumption. The Hill and FOX News both mention the 300,000 tonnes immediate release but differ in the context of the deal's timing, with FOX News noting the deal preceded Trump's "energy ceasefire" announcement. NPR states that Senator Jeanne Shaheen doubts the deal's authenticity and questions whether Russia has enough diesel to supply the U.S. ABC News does not mention the 300,000 tonnes figure or the 500,000 tonnes in November, focusing instead on the broader context of the deal and its political implications.

Sources BBC · The Hill · Washington Examiner · FOX News · NPR · ABC News

Hundreds of supermarkets in France hit by ‘unprecedented’ cyberattack disrupts food access for thousands.

Hundreds of French supermarkets hit by cyberattack causing fridge temperatures to rise to 50°C

A cyberattack on refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets caused fridges and freezers to heat up to as high as 50°C (122F), spoiling food and causing fires at several stores. The attack targeted software provided by Danish engineering firm Danfoss, which allows retailers to manage their cold units remotely. Affected retailers include Intermarché, Cooperative U, Carrefour, Monoprix, and E Leclerc. The Paris prosecutor’s office described the incident as a “large-scale” cyberattack affecting “connected refrigerators in several major food retailers across France.” The attack led to food losses and prompted Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu to order an investigation by France’s National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI). The attack also prompted calls for greater cybersecurity measures in the food supply chain.

The attack disrupted refrigeration systems, leading to the destruction of stocks of fresh and frozen products. Intermarché CEO Thierry Cotillard estimated the losses at “tens of millions of euros nationwide in a single day.” Some stores had to throw away all affected products, while others were working to restore normal operations. The attack also caused issues for staff arriving at stores, with some encountering hot air from freezers and even small fires. The full scale of the attack remains unclear, but estimates suggest around 400 stores were affected out of 15,000 in France.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that Intermarché said at least 300 shops of its Netto brand were targeted, along with more than 100 Cooperative U outlets, while BBC states that Intermarché and its Netto brand affected 180 stores, with another 100 urgently disconnected from the internet. Al Jazeera also mentions that Carrefour said 80 supermarkets were affected, while BBC reports Carrefour said fridges in 80 of its 6,000 stores were hacked. Al Jazeera notes that Monoprix said about 5 percent of its roughly 490 stores were hit, while BBC states Monoprix said 5% of its 400-plus stores were affected. Al Jazeera reports that E Leclerc said several of its stores were targeted, while BBC does not specify the number of E Leclerc stores affected. BBC mentions that Danfoss did not confirm how warm the fridges and freezers could become, while Al Jazeera states that the fridges warmed to 50°C. BBC also reports that two supermarkets in the Alsace region reported losses of €270,000, which Al Jazeera does not mention.

Sources Al Jazeera · BBC
