Iowa braces for controlled bomb blast: ‘We had only five days of warning’

The U.S. Army has scheduled a controlled detonation of 68 barrels of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, on Wednesday. The operation, delayed multiple times due to public outcry and legal challenges, is now set for 10 a.m. EST. Officials claim the detonation is the only viable option to prevent an uncontrolled explosion of the unstable material, which was discovered to be dangerously unstable earlier this year. The blast is expected to release toxic chemicals in a 100-mile radius, with residents within five miles advised to evacuate and those within 20 miles told to stay indoors. The Iowa Department of Health and Human Services recommends staying indoors for 24-48 hours after the blast, conflicting with the timing of Thanksgiving, a major travel day.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Mother Jones both mention that the initial public announcement was made on 15 November, five days before the initially scheduled detonation, but The Guardian notes that neighboring communities were not informed until the army publicly announced plans on 15 November. Mother Jones states that the initial public announcement was made on 15 November, five days before the initially scheduled detonation, but does not specify that neighboring communities were not informed until then. The Hill and CBS News both mention that the detonation was initially planned for last Friday but was rescheduled three times, while The Guardian and Mother Jones also note the rescheduling but do not specify the exact number of times. The Guardian and CBS News both mention that the detonation is scheduled for Wednesday, but CBS News specifies the time as 9 a.m. and the date as the day before Thanksgiving, while The Guardian does not specify the exact time or date.

Sources The Guardian · Mother Jones · ABC News · CBS News · Newsweek · The Hill

Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade postponed for first time in history

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, marking its 100th anniversary, has been postponed for the first time in its history due to severe weather. The event, which typically takes place on Thanksgiving Day, will now occur on Saturday at 8:30 a.m. The decision was made after consultations with city and agency officials, citing safety concerns related to an approaching storm. Macy's stated that the safety of participants and spectators is their top priority. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani acknowledged the inconvenience but emphasized that safety must come first. The National Weather Service has issued high wind warnings, with gusts potentially reaching 60 mph, which could make it unsafe for the balloons to fly.

The parade, which features giant inflatables and live performances, has been a New York tradition since 1924. This year's event was expected to include 100 inflatables, including new designs. The balloons are typically inflated on the Upper West Side, but due to the storm, they will be deflated and stored in New Jersey until Saturday. The NYPD has confirmed that there are no known specific or credible threats to the parade, though an increased police presence will be on hand for security.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that the storm is a "powerful coastal storm" with wind gusts up to 65 mph, while ABC News states the forecast calls for gusts up to 40 mph. Axios mentions wind gusts up to 55 mph, and BBC reports gusts potentially reaching 60 mph. The Daily Wire and Newsweek both cite the National Weather Service's warning about wind gusts exceeding 60 mph. CBS News and ABC News differ in the number of times the parade has been postponed, with CBS stating it's the first time and ABC noting it was only canceled three times during World War II. The Daily Wire also mentions the parade was canceled from 1942 through 1944, which is consistent with other accounts. Newsweek adds that Mayor Mamdani will miss the rescheduled parade due to a scheduled surgery, a detail not included in other accounts.

Sources CBS News · Axios · ABC News · BBC · The Daily Wire · Newsweek

FedEx plane overshoots Miami runway, causing brief suspension of flights at airport

A FedEx aircraft overshot a runway at Miami International Airport on Wednesday evening, prompting the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to issue a ground stop. The incident occurred around 8:40 p.m., according to multiple outlets, and led to the closure of all but one runway at the airport. Flights resumed approximately 90 minutes later, though delays persisted into Thursday morning. No injuries were reported, and the FAA is investigating the cause. The event took place on Thanksgiving Eve, one of the busiest travel days of the year.

The FAA confirmed that flights would be resuming shortly after the incident, though some sources noted that the ground stop remained in effect for several hours. The airport advised passengers to check directly with their airlines for the latest updates. The incident occurred near the site of a previous runway overrun in September, where a Prime Air cargo jet crashed into a road, killing five people.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that the FAA temporarily halted flights bound for Miami International Airport just after 20:15 local time, while New York Post and FOX News state the ground stop was issued around 8:40 p.m. Washington Examiner mentions the ground stop was issued at 8:16 p.m. EST, and ABC News states the incident happened around 8:40 p.m. The New York Post and FOX News attribute the incident to a FedEx cargo plane veering off the runway, while BBC and ABC News refer to it as an airliner that ran past the runway. ABC News also notes that the FedEx plane landed on a different runway than the one involved in the September incident, while FOX News and Just The News mention the FedEx plane ran off Runway 26R.

Sources BBC · New York Post · Washington Examiner · FOX News · ABC News · Just The News

Judge criticizes Trump administration for weaponizing law enforcement in Hunter Biden probe

A federal judge has issued a scathing rebuke of the Trump administration for what he called the "weaponization" of federal law enforcement in the Hunter Biden probe. Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui dismissed a criminal case against two former FBI agents, Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides, for allegedly lying to Congress about their involvement in the investigation. Faruqui criticized the Department of Justice for rushing the prosecution, which he said was motivated by political pressure from the White House. The judge also highlighted the involvement of FBI Director Kash Patel, who assigned the case to the FBI's Kansas City office after President Trump directed the agency to investigate any slights related to the 2020 election.

In a separate ruling, a federal judge blocked the Federal Communications Commission from proceeding with an early review of ABC's broadcast licenses, calling the move an unconstitutional retaliation for the network's speech. U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan found that the FCC's actions were motivated by President Trump's public calls for the agency to strip ABC of its licenses. The judge ruled that the early review created a chilling effect on free speech and that the FCC had weaponized its licensing authority to retaliate against broadcasters for their editorial decisions.

Where the accounts differ

Newsweek, however, reports that the judge blocked the FCC from reviewing ABC's licenses and called the move an unconstitutional retaliation for the network's speech. NPR and CBS News also describe the same ruling, but NPR adds that the judge cited specific examples of Trump's public calls for stripping ABC's licenses, including one in July 2026. Washington Examiner and Newsweek both mention the judge's decision to block the FCC review, but only Newsweek provides details about the judge's reasoning and the specific examples of Trump's rhetoric.

US preparing to unveil plan for new tariffs on global trading partners

The US administration has proposed new tariffs on imports from 14 major trading partners, including China, the European Union, and several Asian countries. The tariffs are being introduced under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, following a legal challenge to previous tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. The proposed duties aim to address what the administration calls unfair foreign manufacturing practices that disadvantage American producers. The tariffs are not expected to take effect until next year and will undergo public consultation and hearings before finalization.

The new tariffs are part of a broader trade policy that includes investigations into the trade practices of various countries. The administration argues that foreign governments have supported domestic manufacturing through subsidies and other policies that drive down prices and weaken American industries. The proposed tariffs vary by country, with China facing up to 20% in some cases, which could be stacked with existing tariffs to reach 40%. Other countries, such as Vietnam, face up to 35%, while the EU, Japan, and South Korea face up to 15% or 18%. The administration claims the tariffs are based on trade agreements and investigations, and they are not automatically stacked on top of existing duties.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that the new tariffs will not take effect until next year and will go through rounds of public consultation and hearings. The Daily Wire states that a public hearing is scheduled to begin January 25, 2027, and no effective date has been announced. The Guardian mentions that the tariffs could go into effect as early as January, with hearings on January 25. Fox Business notes that the review and hearing process for the broader overcapacity duties could push final implementation into January. Axios states that the levies could take effect as early as January.

Sources BBC · The Daily Wire · The Guardian · Fox Business · Axios

Aaron Rodgers released by Steelers after public clash with coach Mike McCarthy

Aaron Rodgers was released by the Pittsburgh Steelers on Wednesday after a public fallout with head coach Mike McCarthy. Rodgers, a four-time NFL Most Valuable Player, was benched as the starting quarterback on Tuesday and did not return to the team. During an appearance on the Pat McAfee Show, Rodgers accused McCarthy of sleeping during meetings and missing key team gatherings. McCarthy admitted to an "embarrassing moment" when he broke a halftime huddle with "1-2-3 Packers" during a loss to the Eagles. The Steelers confirmed Rodgers' release in a brief statement, ending his two-year stint with the team.

Rodgers claimed he discovered his benching during a phone call with teammate Pat Freiermuth, who informed him of the decision. He criticized McCarthy for failing to engage with defensive players and for not keeping up with film study. McCarthy stated that Rodgers had presented an "ultimatum" that was outside his job description, leading to the release. The Steelers named Mason Rudolph as the new starter for Friday's game against the Broncos.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that McCarthy admitted to the "embarrassing moment" during a press conference, while The Guardian states McCarthy admitted to the "Packers" mishap during his own news conference. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention that Rodgers claimed McCarthy told a Steelers staffer he blamed Rodgers for his firing by the Packers in 2018, a claim not included in BBC, NPR, or Newsweek. NPR and The Guardian note that Rodgers said he learned of his benching from Freiermuth, while BBC and Newsweek state Rodgers was informed during a phone call with Freiermuth. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention that Rodgers said McCarthy had watched only clips of game film three days before a game, a detail not in BBC, NPR, or Newsweek.

Melania Trump reunites seventh group of Ukrainian and Russian children with families

First Lady Melania Trump has helped return a seventh group of Ukrainian and Russian children to their families, bringing the total number of children in these reunifications to 43, according to the White House. The first lady said the reunification of nine children with their families took place with the “full cooperation” of Russian and Ukrainian leaders. She has made helping children in conflict zones a key focus of her efforts, including hosting a U.N. Security Council meeting on the issue earlier this year. Her work began in August 2025, when she had President Donald Trump hand-deliver a letter to Russian President Vladimir Putin during a summit in Alaska, calling for peace in Ukraine.

Melania Trump’s efforts have included securing a six-day ceasefire in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky in southern Ukraine, which allowed for the evacuation of 800 people and the delivery of humanitarian aid. The ceasefire, which was in effect from October 27 to November 1, marked a significant truce since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. The first lady also hosted Russian and Ukrainian officials at the White House to mark the milestone, crediting both leaders for their cooperation.

Where the accounts differ

Just The News and New York Post also report the same numbers. However, New York Post mentions that Russia is estimated to have kidnapped more than 35,000 children from Ukraine since the start of the war, a figure not included in the other accounts. Huawei found guilty of stealing US trade secrets but acquitted of racketeering

Huawei was convicted on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud, but acquitted of racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice. The case, which spanned a two-month trial in Brooklyn, involved allegations that Huawei engaged in deceptive practices to gain a competitive edge. Prosecutors claimed Huawei lied to banks about its business dealings in Iran and stole technology from American companies. Huawei's defense argued that the case was built on the assumption that the company was a criminal organization, and that its success was due to innovation and hard work. The verdict follows a long-standing legal battle that began in 2019, after the arrest of Huawei's CFO, Meng Wanzhou, in Canada.

The convictions could result in significant financial penalties for Huawei, though the company has stated it will appeal. Huawei's defense attorney, Brian Heberlig, called the racketeering acquittal a "profound rejection" of the U.S. narrative about the company. He argued that the prosecution cherry-picked incidents to build a 20-year conspiracy. The case did not involve individual defendants, though Meng Wanzhou was previously charged with misleading HSBC about Huawei's Iran business.

Where the accounts differ

BBC and The Daily Caller both state Huawei was acquitted of racketeering conspiracy, but The Daily Caller adds that Huawei was also acquitted of conspiracy to obstruct justice. The Daily Wire and Fox Business mention the acquittal of conspiracy to obstruct justice, while The Daily Wire also specifies that the convictions included stealing T-Mobile’s testing robot technology. Fox Business and The Daily Wire include details about the role of Skycom, a subsidiary used to conduct business in Iran, which The Daily Caller omits. The Daily Wire and Fox Business name specific U.S. companies whose trade secrets were allegedly stolen, including Cisco, T-Mobile, and Quintel, while BBC and The Daily Caller do not specify these companies. The Daily Wire and Fox Business mention the role of the U.S. Attorney General in the case, while BBC and The Daily Caller attribute statements to the U.S. Attorney General differently.

Sources BBC · The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller · Fox Business

Gaza issues meningitis epidemic alert after steep rise in cases

The Gaza health ministry has issued an epidemic alert due to a sharp rise in meningitis infections, with over 270 new cases reported in October. Dr. Nidal Ghoneim, a senior official at the ministry, stated that the spread of the disease poses a threat to hundreds of thousands of people, including children. Health organizations attribute the rise in infections to the destruction of infrastructure, overcrowded shelters, and contaminated drinking water, exacerbated by winter flooding and restrictions on humanitarian aid. The UN’s humanitarian agency (OCHA) previously reported 413 suspected cases of meningitis in Gaza between September 27 and October 31, including 294 viral and 119 bacterial cases. About 68% were recorded in Gaza City. The World Health Organization noted that microbial contamination in water samples had risen from less than 6% in January to nearly 20% in August.

A five-year-old boy, Omar Abu Fayed, has been admitted to the isolation ward at Gaza City’s Rantisi Children’s Hospital multiple times for meningitis. His mother, Zakia Abu Fayed, reported that Omar’s symptoms returned after each discharge, leading to repeated hospitalizations. She believes Omar may have contracted the infection at kindergarten, though the source remains undetermined. The ministry reported 272 cases in October, more than eight times the normal monthly rate of 30 to 50 cases. It also noted 15 deaths from meningitis since the beginning of 2026, including five between August and October. The ministry attributed the epidemic outbreak to the Israeli occupation’s prevention of essential supplies.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports 272 meningitis infections in October, more than eight times the normal monthly rate of 30 to 50 cases, and 15 deaths since the beginning of this year. Al Jazeera states that the ministry reported 15 deaths from the disease since the beginning of 2026, including five between August and October. The Guardian attributes the epidemic outbreak to the Israeli occupation’s prevention of essential supplies, while Al Jazeera does not mention this attribution. The Guardian also reports that the UN’s OCHA had previously reported 413 suspected cases of meningitis in Gaza between September 27 and October 31, including 294 viral and 119 bacterial cases, while Al Jazeera does not include these specific numbers.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera
