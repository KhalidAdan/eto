Trump Just Offered the Press Secretary Gig to UFC’s Dana White

President Donald Trump announced on Saturday that he had offered the position of White House press secretary to Dana White, CEO of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC). In a post on Truth Social, Trump described White as "the Great Dana White, a violent Fighter and Warrior" and requested that he bring along several UFC fighters as assistants. The fighters named included Islam Makhachev, Khabib Nurmagomedov, Justin Gaethje, Daniel Cormier, Amanda Nunes, and Kayla Harris, though the latter’s name was misspelled. Trump claimed the fighters would "defend us against the Lunatic Left." White, who has no governmental experience, has been a long-time friend and business associate of Trump, having owned UFC since 2001. The offer comes as Trump’s administration is undergoing significant changes following the midterm elections, with White House press secretary positions remaining vacant after the brief tenure of Katie Zacharia.

White, who was in London for a boxing event, stated he planned to meet with Trump on Monday at the White House. He did not clarify whether he was seriously considering the position, though he expressed ambitions to expand his Zuffa Boxing organization. The White House and UFC did not immediately respond to inquiries about the offer.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Hill both report that Trump misspelled Kayla Harrison’s name as "Kayla Harris." Al Jazeera and The Hill note that Trump initially posted the offer on Truth Social, while Washington Examiner reports that Trump also posted an animated AI-generated video of UFC fighters attacking the press corps. FOX News, however, does not mention the press secretary offer at all, instead reporting on a separate claim by Trump about ordering a DOJ investigation into Democrats.

Sources Mother Jones · NPR · Al Jazeera · The Hill · Washington Examiner · FOX News

Jensen Huang spy scandal rocks AI race

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, prompting an independent investigation by the company. Huang, who has led Nvidia since its founding in 1993, apologized to his family and employees, acknowledging his infidelity. The company stated it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. U.S. officials believe the woman, identified as Alice Huang, was recruited by Beijing's intelligence agency to gather information and potentially influence Nvidia's business dealings with China. The scandal has led to questions about the White House's awareness of the situation and its impact on U.S.-China relations.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Trump was briefed in 2025 and that Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw a classified intelligence memo in spring 2025. Al Jazeera and The Guardian do not specify the exact timing of the briefing but note that Trump was aware of the intelligence. Fox Business and The Guardian mention that Trump invited Huang to join his delegation to China in May, while Axios and Al Jazeera state that Huang was invited to the state dinner with Xi Jinping in September. The Guardian and Al Jazeera note that Huang described his interactions with Alice Huang as "completely normal and not unusual," while Axios and Fox Business report that Huang admitted to a romantic relationship with her.

Sources Axios · Al Jazeera · The Guardian · Fox Business · BBC · Newsweek

Gen Z voters helped Democrats win midterms with strong margins

Gen Z voters, those under 30, played a key role in Democrats' success in the 2026 midterms, according to exit polling. They carried the youngest generation by nearly 50 points, a sharp contrast to 2024 when the party lost ground with young voters. The shift is attributed to strong opposition to Trump and economic frustrations. Economic issues were the top concern for 18-29 year olds, with 43% citing it as their main issue. Over 80% of young voters said Trump's policies worsened economic conditions. Democrats also made gains with some young voters who had previously supported Trump, including white men and Latino men.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that Gen Z made up 11% of the total vote and that Democrats flipped seats in House and Senate races, with Texas Senator James Talarico winning by 60 points among Gen Z voters. Mother Jones and The Hill focus on Trump's reaction to the losses, with The Hill noting Trump's admission that his immigration policies might have been "too tough." CBS News and The Dispatch provide details on Trump's interview with Ross Douthat, where he blamed the war in Iran and gas prices for the losses. BBC highlights the internal Republican party conflict and the potential for a split between Trump loyalists and more moderate Republicans. The Dispatch also notes the shift in voter priorities, with the economy, corruption, and governance being key concerns.

Sources NPR · Mother Jones · CBS News · The Hill · BBC · The Dispatch

Trump staffing changes post-midterms signal he's battle ready

President Donald Trump announced the replacement of his chief of staff, Susie Wiles, with Stephen Miller, a hard-liner known for his role in shaping Trump's controversial policies. The move comes after a significant loss for Republicans in the midterms. Trump has expressed openness to working with Democrats, though his appointment of Miller suggests a different approach. Miller, who has been a key advisor to Trump, is described by some as a staunch ideologue and is seen as a loyal supporter of the president's agenda.

The new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, is known for his role in implementing Trump's immigration policies, including mass deportation raids. His appointment has drawn criticism from Democrats, who argue that his policies have led to significant backlash and have been associated with incidents involving the deaths of American citizens. Miller's appointment is seen as a signal of continued hardline policies and a potential escalation in the political conflict with the newly elected Democratic majority.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that Trump described the Democratic Party as a "domestic extremist organization," while The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller attribute this characterization to Stephen Miller. The Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire mention that Hakeem Jeffries claims Miller is responsible for the deaths of Alex Pretti and Renee Nicole Good, a claim not made by NPR or Axios. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller include specific details about the incidents involving Pretti and Good, which are not present in the other accounts. Axios does not mention Jeffries's comments about Miller's responsibility for the deaths, while The Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire do.

Sources NPR · Washington Examiner · New York Post · The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller · Axios

Democrats plan legislative agenda, avoid impeachment as Congress shifts power

Following the midterm elections, Democrats are preparing to take control of both the House and Senate in January. Former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton urged the party to focus on enacting their legislative agenda. Meanwhile, Democratic leaders have outlined priorities such as re-implementing Affordable Care Act provisions, undoing Medicaid cuts, and addressing Trump's tariffs. The party also plans to push for legislation that could serve as a platform for future presidential candidates. However, impeachment of Trump is not on the table, despite ongoing investigations into his actions.

The lame-duck session of Congress faces a looming government shutdown deadline on December 11, with both parties needing to reach an agreement on funding. Republicans are expected to push for a short-term extension, while Democrats may use Trump's actions in Iran as leverage. The Senate and House will also tackle the farm bill, transportation infrastructure, and a bipartisan tax package before the year ends.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that Democrats are focusing on legislative action to prepare for the 2028 presidential election, including passing bills on price gouging, Super PACs, and stock trading by lawmakers. This is not mentioned in the Washington Examiner, which only notes Clinton's call for legislative action. The Guardian also states that the Democratic party brand remains deeply unpopular, a claim not present in the Washington Examiner. The New York Post and FOX News differ in their coverage of the shutdown deadline, with the New York Post noting that Democrats may use Trump's Iran actions as leverage, while FOX News reports that some Republicans expect a short-term funding extension through February or March.

Sources Washington Examiner · The Guardian · Just The News · New York Post · The Daily Wire · FOX News

CENTCOM Launches Lifestyle Podcast Amid Ongoing Iran Conflict

The United States Central Command (CENTCOM) launched a lifestyle podcast titled "Inside CENTCOM" on Thursday, featuring Admiral Brad Cooper, the head of CENTCOM. The podcast, which has been compared to a comedic show with a military twist, includes topics such as patience, physical fitness, and personal anecdotes. Cooper discussed his love for French fries and emphasized the importance of mental and spiritual fitness during military operations. The podcast's first episode lasted 11 minutes and did not address the ongoing conflict with Iran. Meanwhile, the U.S. military has been conducting "minor strikes" against Iran's Qeshm Island, with President Trump describing the attacks as a necessary measure to protect Israel. The war in Iran has been widely criticized as a costly and unpopular endeavor, with over 3,400 civilians killed and the U.S. military spending at least $47 billion.

The war in Iran has become a focal point for political blame, with Democrats attributing the conflict's negative consequences to the Trump administration. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, a top Democrat, has publicly supported impeachment proceedings against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, calling him a "hard yes" for impeachment. Jeffries accused Hegseth of abusing his power and engineering a costly war of choice. Meanwhile, outgoing Republican Congressman Thomas Massie plans to reintroduce an impeachment resolution against Hegseth, citing violations of the War Powers Resolution of 1973. Trump has defended Hegseth, claiming he has done a "fantastic job" and intends to keep him in his position.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones reports that the podcast "fields zero questions about the war," while New York Post and The Guardian do not mention this detail. The Guardian and ABC News describe the impeachment effort as a political move, while Mother Jones and Axios note that the impeachment is part of a broader political blame game. The New York Post and The Guardian mention the potential for a diplomatic breakthrough, which is not included in Mother Jones or Axios. The Guardian and ABC News attribute the war's costs and consequences to Hegseth, while Mother Jones and Axios attribute them to the Trump administration.

Sources Mother Jones · New York Post · BBC · Axios · The Guardian · ABC News

US launches task force to disrupt Iran’s land-based supply routes

The United States military’s Central Command (CENTCOM) announced on Saturday the establishment of a new task force, Task Force Iron Gate, aimed at blocking Iran’s land-based supply routes. The task force will work with US government agencies to prevent the movement of advanced weapons systems, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials, and illegal revenue streams across Iran’s borders and the Caspian Sea. CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper stated that the task force will reinforce the existing naval blockade of Iranian ports in the Strait of Hormuz. A commercial cargo vessel, the M/V Huda, was disabled in the Gulf of Oman as part of the ongoing blockade. Iran’s official news agency IRNA reported explosions on Qeshm Island and in other cities near the Strait of Hormuz, while the IRGC Navy reported two oil tankers struck mines near the strait.

Michael Mulroy, a former US deputy assistant defence secretary for the Middle East, told Al Jazeera that the task force’s role will be to identify where weapons systems are coming in and how they could be cut off. He suggested that if President Trump orders an escalation, it would likely involve attacks on economic and infrastructure targets. Meanwhile, Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) spokesperson Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi confirmed that Iran had lifted its previous 2,000-kilometer ballistic missile-range restriction, according to The Associated Press.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that Iran’s IRGC Navy said two oil tankers struck mines near the Strait of Hormuz, while FOX News attributes the mine strikes to the IRGC Navy but does not mention the tankers. Al Jazeera also notes that Trump downplayed strikes on Qeshm Island as “minor,” while FOX News does not mention this. Washington Examiner does not report on the mine strikes or Trump’s comments. FOX News reports that Iran lifted its ballistic missile-range limit and that Mohebbi warned of new weapons that would “astonish the entire world,” which is not mentioned in Al Jazeera or Washington Examiner. Al Jazeera and Washington Examiner do not mention the missile-range limit or Mohebbi’s comments about new weapons.

Sources Al Jazeera · FOX News · Washington Examiner

Dave Chappelle Says He ‘Feels Better’ Since Charlie Kirk’s Murder

Comedian Dave Chappelle made comments during a stand-up set in New York City on Friday that he “feels better” since conservative activist Charlie Kirk was fatally shot in September 2025. Chappelle told the audience, “Ever since that guy Charlie Kirk got murdered … and I shouldn’t say this publicly but, I feel better.” He also said, “I know I shouldn’t say that, but it’s just a feeling.” Chappelle has reportedly made similar remarks at other events during his “Karmageddon” tour. Charlie Kirk was shot at Utah Valley University, where he was speaking at an outdoor event.

Dave Chappelle’s comments came after leftist podcasters Jennifer Welch and Angie “Pumps” Sullivan discussed “digging up” Charlie Kirk’s body on their podcast. The hosts laughed at the idea of “digging up Charlie Kirk and doing it again,” which led to sponsors dropping their support. The pair apologized, stating they regretted laughing at the joke and pledged to support gun safety reform.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and New York Post both report that Chappelle said he “feels better” since Kirk’s murder, while Newsweek provides more context about the broader controversies in Chappelle’s career, including his 2005 departure from his comedy show, 2017 transgender jokes, 2019 LGBTQ-related material, and 2021 remarks about transgender people. The Daily Wire and New York Post attribute Chappelle’s comments to a stand-up set in New York City, while Newsweek specifies the event was at Madison Square Garden. The Daily Wire and New York Post also mention that Chappelle previously spoke about Kirk in his Netflix special “The Unstoppable,” while Newsweek does not include this detail. The Daily Wire and New York Post attribute the claim that Chappelle “shouldn’t say this publicly” to Chappelle himself, while Newsweek does not attribute the quote.

Sources The Daily Wire · Newsweek · New York Post · ABC News
