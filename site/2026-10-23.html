Ramaswamy controversy pits Trump, Vance against Ohio GOP establishment

Vivek Ramaswamy, the Republican gubernatorial candidate in Ohio, faced backlash for an ad featuring Amy Acton’s estranged mother, Donna Arthur, who criticized Acton’s past. The ad, which claims Acton lied about her history, has drawn criticism from some Ohio Republicans, including Gov. Mike DeWine and Sen. Jon Husted. Acton’s brother stated that the family was estranged from their mother due to her past relationship with the man who allegedly sexually abused Acton as a child. Trump and Vice President JD Vance have defended Ramaswamy, with Trump calling DeWine a “RINO” and urging Ramaswamy to continue the ad. Meanwhile, other Ohio Republicans, including former Gov. John Kasich and former Sen. Rob Portman, have criticized the ad as crossing the line. The controversy has also put Husted in an awkward position, as he faces a tough race against Democratic Senate nominee Sherrod Brown.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and Just The News both mention Trump calling DeWine a “RINO,” but The Hill adds that Trump said Ramaswamy “should keep it up” in a Truth Social post, while Just The News attributes this statement to Trump directly. The Hill and Washington Examiner both note that Husted said he “would not have run” the ad, but The Hill adds that Husted’s statement was made to the Associated Press, while Washington Examiner attributes it to the same source. The Hill and Just The News both describe the ad as featuring Acton’s mother insinuating she has mental health issues, but Just The News adds that the ad claims Acton “lies about her past” and “needs help.” The Hill and Washington Examiner both mention Acton’s brother’s statement about the family estrangement, but The Hill adds that the estrangement began when Acton was 12, while Washington Examiner does not specify the age.

Sources The Hill · Mother Jones · Washington Examiner · Newsweek · Just The News · FOX News

Feds Arrest Powerful Virginia Democrat In Cannabis Corruption Probe

Federal agents arrested Virginia Democrat powerbroker Louise Lucas in a “large-scale” racketeering and money laundering conspiracy tied to a cannabis business she co-owns. Lucas, a state senator and one of the most powerful figures in Virginia’s Democratic Party, surrendered to authorities Thursday, according to the Justice Department. The 82-year-old was indicted on five counts: racketeering conspiracy, conspiracy to distribute and possess with intent to distribute marijuana, conspiracy to launder money, conspiracy to commit honest services fraud, and maintaining a drug-involved premises. Six others, including Lucas’ daughters and grandson, were also charged and arrested in connection with the alleged scheme.

The indictment, unsealed on Thursday, charges Lucas and six other defendants, including several of her relatives, with drug trafficking, money laundering, and bribery. Federal prosecutors allege that Lucas led a multi-year scheme to enrich her family through a marijuana enterprise, using her position as chairwoman of the Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee to shield the operation from state law enforcement. The FBI raided Lucas’ office and a cannabis dispensary linked to her in May, and the probe into Lucas, who represents the city of Portsmouth, was launched during former President Joe Biden’s administration.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Guardian both report that Lucas was indicted on five counts, while The Daily Caller and CBS News state she faces five of eight charges. The Daily Wire and The Guardian attribute the claim about Lucas accumulating nearly $5 million in ill-gotten gains to FBI Director Kash Patel, while The Daily Caller and CBS News attribute it to a law enforcement source. The Guardian and CBS News mention that Lucas was charged with conspiracy to commit money laundering, while The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller do not specify the exact charges beyond the indictment. The Daily Wire and The Guardian note that Lucas was charged with maintaining a drug-involved premises, which is not mentioned in The Daily Caller or CBS News. The Daily Wire and The Guardian attribute the quote about “I’ve gotten too old to not be greedy” to a February 2026 conversation with an unindicted co-conspirator, while The Daily Caller and CBS News do not attribute the quote to a specific source.

Wedgewood Polls Admits to Fabricating Data in Polling Scandal

Wedgewood Polls, a polling firm that had its data cited by major media outlets and polling aggregators, admitted to fabricating survey results in a statement released on social media. The firm claimed the project was a "social experiment" designed to expose vulnerabilities in the polling industry. The New York Times and Decision Desk HQ both stated they removed Wedgewood's polls from their databases after the admission. The firm's data was included in several major outlets, including The Washington Examiner, The Guardian, and The New York Times.

The scandal has raised concerns about the integrity of polling data and how it is vetted by media and aggregators. Some candidates, like Michigan state senator Mallory McMorrow, claimed that Wedgewood's polls influenced their campaign strategies, leading to a drop in donations and volunteer morale. The firm's data was also cited in the Wisconsin gubernatorial race, where socialist Francesca Hong was shown as having an edge over Republican candidate Tom Tiffany.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The New York Post mention that Wedgewood claimed to have two members, one progressive and one conservative, while The Guardian and Straight Arrow News note that the firm's website listed no employee names, phone number, or location. The Daily Wire and The New York Post also report that Wedgewood admitted to fabricating data and claimed it was an experiment, while The Guardian and The Washington Examiner describe the firm as a "shady outfit" and a "fraudulent polling firm."

Biden-Appointed Judge Blocks DHS Noncitizen Voting Investigations in Nine States

A federal judge appointed by former President Joe Biden issued a preliminary injunction Wednesday, halting the Trump administration’s investigation into suspected noncitizen voting in nine states. The order, issued by U.S. District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín, restricts DHS from conducting field operations targeting individuals who are known, suspected, or reasonably should be suspected of being U.S. citizens. The ruling applies to California, Connecticut, Georgia, Missouri, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, Washington, and Wisconsin, and remains in effect until Election Day on Nov. 3. The judge paused her order until 5 p.m. Friday to allow the administration time to appeal.

Martínez-Olguín ruled that the methods used by DHS to identify potential noncitizen voters, including state voter rolls, commercial databases, and AI tools, were unreliable and could lead to the wrongful identification of U.S. citizens. She found that the initiative likely violated a federal law prohibiting the intimidation of individuals who assist or encourage others to vote. The plaintiffs in the case include the California Nurses Association, National Nurses United, the Voto Latino Foundation, and local governments in San Francisco and Santa Clara County.

The Department of Homeland Security defended its methods, stating that agents use multiple sources to verify identities and eliminate false positives. A spokesperson said the agency is focused on identifying and removing noncitizens who vote, while noting it has no plans to investigate individuals believed to be U.S. citizens.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and FOX News both mention that the judge was born in Mexico and nominated by Biden, while The Guardian and CBS News refer to her as a Mexican-born judge. The Daily Wire and CBS News note that the judge worked for the American Civil Liberties Union, while FOX News explicitly states she worked for the ACLU. The Guardian and CBS News describe the judge as having worked for the ACLU’s Immigrants’ Rights Project and the National Immigration Law Center, while The Daily Wire and FOX News attribute her prior work to the ACLU. The Daily Wire and CBS News mention the judge’s confirmation by the Senate in February 2023, while FOX News does not specify the confirmation date. The Guardian and CBS News state that the judge’s ruling was based on the unreliability of the data used by DHS, while FOX News adds that the judge found the methods could constitute intimidation due to the use of armed agents.

Sources The Daily Wire · NPR · The Guardian · CBS News · Axios · FOX News

Department of Justice opens criminal investigation into Corey Lewandowski

The Department of Justice (DOJ) has launched a criminal investigation into Corey Lewandowski, a former political adviser to Donald Trump and top aide at the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). The probe follows a referral from the DHS inspector general, according to multiple sources. Lewandowski, who served as a special government employee under former DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, is under scrutiny over allegations that he profited from his role in the agency. A lawyer for Lewandowski, Adam Trigg, stated that his client has not been contacted by the DOJ and denies any wrongdoing. The investigation is in its early stages, with no subpoenas or legal demands issued yet.

President Trump expressed support for the investigation, stating he wants his staff to be honest and that dishonesty amounts to stealing from him. He said he would not interfere with any prosecutions of dishonest officials. The DOJ has not yet commented on the matter, though it has previously dismissed cases involving Trump allies.

Where the accounts differ

ABC NEWS states that the probe is examining contracting activities at DHS and that Lewandowski served as a special government employee during Noem's tenure, effectively acting as her chief of staff. THE NEW YORK POST notes that Lewandowski was swept out of DHS following Trump’s firing of Noem and that allegations of misconduct involving Lewandowski’s role in DHS contracting have been reported for over a year.

LeBron shrugs off Trump blast in home debut win for Philadelphia 76ers

Tyrese Maxey scored 32 points, and Joel Embiid added 24, including a crucial jump shot with 6:54 left in the game. Barclays to cut 5,000 jobs as bank’s bosses hail AI productivity advances

Barclays announced plans to cut 5,000 jobs as part of broader cost-saving measures, with executives attributing the cuts to advances in AI and ongoing modernization efforts. The job cuts will represent about 5% of the bank’s global workforce of 93,000, with 1,000 positions already eliminated. The bank’s chief financial officer, Anna Cross, stated the cuts are part of plans to save over £1.1bn by 2027. The chief executive, CS Venkatakrishnan, described AI as the “icing on the cake” to modernizing the bank’s technology, emphasizing that the bulk of efficiency gains come from years of investment in technology and process simplification. Barclays also announced a £500m share buyback, bringing total shareholder distributions since January to £2.8bn.

The bank reported £2.3bn in pre-tax profits for the July to September quarter, up 12% from the same period last year. This performance is expected to justify potential increases in bank taxes in the upcoming budget. Venkatakrishnan stated Barclays could absorb higher taxes but emphasized the importance of proportion and market signaling. The bank also noted a 6% rise in net interest income at its UK retail banking operations, which has become a focus for campaigners pushing for a bank tax.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that Barclays’ job cuts are part of wider cost-saving plans driven by AI, with Venkatakrishnan calling AI the “icing on the cake.” The BBC states that not all cuts are due to AI, and that the bank’s efficiency gains come from years of investment in technology. The Daily Caller notes that AI is not solely responsible for the cuts, with Venkatakrishnan emphasizing the need for infrastructure to realize AI benefits. The Guardian and BBC both mention that 60% of the cuts will affect support functions, including developers, while the Daily Caller attributes this to Reuters, which is not cited in the other accounts.

Sources The Guardian · BBC · The Daily Caller
