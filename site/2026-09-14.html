Houthis seize Red Sea island, Trump claims they don’t want to fight US

Houthi rebels have captured Perim Island, a strategic location in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, according to multiple outlets. The island is a key chokepoint for global shipping and energy exports. The Houthis, backed by Iran, have expanded their control along Yemen’s Red Sea coast, taking over key ports and islands. The United States has been urged by Saudi Arabia to take military action against the Houthis, but President Trump has not committed to direct intervention. Trump claimed the Houthis have contacted the US and said they do not want to fight, though he did not provide evidence for this claim.

The pipeline, which bypasses the Strait of Hormuz, is vital for Saudi oil exports. The Houthi advances have also raised concerns about the security of the Red Sea, with some analysts warning of a “kill box” scenario if the US decides to act.

Wisconsin Republican Congressman Tom Tiffany sustained minor injuries after his plane made an emergency landing in Lake Wausau. The incident occurred on Saturday evening when the aircraft lost power during its final approach to Wausau Downtown Airport. The pilot, Leonard Boltz, executed an emergency landing in the lake. Both men were later rescued by emergency responders and taken to a hospital for treatment. Tiffany described the situation as a "miracle" and expressed gratitude for the quick response of first responders. He also mentioned that he received 12 stitches above his right eye and that the pilot received four stitches. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) confirmed the crash and stated it is investigating the incident.

Tiffany was returning from a La Crosse County Lincoln Day Dinner when the plane lost power. He and the pilot exited the aircraft and swam to a shallow area before being rescued by a boat. Judge blocks Trump administration directive to cut half of FEMA’s workforce

A federal judge ruled that the Trump administration’s plan to cut FEMA’s workforce in half was unlawful. U.S. District Judge Susan Illston of the Northern District of California found that the Department of Homeland Security acted unlawfully by taking control of FEMA’s authority over its personnel and pushing the agency to slash thousands of disaster-response jobs. The ruling came after unions sued the administration, alleging that DHS was planning a 50% workforce reduction that would include FEMA’s Cadre of On-Call Response/Recovery Employees (CORE) staff.

Illston’s decision emphasized that a 2006 law, the Post-Katrina Emergency Management Reform Act, established FEMA as a “distinct entity” within DHS and forbids the Homeland Security Secretary from “substantially or significantly reducing ‘the authorities, responsibilities, or functions’ of FEMA.” The judge noted that DHS violated this law by seeking to stop FEMA from renewing temporary contracts for thousands of on-call reservists. The ruling also stated that FEMA’s staffing plan appeared to be based on “thin air” and lacked reasoned decision-making.

Where the accounts differ

Washington Examiner states that the judge ruled DHS “acted unlawfully in usurping FEMA’s authority over its personnel,” while Straight Arrow News adds that the judge said FEMA “acted unlawfully in acquiescing to DHS’s exertion of authority over FEMA’s CORE renewals and staffing numbers.” FOX News does not mention this reciprocal unlawfulness. Straight Arrow News also includes details about former DHS Secretary Kristi Noem’s intent to cut CORE staffing, which is not present in the other accounts. Washington Examiner and FOX News both mention the 50% workforce reduction, but Straight Arrow News provides a specific number of 23,000 to 11,500 employees, which is not included in the other accounts. Additionally, Straight Arrow News notes that the judge criticized the staffing plan as appearing “as if pulled from thin air,” a detail not included in the other accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · Straight Arrow News · FOX News
