Trump waives Russia sanctions ordered by law named for Lindsey Graham

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday waiving the sweeping Russian sanctions and tariffs enacted by the bill championed by the late Sen. Lindsey Graham. The move effectively neutralizes the law by lifting provisions that barred the president from removing sanctions against Russia and preventing a host of new sanctions and penalties from taking effect on Oct. 18, the deadline set by the law. The law, named after Graham, authorized tariffs of up to 100% on certain goods imported into the United States from major buyers of Russian oil and gas. Trump cited "national interests" as the rationale for the waiver, which he said would be in place until administration officials "determine that further action is appropriate."

The decision to suspend the sanctions came a week after Trump announced a deal to purchase diesel from Russia to try to lower global fuel prices, a move that drew criticism from both Republicans and Democrats. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy criticized the waiver, saying it would "fuel" Russia's war effort, while some lawmakers, including Sen. Richard Blumenthal, accused Trump of turning his back on lawmakers and European allies.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and CBS News both state that Trump signed the law into law last month, while The Guardian and Al Jazeera mention that the law was passed by Congress in an act of rare bipartisan agreement. The Guardian and Al Jazeera note that the law was named after Lindsey Graham, who died in July, while the Washington Examiner and CBS News refer to the law as the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act." Fox Business and The Guardian mention that Sen. Richard Blumenthal helped draft the Graham Act, while CBS News and The Guardian attribute the drafting of the law to both Graham and Blumenthal. The Guardian and Al Jazeera report that the law was passed by both chambers of Congress, while the Washington Examiner and CBS News do not specify which chamber passed the law. The Guardian and Al Jazeera mention that the law was set to take effect on Oct. 18, while the Washington Examiner and CBS News state that the deadline was set by the law.

Sources Washington Examiner · Fox Business · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · ABC News · CBS News

Violent protests erupt in France as demonstrations over cost of living and education continue

Violent clashes broke out across France on Saturday as protests entered their fourth week, with demonstrations over rising living costs and student grievances escalating into riots. Police used tear gas and water cannons to disperse violent groups in Paris and other major cities, where protesters wearing hoods and masks threw fireworks and other objects at officers. The interior ministry reported assaults on police, looting, and damage to public buildings, with at least 272 arrests nationwide. The unrest follows weeks of student-led protests demanding more investment in education and has drawn criticism from political leaders on both the far left and far right.

French President Emmanuel Macron condemned the violence, stating that those who attacked public buildings and law enforcement would be "prosecuted and punished." Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu accused the student movement of being "instrumentalized by the allies of France Unbowed (LFI)" for electoral reasons, while far-right candidate Marine Le Pen blamed students for the violence. Meanwhile, the protests have also drawn international attention, with U.S. Vice President JD Vance warning that Democrats seek a similar future for the U.S.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that Macron called on his ministers to come up with "concrete responses" to the unrest within weeks, while Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News do not mention this specific statement. Washington Examiner and Al Jazeera both state that Macron promised to prosecute those responsible for looting and attacks on public buildings, but FOX News attributes this to the interior ministry rather than Macron directly. Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News report 1,200 arrests, while FOX News and Al Jazeera report 272 arrests, with Straight Arrow News also noting that the number may be higher. Newsweek reports 400 arrests, while Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News report 272 and 1,200 respectively. Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News mention the burning of Limoges City Hall, while FOX News and Washington Examiner do not specify which buildings were targeted.

Sources FOX News · Washington Examiner · Newsweek · Al Jazeera · NPR · Straight Arrow News

Serious incident at Alberta military base causes deaths and injuries, Canada officials say

A major fire broke out at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta following a serious incident, resulting in fatalities and injuries, according to Canada’s defense minister, David McGuinty. McGuinty described the situation as “very, very active and delicate” and noted that the Royal Canadian Mounted Police were involved in the investigation. The fire spread rapidly due to high winds, crossing the base’s fence and affecting the surrounding area. The nearby community of Wainwright provided hospital and firefighting support, while authorities coordinated an emergency response involving federal, provincial, and local agencies. A doctor at Edmonton’s Royal Alexandra Hospital reported that healthcare facilities were overwhelmed by the number of patients, calling it a “one in every 15 to 25-year disaster.” Prime Minister Mark Carney and Alberta Premier Danielle Smith expressed concern over the incident, with Smith calling it “significant and tragic.” The fire occurred days before Alberta was set to vote on potential independence from Canada.

Pope Leo XIV discharged from hospital after lung cancer surgery

Pope Leo XIV was discharged from Rome’s Gemelli Hospital on Saturday following surgery to remove a cancerous nodule from his right lung. The Vatican confirmed the surgery was successful on Tuesday, and later stated that Leo was walking around, eating normally, and that his blood tests and chest X-rays were normal. The pope is expected to return to the Vatican and resume public appearances, including leading the Angelus prayer on Sunday. The Vatican has not yet said if any additional treatment is needed.

Pope Leo XIV celebrated Mass at the hospital on multiple occasions, including a service on the 10th floor of Gemelli Hospital where he distributed Communion to medical personnel and Vatican gendarmes. During his hospitalization, he also met with several high-ranking officials, including Archbishop Paolo Rudelli and Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle. The Vatican released photos of get-well notes from patients, including a 7-year-old child and a university student, expressing their support for the pope.

Pope Leo XIV is scheduled to travel to South America in November, and the Vatican has released the list of journalists accredited to accompany him on the trip. His discharge from the hospital was earlier than initially anticipated, and he returned to the Vatican after a 10-minute car ride. Doctors confirmed that his post-operative recovery allows him to carry out his normal daily activities completely on his own.

Where the accounts differ

Axios and The Daily Wire both state that Pope Leo XIV was discharged on Saturday, but The Daily Wire adds that he left the hospital at approximately 3:30 p.m. and returned to the Vatican after a 10-minute car ride, a detail not mentioned in Axios. CBS News and FOX News both mention that the pope called Russian President Vladimir Putin during his hospitalization, but CBS News provides more specific details about the content of the call, including the pope’s request for a ceasefire in Oleshky. The Daily Wire and NPR both mention that the pope had a portion of his right lung removed, but NPR specifies that the surgery involved removing the lower lobe of the lung, a detail not included in The Daily Wire. Straight Arrow News mentions that the pope spent about three hours in the operating room, while other accounts state the procedure lasted about 90 minutes.

Elon Musk's $158 million donation to GOP super PACs fuels Texas Senate race

Elon Musk has donated $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund (SLF), a Republican super PAC, according to Federal Election Commission (FEC) filings. This contribution was transferred to Texas PAC, which supports Ken Paxton’s Senate campaign against Democrat James Talarico. The Texas Senate race is one of the most expensive in history, with over $500 million spent or reserved in advertising between the two parties. Musk’s donations have significantly bolstered the GOP’s financial position in the race, allowing them to compete with Democrats who have raised substantial funds in key Senate races.

Democrats, including James Talarico, have raised over $60 million in the third quarter, out-raising their Republican counterparts in several competitive races. However, Republicans continue to have a spending advantage, aided by outside groups like Musk’s super PACs. Musk’s contributions have also extended to other GOP groups, including America PAC and the Congressional Leadership Fund, highlighting his broad influence in shaping the midterm election landscape.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and ABC News also note that Musk donated $273 million in the third quarter, with $158 million from SLF, while Slate refers to Musk’s total as $366 million. Child among 2 killed after vehicle drives onto soccer field at Kansas sports complex

A vehicle drove onto a soccer field during a game at Stryker Sports Complex in Wichita, Kansas, killing two people, including a child, and injuring 11 others. The driver, a 34-year-old woman, was taken into custody and is cooperating with the investigation. Two juveniles were also inside the vehicle at the time. The incident occurred around 3:25 p.m., and multiple agencies, including the Wichita Police Department, responded to the scene. The Kansas State High School Activities Association suspended play at its girls flag football state championship following the incident. Wichita Mayor Lily Wu called the event "heartbreaking," noting that families had come to watch their children play soccer.

The driver, identified as a woman from Garden City, Kansas, had a valid license and no outstanding warrants. Police confirmed that a 6-year-old boy and a 35-year-old man were pronounced dead at the scene. Both were from McPherson, Kansas, but were not related. The injured included five children, ranging in age from 5 to 9, and adults aged 28 to 68. The injured were transported to area hospitals with non-life-threatening injuries, though some had "extremely severe injuries."

Southern California wildfires force evacuations, displacing residents and threatening homes amid escalating climate-driven disasters.

Brush fires set homes ablaze in southern California as it braces for more hot weather

A fast-moving fire spread into neighborhoods in southern California on Friday as the region braces for more hot, windy weather that will amplify fire dangers through Saturday. At least six homes were destroyed, according to Mark Backoff, a division chief with the Orange county fire authority (OCFA). The Norte Fire, which erupted in Orange county on Friday, scorched about 465 acres by the evening, with some reports placing the area burned at 570 acres by Saturday morning. Evacuation orders were issued for 16,000 residents in San Juan Capistrano and San Clemente, with nine schools in the area dismissing early. Two firefighters were injured due to heat exhaustion, and officials emphasized the need to limit the fire's further spread.

The blaze was among several fires in the region fueled by Santa Ana winds, which blow from the interior of southern California toward the coast. These winds, combined with low humidity and dry vegetation, created critical wildfire spread conditions. The National Weather Service issued a red flag warning for several California counties, indicating the potential for extreme fire behavior and rapid spread. Firefighters worked through the night to attack the flames and strengthen containment lines, with the fire reported as 5% contained by Saturday morning.

Straight Arrow News reports the fire held at 570 acres Saturday and was 5% contained after crews worked overnight. NPR and The Guardian both mention the fire was 5% contained, but NPR adds that the fire was less than a square mile as of Friday evening. The Guardian and Straight Arrow News both mention that two firefighters were injured, while NPR and BBC also note firefighter injuries but do not specify the number.
