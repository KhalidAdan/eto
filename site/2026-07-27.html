Republicans and Democrats clash for control across the country as midterms approach.

The 2026 midterm elections are just 100 days away, with both parties vying for control of Congress and statehouses. Republicans currently hold narrow majorities in both chambers, but Democrats need a net gain of just six House seats to reclaim the speaker's gavel and four Senate seats to win back the upper chamber.

In the House, Republicans will be bolstered by redistricting wins in states like Florida and Texas, while Democrats are focusing on the economy and affordability in their messaging. A recent Fox News poll showed that 53% of voters would back the Democratic candidate in their House district, a 7-point edge outside the margin of error.

In the Senate, Republicans are looking to hold on to seats in Alaska, North Carolina, Ohio, and Maine, with record amounts of spending already pouring into those states. Democrats hope to capitalize on the historic, economic, foreign policy, and anti-incumbent mood among voters.

The accounts from Al Jazeera and BBC differ on the details of the Berlin Pride ramming attack. Al Jazeera reports that one person was killed and 17 injured, while BBC reports that one woman was killed and at least 29 others were injured.

US pauses strikes on Iran for third night as Trump weighs diplomatic options

The United States has paused its strikes on Iran for the third consecutive night, with President Donald Trump weighing his next move in the nearly five-month-old conflict. The pause follows a series of attacks and counterattacks between the two countries, which have resulted in dozens of deaths and significant damage to infrastructure.

According to reports, top military officials advised Trump to halt the bombing campaign due to dwindling munitions stockpiles that could hamper a sustained military campaign in the region. Diplomatic negotiations are ongoing to avoid a return to all-out war.

The Iranian army spokesperson announced on Sunday that Tehran had halted "retaliatory" attacks against US allies in the region, citing a pause in US strikes as the reason for this decision.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that President Trump said the U.S. has plenty of ammunition, following concerns about depleted stockpiles. However, The New York Post and Mother Jones report that administration officials have warned about dwindling munitions stockpiles that could hamper a sustained military campaign in the region.

The Christian Science Monitor and BBC report that Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson, Esmail Baghaei, confirmed talks with Oman to manage the safe passage of ships on the Strait of Hormuz. However, The Guardian reports that Iranian officials have demanded payment and control over which ships pass through the strait.

Sources CBS News · Christian Science Monitor · Mother Jones · New York Post · BBC · The Guardian

According to authorities, three people were killed and four others, including a toddler, were wounded. One suspect was arrested, but another remains at large. Investigators believe the two suspects were shooting at each other.

Mitch McConnell's prolonged absence stalls Senate legislation.

Mitch McConnell's extended absence from Washington, D.C., has stalled several legislative packages in the Senate. The party holds only a 53-to-47 majority in the Senate, making every vote critical. McConnell's absence, since a fall and subsequent hospitalization on June 14, has raised scrutiny from GOP colleagues worried about advancing bills before the deadline for the usual appropriations process on Oct. 1. If Congress does not pass the annual appropriations bill by Sept. 30, a partial government shutdown will ensue.

McConnell chairs the Senate Appropriations Defense Subcommittee, making his voice a critical one on the panel, which has fallen into partisan deadlock due to his absence. The Appropriations Committee is split 14-14 between Democrats and Republicans, making it essentially impossible to advance controversial legislation. Senate Democrats have made it a negotiating tactic to not support advancing appropriations bills unless increases for the military are paired with similar increases for domestic programs.

The Agriculture Committee's work to advance the "farm bill," an omnibus, multiyear law that governs an array of agricultural and food programs, has also stalled, as McConnell is the second-ranking Republican on the panel. Republicans don't have enough votes without McConnell to advance it out of the Senate Agriculture Committee, since his absence leaves the panel at 11-11.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts conflict over the extent of McConnell's absence and its impact on legislation. The Washington Examiner reports that McConnell's absence has stalled high-stakes legislative packages, while FOX News notes that McConnell is still recovering from a medical emergency but has not provided a hard date for his return to Congress.

The New York Post quotes President Trump urging Senate Majority Leader John Thune to keep the chamber in session until it passes the SAVE America Act or ends the filibuster. However, The Hill reports that Thune has repeatedly pushed back on this demand by declaring there aren't nearly enough Republican votes to abolish the filibuster rule.

Newsweek provides a health update from Senator McConnell's doctor, stating that he is undergoing multiple physical therapy and rehabilitation sessions each day aimed at rebuilding strength and reducing the risk of future falls. However, the Washington Examiner reports that McConnell's absence has complicated sensitive negotiations to bring back an expired provision of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA).

Sources Washington Examiner · FOX News · New York Post · The Hill · Newsweek

Wildfires force over 300,000 people to evacuate homes in France and Spain.

Wildfires are raging across southwestern Europe, forcing more than 300,000 people to flee their homes. The blazes have burned vast areas of land in France and Spain, with authorities deploying thousands of firefighters to battle the fires. In France, over 220,000 people have been evacuated from their homes, while in Spain, around 75,000 people have been forced to leave.

The wildfires are among the worst in France since World War II, with some areas experiencing unprecedented heatwaves and droughts. The blazes have also affected the Tour de France, with the final stage of the cycling race being shortened due to the fires. In Spain, a fast-moving wildfire killed at least 12 people on July 10.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that at least 240 homes in Gironde have been totally destroyed by the wildfire.

Federal appeals court rejects Trump bid to enforce mail ballot order ahead of midterm elections.

A federal appeals court on Saturday refused to let the Trump administration enforce key parts of President Donald Trump's executive order tightening mail-in voting rules in 23 Democratic-led states. The 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals denied the Department of Justice's request to pause a June ruling blocking several provisions of Trump's March executive order while the administration appeals. The court found that the plaintiff states were already facing looming election deadlines and had begun preparing for potential changes.

The lawsuit was brought by 23 states and the District of Columbia, led by California, Massachusetts, Nevada, and Washington. They argue that the Constitution gives them primary responsibility for administering federal elections and that the president exceeded his authority by directing federal agencies to impose new voting requirements. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani agreed in June, ruling that several provisions of the executive order likely exceeded the president's authority and blocking federal agencies from implementing them in the plaintiff states through the Nov. 3 federal elections.

The Trump administration argued that the lawsuit was premature because federal agencies had not finalized the policies needed to implement the executive order. However, the appeals court rejected this argument, stating that the plaintiff states have no practical choice but to respond to the order now due to rapidly approaching deadlines.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News and The Hill report that the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals made a 2-1 decision, while Washington Examiner reports it as a "declined" request to lift an injunction. Just The News does not mention the vote count in its report. FOX News attributes criticism of the ruling to Ohio Congressman Jim Jordan, while The Hill mentions Trump's July primetime address on election integrity.

ICE To Target Haitians As Their Temporary Protected Status Expires

The US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is planning to target Haitian nationals whose Temporary Protected Status (TPS) is set to expire. Over 300,000 Haitians have been living in the US under TPS since 2010 due to turmoil in their home country. The Trump administration has argued that the program was never intended to be a permanent form of legal status.

The Supreme Court recently ruled in favor of the administration's efforts to terminate TPS for Haitians and Syrians, paving the way for ICE to begin enforcing deportations. According to internal documents obtained by CBS News, ICE plans to ramp up operations to arrest and deport Haitian migrants, with a focus on Ohio where large Haitian communities have been targeted by President Trump.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts from The Daily Wire, The Guardian, FOX News, and CBS News all report that the TPS for Haitians is set to expire. However, there are some discrepancies in the numbers of Haitians affected: The Daily Wire reports 300,000, while The Guardian reports over 330,000.

Additionally, The Guardian attributes a characterization of the Trump administration's decision to end TPS as "racist" to Roxana Rivera, assistant to the president at Service Employees International Union (SEIU) 32BJ. This claim is not made by any other outlet.

The Daily Wire and CBS News both report that ICE plans to target Haitian migrants in Ohio, but The Guardian does not mention this specific detail.

Cyclospora cases mounting in Michigan

Cyclospora cases have been reported in multiple states, including Michigan, where the outbreak is particularly severe. The Centers for Disease Control (CDC) has confirmed at least 7,400 cases of cyclospora, with many originating from Michigan and Ohio. State and local health officials are working to identify the cause of the outbreak, which may be linked to contaminated iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms in central Mexico.

The CDC has reported that cyclospora is generally transmitted through food or water contamination, rather than direct person-to-person contact. The parasite requires days or weeks to become infectious, making it difficult to track and contain outbreaks. In this case, the outbreak is believed to have originated from contaminated iceberg lettuce supplied by Taylor Farms.

The FDA has launched an investigation into the outbreak, which has affected nine states so far. Taylor Farms has recalled its products and temporarily suspended production at its central Mexico facility.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that there are over 11,000 cases of cyclospora reported in 41 states, while The Hill mentions a total of 7,400 confirmed cases. CBS News also quotes Susan Mayne as saying that the outbreak is "record-setting," which is not mentioned in The Hill's account.

Sources The Hill · CBS News
