Top House Democrat questions what Trump accomplished in Xi-Putin summit

Rep. Gregory Meeks, the top Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee, criticized President Trump's recent summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, calling it a missed opportunity for meaningful diplomacy. Meeks argued that the meeting failed to produce agreements on critical issues like nuclear arms control and artificial intelligence safety, and warned that the discussions could undermine U.S. alliances. He also expressed concern that the summit may have encouraged the leaders to divide the world into spheres of influence. Meeks called for U.S. diplomats to have raised concerns about Chinese espionage and intellectual property theft before the summit to set clear goals for the talks. He also urged reducing tariffs on U.S. allies while pressuring China to end its support for Russia's war in Ukraine.

Trump, in a White House press conference, defended the summit, calling it a historic diplomatic milestone. He emphasized the importance of dialogue among rival nuclear powers and claimed the meeting offered an opportunity to address long-standing issues. However, he did not provide specific details on what was discussed or any agreements reached. The summit took place during the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) meeting, where Trump also highlighted his achievements in trade and diplomacy.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that Meeks questioned the lack of meaningful agreements on nuclear weapons and AI safety, and that he called for reducing tariffs on U.S. allies. The Hill notes that Trump's press conference included a defensive tone over the midterms and a controversial remark about Putin being his "friend." FOX News states that Trump described the summit as a diplomatic milestone and emphasized the importance of dialogue among nuclear powers. CBS News focuses on Trump's praise for his chief of staff Stephen Miller and his threats to veto Democratic legislation. The Christian Science Monitor highlights that Putin benefited most from the summit, with Trump publicly calling him "my friend." The New York Post mentions that the summit produced a joint declaration on nuclear risks and energy supplies but stopped short of major breakthroughs on Ukraine or Iran.

Sources NPR · The Hill · FOX News · CBS News · Christian Science Monitor · New York Post

Man accused of sparking deadly Los Angeles wildfire found guilty

A 30-year-old man, Jonathan Rinderknecht, was found guilty of two federal arson charges in a retrial for the 2025 Palisades Fire, which killed 12 people and destroyed thousands of homes. Prosecutors argued that Rinderknecht, a former Uber driver, intentionally started a brush fire in the Santa Monica Mountains near Pacific Palisades on January 1, 2025. The fire, initially called the Lachman Fire, smoldered underground and reignited as the Palisades Fire on January 7, 2025, fueled by strong winds. The fire burned over 23,000 acres and caused extensive damage. Rinderknecht was arrested in Florida nine months after the fire. During the retrial, jurors were instructed not to consider the adequacy of the fire department’s response to the Lachman and Palisades blaze. The defense argued that the fire was ignited by fireworks, but prosecutors dismissed this as "baseless." The conviction follows a mistrial in the first trial, where jurors were deadlocked.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and The Hill both report that Rinderknecht called 911 multiple times after the Lachman Fire began, while CBS News and ABC News also mention this detail. The Guardian and The Hill state that Rinderknecht was at the bottom of a hiking trail when he saw the Lachman Fire, but ABC News and FOX News note that geolocation data showed he was 30 feet away from the flames. The Guardian and The Hill describe Rinderknecht as a disgruntled young man seeking retribution for personal grievances, while BBC and ABC News attribute this characterization to prosecutors. The Guardian and The Hill mention that the defense argued the fire was ignited by fireworks, but CBS News and ABC News do not include this claim. The Guardian and The Hill report that the fire department failed to fully extinguish the Lachman Fire, while FOX News and ABC News do not mention this. The Guardian and The Hill state that the fire was fully contained on January 31, 2025, while CBS News and ABC News report the same date. The Guardian and The Hill mention that the fire destroyed 6,800 structures, while CBS News and ABC News report 16,000 structures destroyed. The Guardian and The Hill report that the fire was the third most destructive in state history, while CBS News and ABC News do not mention this. The Guardian and The Hill report that the fire was one of several that ignited back-to-back across the LA region, while BBC and ABC News do not mention this.

Sources The Guardian · CBS News · BBC · The Hill · FOX News · ABC News

Zohran Mamdani unveils plan to close Rikers Island jail complex

Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced a plan to close the Rikers Island jail complex by the end of 2032, with the goal of relocating its remaining jail population. The plan includes building four new borough-based jails in Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens, and Manhattan, with the Brooklyn facility opening in 2029 and the Manhattan facility by 2032. The city aims to reduce the jail population from around 6,600 to no more than 4,100 by 2032. The plan also includes investments in mental health care, substance use treatment, and therapeutic housing for individuals with serious medical needs. The city faces a legally mandated deadline to close Rikers by August 2027, but Mamdani’s administration states it will require new legislation to extend the deadline.

The plan has faced criticism from some correction officers’ unions, which argue that reducing the jail population to 4,100 could jeopardize public safety. The city has also acknowledged delays in construction and an increase in jail population since 2020, which has raised concerns about meeting the capacity of the new facilities. The federal government previously placed Rikers under the control of an independent remediation manager, and Mamdani’s plan includes working with that official to bring the closure to fruition.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and The Daily Wire both mention the 2019 legislation requiring Rikers to close by 2027, while The Daily Caller and FOX News refer to the same legislation but state that the original deadline was August 2027. The Guardian and The Daily Wire note that the city is not on track to meet the 2027 deadline, while The Daily Caller and FOX News state that the original deadline was August 2027 and that Mamdani’s plan delays it to 2032. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller mention the estimated cost of the project rising from $8.7 billion to $16 billion, while The Guardian and BBC do not mention the cost increase. The Guardian and BBC report that the four new facilities are expected to have about 4,500 beds, while The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller state that the new facilities are designed to accommodate around 4,500 inmates.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · BBC · FOX News · The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller

Senate Republicans reject war powers resolution to end Iran conflict

Senate Republicans on Thursday blocked a measure that would have directed President Trump to end hostilities in Iran. The vote failed 48-51. GOP Sens. Susan Collins (Maine), Lisa Murkowski (Alaska) and Rand Paul (Ky.), who have voted for previous resolutions directing Trump to end the war, joined almost the entire Democratic caucus. Centrist Sen. John Fetterman (D-Pa.), an ardent supporter of Israel, was the only Democrat to vote against it. Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.), who voted for the resolution in September, voted against the current version. The measure, known as a war powers resolution, is mostly a signaling vote. Democrats wanted to put Republicans on the record voting against ending the war, even though if the resolution had passed, Trump likely would not have signed it. Thursday’s vote was the first time either chamber voted on the war powers resolution since the November midterms. The House passed a complementary resolution in mid-September, led by Rep. Seth Moulton (D-Mass.), by a vote of 220-204. Seven Republicans joined Democrats in that case to limit Trump’s military actions against Iran.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill reports that the war powers resolution failed 48-51, while FOX News does not mention the vote outcome. CBS News and Axios focus on the potential for a government shutdown in December if funding for the Iran war is not restricted. The Hill and CBS News both mention the cost of the war, with The Hill citing $48.3 billion as of Nov. 13, while CBS News does not include a specific figure. FOX News focuses on internal Democratic Party conflicts and far-left activism, which the other accounts do not mention. The Hill and Axios both mention Reps. Yassamin Ansari and Ro Khanna opposing funding packages that do not prohibit funds from being used for offensive operations in Iran, while FOX News does not mention these specific lawmakers or their positions.

Sources The Hill · FOX News · CBS News · Axios · Washington Examiner · NPR

Judge approves $7.25bn Bayer payout to settle claims that Roundup causes cancer

A Missouri judge on Friday approved Bayer’s $7.25bn settlement of lawsuits alleging that its weedkiller Roundup causes cancer, potentially resolving litigation that has weighed on the German company for close to a decade. Judge Timothy Boyer of a state court in St Louis found the settlement “fair, reasonable and adequate.” The settlement will be paid to Roundup users who are already sick with non-Hodgkin lymphoma and those who fall ill in the future. The settlement excludes some lawsuits that remain pending in federal court. Bayer has faced approximately 65,000 claims in US state and federal courts from people who said they developed cancer after using Roundup at home or on the job.

The settlement was approved five months after the US Supreme Court overturned a $1.25m jury award for a Missouri man, John Durnell, who blamed years of exposure to glyphosate in Roundup for his non-Hodgkin lymphoma. The court ruled 7-2, saying Bayer cannot be sued in state courts because federal regulations do not mandate a cancer warning label. The settlement comes after the Supreme Court in June ruled in favour of Bayer, overturning a $1.25m settlement for a Missouri man who said Roundup and its ingredient glyphosate was to blame for his non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and BBC both state the settlement is $7.25bn, while the Washington Examiner and Just The News refer to it as $7 billion. The New York Post also mentions $7.25bn but attributes the figure to the settlement amount. The Guardian and BBC mention that the settlement excludes some lawsuits that remain pending in federal court, while the New York Post states that about 200,000 Roundup-related claims have been made against Bayer, many coming from people who used it on residential lawns and gardens. The Guardian and BBC note that the settlement includes $600 million for attorneys’ fees, which the Washington Examiner and Just The News omit. The New York Post and BBC mention that the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified glyphosate as “probably carcinogenic” in 2015, while the Guardian and Washington Examiner do not include this detail.

Sources The Guardian · Washington Examiner · BBC · Just The News · New York Post

The U.K. and EU hold a rare summit to strengthen ties

The U.K. and EU held a summit in Brussels to discuss closer cooperation and trade agreements. Prime Minister Andy Burnham met with European Council President António Costa and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. The summit aimed to address issues such as youth mobility, food trade, and emissions standards. Burnham emphasized the benefits of a closer partnership with Europe, including lower food prices and economic growth. The agreements reached are expected to add 0.4% to Britain's economy. The summit also included discussions on security and defense cooperation, as well as efforts to restrict irregular migration.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that the summit includes an agreement for up to 100,000 young adults from Britain to live and work in EU countries and the same number of Europeans to come to the U.K. The Guardian mentions a compromise on tuition fees for EU students, with the EU dropping its demand for home fees and agreeing to a supplementary agreement for further negotiations. BBC states that the UK and EU will allow up to 100,000 young people from each side to live and work in the other’s countries. Al Jazeera mentions the same number but adds that the details of student fees remain a work in progress. The Guardian also reports that the UK and EU plan to establish formal talks on sending refused asylum seekers to third countries, which is not mentioned in the other accounts.

Sources NPR · The Guardian · BBC · Al Jazeera · Christian Science Monitor

Democrats secure majority in House, Republicans face internal calls for accountability

Democrats achieved a significant victory in the 2026 midterm elections, with 56% of voters supporting them in House races compared to 42% for Republicans, according to NPR. This marks the largest win for either party in 50 years, surpassing previous margins from 2006 and 2018. The Democratic gains resulted in flipping 46 House seats, potentially reaching 49, and could lead to the largest House majority since the early 1990s.

In response to the election results, House Speaker Mike Johnson urged Republicans to remain engaged and attend sessions to advance their legislative agenda before Democrats take control. Johnson highlighted the need for full attendance to pass key priorities, including government funding and the National Defense Authorization Act. Some Republican members, including Rep. Thomas Massie, criticized their colleagues for skipping work while still receiving pay, noting that the House had to adjourn early due to insufficient attendance.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that Democrats secured a 14-point margin in House races, while The Daily Caller and Axios mention that 14 Republican members missed votes, leading to adjournments. FOX News and The Daily Caller note that Massie criticized Republicans for skipping work and still collecting pay, a detail not included in NPR or Newsweek. Newsweek includes quotes from political scientist Grant Reeher, which are absent from other accounts. Axios and The Daily Caller specify that 11 of the 14 absent Republicans will not return in January, a detail not mentioned in NPR or FOX News.

Sources NPR · Newsweek · FOX News · The Daily Caller · Axios

Russia attacks Kyiv bridges and infrastructure in major strike

Russia launched a major missile and drone attack on Kyiv and other parts of Ukraine, targeting bridges, energy facilities, and railway stations. Ukrainian officials reported at least nine deaths and 28 injuries, with Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko noting damage to three main bridges over the Dnipro River. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the attack as "one of the largest" of the war, with Russia firing over 110 missiles and more than 400 drones. Russia’s Defence Ministry claimed it targeted bridges used for military logistics and energy infrastructure. Poland reported three objects, likely two missiles and a drone, entered its airspace during the attack.

The attack followed Ukraine’s partial destruction of the Crimean Bridge, which Russia has called a "prized" symbol of its annexation of Crimea. Russian President Vladimir Putin vowed a "far more painful" response to the bridge attack, which Ukraine claimed damaged the road section. Zelenskyy said the strike on the bridge was part of a weeks-long operation to weaken its defenses. Russia’s Defence Ministry also reported intercepting 310 Ukrainian drones during the attack.
