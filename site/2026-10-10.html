ICE Officer Shoots Man in Car With Five-Year-Old in New York

A federal immigration officer shot a man in the Bronx on Thursday, with his five-year-old son in the car, sparking protests and condemnation from New York Democrats. The incident occurred in the Marble Hill neighborhood, which is part of Manhattan but physically located in the Bronx. The man, identified as Oscar Belgal, was undocumented, from the Dominican Republic, and had a felony record and gang affiliations. He was later booked into the Metropolitan Detention Center in Brooklyn. Belgal was conscious and hospitalized, with a bullet in his body, and had only one phone call with his lawyers. A New York federal judge ordered ICE to keep him in the area, but the Justice Department may argue the transfer to New Jersey made the order moot. ICE officials claimed Belgal refused to exit his car and accelerated, striking two vehicles before being shot.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Mother Jones both report that the man had a five-year-old child in his car, while Al Jazeera, CBS News, and Newsweek do not mention the child. The Guardian and Mother Jones describe the shooting as part of a pattern of ICE shootings, while Al Jazeera, CBS News, and Newsweek focus on the Nobel Peace Prize awarded to Navi Pillay. The Guardian and BBC report that the man was taken to a detention facility in New Jersey before being booked into MDC Brooklyn, while Mother Jones and CBS News do not mention this detail. The Guardian and BBC attribute the claim that the man was shot in the neck to Marcos Charles, a senior ICE official, while Mother Jones and CBS News do not specify who made that claim.

Sources The Guardian · Mother Jones · Al Jazeera · CBS News · Newsweek · BBC

Pentagon's plan to livestream execution sparks backlash

The Pentagon confirmed that the execution of Nidal Hasan, a former Army major convicted of killing 13 people at Fort Hood in 2009, will be livestreamed on December 3. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth stated that the public should be able to watch the execution to understand the consequences of such crimes. President Trump approved the plan, though he said he was not directly involved in the decision to livestream. The execution will be the first public military execution in the U.S. in nearly a century and the first to be livestreamed. Critics, including Republican and Democratic lawmakers, religious leaders, and human rights advocates, have condemned the plan as barbaric and reminiscent of authoritarian regimes.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that President Trump said he "wasn't involved in that decision" to livestream the execution, while The Guardian and BBC do not mention this specific comment by Trump. The Guardian and BBC attribute the approval of the execution to Trump, with The Guardian noting that Trump approved it on the recommendation of Hegseth. NPR states that the execution will be the first public one in nearly a century, while The Hill and BBC mention it as the first since 1945. The Guardian and BBC include quotes from UN officials and human rights advocates, which are not present in CBS News or NPR. The Hill and BBC mention that Hasan apologized for "any mistakes" during his court martial, a detail not included in CBS News or The Guardian.

Sources CBS News · The Guardian · NPR · Axios · BBC · The Hill

Millions on alert as Hurricane Isaias set to bring dangerous storm surge

Hurricane Isaias, the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season, is expected to make landfall along the U.S. Gulf Coast, bringing dangerous storm surge, hurricane-force winds, heavy rainfall, and flooding. The National Weather Service (NWS) reported that Isaias intensified to a Category 2 hurricane with sustained winds of 100 mph. Storm surge warnings are in effect from the mouth of the Mississippi River to the Suwannee River in Florida. Evacuation orders and state of emergency declarations have been issued in Florida, Alabama, and Georgia. Officials urge residents to prepare for possible long-duration power outages and to follow local guidance.

Isaias is expected to bring heavy rain to the central Gulf Coast and much of the Southeast, with 3-7 inches of rain anticipated from southeastern Louisiana to the Florida Big Bend region. Localized rainfall totals of up to 15 inches are possible, along with flash and urban flooding. Isolated tornadoes could develop in coastal areas of Alabama and the Florida Panhandle. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis declared a state of emergency for 25 counties, while Alabama Governor Kay Ivey declared a state of emergency in 40 counties. Georgia Governor Brian Kemp issued a state of emergency for all 159 counties.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Newsweek both report that Isaias is expected to make landfall in the Florida Panhandle or Alabama late Friday or early Saturday. ABC News and BBC state that Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, on Friday night as a Category 2 storm. Al Jazeera reports that Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, with maximum sustained winds of 169 km/h (105 mph). The Guardian and Newsweek also mention that Isaias is expected to strengthen before landfall, while ABC News and BBC note that the storm weakened to a Category 1 and then to a post-tropical cyclone after landfall. Al Jazeera reports that Isaias had maximum sustained winds of 100 km/h (65 mph) on Saturday, while the Guardian and Newsweek state that the storm had sustained winds of 100 mph (160 km/h) on Thursday evening.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · ABC News · Newsweek · BBC · Al Jazeera

Trump-backed Lukoil bid aims to restart refineries, deliver billions to US Treasury

The Trump administration is backing a deal for Russian oil giant Lukoil’s overseas assets that is poised to deliver billions of dollars to the US Treasury and bring shuttered refining capacity back online. A consortium led by investor Todd Boehly and including the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) is seeking to purchase fields that span Asia, Europe, Africa and North America and produce more than 350,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day, as well as three refineries in Bulgaria, Romania, and the Netherlands with a combined refining capacity of 350,000 barrels a day. Officials said the assets are valued at about $20 billion and that the agency’s stake is structured so that profits will flow to the Treasury’s coffers.

President Donald Trump announced a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin to import Russian diesel into the US and global markets, claiming it would lower fuel prices. Trump said Russia would supply 300,000 tons of diesel immediately, 500,000 tons in November, and 1 million tons after that, with an additional 3 million tons depending on refinery conditions. The deal comes amid record-high diesel prices in the US, which have reached nearly $6.28 per gallon.

Microsoft suspended from green-card program as Trump administration accuses of visa abuse

The Trump administration suspended Microsoft from the Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) program, which allows employers to sponsor foreign workers for permanent residency. Vice President JD Vance accused Microsoft of replacing American workers with foreign employees, claiming the company laid off 6,000 workers while filing for 6,300 H-1B visas. Microsoft responded by stating that 80% of its H-1B applications were for existing employees, not new hires. The administration also suspended Adobe and several IT firms from the program. President Trump awarded Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella a medal for innovation at a White House event, highlighting the administration's support for tech leaders despite the suspension.

Where the accounts differ

Axios and Fox Business both report that Microsoft laid off 6,000 American workers while filing for 6,300 H-1B visas, but Axios notes that Vance did not provide a timeline for the layoffs. The Daily Wire and New York Post mention that Vance claimed Microsoft replaced each laid-off worker with 1.5 foreign workers, a claim not explicitly stated in Axios or Fox Business. The Guardian and BBC report that Microsoft stated 80% of its H-1B applications were for existing employees, while The Daily Wire and Fox Business include Microsoft's statement that the remaining filings were for individuals already legally in the U.S. The Guardian and BBC also note that Microsoft claims it pays H-1B workers the same as U.S. employees, a detail included in Axios and Fox Business.

Katie Zacharia tapped as next White House press secretary

Zacharia will replace Karoline Leavitt, who left the administration in August. Trump praised her as a "loyal and brilliant supporter" and highlighted her work as a senior communications adviser at Trump Media & Technology Group. Zacharia thanked Trump for the appointment, calling it "the honor of a lifetime" and expressing confidence in her ability to communicate the administration's accomplishments.

She previously worked for Trump ally Richard Grenell at Fix California, an organization focused on advancing conservative values in California. Christa Pike is walking with help and eating after failed execution attempt

Christa Pike, 50, survived an attempt to execute her by lethal injection in Tennessee on September 30, 2026. Her attorneys reported that she began walking with assistance and eating again at a hospital a week after the failed attempt. Pike was administered two doses of pentobarbital, but the drug did not kill her, leaving her with burned and blistered arms. She regained consciousness on October 6 and was able to speak, though she continued to struggle with concentration and memory. Her lawyers warned that her physical recovery and legal standing remain uncertain. Tennessee Governor Bill Lee has postponed the state's only other scheduled execution this year and ordered a review of the failed attempt. The state's prison chief resigned following the incident.

Pike's lawyers described her recovery as "remarkable," noting she had regained some movement in her arms. However, other accounts suggest she remains confused and unable to move her arms or swallow, with complications such as pneumonia and a blood clot. The failed execution has sparked renewed scrutiny of the death penalty, with experts highlighting the frequent mishaps associated with lethal injection. The case has also led to calls for a comprehensive review of the execution process and raised questions about the long-term effects of the botched attempt on Pike's health and mental state.
