Federal agents raided Rep. Ilhan Omar’s DC home as part of an immigration investigation

Federal law enforcement agents executed a search warrant at Rep. Ilhan Omar’s Washington, D.C., home on Monday morning, according to the Department of Justice and reports from Fox News and the Associated Press. The Justice Department stated the search was part of an ongoing criminal investigation. Omar responded to the raid with a fundraising message, calling the action “the latest step in their ludicrous, partisan investigation into my immigration status” and framing it as an attempt by the Trump administration to “target political enemies and weaponize law enforcement.” The search reportedly focused on Omar’s immigration paperwork, which her late father filled out when she was a child, according to unnamed sources cited by Fox News. The New York Times reported that the Justice Department had previously investigated Omar’s finances and interactions with a foreign citizen during Biden’s presidency, though the investigation stalled without charges.

The search has reignited debates about the legitimacy of the investigation, with some outlets suggesting it is part of a broader effort to scrutinize Omar’s immigration history. The Daily Wire and The Federalist reported that the investigation centers on Omar’s father’s immigration records, which may have been incomplete or inaccurate. The New York Post highlighted that Omar’s father, Nur Omar Mohamed, has no official record of being a naturalized U.S. citizen, according to documents obtained by a Minnesota activist in 2023.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both describe Omar as soliciting donations after the raid, with the Examiner citing a fundraising text message and the Guardian noting her social media response. The Slate article, however, does not mention any fundraising activity and instead focuses on the broader context of Trump’s alleged political motivations. The Daily Wire and The Federalist provide more detailed accounts of Omar’s personal history, including her marriage to her alleged biological brother, which other outlets either omit or only briefly mention. The New York Post and The Guardian both reference the lack of official records for Omar’s father, while The Federalist adds context about his military service under a Somali dictator.

Sources Washington Examiner · Slate · New York Post · The Daily Wire · The Federalist · The Guardian

Death on Six Flags rollercoaster in US triggers ride closures across six states

A man died and several others were injured after an incident involving a rollercoaster at Six Flags Over Georgia on Monday. The park temporarily closed nine similar rollercoasters across six of its amusement parks as a precaution. Authorities and Six Flags have not confirmed the cause of the death or the nature of the injuries. Witnesses reported seeing a man fall from the Goliath rollercoaster, hitting other passengers before landing on the roof of a haunted house attraction. The incident has prompted an investigation by Cobb County police.

The Goliath rollercoaster, which opened in 2006, reaches speeds of 70mph and heights of 200 feet. It is described as a "hyper coaster" with a construction cost of $20 million. The ride has a history of incidents, including a 2006 death of a 45-year-old man attributed to a congenital heart condition. The park has also recently closed its X2 rollercoaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain in California following lawsuits related to serious injuries.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News both report that the man who died was identified as Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem of Atlanta, while the other accounts do not mention his name. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention that the man was on the ride with a child, a detail not included in the other accounts. FOX News and The Guardian attribute the claim about the man's son being seated beside him to an unnamed woman, while the other accounts do not mention this. The Washington Examiner does not specify the number of injured people, while The Guardian and CBS News state that several others were injured. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention that witnesses saw the man fall and hit other passengers three times, while the other accounts do not specify the number of impacts.

Sources The Guardian · Newsweek · Washington Examiner · FOX News · The Daily Wire · CBS News

Trump ousts Kimberly Guilfoyle as US ambassador to Greece after corruption allegations

President Donald Trump's administration has asked Kimberly Guilfoyle to resign from her position as US ambassador to Greece, according to White House Press Secretary Katie Zacharia. Guilfoyle, a former Fox News host, was facing mounting scrutiny over reports she allegedly asked a Trump donor to pay her hefty credit card bill in exchange for access to the administration and behaved inappropriately while in Athens. Trump said on Tuesday that she had done a good job, but will "not be there" in Greece any longer. "I didn't like what I read," he said. Guilfoyle vowed to defend herself, calling the claims false, and said the ambassadorship had been the "honour of a lifetime". Zacharia announced during her first - and unusually short - media briefing that Guilfoyle "had been asked to resign" without providing more details. "She has been a wonderful asset to the team, and we wish her well," she said. Guilfoyle, the 57-year-old ex-wife of California Governor Gavin Newsom, was first nominated as envoy to Athens in December 2024, a month before Trump returned to the White House as president. Around the same time, news broke that she had broken up with Donald Trump Jr, to whom she was engaged.

Pope Leo XIV diagnosed with lung cancer, undergoes successful surgery to remove lower lobe of right lung

Pope Leo XIV was diagnosed with lung cancer and underwent surgery to remove the lower lobe of his right lung, the Vatican announced Tuesday. The procedure, performed at Rome's Gemelli Hospital, was successful and lasted about 90 minutes. The Pope awoke normally after the surgery and returned to his room following a brief observation period. The Vatican confirmed the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma, which was suspected by preoperative tests and confirmed during the procedure. The Pope, who is 71 years old, has not been known to smoke and has a family history of cancer, including his mother and older brother.

The Vatican stated that the Pope's schedule has been cleared for the next several days, with no planned changes to his hospitalization, which is set at one week. The Pope announced the operation during his weekly Angelus blessing on Sunday, stating the nodule was detected at an early stage and was well localized. The Vatican has not yet announced further treatment plans.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that the Pope's surgery involved removing the lower lobe of his right lung and that the Vatican has not announced further treatments. Axios states that the Pope was in the operating room for approximately three hours, while the surgery itself lasted 90 minutes. BBC mentions that the entire lower lobe was removed to ensure a radical procedure and includes quotes from Cardinal Kikuchi Tarcisio Isao and Carla and Elsa, who were present at the hospital. The Hill notes that the Vatican has not announced a change to the Pope's planned trip to South America. Straight Arrow News adds that the surgery used a video-assisted technique and includes a promotional message for their app. Just The News states that the surgery was led by Dr. Stefano Margaritora and that the Pope was in the operating room for three hours.

Cyberattack disrupts food supply chains, risking hunger and safety for thousands of shoppers in France.

Hackers targeted refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets, raising temperatures to 122°F and causing fires and food spoilage

Hackers remotely accessed refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets, raising temperatures to as high as 122°F (50°C), which led to food spoilage and fires at several stores. The Paris prosecutor’s office described the attack as “large-scale,” affecting connected refrigerators in several major food retailers across France. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu ordered France’s National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI) to investigate, with officials coordinating with European counterparts due to potential cross-border impacts. The attack disrupted the cold chain, leading to the destruction of fresh and frozen products, with affected stores instructed to discard spoiled food.

The attack targeted refrigeration systems from Danish company Danfoss, which manage temperature settings for store freezers and fridges. Danfoss stated that affected units were running outdated firmware and had been advised to update or replace older systems. Cybersecurity firm Claroty had previously published research on vulnerabilities in Danfoss systems that could allow attackers to bypass authentication. French authorities and Danfoss have not yet linked these flaws to the recent attack. Some affected stores have resumed operations, though others continue to assess damage.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that Intermarche said 300 of its Netto brand stores were targeted, while Straight Arrow News states Intermarché said between 150 and 200 stores were affected. The Daily Wire claims Les Mousquetaires said 180 of its stores were impacted. Al Jazeera and The Daily Wire mention that Intermarché CEO Thierry Cotillard estimated losses at “tens of millions of euros nationwide in a single day,” while Straight Arrow News attributes the same quote to Cotillard but does not specify the timeframe. Al Jazeera and The Daily Wire note that Danfoss identified the affected units as “legacy” products no longer receiving updates, while Straight Arrow News states Danfoss said the affected units were running outdated firmware and had been advised to update.

Sources Al Jazeera · Straight Arrow News · The Daily Wire
