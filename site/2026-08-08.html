Todd Blanche narrowly confirmed as Trump’s attorney general in overnight vote

The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general in a vote early Saturday morning, cementing President Donald Trump’s former personal attorney’s command of a Justice Department the Republican president has sought to bend to his will. The Republican-led Senate voted 50-49 to make Blanche the second confirmed attorney general since Trump returned to the White House last year with a stated desire to use the law enforcement agency to investigate his political enemies. While Blanche has already been leading the department in an acting capacity, his confirmation could free him to pursue the Trump administration’s agenda even more aggressively. The vote followed a tumultuous confirmation fight that exposed deep concerns — from some Republicans as well as Democrats — about installing Trump’s close ally atop a Justice Department that historically prided itself on its independence from the White House.

Blanche’s confirmation hinged on the support of Republican Sen. Bill Cassidy, who announced his decision to vote for Blanche on Friday morning. Cassidy said the decision was not a referendum on Trump but rather a judgment about whether Blanche would be able to effectively lead the agency and provide legal counsel to the president. He acknowledged concerns about Blanche’s role in a controversial settlement of Trump’s lawsuit against the IRS, which included a proposed $1.8 billion compensation fund for Trump’s allies and an exemption from federal tax audits. Blanche publicly promised to abandon the fund and rein in the settlement’s other provisions.

Where the accounts differ

The Associated Press and The Hill both report that Blanche’s confirmation was 50-49, while The Daily Wire and The Hill mention that Blanche’s confirmation was narrowly secured with Cassidy’s support. The BBC and The Hill note that Senator Lisa Murkowski opposed Blanche’s confirmation, citing concerns over the “politicisation” of the justice department. The Daily Wire and The Hill also mention that Senator Susan Collins opposed Blanche’s confirmation, citing concerns over the independence of the Justice Department. The Associated Press and The Hill report that Blanche publicly promised to abandon the $1.8 billion fund and rein in the settlement’s other provisions, while the BBC and The Daily Wire attribute these promises to Blanche.

Abdul El-Sayed narrowly wins Michigan Democratic Senate primary

Abdul El-Sayed, a progressive physician and former public health official, won the Democratic Senate primary in Michigan by less than 16,000 votes, overcoming a massive outside spending effort. His campaign rejected corporate PAC money, while his opponent, Rep. Haley Stevens, received over $30 million from the American Israel Public Affairs Committee’s United Democracy Project super PAC. El-Sayed’s victory has sparked internal debate within the Democratic Party over the viability of progressive candidates in statewide races. Political strategist David Axelrod, a former Obama adviser, acknowledged the win as impressive given the financial disadvantage but noted El-Sayed’s need to focus on issues like economic security rather than more radical positions. Meanwhile, Republican nominee Mike Rogers, who previously criticized Donald Trump, has launched a campaign targeting moderate Democrats, framing El-Sayed’s victory as a sign of a “divisive” movement.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Nation differ on the nature of El-Sayed’s platform and its implications. The Daily Wire claims El-Sayed is not a democratic socialist but holds many of the radical party’s viewpoints, including calls to abolish ICE and defund the police, and notes he has campaigned with communist influencer Hasan Piker. The Nation, however, portrays El-Sayed as a pro-Palestine candidate who ran on a platform centering Palestinian humanity and opposing U.S. military funding to Israel, and highlights his victory as a landmark moment for the Palestine movement. The Daily Wire also states El-Sayed wiped social media posts advocating for defunding the police, while The Nation does not mention this. The New York Post and The Nation both note El-Sayed’s criticism of Israel, but only The Nation frames it as part of a broader pro-Palestine movement.

US Senate passes sweeping Russian energy sanctions bill amid Ukraine war

The US Senate passed a sanctions bill targeting Russia’s energy revenues amid its invasion of Ukraine. The legislation, named the “Lindsey O Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026,” includes tariffs on Russian oil and gas imports. The bill passed with a vote of 86–11, sending it to the House of Representatives. The measure aims to cut off financial support for Russia’s war efforts by imposing tariffs on major importers, including China and India. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy welcomed the move, calling it a significant step toward ending the war. The bill also extends sanctions against Iran, targeting its energy and weapons sectors. The Russian Embassy in Washington condemned the legislation, citing potential energy constraints and rising gas prices in the US.

Where the accounts differ

Trump revives effort to fire Governor Lisa Cook from the Fed

President Donald Trump is once again attempting to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, citing mortgage fraud allegations. The White House has sent a letter to Cook demanding a written response within 21 days, following a Supreme Court ruling that required notice and an opportunity to respond before removal. The letter, obtained by multiple outlets, claims there is "sufficient reason to believe that you made false statements on one or more mortgage agreements." The Supreme Court previously blocked Trump's first attempt to remove Cook in June, ruling that the president could not remove a Fed member without proper justification.

Cook's legal team, including Abbe Lowell, has called the allegations "as baseless now as they were a year ago," arguing that the claims are part of a "manufactured pretext" to interfere with the Fed's independence. The White House has not provided specific details about the nature of the mortgage fraud allegations, though they were initially raised by Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte, who submitted a criminal referral to the Justice Department.

US appeals court halts Trump ballroom project paving way for Supreme Court showdown

A federal appeals court ruled on Friday that the Trump administration cannot proceed with above-ground construction of a planned White House ballroom without congressional approval. The three-judge panel, in a 2-1 decision, stated that the White House is not the property of any single president but belongs to the American people. The ruling upheld a lower court order blocking above-ground work, while allowing underground construction to continue. The administration has appealed the decision to the Supreme Court, which has stayed the ruling for 14 days. President Trump criticized the decision as "horrendous" and "politically motivated," vowing to have it overturned. The project, which includes a 90,000-square-foot ballroom and security infrastructure, has faced legal challenges since October when the East Wing was demolished to make way for the new structure. The National Trust for Historic Preservation supported the ruling, arguing that the White House should not be altered without congressional approval.

Court orders Instagram and Facebook's Meta to pay $567M to address kids' mental health online

A New Mexico court has ordered Meta, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, to pay $567 million to address harms to young people from its platforms. The ruling comes in the second phase of a landmark trial, following a March jury decision that found Meta knowingly harmed children's mental health and concealed knowledge about child sexual exploitation. The total amount Meta is responsible for — $942 million — includes the $375 million in civil penalties from the first phase. The court ordered the bulk of the money, $420 million, to be used for treatment services for young people, with the remainder allocated for awareness, prevention, and screening services over the next five years. The ruling also mandates changes to Meta's platforms, including the implementation of informational screens, improved age assurance tools, and restrictions on adult contact with minors.

The court found that federal children's privacy laws prevent Meta from applying age-verification tools to children under 13. Instead, Meta must continue to improve its age-assurance tools, using artificial intelligence to estimate age based on user data. The company is also required to request proof of age for users in New Mexico it estimates to be under 13 and delete personal information collected on users under 13. Meta must partner with schools or child safety organizations to create a reporting portal for flagging underage users and report on its progress twice a year.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and The Guardian both report that the court ordered Meta to pay $567 million and that the total liability is $942 million, including the $375 million from the first phase. Al Jazeera and CBS News also mention the $567 million and $942 million total, but Al Jazeera notes that the ruling comes in the second phase of a landmark trial, while CBS News refers to it as the second phase of a landmark trial the social media giant lost in March. UPI adds that the court found Meta created a public nuisance and that the case was bifurcated into two phases, with Phase 1 being a jury trial and Phase 2 a bench trial. Fox Business states that the $567 million is on top of the $375 million in civil penalties and that the court found Meta created a public nuisance, with specific details about the youth-safety measures ordered.

Sources NPR · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · CBS News · UPI · Fox Business
