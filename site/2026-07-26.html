At least 1 dead, several injured after van rams into crowd at Pride festival

A white van rammed into a crowd of people attending a Pride festival in Berlin on Saturday evening, killing one person and injuring at least 16 others. The incident occurred during the European city's annual Christopher Street Day festival, an LGBTQ+ Pride event that attracts hundreds of thousands of people. Police said they have identified a suspect who is known to them and has links to "Islamic circles" in Berlin.

The attack happened around 10pm local time at Tiergarten Park, near the iconic Brandenburg Gate. The van crashed into a tree after hitting several people, and police later found the vehicle abandoned at the scene. Emergency services treated the injured, with some suffering life-threatening injuries.

Police launched an intensive manhunt for the suspect, who is believed to be armed and dangerous. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz condemned the incident as "a heinous act" on social media, saying that it was an attack on society's values of kindness and tolerance.

Where the accounts differ

  • The number of injured people reported by different outlets varies: 16 (FOX News), 17 (NPR), 29 (BBC).
  • The BBC reports that the suspect is a 21-year-old German citizen of Lebanese background, while other outlets do not provide this information.
  • The Washington Examiner mentions that police raided an apartment in connection with the investigation, but does not specify whether anyone was found there.

Sources FOX News · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · NPR · BBC · Washington Examiner

Trump delivers jokes and jabs at White House Correspondents' Association Dinner

President Donald Trump attended the rescheduled White House Correspondents' Association (WHCA) Dinner on July 24, 2026, at the Waldorf Astoria hotel in Washington D.C. The event was originally scheduled for April but was disrupted by an assassination attempt on the president. Trump's speech included jokes and jabs at the press, politicians, and himself. He praised the Secret Service and law enforcement officers who responded to the April shooting, awarding Officer Victor Gonzales with the WHCA President's Award for Exceptional Service.

Trump also quipped about his relationship with the press, saying "sometimes I really do think that some of you don't like me." He joked that when he leaves office, the news industry will be "broke" because there won't be anyone to report on. Trump produced a "Trump 2028" hat and said he would run for a fourth term as president.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post reports that Trump mocked California Governor Gavin Newsom's 2028 White House ambitions, claiming Newsom had doomed himself with praise for Kamala Harris. The Guardian notes that Trump is reviving old tropes about communism and has identified it as the US's biggest threat.

FOX News highlights Trump's jokes at the press and members of his cabinet, including a quip about Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. cutting up a cow to bring beef tenderloin for dinner. The BBC reports that Trump took swipes at the press during the dinner, but also praised the Secret Service and law enforcement officers who responded to the April shooting.

The Hill notes that Trump's speech included a tribute to the U.S. Secret Service, law enforcement officers, and hotel staff who responded to the April incident. CBS News reports that Trump cracked jokes at reporters and politicians during the event, but also acknowledged the sometimes-tense relationship with the press.

Sources New York Post · FOX News · The Guardian · BBC · The Hill · CBS News

Maine Democrats crown Troy Jackson as Platner replacement as fresh scrutiny clouds Senate reset

Troy Jackson was elected as the Maine Democratic Party's nominee for U.S. Senate on Saturday at a nominating convention in Bangor. He will face Republican incumbent Susan Collins in November. Jackson, a former state Senate president and fifth-generation logger, won 566 of 571 delegate votes to secure the nomination.

The selection comes after Graham Platner dropped out of the race earlier this month due to allegations of sexual assault made by a former girlfriend. Platner denied the accusations but withdrew from the campaign.

Jackson's nomination caps an abbreviated process to find a replacement for Platner, who was backed by progressive champion and Independent Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont. Jackson has been endorsed by Sanders in his past campaigns and supports progressive policies such as Medicare for All and abolishing Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that there are concerns within the party about Jackson's alleged temper and workplace conduct, while NPR notes that these allegations have not led to significant public pushback from state leaders.

CBS News highlights the abbreviated process to find a replacement for Platner, which was triggered by his withdrawal from the race. The New York Post notes that many viewed Jackson as the favorite to succeed Platner due to his populist brand of politics.

The Daily Wire criticizes the Maine Democratic Party's decision-making process, suggesting that Jackson was hand-selected without a single vote being cast by Mainers.

Sources FOX News · NPR · CBS News · The New York Post · The Daily Wire

Iran-backed Houthis claim missile attack on Saudi Arabia as regional tensions escalate

The Iranian-backed Houthi rebels claimed a missile attack on the southern Saudi city of Jizan on Saturday, after vowing retaliation against the kingdom for attacks this week. The US and Iran have been engaged in a series of strikes over the past two weeks, with the US carrying out 13 consecutive nights of attacks on Iran. Iranian officials have publicly pushed back against US pressure while divisions persist inside Tehran over whether diplomacy or confrontation is the best path forward.

The Houthis said the attack was retaliation for Saudi airstrikes on Houthi targets in the Yemeni port city of Hodeida and a nearby island on Friday. The group had vowed to target ships attempting to transit the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, which connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden. The US military has fired on and disabled a tanker ship in the Gulf of Oman that was attempting to evade the American blockade on Iranian ports.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post reports that President Trump halted new US strikes on Iran as he weighs whether Tehran is ready to cut a deal, while the BBC states that there were no overnight reports of air strikes in the wider region by the US or Iran for the first time in two weeks. The Christian Science Monitor notes that the conflict is threatening to disrupt international shipping through the Red Sea.

Sources The Guardian · The Hill · New York Post · BBC · Christian Science Monitor · Just The News

More than 300,000 flee homes due to wildfires in France and Spain.

Wildfires have forced over 300,000 people to evacuate their homes in France and Spain. The fires have been burning for days, fueled by strong winds and high temperatures. In France, more than 167,000 people had been evacuated as of Saturday night, with the most devastating fires in the Southwest, particularly in the Gironde and Landes regions. French President Emmanuel Macron said that the situation remains critical in the Gironde department.

In Spain, at least one person has died from the fires, and over 60,000 people were evacuated from Avila and near Madrid. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared a national state of emergency due to the wildfires. The final stage of the Tour de France was shortened due to the fires, with security forces redeployed to assist with the response.

Where the accounts differ

  • NPR reports that over 242,000 acres have been burned in France this year, a historic record, according to French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez. The Guardian does not mention this specific figure.
  • The BBC quotes EU Commissioner for Crisis Management Hadja Lahbib as saying that last year one million hectares burned during the summer and they are expecting another historical record. This is also reported by NPR but not by other outlets.
  • CBS News reports that at least 197,000 people have been evacuated in France due to the wildfires, which French Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said was "probably" the biggest peacetime evacuation the country has seen. The Guardian and BBC do not mention this specific figure.

Sources NPR · The Guardian · BBC · The Hill · CBS News · Al Jazeera

India's Education Minister Resigns Amid Nationwide Protests Over Exam Leaks and Education Reforms

India's Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on Saturday after weeks of protests led by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), a youth movement that has been demanding education reforms, compensation for families of students who died by suicide due to exam leaks, and accountability from the government. The protests began in May after allegations of leaks in some of India's biggest entrance exams.

The CJP, which started as a satirical campaign on social media, has grown into a powerful platform for young people's anxieties and dissent. Its founder, Abhijeet Dipke, rallied tens of thousands of students and supporters in New Delhi to force Pradhan's resignation. The movement has been marked by widespread protests, sit-ins, and hunger strikes across the country.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts from different outlets highlight some differences in emphasis and reporting. NPR focuses on the government's shift in tactics and the role of Milan Vaishnav, a senior fellow at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, who suggests that the government realized its usual playbook was not working. The Guardian emphasizes the significance of Pradhan's resignation as a major victory for the CJP and a challenge to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's strongman image.

Sources NPR · The Guardian · BBC · Al Jazeera · Washington Examiner · CBS News

Federal appeals court rejects Trump bid to enforce key parts of mail-in voting order ahead of midterm elections.

A federal appeals court on Saturday refused to let the Trump administration enforce key parts of President Donald Trump's executive order tightening mail-in voting rules in 23 Democratic-led states. The 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals declined to lift a lower-court injunction from June that ruled parts of the president's order was unconstitutional after Democratic-led states, including California, Massachusetts and Washington, sued the administration.

The appeals court rejected the administration's appeal in a 2-1 decision. The Trump order directed the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to compile lists of confirmed U.S. citizens eligible to vote and provide them to states, instructs the U.S. Postal Service (USPS) to establish new standards for mail-in ballots and directs the Justice Department to prioritize investigations into state and local election officials who issue federal ballots to people deemed ineligible to vote.

The lawsuit was brought by 23 states and the District of Columbia, led by California, Massachusetts, Nevada and Washington. The states argue the Constitution gives them primary responsibility for administering federal elections and that the president exceeded his authority by directing federal agencies to impose new voting requirements.

Where the accounts differ

  • FOX News and New York Post both report on Congressman Jim Jordan's criticism of the ruling, but only FOX News provides a quote from him.
  • The Hill reports on President Trump's July primetime address on election integrity, while The Guardian mentions his claims about widespread voter fraud in 2020.

Sources FOX News · Washington Examiner · New York Post · The Hill · The Guardian

Israeli forces detain over 70 people in West Bank after deadly clashes between soldiers and Palestinian villagers.

At least four Palestinians and two Israeli soldiers were killed in a shootout in the northern village of Tal in the occupied West Bank. The incident occurred when a group of about 20 Israeli settlers, many armed, approached the village with the intent to cause violence. Villagers confronted the settlers, and as tensions rose, Israeli soldiers arrived on the scene. A Palestinian man grabbed a gun from one of the settlers, leading to a shootout that resulted in the deaths of four Palestinians and two Israeli soldiers.

The Israeli military confirmed that it had arrested more than 70 people across the West Bank overnight and into Saturday, with troops remaining in the village of Tell where the clashes erupted. The arrests were part of a wider crackdown on Palestinian residents, with reports of widespread searches and raids at hospitals and homes.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts from different outlets diverge in their descriptions of the events leading up to the shootout. The BBC reports that Palestinians confronted Jewish settlers who were attacking them, while Israel's military said it had responded to reports that hikers had been attacked. Al Jazeera states that a group of about 20 Israeli settlers approached the village with the intent to cause violence.

An 83-year-old man who went missing near the Grand Canyon was rescued after an overnight search.

Man, 83, who went missing near Grand Canyon rescued after overnight search

Gerald Guinn, an 83-year-old man from Utah, was hiking with another person in a remote location on the edge of the Kaibab National Forest at the Grand Canyon National Park. They split up to search for a nearby historic object, but Guinn never reunited with his companion. The other hiker searched for several hours before requesting help.

The Kane County Sheriff's Office Search and Rescue team conducted an overnight search using drones and search dogs, but found no sign of Guinn. The next morning, the Grand Canyon National Park Service took over the search efforts, adding a helicopter and ground teams to the search. A cell phone ping indicated Guinn's general location, leading the NPS to move the search to the east.

The NPS helicopter located Guinn, who was well enough to wave to the pilot. However, due to the terrain, the helicopter could not land, so a Kane County search and rescue team was sent to assess and stabilize Guinn.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that the operation was completed at 1:30 a.m., about a day and a half after Guinn first began his hike. FOX News does not provide this specific time frame.

Sources CBS News · FOX News
