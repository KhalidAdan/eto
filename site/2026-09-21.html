Hispanic voters’ support for Trump wanes over economy and immigration

Hispanic voters who helped re-elect Donald Trump in 2024 are discontent with his second-term economic and immigration policies, according to recent polling – indicating they may not be willing to support the president’s party in the midterm elections. More than 65% of Hispanic voters said they are displeased with Trump’s handling of his job, and 78% said they would prefer to see the Democratic party in control of Congress, according to a September poll from the New York Times/Siena University. The economy and cost of living are top concerns for Hispanic voters, polls show. Trump’s message of economic populism helped him get elected in 2024, but voters, including Republicans, have soured on his handling of the economy amid high inflation and rising costs. The Iran war has pushed gas prices past $4 per gallon on average. Inflation has risen over the last year and remained stubborn at 3.4%, elevating grocery costs.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that 63% of Hispanic respondents told a Fox News survey in September they plan to vote for the Democratic congressional candidate in their district, while Al Jazeera and ABC News do not mention this specific poll. The Guardian also states that 54% of Hispanic voters said they would vote for the Democratic House candidate in an UnidosUS poll from April/May, compared with 27% who said they would favor the Republican, while ABC News and CBS News do not include this detail. The Guardian attributes the claim about Trump’s economic policies to the White House spokesperson, while other outlets do not mention this.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · ABC News · CBS News · Axios · BBC

ICE agent shoots and wounds man at traffic stop in Austin, Texas, officials say

An Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot and wounded a Venezuelan man during a traffic stop in Austin, Texas, on Sunday, according to multiple local officials. The man, identified as 28-year-old Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, was taken to a hospital in Austin in serious but stable condition before being placed into ICE custody. Austin Police Chief Lisa Davis confirmed that the shooting occurred after a traffic stop and that city police were not involved in the operation or the shooting. The city’s police arrived at the scene after the shooting and provided first aid to the injured man. Austin Mayor Kirk Watson called for a full investigation, emphasizing the city’s right to transparency and safety.

Eyewitness video captured moments after the shooting, showing the man clutching his chest and appearing distressed. A bystander, Nedavia Bolaños, recorded footage of the incident, expressing shock at the presence of federal officers in tactical gear who did not respond to her and her husband’s questions. The man was later seen being handcuffed and placed on the ground by ICE officers, with at least one visible bullet wound between his shoulder blades. His lawyer, Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch, stated that Garcés Pérez had arrived in the U.S. two years ago from Venezuela and was making a DoorDash delivery when he encountered ICE.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and ABC News both state that the man was in the U.S. illegally and had a final order of removal, a claim not made by The Guardian, The Hill, or Straight Arrow News. The New York Post and ABC News also mention that the shooting occurred during a “small foot pursuit,” a detail absent in other accounts. The Washington Examiner and Straight Arrow News note that the man was shot in the torso, while The Guardian and The Hill do not specify the location of the wound. The New York Post and ABC News claim that the man was not legally in the U.S., while The Guardian and The Hill do not specify his immigration status beyond his arrival from Venezuela.

Sources The Guardian · New York Post · The Hill · ABC News · Straight Arrow News · Washington Examiner

Hassett on Iran war: 'The president had to act'

National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett defended President Trump’s handling of the Iran war, stating the president “had to act” to prevent a dire outcome. Hassett told Fox News that Iran’s refusal to engage in talks and its aggressive actions in the Strait of Hormuz, including shooting ships, necessitated U.S. intervention. He claimed the U.S. Navy has escorted over 1 billion barrels of oil through the strait, keeping global prices low and preventing a potential spike to $150 or $200 per barrel. Hassett also noted that Iran is facing economic collapse, with hyperinflation and a dwindling oil supply. U.S. Central Command confirmed that more than 1 billion barrels of crude oil have been transported through the strait in recent months, though Iran disputes these claims. Gas prices in the U.S. have risen to $4.48, up 17 cents from the previous week, while diesel reached $6.50. Trump has downplayed the impact of the conflict on gas prices, calling it “a very inexpensive price to pay.”

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and The Daily Wire both report Trump’s comments about being in a “deciding mode” on Iran and considering options like “wiping Iran out” or “letting them rot economically.” The Daily Wire adds that Trump said he would be open to meeting with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian at the UN. The Daily Caller and New York Post report that Trump cut his Camp David stay short and returned to the White House a day early, citing Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia. Al Jazeera and CBS News describe the Houthi missile attack on Riyadh, with Al Jazeera noting that the strike was intercepted and no casualties were confirmed. The Daily Wire and The Hill both mention the U.S. naval escort of oil through the Strait of Hormuz, though The Daily Wire adds that Iran disputes these claims.

Sources The Hill · The Daily Caller · Al Jazeera · CBS News · New York Post · The Daily Wire

Trump Wants to Make His Triumphal Arch a Killing Machine

President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that he plans to convert his proposed 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington, D.C. into a "top grade military complex" equipped with snipers and drone storage. The arch, which was first proposed in October 2025 to commemorate America’s 250th anniversary, would be located near the National Mall, between Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial. Trump cited a "strong request" from the U.S. military and national security concerns as the rationale for the change. The project has faced legal challenges, including a lawsuit from Vietnam War veterans who argue it blocks views of Arlington National Cemetery and lacks congressional approval. A federal judge ordered the administration to provide 48 hours’ notice before any work on the project. The National Park Service has also raised concerns about the arch’s potential adverse effects on nearby landmarks.

The arch, which would be one of the tallest structures in Washington, D.C., has drawn criticism for its height and location. The Federal Aviation Administration stated the arch poses no hazard to flights at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. However, Democratic lawmakers and heritage groups have raised concerns about the project’s impact on historic sites and airspace. Trump has previously used similar national security arguments to justify other projects, including a White House ballroom, which the Supreme Court allowed to proceed despite opposition.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and Straight Arrow News both mention Rep. Don Beyer calling Trump’s plan "obviously idiotic" and suggesting Trump expects to lose a lawsuit. NPR and Axios do not include this quote. Mother Jones and CBS News both state that the arch would be twice the height of the Lincoln Memorial, while BBC and Axios describe it as being half as high as the Washington Monument. These are conflicting descriptions of the arch’s height. Axios and CBS News mention the National Park Service is rushing through a final review, while Mother Jones and Straight Arrow News note the NPS found the arch could have "adverse effects" on historic properties.

Sources Mother Jones · NPR · CBS News · Axios · BBC · Straight Arrow News

Russia election results show Putin’s party winning; Ukraine launches drone attacks on final day

Russia’s parliamentary elections, held since the start of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, saw President Vladimir Putin’s United Russia party dominate early results, according to state media. The Central Election Commission reported 90.43% of ballots counted, with United Russia securing 57.86% of the vote. Other parties, including the Communist Party and the Liberal Democratic Party, trailed behind but were still projected to win seats. The vote, which included residents of annexed Ukrainian regions and Crimea, was described as tightly controlled with limited opposition to Putin’s policies.

Ukraine launched a major drone attack on the final day of the election, targeting Moscow and its surrounding areas. Russian officials reported shooting down over 1,000 drones, with at least two people killed and 20 injured. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy claimed the strikes hit a Russian oil refinery and logistics facilities, aiming to disrupt the election and pressure Russia’s economy. The attack coincided with Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure, including a monastery and residential areas, which killed additional civilians.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and BBC both note that Yabloko, the only anti-war party, was removed from the ballot by the Supreme Court, but Al Jazeera reports that Yabloko’s leader, Nikolai Rybakov, spoiled his ballot paper, while BBC states that Yabloko’s candidates were barred from running and some election observers were unregistered. NPR and Newsweek report that the attack on Moscow killed two people, while Straight Arrow News and Newsweek specify the victims as a 74-year-old man and a 44-year-old woman. The Guardian and NPR mention that the attack damaged a Moscow oil refinery, while Al Jazeera and BBC do not specify the location of the damage. The Guardian and NPR also note that the attack killed two people and wounded 20, while Straight Arrow News and Newsweek report similar numbers but with different specifics.

Sources Al Jazeera · Straight Arrow News · The Guardian · NPR · Newsweek · BBC

Lindsay Clancy Holdout Juror Just Got An Unexpected Trump Connection

The lone holdout juror in the Lindsay Clancy triple murder case, Michael Desronvil, has retained attorney Edward Paltzik, who previously represented President Donald Trump in several high-profile lawsuits. Paltzik described Desronvil as an “American Hero and Champion of Justice” and claimed he is “safe and sound” in a secure location. The jury deadlocked 11-1, leading to a mistrial on September 4. Clancy’s defense argued she was suffering from postpartum psychosis and not criminally responsible. Desronvil has faced criticism from other jurors for being “arrogant” and disengaged, but he denied these claims, stating he was “cut off” by other jurors when explaining alternative theories. Clancy admitted to killing her children in January 2023 and remains at Tewksbury Hospital while prosecutors decide next steps.

Patrick Clancy, Lindsay Clancy’s ex-husband, described how his youngest son, Callan, died in his arms after Lindsay strangled their three children. He told CBS’s 60 Minutes that he still talks to his children “all the time” and asked them for help. Clancy’s defense argued she was not criminally responsible due to postpartum psychosis, and the mistrial followed a deadlock among jurors. Clancy remains at Tewksbury Hospital, and a hearing is scheduled for September 29 to determine next steps.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire reports that Paltzik represented Trump in lawsuits against The New York Times and CBS, with the latter settling for $16 million. The Guardian and CBS News do not mention the $16 million settlement, though both note Paltzik represented Trump in lawsuits against CBS and ABC News. The Guardian and CBS News describe Clancy’s interview with 60 Minutes, while The Daily Wire and Newsweek do not. The Guardian and CBS News mention Clancy’s statement about talking to her children, which The Daily Wire and Newsweek omit. The Daily Wire attributes the $16 million settlement to Paramount Global, while the Guardian and CBS News do not specify the settlement amount or the party involved.

Sources The Daily Wire · New York Post · Newsweek · The Guardian · CBS News · BBC

Trump to meet Mamdani in New York City on Monday

President Donald Trump and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani are set to meet Monday at Gracie Mansion, the mayor's residence in Manhattan. A White House official and the mayor's press office confirmed the meeting, which will focus on issues affecting New York City and its residents. The meeting is expected to take place around 4 p.m. and will be closed to the press. This will be the first time the two meet in New York City, though they have previously met in Washington, D.C. The meeting follows a November 2025 meeting in the Oval Office, where Trump praised Mamdani and expressed optimism about working together on issues like housing.

The meeting comes amid preparations for the United Nations General Assembly in New York City, which will host world leaders. Mamdani has made affordability a central theme of his administration, with lowering housing costs a key promise. The two have maintained a cordial relationship despite their political differences, with Trump previously calling Mamdani a "communist" and Mamdani criticizing Trump as a "fascist."

Where the accounts differ

CBS News states that the meeting will take place around 4 p.m. and be closed to the press, while FOX News does not mention a specific time. The Daily Wire notes that presidential visits to Gracie Mansion are rare, citing historical examples. The New York Post mentions that the meeting will mark the first time Mamdani invites Trump to his residence, a detail not included in other accounts. The Hill reports that Trump praised Mamdani during their November 2025 meeting, a claim not made by CBS News. The Washington Examiner does not mention the meeting time or the rarity of the visit, while The Daily Wire includes details about a mock newspaper cover presented by Mamdani to Trump in February.

Sources CBS News · The Hill · Washington Examiner · FOX News · New York Post · The Daily Wire
