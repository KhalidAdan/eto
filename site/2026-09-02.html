Dan Driscoll Resigns, Leaving Army Without Confirmed Leadership

Army Secretary Dan Driscoll submitted his resignation to President Donald Trump, according to multiple outlets. The resignation is pending acceptance by the White House. Driscoll, who became the youngest Army secretary in 2025, has been at the center of tensions with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who has dismissed several top military officers. Driscoll’s resignation leaves the Army without Senate-confirmed leadership, as the Senate has not confirmed a new secretary. Under Secretary of the Army Mike Obadal is next in line to serve until a new secretary is confirmed.

Driscoll’s resignation comes amid ongoing turmoil at the Pentagon under Hegseth, who has faced criticism for his management style and the removal of senior military leaders. The Army secretary manages the Army’s budget and long-term planning but does not command troops in the field. Driscoll is close to Vice President JD Vance, and there were prior speculations that he might join Vance’s presidential campaign. The White House has praised Driscoll for his work advancing Trump’s agenda, though the exact nature of his contributions remains unclear.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Caller and FOX News both report that Driscoll submitted his resignation to Trump, but only FOX News attributes the claim to a senior U.S. official. The Daily Wire and ABC News mention Driscoll’s resignation followed a strained relationship with Hegseth and repeated warnings to the White House about Hegseth’s firing of senior officers. CBS News and The Daily Wire note that Driscoll expressed concerns about Hegseth’s impact on Army transformation and readiness. NPR does not mention Driscoll’s concerns about Hegseth’s actions, instead focusing on his relationship with Vance and the broader context of the Iran war.

Sources The Daily Caller · NPR · CBS News · FOX News · The Daily Wire · ABC News

Iran, US exchange new attacks: Who was hit in latest strikes?

The United States and Iran have exchanged a new round of attacks, with the US targeting Iranian military sites and Iran retaliating with missile and drone strikes. The US Central Command confirmed strikes on Iranian military targets along the southern coast, while Iran claimed US attacks hit a wedding in Kuhestak, killing at least four people. Iran also fired ballistic missiles towards Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Iraq’s Kurdish region. US President Donald Trump warned that Iran would be “totally wiped out” if it retaliates. The conflict has raised fears of escalation into a wider regional war.

Iran’s military said it launched attacks on US bases and assets in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Iraq’s Erbil. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claimed it hit the Sheikh Isa Airbase in Bahrain and US bases in Jordan and Kuwait. Iran also said it destroyed several US drones and claimed to have killed a number of US soldiers. Kuwait reported intercepting drones and missiles but did not report casualties. Jordanian forces said air defense systems intercepted and destroyed 10 of 13 Iranian ballistic missiles that entered their airspace.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and Washington Examiner both report that Iran claimed a US airstrike hit a wedding in Kuhestak, killing at least four people and wounding 68. The Washington Examiner adds that Iranian officials said at least five people, including a child, were killed. The Daily Caller and UPI do not mention the wedding attack, with UPI instead reporting that the US military struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island and Iran retaliated with strikes on US bases in Jordan. The Associated Press and Christian Science Monitor do not mention the wedding attack, focusing instead on the broader conflict and its economic impact.

Sources Al Jazeera · UPI · Associated Press · Christian Science Monitor · Washington Examiner · The Daily Caller

Trump Faces Calls To Spend In Texas As Dems Blitz Republicans

Seth Moulton in a primary, marking a rare win for older Democratic incumbents. Markey, 80, ran against Moulton, 47, who criticized Markey’s age. The race highlighted internal Democratic debates over generational politics.

Charlie Kirk Assassination Case Faces Make-Or-Break Court Moment

A Utah judge is expected to decide whether Tyler Robinson will stand trial for the killing of conservative activist Charlie Kirk, and whether prosecutors will be allowed to pursue the death penalty. Following a five-day preliminary hearing, Judge Tony Graf will hear closing arguments from the state and defense for a maximum of two hours each before ultimately giving his ruling. Robinson, 23, is charged with aggravated murder and multiple other felonies in the September 2025 fatal shooting of Kirk at Utah Valley University, where he was engaging others with differing political views. Prosecutors have said they intend to seek the death penalty if Robinson is convicted. Robinson’s attorneys have asked Graf to reject the aggravating circumstance that could make the killing eligible for capital punishment, arguing that prosecutors have not shown the shooter knowingly created a “great risk of death” to anyone other than Kirk. Prosecutors counter that others were indeed at risk when the shot was fired.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and FOX News both report that Robinson surrendered to the Washington County Sheriff’s Office about 33 hours after Kirk was shot, arriving with his parents and a family friend. The Daily Wire states that Robinson’s father had reportedly confronted him after recognizing images of the suspected shooter released by authorities, while FOX News does not mention this detail. The Guardian and CBS News both state that Robinson’s DNA was found on the murder weapon, but the Guardian adds that the DNA testing was questioned by Robinson’s lawyers, while CBS News does not mention this. The Guardian and CBS News both report that prosecutors allege Robinson targeted Kirk for his political views against gay marriage and transgender rights, while FOX News and The Daily Wire do not mention this specific motive. The Guardian and CBS News both state that the judge ruled there is sufficient evidence to try Robinson for aggravated murder, while The Daily Wire and FOX News do not mention this ruling explicitly.

Kalshi bans ex-congressman George Santos from prediction platform for life

Kalshi, a prediction market platform, announced on Monday that it has permanently banned former Republican congressman George Santos from its platform. The ban comes after Santos was accused of profiting from bets tied to his own attendance at Donald Trump’s State of the Union address earlier this year. Kalshi imposed a $71,356 fine alongside the lifetime ban, citing Santos’s refusal to cooperate fully with its internal investigation. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) previously fined Santos $17,500 and ordered him to return profits from his trades. Santos responded on X, thanking Kalshi for the ban and mocking the platform’s longevity.

Santos, who was expelled from Congress in December 2023 after being found to have defrauded donors and lied about his finances, pleaded guilty to federal wire fraud and identity theft charges in 2024. He was sentenced to more than seven years in prison but served less than three months before Trump commuted his sentence. In July, Santos agreed to pay $35,000 to settle the CFTC’s case without admitting wrongdoing, which also barred him from trading on any platform for three years. Kalshi’s lifetime ban adds an additional penalty.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and BBC both state that Santos made over $17,500 in profit from his trades, while the Straight Arrow News reports he earned $17,839.57. The Guardian and ABC News mention Santos was fined $71,356 by Kalshi, while the BBC reports the fine as $71,356 (£38,000). The Guardian and NPR note Santos made more than $17,000 in profit, while the Straight Arrow News states he wagered more than $17,000. The Guardian and CBS News report Santos was banned for refusing to cooperate with Kalshi’s investigation, while the Straight Arrow News adds that Santos criticized Kalshi for not following its own notices and deadlines. The Guardian and BBC attribute the $17,500 profit to the CFTC, while the Straight Arrow News attributes it to Kalshi.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · ABC News · CBS News · BBC · Straight Arrow News

Police kill knife-wielding woman near Times Square after deadly stabbing

A woman armed with knives stabbed two people in Times Square on Monday, prompting police to shoot and kill her. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani confirmed the incident, stating one victim died and the other was in stable condition. The suspect, identified as Pamela Cisneros, was also killed in the shooting. Both victims and the suspect were taken to Bellevue Hospital. The New York City Police Department described the attack as random and unprovoked, with no terrorism involvement. The shooting occurred near West 42nd Street and Seventh Avenue, just before 4:30 p.m.

The suspect, Pamela Cisneros, 49, of Queens, was identified as the attacker. She stabbed a 68-year-old man and a woman, both of whom were taken to Bellevue Hospital. The woman victim, Erin Piacenti, 32, of Chester, New Jersey, was a Bank of America vice president. The male victim was in stable condition. Police said Cisneros had a history of mental health issues and was emotionally disturbed. The confrontation with officers lasted about four minutes before she was shot after refusing to drop her knives.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both report that the woman was shot after she approached officers with a knife, but The Guardian adds that she told police, “I’m not dropping anything, I would rather kill both of you.” The Daily Wire and CBS News also include this quote, while FOX News and Fox Business attribute the quote to police commissioner Jessica Tisch. The Washington Examiner and The Guardian differ in the number of victims, with The Examiner stating two victims and one suspect died, while The Guardian and others specify that the woman victim and the suspect died, with the male victim surviving. The Daily Wire and CBS News provide additional details about Erin Piacenti’s employment and personal life, which are not included in the other accounts.

Lindsay Clancy trial: Jury sent home for the day without a verdict

The jury in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy was sent home on Tuesday after failing to reach a unanimous verdict. The jury, composed of nine women and three men, had been deliberating for over 23 hours across four days. The judge, William Sullivan, instructed them to continue their deliberations. ABC News reported that if the jury remains deadlocked, the judge may declare a mistrial, allowing the case to be retried. The trial has been ongoing since July 20, 2026, and has drawn significant public attention.

The case centers on whether Clancy, 36, acted with premeditation or was suffering from postpartum psychosis when she killed her three children in 2023. The prosecution argues she made a calculated decision, while the defense claims she was not criminally responsible due to her mental state. The jury is considering several verdict options, including first-degree murder, second-degree murder, and not guilty by reason of insanity. The outcome will determine whether Clancy faces life in prison or psychiatric detention.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that under Massachusetts law, the prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt that Clancy was criminally responsible, while The Free Press mentions that the insanity defense is treated differently in Massachusetts courts. FOX News states that a retired judge believes a verdict is still possible despite the deadlock, whereas The Federalist focuses on public reaction to the trial rather than the legal process. The New York Post notes that a woman was arrested for allegedly filming jurors, a detail not mentioned in other accounts.

Sources ABC News · BBC · The Free Press · FOX News · The Federalist · New York Post

Eight Democrats join Republicans in voting to condemn socialism

The House of Representatives passed a resolution condemning socialism in a 220-192 vote, with two Democrats voting present. The resolution, which includes a condemnation of the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA), was supported by eight Democrats, including Reps. Jared Golden (D-ME) and Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-WA). The vote followed a procedural rule vote that passed 210-208, with Golden and Perez defecting from their party to ensure the resolution could move forward. The resolution also includes support for the SAVE America Act, a Republican-backed bill aimed at restricting voting rights. The vote was part of a broader effort by Republicans to frame Democrats as supporting socialist policies, with some Republicans using the vote to highlight perceived failures in Senate leadership.

The resolution was part of a larger procedural package that included other bills, such as the Northeast Lobsterman Protection Act, which Rep. Jared Golden (D-ME) co-authored. The vote was met with criticism from some Democrats, who accused Golden and Perez of betraying their party. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) called the defections a "significant breach of trust" and stated that the Democratic Caucus would address the issue.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and CBS News both report that the resolution passed with a 220-192 vote, but the Washington Examiner notes that the vote included two Democrats voting present, while CBS News states the same. The Free Press and Just The News both mention the 220-192-2 split, with two Democrats voting present. The Axios account does not mention the exact vote count, instead focusing on the procedural vote that allowed the resolution to move forward. The FOX News account does not mention the exact vote count, instead focusing on the procedural vote and the debate over the resolution.
