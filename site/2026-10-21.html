Trump cites French protests in warning to Kansas voters: ‘Never happening here’

President Donald Trump warned Kansas voters during a rally for Sen. Roger Marshall (R-KS) that the United States could face similar unrest to France, which he claimed is being “conquered, looted, and burned to the ground by migrants, jihadists, and communists.” Trump described the French protests as a cautionary tale, urging Americans to avoid the fate of France. The unrest in France, which began as student-led demonstrations over education and living costs, escalated into widespread riots, looting, and clashes with police, with some cities imposing overnight curfews.

French President Emmanuel Macron attributed the unrest to a combination of economic grievances, including rising fuel prices and the cost of living, which he linked to the war in Iran. Macron also condemned the violence as the work of “insurrectionist elements from radical far-left groups, joined by numerous criminals.” The protests, which drew hundreds of thousands of participants, saw some 2,400 arrests in a single day and widespread damage to infrastructure and businesses.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both report that Trump claimed France is being “conquered, looted, and burned to the ground by migrants, jihadists, and communists,” while the New York Post and Newsweek attribute the same claim to Trump. The Axios and The Guardian accounts state that Macron linked the unrest to the war in Iran and rising fuel prices, while the FOX News account attributes the same claims to Macron and includes a quote from Lisa Daftari, an Iran expert, who disputes Macron’s explanation. The New York Post and Newsweek also mention that Trump claimed the unrest was about “immigration, not schools,” a claim not present in the Washington Examiner or The Guardian accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · FOX News · New York Post · Axios · The Guardian · Newsweek

Ohio governor’s race takes ugly turn with viral video featuring Acton’s mother

A viral video targeting Amy Acton, the Democratic candidate for Ohio governor, has sparked controversy in the race against GOP nominee Vivek Ramaswamy. The clip features Acton’s estranged mother, Donna Arthur, calling Acton a “liar” and urging voters to reject her. The video, which has circulated widely on social media, does not include a disclaimer about who paid for it. Acton’s campaign has condemned the ad, calling it a “despicable low” and accusing Ramaswamy of exploiting her personal history. Acton, a former director of the Ohio Department of Health, has spoken publicly about her childhood, including allegations of abuse by her stepfather, Cledith Arthur, who was charged with rape in 1978 but never convicted. The video does not directly reference the abuse allegations but challenges Acton’s account of living in a tent during her youth. Ramaswamy’s campaign has defended the ad, claiming that multiple people who know Acton “have told voters not to vote for her.”

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and CBS News both include the claim that Acton’s mother stayed married to Cledith Arthur until his death, while The Hill and The Daily Wire do not. Jalen Hurts Diagnosed With Blood Clots in Lungs After Playing in London Game

Jalen Hurts, the Philadelphia Eagles' quarterback, was diagnosed with deep vein thrombosis and bilateral pulmonary emboli after playing in the team's Week 5 game against the Jacksonville Jaguars in London on October 11, 2026. The Eagles initially reported a superficial blood clot in Hurts' calf, which was discovered during an ultrasound in London before the game. Hurts played the game despite being told not to sleep due to the risk of the clot spreading. He later underwent further testing in Philadelphia, which revealed the condition had worsened. Hurts is now on blood thinners and is expected to miss the rest of the 2026 season. The Eagles and Hurts' agent stated he is clinically stable and will not return to football activities until he is off medication.

The Eagles' medical staff and coach Nick Sirianni defended their handling of Hurts' condition, emphasizing that the decision to play him was based on the advice of medical experts. Sirianni stated that the team's priority is the health of the players and that the same doctors who helped the team win the Super Bowl were involved in Hurts' care. The team also noted that Hurts was hospitalized in London until 4 a.m. before the game and was advised not to sleep.

Where the accounts differ

The Nation reports that Hurts may have taken a nap at the stadium before the game, though this is unclear, while The Daily Wire states that Hurts did not sleep for 36 hours before the game. The Guardian notes that Hurts' agent, Nicole Lynn, clarified that he did get some sleep before the game. ABC News and The Daily Caller both mention that Hurts was hospitalized in London until 4 a.m. and that the team's medical staff advised him not to sleep. The Nation and The Guardian attribute the claim about Hurts not sleeping to Tom Pelissero, an NFL insider, who later walked back the “awake for 36 hours” part, which was disputed by Hurts' agent.

Sources The Nation · ABC News · Newsweek · The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller · The Guardian

Elon Musk says Tesla and SpaceX are exploring a merger

Elon Musk confirmed that Tesla and SpaceX are exploring a potential merger, with both companies forming special committees to assess the possibility. Musk stated that while he supports the idea, the merger is not guaranteed and would require shareholder approval. He emphasized that the committees have not yet reached an agreement on the terms of any potential deal. Musk also highlighted the complementary nature of the companies' work in areas such as artificial intelligence, chip design, and robotics. The merger would create a combined entity with a market value exceeding $3.5 trillion, making it the largest corporate tie-up in history. SpaceX reported its first quarterly profit, and Tesla shares rose following Musk's comments.

The unveiling of the Tesla Roadster, a car equipped with SpaceX-designed thrusters, was described by some as a symbolic gesture hinting at the potential merger. The vehicle performed a midair maneuver during its recent showcase, though it was noted that it can only briefly elevate rather than fully fly. Musk has also mentioned that the Roadster will be the last manually drivable car Tesla builds, signaling a shift toward autonomous vehicles and robotics.

Trump’s Voter Raids Have Begun

Federal authorities in California arrested at least 11 people and charged 28 others with alleged voter fraud, including registering as noncitizens and casting ballots. The operation, called “Operation Protect the Vote,” is part of the Trump administration’s efforts to address claims of noncitizen voting ahead of the midterm elections. The Justice Department alleges that all 28 charged individuals are legal permanent residents but not U.S. citizens. The administration has threatened to criminally investigate local officials who process ballots from those on a federal list of suspected noncitizens.

The Justice Department sent California election officials a list of 20,000 people it claims are “likely noncitizens” registered to vote. Officials rejected the demand to withhold ballots, prompting the Justice Department to sue for a court order to prevent California from counting ballots from those on the list. The Trump administration has also threatened local officials with criminal prosecution for processing those ballots.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Federalist both report that the Justice Department charged 28 people, with 11 arrested. The Daily Wire states that 28 foreigners were arrested, with at least 11 in custody. The Dispatch provides no specific numbers or details about arrests or charges. The Federalist mentions that 23 of the 28 charged allegedly voted in U.S. elections, a claim not made by Mother Jones or The Daily Wire. The Daily Wire includes specific cases, such as Alexander Muñoz and Amandeep Kaur, which are not detailed in the other accounts. The Federalist also notes that Leslie Stahl downplayed noncitizen voting on CBS News, a detail absent from the other accounts.

Sources Mother Jones · The Daily Wire · The Dispatch · The Federalist

Trump unveils $25M whistleblower program offering up to $30,000 for fraud tips

President Donald Trump announced a new Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program during an Oval Office event with Vice President JD Vance and independent journalist Nick Shirley. The program offers rewards for tips leading to fraud convictions or significant federal enforcement actions. Trump stated that whistleblowers could receive 1% of the amount of fraud they report, with individual awards capped at $30,000. The White House said the initiative is administered by the Justice Department and that Americans can report suspected fraud through fraud.gov. Trump also mentioned he would personally resolve disputes over reward distribution.

The program was described as a significant expansion of efforts to combat taxpayer fraud, with Trump emphasizing the importance of holding fraudsters accountable. The White House credited independent journalist Nick Shirley with prompting increased federal law enforcement activity in Minnesota, which led to investigations, arrests, and new charges against several individuals and organizations linked to alleged fraud at Somali-run daycare centers.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that Trump described a different payout structure, stating journalists could also participate, subject to approval from their employers. It also mentions that a White House official initially described a $25 million initiative with rewards of up to $30,000. The Daily Wire and CBS News both state that the program caps individual awards at $30,000, while FOX News does not mention a cap. The New York Post and CBS News note that Trump said there is no limit to whistleblower compensation, contradicting the cap mentioned by other outlets. The Daily Wire and CBS News also mention that Trump presented a $10,000 check to Nick Shirley, while FOX News and the New York Post do not specify the amount given to Shirley.

Cuts force World Food Programme to end support for half a million Palestinians in Gaza

The World Food Programme (WFP) has scaled back its aid to Gaza, reducing support from 1.6 million to 1.1 million people each month. This follows a more than 50% drop in funding from 2025 levels. The WFP’s director for Palestine, Shaun Hughes, warned that the situation remains dire, with two-thirds of the population facing crisis levels of food insecurity. The agency needs over £300m over six months to maintain its programmes. Food insecurity is at emergency levels for 200,000 Palestinians, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). The WFP has also reduced food rations and hot meals, with deliveries now occurring every other day in October. Most families in Gaza still rely on aid due to high unemployment, with about 80% of the population unemployed, according to UN data.

Gaza’s agricultural and fishing sectors have been severely impacted, with farmland occupied by Israeli forces and fishing boats barred from coastal waters. A recent Unicef survey found that one in eight children in Gaza are chronically malnourished or stunted. Humanitarian organisations are struggling with funding after the Trump administration cut USAID and donor nations reduced aid due to cost of living crises. Aid will be needed until Israel and the international community launch promised reconstruction efforts.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that the WFP reduced support from 1.6 million to 1.1 million people in October, while Al Jazeera states that the WFP cut support from 1.6 million in September to 1.1 million in October. The Guardian attributes the reduction to funding cuts, while Al Jazeera notes that the WFP cited funding shortages as the reason for the cuts. The Guardian mentions that the famine declared in summer 2025 has receded, while Al Jazeera states that famine was declared in Gaza City in August 2025 and that the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification indicated it had receded after the ceasefire began in June 2025. The Guardian attributes the reduction in hot meals to funding cuts, while Al Jazeera specifies that the WFP halved food rations and reduced hot meals from 250,000 to 150,000 daily.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera
