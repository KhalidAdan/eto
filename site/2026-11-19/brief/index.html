Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang to Testify Before Congress on National Security Allegations

Jensen Huang, former CEO of Nvidia, is set to testify before Congress on December 1, according to a joint committee announcement. Huang will appear in closed-door sessions followed by Nvidia’s General Counsel Tim Teter, who will testify the next day. The committees involved include the Select Committee on China, the House Foreign Affairs Committee, and the House Intelligence Committee. The chairmen of these committees stated they are seeking answers on Huang’s alleged role in a potential breach of national security, including his possible involvement with a Chinese intelligence asset.

Huang resigned from Nvidia after a second extramarital affair was revealed, following initial accusations of an affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive. Alice Huang is alleged to have been a spy for Beijing and facilitated the smuggling of advanced Nvidia chips to China. Huang agreed to cooperate with an internal investigation after his affair was first disclosed. The company announced it was searching for a new CEO and named CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO. Nvidia also formed a national security and export control oversight committee on its board.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Guardian both report that Huang will testify on December 1, with Teter testifying the next day. The Guardian adds that Congressmen Brian Mast, John Moolenaar, and Rick Crawford, who chair the relevant committees, had previously warned Huang of potential subpoenas. The Guardian also details the involvement of Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based company that allegedly facilitated $12bn in chip sales in violation of U.S. export controls. The Daily Wire mentions Trump’s praise for Nvidia’s new oversight committee but does not reference Megaspeed or the export control violations. The Guardian and The Daily Wire differ on the exact date of Huang’s resignation, with The Guardian stating it occurred on November 12 and The Daily Wire noting it was last Thursday, which would be November 10.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Guardian · NPR · Axios · Newsweek · BBC

Trump lands in China with key meetings with Xi and Putin on the agenda

President Donald Trump plans to meet separately with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Thursday in China, in addition to their already scheduled three-way meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping. A U.S. official confirmed the Trump-Putin meeting, which will take place on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen. Analysts are not expecting substantive breakthroughs despite the unprecedented nature of the meeting. Trump said he plans to talk with Xi and Putin in their joint meeting about limiting nuclear weapons and bringing an end to the war in Ukraine, which began in 2022 with Russia's invasion. Trump has frequently boasted of his rapport with Putin as relations between the United States and Ukraine appear to have cratered recently. Trump rejected new Russia sanctions while Kyiv was undermined by disruptions in satellite support for its drones during the war.

Trump landed in China on Wednesday seeking to reinforce America's status as the world's leading technology power, but a distressing series of technical outages back home has left his administration scrambling to calm the U.S. public. The issues put the administration on the defensive at a moment when the president wants to project strength and certainty before the meeting with Xi and Putin. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio stepped in to reassure Americans on Trump's behalf, stating there was "zero" evidence that the outages were connected or tied to rival nations, cyber hackers or criminals.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill reports that the meeting is drawing comparisons to the Yalta Conference and that Trump is on the opposite side of Xi and Putin in the U.S.-Israel war with Iran, though he has played down their support for Tehran. The Free Press includes a segment about the technical outages and mentions a report from CNN about Iran developing plans to deploy a dirty bomb in Washington, D.C. FOX News states that the meeting is a "two versus one" scenario with China and Russia working together against Trump, and that China is fueling Russia's war machine in Ukraine. Al Jazeera and Newsweek focus on the nuclear disarmament agenda, with Newsweek noting that China's nuclear stockpile is growing and that Beijing may not be interested in reducing its arsenal. The Hill and Newsweek both mention the U.S. Defense Department's estimate that China's nuclear warhead count is likely to exceed 1,000 by the end of the decade.

Anthropic raises over $100 billion in record-setting IPO

Anthropic, an artificial intelligence company, raised more than $100 billion in its initial public offering (IPO), setting a new record. The company, which was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, is valued at around $1.5 trillion. The IPO, which includes the sale of 956 million shares at $83 each, is expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT. Anthropic's revenue in the first half of 2026 surged 1,300% to $16.3 billion, with adjusted operating income reaching $1.6 billion. The company also plans to raise an additional $26 billion through private placements with tech giants like Google, Microsoft, and Nvidia.

The IPO comes amid growing concerns about an AI bubble, with investors wary of the sector's volatility. Despite these concerns, the IPO is seen as a significant milestone for the AI industry, with some analysts predicting the stock could rise sharply on its first day of trading. Anthropic's rapid growth and dominance in the AI market have positioned it as a key player in the ongoing competition with companies like OpenAI and Meta.

Where the accounts differ

NPR states that Anthropic raised more than $107 billion, while Al Jazeera reports $81.3 billion. CBS News mentions $107 billion as well but notes that the total haul is more than $107 billion, which conflicts with Al Jazeera's figure. Axios and BBC do not specify the exact amount raised, focusing instead on broader implications and market context. The discrepancy in the total amount raised is attributed to different sources and reporting methods.

House Democrats nominate Hakeem Jeffries for speaker of the House

House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries as their nominee for speaker of the House in a closed-door vote, with 251 yes votes and 11 no votes. Jeffries, a Brooklyn native and current House minority leader, is set to become the first Black speaker of the House if he secures the required 218 votes in January. The vote followed a blue wave that gave Democrats control of both chambers of Congress. Jeffries emphasized the economy and affordability as central to his leadership, vowing to fight for every American and challenge the status quo. Other historic leadership choices included Rep. Katherine Clark becoming the first woman to serve as majority leader and Rep. Pete Aguilar becoming the first Latino majority whip.

Jeffries faced opposition from a group of progressive Democrats, including Rep.-elects Darializa Avila Chevalier, Adam Hamawy, Frederick Haynes, Melat Kiros, Donavan McKinney, Chris Rabb, and Claire Valdez, who stated they voted no because they believe the party's direction does not fully reflect constituent demands. These members cited issues such as Medicare for All, ending the war in Israel, and reforming immigration policies as priorities. Jeffries also faced criticism from some members for his support of Israel and ties to corporate political action committees.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and NPR both state that Jeffries received 251 yes votes and 11 no votes, while FOX News and the New York Post report the same numbers. However, FOX News and the New York Post mention that two members did not vote, whereas ABC News and NPR do not. The New York Post and FOX News note that Jeffries requested a tallied vote, while ABC News and NPR state he requested a recorded vote. The New York Post and FOX News also mention that Jeffries changed his approach to follow the 2018 model, while ABC News and NPR do not. The New York Post and FOX News report that Rep.-elect Don Leonard was threatened by senior Democrats if he voted no, a detail not included in ABC News or NPR.

Sources ABC News · Axios · NPR · BBC · FOX News · New York Post

Duffy's $50 billion request gets chilly reception from Senate GOP

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy is seeking $50 billion in additional funding to overhaul the nation's air traffic control system, a request that has drawn skepticism from Senate Republicans. The funding request follows a nationwide ground stop that disrupted over 18,000 flights and caused nearly 28,000 delays. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford stated that the disruption was caused by a newly installed router in El Dorado, Arkansas, and that no evidence of foul play or AI involvement was found. The FAA is currently relying on older Cisco routers to maintain operations while investigating the cause.

Duffy and FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford pitched the funding request to Senate Republicans at their weekly lunch, with some lawmakers questioning whether the full $50 billion is necessary. Commerce Chairman Ted Cruz suggested using the fast-track reconciliation process to secure additional funds but emphasized the need to demonstrate effective use of the $12.5 billion already allocated. Senate Majority Leader John Thune expressed concerns about unspent funds and preferred working with Democrats through the normal appropriations process. Democrats also expressed skepticism, with Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer noting that Duffy and the FAA must show they can effectively implement the funding.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Sen. John Kennedy criticized Duffy for initially requesting $30 billion and then increasing the ask to $50 billion, suggesting Duffy may have been taking orders. The Daily Wire states that Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy linked the ground stop to routine maintenance performed by L3Harris. ABC News notes that Duffy increased his funding request from $30 billion to $50 billion after the ground stop, citing a need for a "massive portion of the needs of the airspace." NPR and The Hill mention that Duffy's request has drawn criticism for lacking a detailed plan, with Sen. Jack Reed and Rep. Rosa DeLauro questioning the justification for the additional $50 billion.

Sources Axios · The Daily Wire · The Dispatch · ABC News · NPR · The Hill

Photos Show Damage to $60M US Military Aircraft As Irish National Charged

Federal prosecutors have charged an Irish national with destroying a U.S. military aircraft after he allegedly broke into a U.S. Air National Guard C-130H at Shannon Airport and caused damage that left the $60 million plane unable to return to active flight. The indictment against Daithí O’Corrain, also known as David Curran, includes photos of damage to the aircraft’s cockpit and exterior. Prosecutors allege that O’Corrain cut wiring and hydraulic lines, smashed cockpit gauges, punctured air ducts and brought gallium onto the plane. They say he also struck the aircraft with an axe and put objects, including ball bearings, into its fuel tanks. The U.S. charge is destruction of government property, which carries a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison if he is convicted. O’Corrain is also facing proceedings in Ireland over the April incident.

The incident is described as part of a pattern of security breaches at Shannon Airport, which is a transatlantic hub for U.S. military personnel and equipment. Prosecutors cite three incidents in 2025 involving other people, including a van breach on May 1, paint-spraying on a U.S. military aircraft on May 17, and a van breach on November 22. The indictment also mentions that O’Corrain planned the attack for months, researching the aircraft and airport, acquiring tools and gallium, and using an app to track aircraft movements.

Where the accounts differ

Newsweek and The Guardian both mention that O’Corrain was arrested by Irish law enforcement officers, while FOX News and the New York Post describe him as being "nabbed by Irish authorities shortly after." The Guardian and ABC News state that O’Corrain is not in U.S. custody and his arraignment has not been scheduled, whereas the New York Post claims he is being held at an Irish prison. The Guardian and ABC News also mention an online fundraiser organized by people describing themselves as O’Corrain’s friends, which the other accounts do not. The New York Post refers to O’Corrain as an "independent peace activist," a characterization not present in the other accounts. The Washington Examiner and FOX News both refer to O’Corrain as an "anti-Israel activist," while the Guardian and ABC News do not make this claim.

Sources Newsweek · Washington Examiner · FOX News · The Guardian · ABC News · New York Post

Ukraine strikes Crimean Bridge, damaging key Russian supply route

Ukrainian forces launched a coordinated attack on the Kerch Bridge, a critical link between Russia and occupied Crimea, using a combination of drones and missiles. The attack, which occurred over several days, resulted in significant damage to the bridge’s road section, with a large portion collapsing into the sea. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the operation as “unprecedented,” noting it had been planned over weeks. The strike disrupted rail and road traffic, with Russia confirming temporary suspensions of train services. The bridge, built in 2018, has been a frequent target for Kyiv, with previous attacks in 2022 and 2023 causing partial damage. The latest strike is part of Ukraine’s broader strategy to isolate Crimea from Russia, though a complete blockade remains elusive.

The attack involved multiple stages, including the use of drones to disable Russian air defenses and a relay system for real-time communication. Ukrainian military sources indicated the operation was designed to send a message to global leaders, particularly ahead of a trilateral summit between Putin, Trump, and Xi Jinping. The bridge, which Putin personally opened in 2018, is seen as a symbol of Russian dominance and has been repeatedly targeted by Kyiv as part of its efforts to undermine Moscow’s military logistics.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that the attack was part of a “seventh stage of an operation” and that the bridge was damaged in a multi-stage plan involving drones and missiles. FOX News states that the attack destroyed one of the bridge’s two steel arches and the road span, with footage showing half the bridge missing. Newsweek mentions that the attack occurred just before a meeting between Trump and Putin, and that the bridge was struck for the third time in a week. The BBC describes the attack as a “seven-stage operation” that culminated in the collapse of the road arch, with a verified video showing the missile strike. The Guardian also notes that the bridge was previously damaged in 2022 and 2023, while FOX News and Newsweek provide details on the extent of the damage and the involvement of specific weapons like the FP-5 Flamingo missile. The BBC and The Guardian differ in their descriptions of the bridge’s structural impact, with the BBC emphasizing the catastrophic nature of the damage and the difficulty of rebuilding.
