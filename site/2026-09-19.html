Trump says US has ‘permanent control’ of Greenland security: Does it?

The U.S. and Denmark have reached a deal on Greenland's security, with President Donald Trump claiming the U.S. has "permanent control" over the territory's security and "all other needs." The agreement, expected to be signed at the U.S. UN General Assembly, grants the U.S. the right to build additional military sites on Greenland without approval from Denmark or Greenland. The deal also bars U.S. adversaries from establishing military bases or making "sensitive investments" in Greenland without U.S. approval. Denmark and Greenland have acknowledged the deal but have not provided specific details. Greenland's Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen stated the agreement "recognises Greenland’s interests" and is "to the benefit of us all." Denmark's Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called it "great for NATO and Europe" as it "strengthens our common security in the Arctic and the North Atlantic Area."

The agreement, which Trump called an "Infinite Life" Agreement, would remain in force even if Greenland were to become an independent state. The U.S. Department of State official cited by the BBC said the deal prevents non-NATO states like China and Russia from establishing military presence or making investments in Greenland that could threaten the U.S. However, the official did not mention potential U.S. mining rights in Greenland, which has significant rare-earth mineral deposits. Some observers suggest this could be what Trump refers to by "sensitive investments."

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and the BBC both state that the deal does not give the U.S. ownership of Greenland, while Just The News and FOX News do not mention this clarification. The BBC and Al Jazeera note that the deal requires parliamentary ratification in Denmark and Greenland, whereas Just The News and FOX News do not specify this requirement. The BBC and Al Jazeera mention that the deal bars non-NATO states from making "sensitive investments" in Greenland, while Just The News and FOX News refer to this as restricting adversaries from making such investments. The Washington Examiner does not provide specific details about the agreement, while the New York Post mentions that the U.S. will "immediately begin the process of developing a large Military presence" in Greenland.

Sources Al Jazeera · BBC · Washington Examiner · Just The News · FOX News · New York Post

Trump Threatens to Demolish Kennedy Center Over Naming Dispute

President Donald Trump has threatened to shut down or demolish the Kennedy Center unless his name is added to the building, following a federal court ruling that blocked his attempt to rename the facility. The center, a national arts complex and memorial to President John F. Kennedy, was closed to the public after its board, controlled by Trump appointees, voted to shut it down for renovations. Trump claimed the center is in financial trouble and that its closure is necessary to prevent its collapse. A federal judge ordered the administration to provide 30 days’ notice before making any major structural changes, including demolition.

The dispute began when Trump’s appointees sought to rename the center “The Trump-Kennedy Center” and add his name to its facade. A federal judge rejected the plan, stating only Congress can rename the facility. Trump responded by threatening to close the center unless his name is included, and he was photographed reviewing a poster that appeared to show a plan to demolish it. The administration has cited “severe structural deterioration” as a reason for the closure, though critics argue the takeover accelerated the center’s financial decline.

Where the accounts differ

Slate and ABC News both report that Trump threatened to close or demolish the Kennedy Center unless his name is added, and that the center was closed after a court blocked his renaming attempt. The Guardian and Mother Jones add that Trump claimed the center is in financial trouble and that a ceiling collapse during a storm was cited as a safety concern for the closure. The Federalist, however, focuses on the cultural and political significance of the center, criticizing its exclusivity and the motives of its defenders, without mentioning the financial or structural reasons for the closure. The Federalist also attributes the center’s value to its exclusive events and proximity to Georgetown, which other accounts do not mention.

Sources Slate · Mother Jones · The Federalist · The Guardian · NPR · ABC News

Tech power players land seat at table for high-stakes dinner with Trump, Xi

President Donald Trump is planning to hold a state dinner next week to mark a visit by Chinese President Xi Jinping, and several tech industry leaders are expected to attend. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Apple Executive Chairman Tim Cook are both reportedly planning to attend the state dinner. NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang is also expected to attend the event. The tech leaders' anticipated attendance at the Trump-Xi state dinner comes at a time of geopolitical tensions, including over the development of artificial intelligence (AI) and access to both models and the chips that power them. The U.S. and China are locked in a competition in which the two world powers are racing to develop more capable AI tools, which have been a source of tension between the countries. Huang has been critical of those restrictions and said in May that China has "all the chips they need" despite the U.S. restrictions.

Where the accounts differ

Fox News Poll: 71% think Trump administration lacks plan to end Iran war

A new Fox News national survey indicates that a majority of voters believe the Trump administration lacks a clear strategy for ending the war with Iran. The poll found that 71% of voters think the administration lacks a plan to end the conflict. Additionally, 47% of voters say the war will make the U.S. less safe in the long run, up from 39% in April. The poll also shows that 60% of voters believe the decision to take military action against Iran was the wrong decision, compared to 37% who think it was right.

The survey highlights growing concerns about the war's impact on national security and public opinion. It notes that attitudes against the war have increased across most demographic groups, with the largest shifts among Hispanic voters, voters under age 30, moderates, and voters 65 and over. The poll also found that 32% of voters think the war will make the country safer, while 20% say there will be no difference.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that 71% of voters think the Trump administration lacks a plan to end the Iran war, while the other outlets do not mention this specific poll or its findings. The Washington Examiner focuses on congressional concerns and the midterm elections, without addressing the Iran war or public opinion on it. CBS News, NPR, and Axios cover the immigration enforcement criticism by Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar, but none mention the Fox News poll or the Iran war. The Daily Caller also covers Salazar’s ad but does not reference the Fox News survey or the Iran war.

Sources FOX News · Washington Examiner · CBS News · NPR · Axios · The Daily Caller

Lone Lindsay Clancy Holdout Juror Breaks His Silence

The lone male holdout juror in Lindsay Clancy’s triple-murder trial has spoken out, stating he had no doubts about her criminal responsibility. Michael P. Desronvil, 48, said he was cut off during deliberations when trying to explain different theories, and that the evidence presented by the prosecution was sufficient to prove Clancy knew exactly what she was doing. The jury deadlocked on whether Clancy was criminally responsible, leading to a mistrial declared by Judge William Sullivan on September 4. Clancy admitted to killing her three children but her defense argued she suffered from postpartum psychosis, while prosecutors claimed she understood the nature of her actions.

The trial, which drew national attention, centered on whether Clancy was criminally responsible for the deaths of her children. Jurors were split 11-1, with Desronvil the only one who believed she was guilty. His comments come after other jurors described the holdout as resistant to persuasion and accused him of refusing to listen to the mostly white women on the jury.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and Newsweek both report that Desronvil is a father from a Haitian immigrant family who moved to the United States in 1982. The New York Post does not mention his family background. The Daily Wire and FOX News both mention that Desronvil’s brother previously denied that religion played any role in his decision, while Newsweek does not. CBS News and BBC both report that Desronvil said he had no doubts, but the Daily Wire adds that he directly refuted claims he disregarded the law or lied under oath. The Daily Wire and BBC report that a juror named Roni described a moment when she believed the panel had reached a breakthrough, while CBS News and Newsweek do not mention this. The Daily Wire and FOX News both report that Desronvil’s lawyer, Edward Paltzik, described him as an “American Hero and Champion of Justice,” while the New York Post does not mention this.

Sources The Daily Wire · CBS News · Newsweek · FOX News · BBC · New York Post

Russians head to the polls to choose lawmakers for their parliament

Russian voters are casting ballots in parliamentary elections from Friday to Sunday, marking the first such vote since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The elections are expected to be dominated by United Russia, the ruling party led by President Vladimir Putin, with little genuine opposition on the ballot. Yabloko, the only registered party openly opposing the war, was removed from the ballot by the Supreme Court on a technicality related to American copyright law. The election is seen as a means for the Kremlin to reinforce its control and legitimacy, with candidates largely avoiding discussion of the war or economic concerns.

The election environment is characterized by strict government control, with candidates avoiding sensitive topics and focusing on safe, non-controversial messages. Campaigns have been minimal, and televised debates have often been empty or scripted. The Kremlin is believed to be managing the election to ensure a predictable outcome, with little room for public dissent or alternative viewpoints.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that Yabloko was banned from the ballot due to a technicality involving American copyright law, while Al Jazeera states the Supreme Court cited the party's antiwar stance as akin to seeking the violation of Russia’s territorial integrity. The Guardian and Newsweek both mention Yabloko's removal but do not specify the legal reasoning. The Christian Science Monitor and Al Jazeera note that the Kremlin is managing the election to suppress dissent, with the latter emphasizing the removal of managed opposition parties like Yabloko and the Communist Party of the Russian Federation (CPRF).

Sources NPR · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · Newsweek · Christian Science Monitor

House GOP report details how Dem fundraising platform ActBlue allowed illegal foreign donations

A House Republican-led report released this week alleges that ActBlue, a Democratic fundraising platform, allowed illegal foreign donations to flow into U.S. campaigns with minimal verification. The report, obtained by the House Oversight, Judiciary, and Administration Committees, claims that ActBlue’s fraud safeguards, including passport verification, could be bypassed by foreign actors. Internal documents show that ActBlue’s system only checks for the number of characters in a passport number, without verifying its validity against a government database. The report also states that ActBlue instructed its fraud analysts to “give the donor the benefit of the doubt,” even when contributions originated from foreign IP addresses or had other red flags.

ActBlue’s CEO, Regina Wallace-Jones, refused to testify before the House Administration Committee, invoking her Fifth Amendment rights. The report claims that every member of ActBlue’s legal and compliance team left the platform after the 2024 election due to its “knowing and willful” acceptance of illegal foreign contributions. ActBlue has denied wrongdoing, calling the investigation a “political stunt” and accusing Republicans of attempting to silence organizations that threaten their agenda.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and The Federalist both report that ActBlue’s passport verification process only checks for the number of characters in a passport number, without verifying its validity. The Daily Caller adds that foreign donors could bypass the system by providing a random number with the correct number of digits. The Federalist also notes that ActBlue’s internal documents show supervisors instructed analysts to accept donations from donors with Hong Kong IP addresses and “a few foreign IPs” because they were “all consistently in the same place.” The New York Post and The Federalist do not mention this specific instruction. The Daily Caller also reports that ActBlue’s internal Slack messages show that the company retaliated against attorneys who raised concerns about lawbreaking, including deleting messages and terminating a law firm.

Sources New York Post · The Federalist · The Daily Caller
