At least five people dead in Miami plane crash: Officials

An Amazon cargo plane crashed at Miami International Airport on Sunday afternoon after overrunning the runway, according to multiple accounts. The Boeing 767-300, operated by 21 Air, was flying from San Juan, Puerto Rico, and came to rest in a field adjacent to the airport. At least five people were confirmed dead, with five others injured. Firefighters responded to a scene engulfed in flames and smoke, conducting search and rescue operations. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) confirmed the crash and stated it would investigate. Miami-Dade County officials, including Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz and Mayor Daniella Levine Cava, expressed condolences and thanked first responders for their quick action.

The crash led to a ground stop at Miami International Airport, halting all flights for several hours. Fire crews worked to contain a fuel leak and extinguish the fire, with some victims trapped in vehicles struck by the plane. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) and the FAA are both involved in the investigation. Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel confirmed the incident and expressed condolences to the families of the victims.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both report that the plane struck several vehicles, but The Guardian specifies that the plane crashed into multiple vehicles and trapped several people inside them. The Daily Caller and FOX News also mention that the plane struck multiple vehicles, but FOX News adds that some victims required "extrication" by technical rescue personnel. The Guardian and Al Jazeera note that three victims were in critical condition, while The Daily Caller and FOX News state that three were taken to a trauma center in critical condition. The Washington Examiner and The Guardian report that the plane came to rest in a field adjacent to the airport, while FOX News and Al Jazeera mention that the plane was disabled at the northwest end of the airport. The Guardian and Al Jazeera mention that the cause of the crash is under investigation, while The Washington Examiner and The Daily Caller do not specify the cause.

Sources Washington Examiner · The Guardian · The Daily Caller · FOX News · NPR · Al Jazeera

Trump envoys and Putin discussed ‘next steps’ for ending Ukraine war, White House says

U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Saturday to discuss "substantive plans for next steps" toward ending the war in Ukraine, according to a White House official. The meeting lasted about three hours, and the envoys are set to visit Kyiv on Sunday for further talks. The White House stated that the plans would be announced in "coming weeks." Russian President Putin expressed confidence in Russia's battlefield progress and emphasized the need to address "all the root causes of the conflict." Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy stated that Russian attacks on Kyiv and Boryspil international airports were a reaction to the U.S. envoys' visit and warned that Ukraine would adjust its operations in Russian airspace. Both sides agreed to a short pause in airstrikes on each other’s capitals during the envoys' visit.

The meeting in Moscow was part of a renewed push for peace amid escalating aerial attacks by both sides. The U.S. has been trying to revive stalled peace talks, but progress has been slow as U.S. attention has shifted to its war with Iran. The envoys were joined by officials from the National Security Council, State Department, and Treasury Department during the discussions. Putin thanked the U.S. for releasing former Marine Robert Gilman from Russian custody and acknowledged the pause in airstrikes. Zelenskyy said Ukraine would not target Moscow with drones and missiles during the envoys' visit and urged Russia to reciprocate.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and BBC report that Zelenskyy linked Russian attacks on Kyiv and Boryspil airports to the U.S. envoys' visit, while the BBC adds that Zelenskyy described the Donbas region as "difficult" and insisted Ukraine would not give up territory there. The Guardian and Axios note that Putin told the envoys Russia was making "real progress" on the battlefield, while the BBC states Ukrainian officials claim Russia suffered 42,000 casualties in August. The Associated Press and Axios mention that the U.S. envoys were joined by Gene Lange, the acting sanctions czar, during the Moscow meeting, which the other accounts do not mention. The Guardian and NPR report that the envoys are visiting Kyiv for the first time in their roles, while the BBC notes that they had never visited Kyiv before.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · ABC News · Axios · Associated Press · BBC

Far-right AfD wins key German state election but falls just short of majority

The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) won a significant victory in the eastern German state of Saxony-Anhalt, securing 43.8% of the vote and 39 seats in an 83-member parliament. The party fell three seats short of an absolute majority needed to govern alone. The Christian Democratic Union (CDU), led by Chancellor Friedrich Merz, saw its support drop to 17.2%, winning 15 seats. The Social Democrats (SPD), Greens, and the Left party each won around 9% to 8.6% of the vote, securing eight seats apiece. The BSW party, which opposes the "firewall" against AfD, won 5.3% and five seats. Turnout reached a record high of 76.5% in the Guardian, while CBS News reported 77.8%.

AfD candidate Ulrich Siegmund claimed the result was a mandate to govern and called for a "self-confident signal" for Germany. He attributed the party's success to Merz's campaign efforts. Alice Weidel, AfD’s co-leader, said the result gave the party a "clear government mandate." The CDU described its result as "bitter" and ruled out any coalition with AfD. Siegmund also outlined the party's "remigration" policy, which includes deportations and segregation of refugees.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News differ on turnout, with the Guardian reporting 76.5% and CBS News 77.8%. The Guardian mentions that the CDU described its result as "bitter" and ruled out any discussion over Merz’s future, while CBS News does not include this detail. The Axios account notes that AfD won 44% in Saxony-Anhalt, while the Guardian and CBS News report 43.8%. The Newsweek account includes specific voter transfer analysis and mentions that 170,000 AfD votes came from previous nonvoters, which the other accounts do not include.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · NPR · CBS News · Axios · Newsweek

Armed assailant lunged at Democratic candidate for Ohio governor, campaign says

An armed individual lunged at Amy Acton, the Democratic candidate for Ohio governor, during a campaign stop at the Canfield Fair on Sunday, according to multiple outlets. Campaign officials confirmed that several people were injured in the incident, though Acton herself was not harmed. The suspect, identified as Patrick Havas, 38, was arrested and is expected to face charges of assault and disorderly conduct. Mahoning County Sheriff Jerry Greene described Havas as having knocked down two older adults during the incident. The Ohio State Highway Patrol was involved in the arrest, and Havas was found to be carrying two handguns and a set of brass knuckles. Campaign officials and political figures, including Acton’s opponent Vivek Ramaswamy, condemned the attack as unacceptable and called for an end to political violence.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Al Jazeera both mention that Havas knocked down two older adults, but only Al Jazeera specifies that the sheriff told 21 News about this. CBS News and Washington Examiner also note that Havas knocked over two older adults, with CBS adding that one of them fell to the ground. Newsweek and Just The News do not mention the specific detail about the older adults being knocked down. Additionally, Newsweek is the only outlet that identifies Havas as a registered Republican and licensed nurse, based on records reviewed by the publication. The Guardian and Al Jazeera both mention that Ramaswamy condemned the attack, but only the Guardian includes a quote from his communications director, Connie Luck, stating the incident was “completely unacceptable.” The Guardian also includes a quote from Abdul El-Sayad, a Michigan Democratic Senate candidate, denouncing the attack, which is absent from the other accounts.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · CBS News · Washington Examiner · Newsweek · Just The News

Lindsay Clancy murder trial ends in dramatic deadlock – what comes next?

Lindsay Clancy’s murder trial ended in a mistrial after jurors failed to reach a unanimous verdict. The jury deadlocked 11 to 1, with one juror refusing to convict despite expressing reasonable doubt. Clancy, a 36-year-old former nurse, did not deny killing her three children but pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity, citing postpartum psychosis and overmedication. Prosecutors argued she was fully aware of her actions. The mistrial followed a motion by Clancy’s defense to replace the holdout juror, which the court denied. Clancy remains in a hospital, and a new trial date is pending. The case has drawn national attention, with supporters and critics divided over her mental health and legal culpability.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Slate describe the jury deadlock as 11 to 1, while Straight Arrow News does not specify the vote count. The Guardian and FOX News both mention the holdout juror’s refusal to apply the legal standard, but FOX News adds that the holdout was a man, while The Guardian does not specify the juror’s gender. The Guardian and Slate describe the holdout juror as expressing reasonable doubt, while FOX News adds that the juror refused to apply the legal standard. The Guardian and Slate mention the complexity of Massachusetts law on criminal responsibility, while Straight Arrow News does not address this. The Guardian and Slate describe the public reaction to the mistrial, while Straight Arrow News focuses on a separate case in Illinois.

Trump administration makes another Supreme Court appeal on mail ballot fight

The Trump administration has filed a third appeal with the Supreme Court seeking to enforce new restrictions on mail ballots ahead of the midterms. The filing follows a federal judge’s order blocking the implementation of the rules, which were based on an executive order from President Trump. The administration argues that the current injunction creates confusion and chaos as states begin mailing ballots, with some states already having started the process. Solicitor General John Sauer stated that once ballots enter the mailstream, they cannot be retrieved, and the voluntary nature of compliance under the injunction risks undermining election integrity.

The dispute centers on Trump’s executive order directing the Postal Service to implement new rules for mail ballots, including unique barcodes and specific envelope requirements. The administration claims these measures are lawful and aimed at improving mail handling and preventing fraud. However, Democratic states and voting rights groups argue the changes are unconstitutional, while election officials say there is insufficient time to adjust their systems to meet the new federal directives. The Supreme Court has previously allowed the plan to proceed but has not ruled on its legality.

The outcome of the legal battle could significantly impact the November elections, where control of Congress is at stake. Nearly one-third of U.S. voters cast ballots by mail, making the issue critical for both parties. The administration continues to push for a stay of the injunction, arguing that it is necessary to ensure compliance with the new rules and maintain election security.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and The Guardian both state that the Supreme Court has allowed the plan to move forward but not ruled on its legality. FOX News and Straight Arrow News also mention this, but FOX News adds that the administration disputes the claim that the rule dictates ballot content, determines voter eligibility, or governs how ballots are counted. The New York Post does not mention this specific point, instead focusing on the administration’s framing of the restrictions as "commonsense changes."

Sources CBS News · Washington Examiner · The Guardian · Straight Arrow News · FOX News · New York Post

Trump Posts Map Renaming New Mexico as 'New America'

President Donald Trump shared a map on Truth Social Sunday showing New Mexico with the word "Mexico" crossed out and replaced with "America," suggesting the state could be renamed "New America." The post came days after Trump signed an executive order renaming Lake Ontario to "Lake America" amid trade tensions with Canada. The White House did not immediately confirm any formal plan for renaming New Mexico, though the post appeared alongside other unrelated content, including an AI-generated video of Trump kicking over a sign at Lake Ontario.

The idea of renaming New Mexico has circulated before, with fact-checkers debunking similar claims in the past. Trump has previously attempted to rename geographic features, including the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America and reverting Mt. Denali to Mt. McKinley. While some private companies and international bodies have adopted Trump's name changes, states are not subject to federal renaming efforts. New Mexico Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham criticized the post, stating the state's name is not up for debate and highlighting state efforts to support working families.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and Newsweek both mention that Trump's post was part of a broader series of AI-generated images and memes, including one declaring "The Moon Is Ours." The Washington Examiner and New York Post do not reference the moon post or the AI-generated content. The Daily Wire and Newsweek also note that Trump did not clarify whether the New Mexico post was a joke or a serious proposal, while the Washington Examiner and New York Post do not address this ambiguity. The New York Post and FOX News both mention that renaming a state is not possible through executive action, while the Daily Wire and Newsweek do not explicitly state this.

Sources The Daily Wire · Washington Examiner · FOX News · Newsweek · New York Post

Pete Hegseth faces criticism for leadership style and military decisions

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has drawn scrutiny for his leadership approach and military decisions. Hegseth has also faced criticism for firing or blocking promotions of over 80 generals and admirals, with reports indicating that a significant portion of those removed were Black or female. The military has raised concerns about extended deployments in the Middle East, with some commanders objecting to the plans. Hegseth has also been criticized for his handling of civilian harm mitigation, particularly following strikes in Iran that resulted in civilian casualties. The US has not formally admitted responsibility for the strikes, though an investigation is ongoing.

The resumption of strikes on Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz has raised questions about the US's objectives in the conflict with Iran. These strikes, which included attacks on mainland Iran and Iranian islands in the Gulf, caused multiple casualties, including a six-year-old child in Kuhestak. Iranian authorities have labeled the deaths a war crime, drawing comparisons to previous strikes that killed over 150 people, including children, in Minab. The US has not officially acknowledged responsibility for the Minab strike, which occurred on the first day of the war.

Where the accounts differ
