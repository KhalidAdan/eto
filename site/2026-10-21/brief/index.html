Trump cites French protests in warning to Kansas voters: ‘Never happening here’

President Donald Trump warned Kansas voters during a rally for Sen. Roger Marshall (R-KS) that the United States could face similar unrest to France, which he claimed is being “conquered, looted, and burned to the ground by migrants, jihadists, and communists.” Trump described the French protests as a cautionary tale, urging Americans to avoid the fate of France. The unrest in France, which began as student-led demonstrations over education and living costs, escalated into widespread riots, looting, and clashes with police, with some cities imposing overnight curfews.

French President Emmanuel Macron attributed the unrest to a combination of economic grievances, including rising fuel prices and the cost of living, which he linked to the war in Iran. Macron also condemned the violence as the work of “insurrectionist elements from radical far-left groups, joined by numerous criminals.” The protests, which drew hundreds of thousands of participants, saw some 2,400 arrests in a single day and widespread damage to infrastructure and businesses.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both report that Trump claimed France is being “conquered, looted, and burned to the ground by migrants, jihadists, and communists,” while the New York Post and Newsweek attribute the same claim to Trump. The Axios and The Guardian accounts state that Macron linked the unrest to the war in Iran and rising fuel prices, while the FOX News account attributes the same claims to Macron and includes a quote from Lisa Daftari, an Iran expert, who disputes Macron’s explanation. The New York Post and Newsweek also mention that Trump claimed the unrest was about “immigration, not schools,” a claim not present in the Washington Examiner or The Guardian accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · FOX News · New York Post · Axios · The Guardian · Newsweek

Trump’s Voter Raids Have Begun

Federal authorities in California arrested at least 11 people and charged 28 others with alleged voter fraud, including registering as noncitizens and casting ballots. The operation, called “Operation Protect the Vote,” is part of the Trump administration’s efforts to address claims of noncitizen voting ahead of the midterm elections. The Justice Department alleges that all 28 charged individuals are legal permanent residents but not U.S. citizens. The administration has threatened to criminally investigate local officials who process ballots from those on a federal list of suspected noncitizens.

The Justice Department sent California election officials a list of 20,000 people it claims are “likely noncitizens” registered to vote. Officials rejected the demand to withhold ballots, prompting the Justice Department to sue for a court order to prevent California from counting ballots from those on the list. The Trump administration has also threatened local officials with criminal prosecution for processing those ballots.

Where the accounts differ

The Federalist mentions that 23 of the 28 charged allegedly voted in U.S. elections, a claim not made by Mother Jones or The Daily Wire. The Daily Wire includes specific cases, such as Alexander Muñoz and Amandeep Kaur, which are not detailed in the other accounts. The Federalist also notes that Leslie Stahl downplayed noncitizen voting on CBS News, a detail absent from the other accounts.

Cuts force World Food Programme to end support for half a million Palestinians in Gaza

The World Food Programme (WFP) has scaled back its aid to Gaza, reducing support from 1.6 million to 1.1 million people each month. This follows a more than 50% drop in funding from 2025 levels. The WFP’s director for Palestine, Shaun Hughes, warned that the situation remains dire, with two-thirds of the population facing crisis levels of food insecurity. The agency needs over £300m over six months to maintain its programmes. Food insecurity is at emergency levels for 200,000 Palestinians, according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). The WFP has also reduced food rations and hot meals, with deliveries now occurring every other day in October. Most families in Gaza still rely on aid due to high unemployment, with about 80% of the population unemployed, according to UN data.

Gaza’s agricultural and fishing sectors have been severely impacted, with farmland occupied by Israeli forces and fishing boats barred from coastal waters. A recent Unicef survey found that one in eight children in Gaza are chronically malnourished or stunted. Humanitarian organisations are struggling with funding after the Trump administration cut USAID and donor nations reduced aid due to cost of living crises. Aid will be needed until Israel and the international community launch promised reconstruction efforts.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that the WFP reduced support from 1.6 million to 1.1 million people in October, while Al Jazeera states that the WFP cut support from 1.6 million in September to 1.1 million in October. The Guardian attributes the reduction to funding cuts, while Al Jazeera notes that the WFP cited funding shortages as the reason for the cuts. The Guardian mentions that the famine declared in summer 2025 has receded, while Al Jazeera states that famine was declared in Gaza City in August 2025 and that the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification indicated it had receded after the ceasefire began in June 2025. The Guardian attributes the reduction in hot meals to funding cuts, while Al Jazeera specifies that the WFP halved food rations and reduced hot meals from 250,000 to 150,000 daily.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera
