FAA Ground Stop Strands Travelers, Sparks Debate Over System Modernization

On November 13, 2026, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a nationwide ground stop, halting all departures across the U.S. due to a telecommunications outage. The stop, which lasted over four hours, stranded hundreds of thousands of travelers and disrupted air traffic. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy attributed the outage to an overloaded network caused by maintenance work by FAA contractor L3Harris, which was replacing copper wiring with fiber-optic cables. Duffy called for additional congressional funding to modernize the FAA’s system, which he said had been underfunded for decades. The FAA administrator, Bryan Bedford, confirmed that the system was stable after the ground stop and apologized for the disruption. President Trump also weighed in, urging Congress to approve more funding for air traffic control modernization.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and CBS News both report that Duffy claimed the FAA had spent 38% of the $12.5 billion allocated for modernization, while The Hill and Christian Science Monitor note that only one-third of the funds had been spent. The Hill and CBS News mention that the Trump administration had requested $50 billion for the FAA, while Mother Jones and FOX News report that the request was for $50 billion, with The Hill noting that the Trump administration had previously requested $30 billion. The Hill and Christian Science Monitor provide specific numbers on flight cancellations, while Mother Jones and CBS News do not. FOX News and The Hill attribute the outage to a communications failure, while CBS News and Mother Jones attribute it to overloaded routers caused by maintenance work.

Sources Mother Jones · The Hill · Washington Examiner · FOX News · CBS News · Christian Science Monitor

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns amid allegations of affairs and China spy ties

Jensen Huang, co-founder and former CEO of Nvidia, resigned abruptly after reports surfaced of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman suspected of espionage. The scandal has raised concerns about potential illegal chip exports to China, despite U.S. export restrictions. Huang's resignation followed a staff-wide memo admitting to the affair, which led to his immediate dismissal. Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO, was named interim CEO. The company faces scrutiny over its financial practices and the sustainability of the AI investment boom, with some analysts warning of a potential market correction. Meanwhile, a bipartisan push for tighter export controls on advanced chips to China has intensified, with calls for stricter oversight of companies like Megaspeed, a major Nvidia client.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that the AI investment bubble is at risk due to financial strain and political uncertainty, citing projections of $7 trillion in infrastructure spending versus $3.6 trillion in revenue. The BBC highlights the potential for high-end AI chips to reach China through Megaspeed, noting the company's internal investigation and its denial of wrongdoing. The New York Post and The Guardian both mention Scott Bessent's call for Nvidia to hire an external CEO, while The Guardian adds that Bessent prioritizes questions about chip sales to China over Huang's personal conduct. Fox Business notes the campaign for Huang's return, including support from Elon Musk and others, while The Hill states that Rep. Rick Crawford believes it is premature to blame Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick for delays in export controls.

Sources Axios · BBC · New York Post · The Guardian · Fox Business · The Hill

Colom stated he is in "listening mode" and emphasized the importance of understanding the needs of the caucus and identifying potential leaders. At least 100 people pulled from Mississippi River after New Orleans ship crash as lengthy salvage effort looms

A tanker struck a docked cruise ship in New Orleans on Friday, resulting in over 100 people being rescued from the Mississippi River. Officials confirmed that everyone aboard the American Heritage cruise ship was accounted for, though eight people remained hospitalized, including two in serious condition. The U.S. Coast Guard is preparing for a potentially weeks-long operation to remove the capsized vessel, which will involve removing 16,000 gallons of fuel and assessing the vessel for potential hazardous material leaks. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) has recovered the tanker's voyage data recorder and is expected to release a preliminary report within 30 days.

The Port of New Orleans filed a lawsuit seeking over $1 million in damages from the owner and operator of the Atlantic Asphalt tanker, alleging negligence in the collision. The lawsuit claims the tanker failed to take adequate precautions to avoid the collision, which caused significant damage to the port's terminal. The NTSB and Coast Guard are investigating the incident, with the exact cause remaining under review. The collision is described as a "miracle on the Mississippi" due to the survival of all passengers and crew.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that 191 passengers and crew were aboard the American Heritage, while The Guardian and CBS News state there were 129 passengers plus crew. The Guardian mentions that 32 people were hospitalized with non-critical injuries, while BBC and CBS News also note 32 people were hospitalized. The Guardian and BBC report that the American Heritage had 129 passengers, while FOX News and CBS News state there were 191 people aboard. The Guardian and BBC mention that the American Heritage was partially submerged, while FOX News and CBS News describe it as capsizing. The Guardian and BBC report that the Port of New Orleans filed a lawsuit, while FOX News and CBS News also mention the lawsuit but differ in the exact wording and details.

Iran claims drone attack on US radar in Bahrain; US intercepts drones

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said it launched six drones at a US air defense radar system at Bahrain's Riffa Air Base in retaliation for what it described as "hostile US actions." The US military confirmed intercepting two Iranian drones on Friday but has not yet commented on Sunday's claim. Bahrain's authorities reported intercepting several aerial attacks and said no injuries or damage occurred. The IRGC also claimed that the Strait of Hormuz remains closed and that US efforts to escort ships through the waterway have failed.

Former Secretary of State Antony Blinken warned that a US ground invasion of Iran would be an "absolute disaster," citing the country's vast territory, population, and ability to produce weapons deep within its borders. Blinken emphasized that Iran's weapons capabilities are too deeply embedded for air power alone to neutralize. He also noted the geographic challenges of conducting a ground operation, including the long coastline along the Strait of Hormuz.

The attacks in Bahrain come amid ongoing tensions in Yemen, where Iran's Houthi allies have made advances against Saudi-backed forces. The IRGC also claimed that two oil tankers struck sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, though the US military has not confirmed these incidents. The US has conducted several strikes against Iranian targets in the region, including Qeshm Island, which Iran has condemned as an act of aggression.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that Blinken warned about a potential US ground invasion of Iran and attributed the claim about Iran's weapons production to his own statements. Washington Examiner and Al Jazeera do not mention Blinken's warning, focusing instead on the drone attacks in Bahrain. The Guardian includes details about the US strikes in Iran and the Houthi advances in Yemen, which are not covered in the other accounts. Two planes collide on taxiway at Miami airport one day after national grounding

Two planes collided with one another on a taxiway at Miami’s international airport on Saturday night – a day after the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued an hours-long nationwide ground stop due to communication issues affecting multiple air traffic control centers. The incident involved a Copa Airlines Boeing 737 and a United Parcel Service Boeing 767. Two people reportedly had minor injuries in the collision and subsequently received medical treatment. The FAA said it was investigating the matter, which was reported at about 9pm local time. Air traffic controllers had told the UPS crew to give way to the Copa flight before the collision. The Copa flight was taxiing to the gate and the UPS flight was taxiing for departure to Bogotá, Colombia.

The collision happened at approximately 9:15 p.m., according to airport officials. Damage to the nose of the aircraft at Miami International Airport is seen in this photo. Photos showed damage to the nose of one of the planes. The airport said flights were being directed to other taxiways, so there was no impact to airport operations. Two patients were treated on the scene by Miami-Dade Fire Rescue for minor injuries and did not require medical transport. UPS confirmed that one of its aircraft was involved in the collision, saying its crew was safe.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and The Daily Wire both mention the FAA’s statement about the wingtip of the Copa flight striking the nose of the UPS plane, while FOX News and The New York Post describe the collision as the wing clipping the nose. The Guardian and The Daily Wire attribute the collision to the Copa flight taxiing to the gate and the UPS flight taxiing for departure to Bogotá, Colombia. The New York Post and FOX News include a direct quote from the UPS pilot saying, “Yeah, we went too far and he clipped us with his wing,” which is not present in The Guardian or The Daily Wire. The Guardian and The Daily Wire do not include the audio exchange between the pilots and air traffic controllers that FOX News and The New York Post report.

White House lawyer warns Democrats against overreach in oversight investigations

White House Counsel William Scharf sent a letter to California Democratic Rep. Robert Garcia on November 9, warning Democrats against running afoul of their constitutional limits on investigations and subpoenas. The letter emphasized the "foundational limits that the Constitution imposes on congressional investigations and subpoenas" and suggested that Democratic actions could cross into criminal misconduct. Garcia responded by stating that Congress has wide latitude to conduct investigations and oversight under Article I of the Constitution. The letter came a week after Democrats issued statements and social media posts calling for subpoenas and impeachment trials for Trump administration officials following the 2026 midterms.

Scharf told Fox News Sunday that Democrats’ intent to investigate the Trump administration is "out of pettiness," arguing that such efforts must be tied to a "legitimate legislative purpose" rather than self-aggrandizement or threatening behavior. He warned that if Democrats continue with their approach, they would face a "vigorous response" from the White House and the Department of Justice. Legal experts from across the political spectrum suggested that while the administration can challenge individual subpoenas, criminal prosecutions would face a higher legal bar.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that legal experts, including Ilya Shapiro of the Manhattan Institute, suggested that while the White House can challenge subpoenas, criminal prosecutions would face a higher bar. Washington Examiner mentions that Scharf promised a "vigorous response" to Democrats’ oversight threats but does not include the legal expert commentary. The Daily Caller states that Scharf called Garcia’s intent to investigate the administration "out of pettiness" and warned of legal action, but does not mention the legal expert analysis provided by FOX News.
