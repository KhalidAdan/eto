Storms across US disrupt travel and force Thanksgiving parade cancellations

A severe winter storm brought heavy rain, winds, flooding and high tides across the US on Thanksgiving, prompting the cancellations of at least two commercial Thanksgiving parades. The storm caused widespread power outages, with more than 900,000 homes and businesses without electricity on Thursday afternoon. In New York, the traditional Macy’s parade was postponed to Saturday for the first time in its history, while Philadelphia’s parade was moved to an “alternative TV event.” The storm also led to coastal flooding, road closures and emergency rescues in several states, including New Jersey and Virginia.

The storm affected air travel at eastern US airports, with delays expected to last into Friday morning. In Florida, President Donald Trump was joined by state leaders to sign a major disaster declaration for the state, which makes federal assistance available to those affected by severe storms and flooding. The National Weather Service reported high tide records in several California cities, including Los Angeles and Santa Barbara, while winter storm warnings were posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News both report that more than 900,000 homes and businesses were without electricity on Thursday afternoon, while ABC News states that more than 925,000 utility customers in nine states were without power. The Guardian and CBS News also note that the Macy’s parade was postponed to Saturday, while ABC News and The Hill mention that the parade was postponed due to severe weather. The Guardian and CBS News report that the storm caused flooding in coastal villages and cities from North Carolina through New Jersey, while The Hill and ABC News mention that the storm disrupted travel in the Washington, D.C., area. The Guardian and CBS News also report that the storm caused power outages in the Midwest, while The Hill and ABC News mention that the storm knocked out power for more than 850,000 utility customers nationwide.

Sources The Guardian · ABC News · CBS News · Axios · BBC · The Hill

GOP lawmakers warn Trump made himself a target in Nvidia scandal

President Donald Trump’s intervention to reinstate Jensen Huang as Nvidia’s chief executive is drawing mounting criticism from Republicans, who are questioning whether the president put the company’s financial interests ahead of national security. Republican lawmakers are demanding answers about the Trump administration’s role in Huang’s reinstatement following revelations of his affair with tech executive Alice Huang, who has alleged ties to Chinese intelligence. Of particular concern are reports that Trump and members of his orbit assured Nvidia’s board that Huang posed no national security risk, raising questions about who provided those assurances and what evidence they relied upon.

Nvidia said it received assurances from senior officials that Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk. But federal investigations were still underway into the Nvidia CEO's affair with a woman whose alleged ties to Chinese intelligence remain in dispute, as well as questions about advanced U.S. chips reaching China when the president began his push. The decision to reinstate Huang has left Republican lawmakers demanding to know what senior administration officials told Nvidia’s board that led them to reverse course less than two weeks after Huang's resignation.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both report that Trump and members of his orbit assured Nvidia’s board that Huang posed no national security risk, but the Washington Examiner notes that these assurances raised questions about who provided them and what evidence was used. The Guardian adds that the Department of Justice has not confirmed whether a formal investigation was conducted into Huang. The Fox News and Fox Business accounts differ in that Fox News states that Attorney General Todd Blanche assured Vice President JD Vance that Huang was not under investigation, while Fox Business reports that Trump said he had not spoken with Huang since his reinstatement. The The Hill and The Guardian both mention that Trump did not rule out pardoning Huang, but The Guardian adds that JD Vance stated Huang did not need a pardon as he was never under criminal investigation.

Trump calls for investigation into Commerce Department’s revised GDP figures

The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Economic Analysis revised its third quarter GDP report on Wednesday, increasing the annualized growth rate from 3.4% to 4.3%. President Trump responded by demanding an investigation, accusing the department of making a “massive and purposeful” error. The revision came after the November midterm elections, which saw significant losses for Republicans. Trump claimed the initial figures were “FAKE” and that the correct number was not released before the election.

Fox Business reported that the revised GDP figure of 4.3% exceeded economists’ expectations of 3.5% for the quarter. The revision was attributed to higher investment and consumer spending, particularly in software and pharmaceuticals. The report also noted that the upward revision was larger than usual, with the BEA citing updated data on software prices and retail sales as key factors.

Axios highlighted that the initial GDP figure of 3.4% already indicated a strong economy, and the upward revision simply made that picture more favorable. The article also noted that the Commerce Department and BEA did not respond to requests for comment. The revision could bolster the case for further interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve, which Trump opposes.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and Axios both state that Trump accused the Commerce Department of making a “massive and purposeful” error, while Fox Business does not mention Trump’s accusation of a “purposeful” error. The New York Post and The Guardian attribute Trump’s claim that the figures were “FAKE” to his statements, while CBS News and Axios note that Trump did not provide evidence for his allegations. The Guardian and CBS News include additional context about Trump’s Thanksgiving message and his broader grievances, which are not present in the other accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · Fox Business · Axios · The Guardian · CBS News · New York Post

Accused MS-13 Gangbanger Kilmar Abrego Garcia Lands In Spain After Self-Deporting

Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Salvadoran national accused of gang activity and human smuggling, arrived in Spain on Wednesday after self-deporting from the United States. He was seen boarding a flight to Madrid from Dulles Airport on Tuesday after having his ankle monitor removed. Garcia, who is married to an American citizen and has a U.S. citizen child, has been fighting deportation for over two years. His attorney, Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, stated that Garcia left the U.S. as a "free man" and plans to return in the future. Federal law bars him from re-entering the U.S. for 10 years due to his long-term presence without legal status.

Garcia’s case became a focal point of the Trump administration’s deportation efforts after he was mistakenly deported to El Salvador in 2025. He was initially sent to a notorious prison in El Salvador before being returned to the U.S. after a court order. He was later charged with human smuggling, which was dismissed in 2026 due to concerns about vindictive prosecution. The Justice Department appealed the ruling, and arguments were scheduled for February 2027. Garcia’s lawyer described his departure as temporary, with plans to stay abroad for two years before potentially returning to the U.S.

Where the accounts differ

The Federalist adds that Garcia was a "known member" of MS-13 and was in possession of drugs and money during his arrest. The Federalist also claims he was accused of being a "child predator" and of domestic violence, including allegations of bruising, punching, and scratching his wife. The Daily Wire and The Guardian both mention that Garcia’s wife has sponsored him for legal residency, while The Federalist attributes these claims to DHS. The NPR account omits details about Garcia’s alleged crimes and focuses more on the legal battle and his family status.

Trump administration approves Elon Musk’s plan for datacenters in space

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) approved SpaceX’s plan to deploy 1 million satellites to create data centers in space, calling it a step toward a "Kardashev II civilization." The authorization, announced on Wednesday, allows SpaceX to begin deploying satellites no later than November 2033, with 10% deployed by November 2035 and 50% by November 2038. The FCC stated that the project would "support America’s booming super intelligence economy" and "bring the human race closer to a Kardashev II civilization – one that can harness the full power of the sun." SpaceX argued that space-based data centers would reduce environmental impact and energy costs compared to terrestrial ones.

Critics, including scientists and industry experts, have raised concerns about the feasibility and risks of such a project. The Government Accountability Office (GAO) warned of "engineering and economic barriers to deployment," noting that space does not cool computing hardware efficiently and that a large number of satellites could lead to collisions. The American Astronomical Society (AAS) criticized the plan for potentially undermining astronomical research and polluting the night sky with bright satellites.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Fox Business both mention that SpaceX sought approval for 42,000 Starlink satellites before deployment, but the Guardian notes that the current network has just over 11,000 satellites in space, while Fox Business states that the growing network currently has just over 11,000 satellites in space. The Guardian also states that it was unclear whether SpaceX would deploy 1 million satellites, while Fox Business mentions that it was unclear whether SpaceX would put 1 million satellites in space. The Guardian and BBC both note that the FCC did not specify whether SpaceX is required to launch all 1 million satellites, while Newsweek states that the authorization does not specify if SpaceX is required to launch all 1 million satellites.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · Newsweek · BBC · Fox Business

DR Congo Ebola cases surpass 12,000 as fighting disrupts response in east

The number of confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has surpassed 12,000, according to government data, as escalating fighting in the east disrupts efforts to contain the country’s deadliest outbreak on record. Health officials and humanitarian workers reported that Ebola response workers have been forced to leave affected areas amid worsening violence in the eastern DRC. The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo species of the Ebola virus, was declared in mid-May in Ituri province in the northeastern DRC. There are no approved treatments or vaccines for this species.

Fighting between the Congolese army and fighters from the Convention for the Popular Revolution (CRP) has intensified in recent days, displacing civilians, according to the United Nations mission. Catherine Smallwood, the World Health Organization’s Ebola incident manager, said about a dozen people, including two WHO personnel and Congolese health officials, were evacuated to Bunia after three days trapped in a hospital in Akwe, northeast of the provincial capital, amid intense crossfire. The CRP is led by Thomas Lubanga, who was convicted by the International Criminal Court in 2012 of conscripting child soldiers. The group declared a unilateral ceasefire in May, but fighting has continued.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that at least 12,021 infections have been confirmed, including 5,904 deaths, while NPR states that nearly half the 12,021 people confirmed to have the virus have died. Al Jazeera does not mention the death toll percentage, while NPR provides it. NPR also states that the outbreak is the fastest-growing in history and on track to surpass the death toll from the West African outbreak that killed 11,000 people between 2014 and 2016, a claim not made by Al Jazeera. Al Jazeera notes that the outbreak is the deadliest of Congo's 17 outbreaks, a detail not included in NPR's account.

Sources Al Jazeera · NPR
