Chuck Edwards' abrupt exit leaves Republicans scrambling

Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-N.C.) abruptly withdrew from his re-election campaign after the House Ethics Committee recommended his censure for violating House rules over sexual harassment and creating a hostile work environment. Edwards' decision came after the committee's findings, which included incidents of harassment and inappropriate conduct toward female staffers, were made public. His exit has complicated the Republican strategy for the 11th District, which was already a toss-up race. Democratic opponent Jamie Ager significantly out-raised Edwards in the second quarter, shifting the race from "lean Republican" to "toss-up" according to Sabato's Crystal Ball. Edwards' withdrawal has weakened the GOP position, with some Republicans acknowledging the seat is now in trouble. The replacement nominee will have to start from scratch three months before Election Day, requiring significant fundraising efforts from the GOP.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Edwards' decision to drop out was made via social media, while The Hill notes that the House Ethics Committee's findings were corroborated by Axios and included handwritten correspondence, personal gifts, and other incidents. The Daily Wire and Mother Jones focus on different representatives, Max Miller, and his allegations of abuse, which are not mentioned in Axios or The Hill. The Daily Caller and New York Post discuss Cory Mills' political troubles and primary challenges, which are not covered in the other accounts. The Hill and Axios both mention the House Democratic Women’s Caucus calling for Edwards' resignation, but The Hill adds that the Democratic Women’s Caucus is working on a bipartisan measure to prohibit romantic relationships between members of Congress and all staffers.

Sources Axios · The Hill · The Daily Wire · Mother Jones · New York Post · The Daily Caller

Senate panel votes to hold Dr. Fauci in contempt over refusal to answer questions

The Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee voted Thursday to hold Dr. Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress for repeatedly invoking the Fifth Amendment during a hearing last week. The vote, which was 8-5, followed Fauci’s refusal to answer questions about the origins of the COVID-19 pandemic and his role in the federal response. Rand Paul, the committee’s chairman, said he would immediately refer the contempt resolution to the Department of Justice for possible prosecution. Fauci, who was pardoned by President Joe Biden before leaving office, argued that the Fifth Amendment still protected him from self-incrimination. Democrats criticized the vote as a political stunt that could harm future oversight efforts.

Paul claimed that Fauci had no legal basis to invoke the Fifth Amendment because of the pardon, which he said covers any crimes committed from 2014 to 2025. Fauci’s attorneys argued that the pardon only protects against federal crimes and does not negate his Fifth Amendment rights. The Department of Justice has not yet commented on whether it will investigate the referral.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and ABC News both state that Paul plans to send the contempt resolution directly to the Department of Justice, while The Hill and Axios note that the resolution could also be sent to the full Senate for a vote. The Daily Wire mentions that Paul’s office sent two criminal referrals to the Biden Justice Department and two more under Trump, while The Hill and Axios do not specify the number of referrals. The Daily Wire also includes details about the potential prison sentences for Bannon and Navarro, which are not mentioned in the other accounts. The Hill and Axios describe the legal argument around the pardon and the Fifth Amendment, while The Daily Wire attributes the characterization of the pardon as a "sweeping" one to Paul.

Sources The Daily Wire · Axios · Just The News · The Hill · NPR · ABC News

Sen. Mitch McConnell Gives Major Update After Months In Hospital

Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY) announced Thursday that he has been released from a rehabilitation facility and will continue his recovery at his Kentucky home, ending a months-long hospitalization. The 84-year-old senator, who is not running for reelection, provided no timeline on a possible return to Washington, D.C. His absence has complicated efforts to confirm Todd Blanche’s nomination to be the U.S. Attorney General, as the acting Attorney General is struggling to earn 50 votes for confirmation. McConnell stated he will maintain an intensive regimen of physical therapy from home during the state work period and continue to engage with his staff and colleagues on important Senate business.

McConnell’s extended hospitalization has fueled questions about his health and what would happen if he were unable to complete his final term. He has remained hospitalized since June 14, when emergency responders were dispatched to his Washington residence following what was widely reported as an emergency radio traffic describing a cardiac arrest requiring CPR. The lack of information sparked widespread speculation online. The Kentucky Republican’s absence has also drawn scrutiny from lawmakers in both parties, with Kentucky’s Democratic governor, Andy Beshear, publicly urging McConnell to release more information about his health.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Guardian both state that McConnell was hospitalized following a fall at home in June and that he was briefly unconscious. The New York Post and The Daily Caller mention that McConnell was found “unconscious” and taken to the hospital in an ambulance, while The Daily Wire and The Guardian do not specify the exact nature of the emergency. The Daily Wire and The Guardian note that McConnell’s office initially provided few details, while The New York Post and The Daily Caller include quotes from McConnell’s July 12 statement, which he said he had been hospitalized due to a fall and a “mild case of pneumonia.” The Daily Wire and The Guardian also mention that McConnell’s doctors have stated he is not yet medically cleared to return to the office, while The New York Post and The Daily Caller do not include this detail. The Daily Wire and The Guardian report that McConnell’s absence has complicated the confirmation of Todd Blanche, while The New York Post and The Daily Caller do not mention this explicitly.

Sources The Daily Wire · Axios · The Guardian · New York Post · The Daily Caller · ABC News

Trump says he hasn't made a determination on Pirro's future

President Trump stated Tuesday he has not decided whether to retain Jeanine Pirro as U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia after she dropped charges against Olympian David Hearn for allegedly damaging the Reflecting Pool. Pirro cited contractor issues as the cause of the damage, not Hearn. The case was dismissed by a D.C. judge, with Pirro’s office citing insufficient information from the Department of Interior. Trump criticized Pirro publicly, calling her performance in court “vicious” and accusing her of “choking.” Meanwhile, New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez filed a lawsuit against the Department of Justice, alleging the agency is withholding Epstein-related records needed for a state investigation into Zorro Ranch.

Trump-endorsed Rep. Andy Ogles loses GOP primary in Tennessee's 5th District

Rep. Andy Ogles, R-Tenn., lost the Republican primary for Tennessee's 5th Congressional District to challenger Charlie Hatcher, a former state Agriculture Commissioner. The Associated Press projected Hatcher's victory with 53.2% of the vote to Ogles' 46.8%. Hatcher secured the endorsement of Gov. Bill Lee, while Ogles received the backing of President Donald Trump. The race was influenced by redistricting, which reshaped the district to favor Hatcher. The Invest in Tomorrow Coalition, a pro-clean energy super PAC, spent around $2 million supporting Hatcher and opposing Ogles.

Ogles, a member of the House Freedom Caucus, has faced controversy for his rhetoric, including past social media posts deemed homophobic and inflammatory. He also faced an FBI investigation into campaign finance records, which concluded without charges. Hatcher, a fifth-generation dairy farmer, ran on a platform of pro-gun, anti-abortion, and pro-Trump positions. He criticized Ogles for being a "show horse" and for not spending enough time on legislative action.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that the Invest in Tomorrow Coalition is bankrolled by Chris Larsen, a Democratic donor and Ripple chairman, and that Ogles accused the group of trying to "buy his seat." CBS News and Washington Examiner also mention Larsen as a donor but do not attribute the accusation to Ogles. The Hill and Just The News do not mention Larsen's political affiliations. CBS News states that Ogles deleted a post about homosexuality, while FOX News and Washington Examiner describe the post as deleted and reprimanded. The Hill and Washington Examiner note that Ogles' district was redrawn to favor Hatcher, while CBS News and Just The News provide more detail on the redistricting's impact.

Sources FOX News · CBS News · The Hill · Washington Examiner · Al Jazeera · Just The News

Trump denies U.S. running low on some munitions, says vast resupplies being made

President Trump publicly denied reports of U.S. munitions shortages during the war with Iran, calling the claims "treasonous" and vowing to jail those who leak such information. Trump targeting 'birth tourism' in new birthright citizenship effort

U.S. President Donald Trump signed two executive orders on Thursday aimed at restricting birthright citizenship and banning "birth tourism," the practice of entering the U.S. to give birth for the purpose of securing automatic citizenship for the child. The orders seek to deny birthright citizenship to children of foreign diplomats, alien enemies, and those who enter the U.S. with the intent to give birth. Trump criticized the Supreme Court's rejection of his earlier attempt to end birthright citizenship, calling it a "very unfair decision." He claimed that "hundreds of thousands" of people are exploiting the system, with some building businesses around birth tourism.

The orders also expand definitions of non-citizens whose children are not eligible for birthright citizenship, including members of foreign terrorist organizations and individuals who fraudulently obtain visas. The administration argues that these measures are permitted under the Supreme Court's ruling, which upheld the 14th Amendment's guarantee of birthright citizenship. The orders apply to future births and do not affect current citizens.

Where the accounts differ

UPI and The Daily Wire both report that Trump claimed birthright citizenship was intended for "the babies of slaves," while Axios and the New York Post attribute this characterization to Trump. The Washington Examiner and BBC state that Trump claimed birthright citizenship was introduced after the Civil War to ensure the children of slaves were considered citizens. The Daily Wire and BBC mention that Trump estimated "hundreds of thousands" of foreigners have exploited birthright citizenship, while UPI and the New York Post attribute this claim to Trump. The Daily Wire provides specific examples of birth tourism centers and lawsuits, which are not included in the other accounts.

Sources UPI · Washington Examiner · The Daily Wire · Axios · BBC · New York Post

Sen. Marsha Blackburn Wins Tennessee GOP Primary for Governor

Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) won the Republican primary for Tennessee governor on Thursday, defeating Rep. John Rose (R-TN) and state Rep. Monty Fritts. With over 90% of the vote in, Blackburn led Rose with 43.5% to 32.7%, according to Decision Desk HQ. The race was seen as a foregone conclusion in a deeply red state that has not elected a Democrat to statewide office since 2006. Blackburn, a longtime Republican, is expected to face Memphis City Councilwoman Jerri Green (D-TN) in the November general election.

Blackburn’s campaign focused on issues like immigration, China, and economic conservatism. She ran a viral ad in which she smashed a fortune cookie, declaring, “It doesn’t take a fortune cookie to figure it out: here in Tennessee, we’re going to stop Communist China and protect Tennessee land.” She also pledged to work with the Trump administration to continue efforts against China and to deport illegal immigrants. Rose, a four-term U.S. Rep., campaigned on rural issues and criticized a $30 million incentive package for a Starbucks expansion, arguing it should support local businesses instead. Fritts, who raised little money, centered his campaign on religious issues, including a call to ban mosques and other non-Christian religious groups.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and Washington Examiner both report Blackburn received 43.5% of the vote, while CBS News and Just The News state she received 43.8%. FOX News reports Blackburn received 43.9%. The Daily Wire and Washington Examiner note Blackburn led Rose by over 10 points, while CBS News and Just The News report a margin of over 11 points. The Daily Wire and FOX News mention Blackburn’s victory speech and her comments about uniting conservatives, while CBS News and Just The News focus on her expected general election matchup with Jerri Green. The Daily Wire and FOX News attribute Blackburn’s anti-China ad to her campaign, while CBS News and Washington Examiner note the ad was part of her campaign strategy. The Daily Wire and FOX News mention Blackburn’s comments about Trump’s endorsement, while CBS News and Washington Examiner note Trump did not endorse in the race.

Sources The Daily Wire · CBS News · Washington Examiner · Just The News · FOX News

At least 12 states report cyberattacks on water systems possibly linked to Iran

CBS News reports that cyberattacks on U.S. water systems, suspected to be linked to Iran-backed hackers, have been reported in at least a dozen states, including Michigan, Minnesota, Georgia, New Jersey, and South Dakota. In Minnesota, over 30 community water systems were impacted, with some utilities losing remote-control capabilities and forced to switch to manual operations. In Georgia, the Clayton County Water Authority experienced a water pressure drop due to cyber activity, leading to a boil water advisory, though service was restored quickly. Federal agencies, including the FBI and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, warned about the attacks on July 30, advising utilities to disconnect from the internet and improve security measures.

Axios highlights the broader vulnerabilities in U.S. water systems, noting the fragmented nature of over 144,000 public water systems, many of which lack the resources to address cybersecurity threats. The article emphasizes the challenges of aging infrastructure and the need for federal support, citing the American Water Works Association's call for nationwide cybersecurity standards. It also mentions the impact of climate change and extreme weather on water systems, with examples like the 2021 Marshall Fire in Colorado.

Straight Arrow News adds that the attacks appear to be broader than previously thought, with at least 100 municipalities reporting signs of malicious activity. It notes that in Rapid City, South Dakota, a cyberincident affected a specialized pump in the wastewater system, though the city declined to comment on the connection to other attacks. The article also mentions the FBI's preliminary suspicion of Iran-backed involvement, without formal attribution.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News specifies that in Minnesota, over 30 community water systems were impacted, while Axios does not mention the number of systems affected in Minnesota. Straight Arrow News reports that at least 100 municipalities have found signs of malicious activity, a figure not mentioned by CBS or Axios. Axios provides more context on the broader infrastructure challenges and the role of local governments, which CBS and Straight Arrow do not detail. CBS and Straight Arrow both mention the FBI's preliminary suspicion of Iran-backed involvement, but Axios does not attribute the attacks to any specific group. Straight Arrow News also notes that the New York Times reported on the 100 municipalities, a source not mentioned by CBS or Axios.

Sources CBS News · Axios · Straight Arrow News
