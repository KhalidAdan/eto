US, Canada hold last-minute talks to stop 50% U.S. tariffs

President Donald Trump announced a three-day pause on 50% tariffs on Canadian goods hours before they were set to take effect, citing a pending deal. The tariffs, which would have applied to $20 billion in Canadian imports, including liquor, dairy, and vehicles, were scheduled to begin at 12:01 a.m. on August 19. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney acknowledged the postponement but stated that work continued on reaching an agreement. The White House claimed the tariffs were imposed in response to Canada’s “discriminatory treatment” of U.S. commerce, while Canadian officials expressed concerns over U.S. tariffs on steel, aluminum, and softwood lumber. Both sides emphasized the importance of avoiding the tariffs, with U.S. trade officials noting the potential impact on American consumers and Canadian businesses.

The negotiations involved discussions over market access, economic security, and digital trade, with the U.S. seeking greater access to Canadian resources and Canada pushing for relief from existing tariffs. Trump also mentioned the potential revival of the Keystone XL Pipeline as part of the deal. Canadian officials, including Prime Minister Mark Carney, emphasized the need to build a “stronger, more competitive economy” while continuing to negotiate. The delay allowed for further talks, though no final agreement had been reached by the deadline.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and UPI both mention the three-day pause on tariffs and Trump’s claim of a deal, but ABC includes details about the U.S. seeking military equipment and access to critical minerals, which UPI omits. UPI notes that Carney did not confirm a deal was reached, while ABC states that Carney said negotiations were “intense and delicate.” Fox Business and FOX News both report Trump’s mention of the Keystone XL Pipeline, but Fox Business attributes this to Trump’s social media post, while FOX News includes it in a quote from Trump. UPI and Washington Examiner both mention the postponement of tariffs until August 21, but UPI does not specify the three-day pause, while Washington Examiner notes the three-day delay. ABC and Fox Business both mention the impact on Canadian spirits, but Fox Business includes quotes from Chris Swonger, while ABC does not.

Sources ABC News · Slate · UPI · Washington Examiner · Fox Business · FOX News

Byron Donalds and David Jolly to face off in Florida governor's race

Florida voters cast their ballots in the primary elections on Tuesday, with Republican Rep. Byron Donalds and Democrat David Jolly emerging as the top contenders for governor. Donalds, backed by former President Donald Trump, won the Republican primary with 48.7% of the vote, defeating Lt. Gov. Jay Collins (24.8%) and far-right candidate James Fishback (10.5%). Jolly, a former Republican congressman who switched parties, secured 61.6% of the vote in the Democratic primary, defeating state lawmaker Dotie Joseph and criminal justice reform advocate Evelyn Castillo-Bach. The race for governor marks the first elections in Florida to use the new congressional districts signed into law in May, which were drawn by the state’s Republican-dominated legislature to favor right-wing candidates.

The race for governor is part of a broader trend of Florida shifting to the right, with Republicans dominating key statewide positions since 2019. Democrats, however, remain hopeful of making inroads, with Jolly seen as a potential candidate to attract more moderate voters. The state’s redistricting efforts, part of a national strategy under Trump to boost Republican chances in the midterms, have left Democrats struggling to win major statewide races.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and Washington Examiner both report that Donalds received Trump’s endorsement, while CBS News and Axios attribute the endorsement to Trump as well. However, Al Jazeera and Washington Examiner mention that DeSantis did not endorse any Republican candidate, while CBS News and Axios do not explicitly state this. The Washington Examiner provides specific vote percentages (48.7% for Donalds, 24.8% for Collins, 10.5% for Fishback), while Al Jazeera and CBS News do not include these figures. Axios and Washington Examiner note that Fishback made inflammatory comments, including calling Donalds a “Section 8 ghetto” and a “token black,” while Al Jazeera and CBS News do not mention these specific remarks.

Sources Al Jazeera · CBS News · Axios · BBC · Washington Examiner · New York Post

Florida Senate race set between Ashley Moody and Angie Nixon

In the Florida Senate primary, Democratic state Rep. Angie Nixon defeated Republican candidate Alex Vindman, securing the Democratic nomination. Nixon, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, ran on progressive policies including Medicare for All, free childcare, and a national rent freeze. She will face off against Republican Sen. Ashley Moody in the November general election. Moody, appointed by Gov. Ron DeSantis to replace Marco Rubio, is backed by former President Donald Trump and has a history of supporting his agenda.

Nixon's victory was notable given her limited fundraising compared to Vindman, who raised over $16 million. Nixon's campaign focused on grassroots organizing and local support, particularly in Jacksonville and other parts of Florida. The race highlights the growing influence of progressive candidates within the Democratic Party, despite the state's traditionally Republican leanings.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and Axios both report that Nixon defeated Vindman, but Axios notes that Nixon's win was a "stunning" upset due to her limited fundraising compared to Vindman's $16.3 million. CBS News does not mention the fundraising disparity. Straight Arrow News states that Vindman spent $850,000 on digital advertising in February, while Nixon's total campaign spending was over $700,000 by the end of last month. Washington Examiner and FOX News both mention Vindman's fundraising advantage, with FOX News citing FEC records showing Vindman reported $14.6 million in campaign receipts through June 30, more than 20 times Nixon's $625,000. Just The News does not provide specific figures on fundraising but notes Vindman's role in Trump's first impeachment.

Sources CBS News · Axios · Straight Arrow News · Washington Examiner · Just The News · FOX News

Lindsay Clancy had ‘thoughts of harming the children’, her mother testifies at trial

Lindsay Clancy’s mother, Paula Musgrove, testified in her daughter’s Massachusetts triple-murder trial that Clancy had told her she had thoughts of harming the children. Musgrove described her daughter’s deteriorating mental state in the months leading up to the killings, including text messages in which Clancy said she was “really sick” and “I don’t want to be alone.” Clancy’s defense is arguing that she suffered from postpartum psychosis and should not be held criminally responsible. The prosecution, however, claims she planned the murders and faked a suicide attempt. During the trial, a digital forensic expert testified that Clancy had searched for terms like “symptoms of postpartum psychosis” and “ketamine for suicidal ideation” in the weeks before the killings.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News both report that Musgrove testified Clancy told her she had thoughts of harming the children. However, CBS News includes a text message from Clancy to Musgrove in which she wrote, “I’m really sick. Something is wrong,” and that she had taken medication for anxiety, which she said made things worse. The Guardian does not include this specific text message. Additionally, CBS News notes that Musgrove said Clancy told her she was “doing better” in the days before the killings, while The Guardian does not mention this. UPI and The Daily Wire report that Clancy told a medical expert she heard a man’s voice telling her to kill the children and herself, but The Guardian and CBS News do not mention this claim. The Washington Examiner and FOX News report that Clancy told her husband she heard a voice telling her to kill the children and herself, but The Guardian and CBS News do not include this detail.

Rep. Cory Mills Loses Florida GOP Primary Amid Ethics Probe and Scandals

Rep. Cory Mills, a Republican congressman from Florida, lost his primary bid for re-election in the state's 7th Congressional District to Ryan Elijah, a former local TV news anchor. The race was marked by a series of allegations against Mills, including domestic abuse, campaign finance violations, and potential misconduct involving former partners. The House Ethics Committee has launched an investigation into these claims, which have also drawn scrutiny from the Department of Justice. Mills, who received the endorsement of President Donald Trump, faced significant opposition from within his own party, with several Florida Republicans endorsing Elijah instead. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis also distanced himself from Mills, stating he does not support him. Elijah's victory positions him to face Democratic nominee Bale Dalton in the general election, which Democrats view as a potential opportunity to flip the Republican-leaning district.

Mills' campaign has struggled with fundraising, with recent reports showing he raised only about $16,000 in July, compared to Elijah's over $186,000. The race has drawn attention due to the high-profile nature of Mills' controversies, which include allegations of domestic violence, stolen valor, and financial impropriety. Despite these issues, Mills' campaign highlighted his record of constituent services and efforts to evacuate Americans from conflict zones. The outcome of the primary has raised questions about the influence of Trump's endorsement and the role of ethics investigations in shaping congressional races.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Daily Wire both report that Mills faced allegations of domestic abuse and that the House Ethics Committee is investigating him, but only The Daily Wire and CBS News mention that a former girlfriend accused Mills of threatening to release nude photos, leading to a restraining order. Mother Jones and CBS News also note that Mills' campaign raised only about $16,000 in July, while The Daily Wire states that the campaign has less than $70,000 in the bank. FOX News and Just The News do not mention the specific financial figures, while NPR and CBS News note that Elijah's campaign has over $186,000 in cash on hand. The Daily Wire and CBS News both mention that Mills is under investigation by the Department of Justice, while Mother Jones and FOX News report that Mills denied the existence of the DOJ probe. The Daily Wire and CBS News also state that Mills has denied all wrongdoing, while Mother Jones and NPR note that Mills has not been charged with any crime.

Sources Mother Jones · FOX News · The Daily Wire · CBS News · NPR · Just The News

Sen. Dan Sullivan and Mary Peltola Advance in Alaska Senate Primary

Sen. Dan Sullivan (R-AK) and former Rep. Mary Peltola (D-AK) advanced from Alaska’s nonpartisan primary, setting up a November general election race for the U.S. Senate. Sullivan, a veteran Republican and Trump ally, faces Peltola, the first Alaska Native to serve in Congress and a former House representative. The race is seen as a key contest for Democratic control of the Senate, with both candidates vying for a seat in a state that has traditionally favored Republicans. Peltola’s campaign recently distanced itself from an unsolicited endorsement from former Vice President Kamala Harris, who had previously supported her. The primary also featured another candidate named Dan Sullivan, a retired teacher, who was allowed to remain on the ballot after a judge rejected an effort to remove him. Alaska’s top-four primary system means the top four vote-getters advance to the general election, where ranked-choice voting will determine the winner.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire reports that Peltola’s campaign manager, Anton McParland, left the campaign just hours before the primary, citing a “Saturday Night Massacre” of staff firings. The Associated Press and Washington Examiner do not mention McParland’s departure, though the Examiner notes Peltola made staffing changes as she transitioned to the general election. The Daily Wire also claims Peltola rejected an unsolicited endorsement from Kamala Harris, while the Washington Examiner and Newsweek note that Peltola’s campaign dismissed an email from Harris’s PAC as unsolicited. The FOX News account states that Peltola was the only Democrat to win a statewide election in Alaska since 1972, a claim not made by other outlets. The NPR account does not mention the primary or the candidates, focusing instead on unrelated news.

Sources The Daily Wire · Associated Press · Washington Examiner · Newsweek · FOX News · NPR

Trump dismisses USS Lincoln criticism and snaps at CNN reporter to ‘be quiet’

President Donald Trump claimed he had third-hand reports that the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln was “beautifully maintained” despite extensive accounts of deteriorating living conditions during its lengthy deployment in the strait of Hormuz. At the White House on Monday, the president berated CNN reporter Kristen Holmes during a press availability, insisting she “be quiet” and describing the reporting as fake news. Trump also did not identify the “other people” he claimed had verified the anonymous admiral’s account. The Lincoln, a nuclear-powered aircraft carrier, left port from San Diego on 21 November 2025 and has now set a modern record for the longest time at sea without a port call. It has been more than 250 days since it has touched land. The Navy Times and Stars and Stripes have also largely confirmed reports of the Lincoln’s conditions, describing shortages of supplies and water and plumbing problems. Several sailors have attempted to leap from the ship according to their reports, prompting Democratic lawmakers to demand answers from the Pentagon.

Where the accounts differ

The Straight Arrow News states that Admiral Brad Cooper said problems involving food and hot water were reported months ago and have since been addressed by the ship’s leadership, while the Nation and the BBC report that the Navy denies a widespread mental health crisis on the Lincoln.

Mobile gold miners in eastern Congo complicate health workers’ efforts to contain Ebola

The Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo has reached 5,021 confirmed cases, including 2,378 deaths, according to government data. The Bundibugyo virus, which causes the current outbreak, has no approved vaccines or treatments. Health workers report that the outbreak is spreading faster than any previous Ebola outbreak in history, with cases increasing three times more rapidly than the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak. The outbreak is concentrated in Ituri province, where artisanal gold mining is prevalent. Miners, including many children, work in hard-to-reach areas and often move between villages, complicating efforts to track and contain the virus. Health workers say that measures like handwashing and social distancing are not followed at mining sites, increasing the risk of transmission.

Miners and small-scale traders move frequently between mining areas and population centers, making it difficult to trace infected individuals and monitor the spread of the virus. Health workers have lost track of two miners who were identified as Ebola contacts after they moved to another mining site. The outbreak has also led to attacks on health teams, with residents in Ituri province attacking a health team in the territory of Aru, setting fire to an ambulance and damaging two other vehicles.

Where the accounts differ

The Associated Press (AP) reports that the outbreak has reached 5,021 cases and 2,378 deaths, while CBS News and the second AP article both cite these same numbers. Al Jazeera reports 4,900 confirmed cases and 2,300 deaths, which is slightly lower than the numbers cited by the other outlets. AP also mentions that the Bundibugyo virus is less common than other Ebola strains, complicating the response. Al Jazeera notes that the current outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a rare form of Ebola first identified in Uganda in 2007, and that there are no licensed vaccines or specific approved therapies for it. CBS News and the second AP article do not mention the Bundibugyo virus specifically, only that the outbreak is caused by a species of Ebola with no approved vaccines or treatments.

Sources Associated Press · Al Jazeera · CBS News
