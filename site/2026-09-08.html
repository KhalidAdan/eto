Energy Secretary Chris Wright expresses cautious optimism about gasoline prices amid ongoing Iran conflict

Energy Secretary Chris Wright told Sunday political shows that gasoline prices are likely to decline, though he stopped short of making firm promises. He cited falling demand after the Labor Day holiday and recent changes to refinery specifications that ease production. Pump prices remain at record highs, averaging $4.15 per gallon, according to AAA data. Wright also mentioned that gasoline futures contracts suggest prices could drop significantly in the coming months. However, he acknowledged that future prices are volatile and not guaranteed. The conflict with Iran has contributed to rising oil prices, with crude oil hitting a six-week high above $96 per barrel.

Wright also discussed the U.S. strategy to secure the Strait of Hormuz, noting that oil transits through the waterway have reached over 9 million barrels a day, with additional flows via bypass pipelines. He emphasized that the U.S. is working with allies to maintain the flow of oil, though he admitted that Iran continues to pose a threat to the region's stability. The situation remains tense, with both sides engaging in periodic attacks and retaliations.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and The Guardian report that Wright suggested the U.S. may not reach a nuclear deal with Iran and instead focus on degrading Iran’s nuclear capabilities. The Guardian adds that former Defense Secretary Leon Panetta warned the Iran war could last another six months. The Daily Caller and ABC News both mention Wright’s comments about the U.S. military taking out Iranian oil tankers and the ongoing conflict over the Strait of Hormuz. The BBC reports that Iran claimed to have attacked an American remote-controlled vessel in the Strait of Hormuz, which the U.S. denied. The New York Post and ABC News both include Wright’s remarks about Operation Economic Outcast and the economic pressure on Iran, while the Guardian does not mention this.

Sources Axios · New York Post · The Daily Caller · The Guardian · ABC News · BBC

U.S. Envoys Meet Zelensky in Kyiv After Talks with Putin

U.S. special envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky in Kyiv on Sunday, following a three-hour meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Saturday. Both sides expressed optimism about advancing peace talks, though specifics remain unclear. Zelensky emphasized the need for security guarantees and a dignified peace, while Kushner and Witkoff highlighted the importance of trilateral discussions. The meeting coincided with a three-day cease-fire agreed upon by Russia and Ukraine to mark the envoys' trip. Zelensky also mentioned the U.S. is exploring winter de-escalation steps, including energy and grain shipments, to ease tensions before the harsh winter.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and Straight Arrow News both mention that the cease-fire was agreed upon by Russia and Ukraine, while ABC News and NPR do not specify who agreed to it. The Washington Examiner and Straight Arrow News state that the cease-fire started at midnight Saturday, while the New York Post and ABC News do not mention a specific start time. The New York Post and Straight Arrow News report that the U.S. is working on a peace plan, while Axios and NPR describe the U.S. as exploring de-escalation steps without a detailed plan. The Washington Examiner and Straight Arrow News attribute the idea of trilateral talks to Kushner, while the New York Post and ABC News attribute it to Kushner and Witkoff.

Sources New York Post · Axios · Washington Examiner · ABC News · NPR · Straight Arrow News

Canada's retaliatory tariffs hit US products as Trump warns of escalation

Retaliatory tariffs issued by Canada were set to hit some imports from the United States at one minute after midnight Eastern Time on Tuesday, intensifying a trade war between the longtime allies and risking an escalation from President Donald Trump. Levies as high as 50% will be applied to hundreds of U.S. products, ranging from aluminum foil to raincoats to cheese. Canadian officials said the retaliatory measure would target about $20 billion worth of U.S. products, mirroring the size of a recent batch of U.S. levies. The targeted goods amount to nearly 6% of annual U.S. exports to Canada, according to U.S. Census Bureau data for 2025. President Donald Trump has repeatedly warned of a forceful response if the Canadian tariffs were to take hold. On Monday afternoon, Trump threatened to bar the sale of planes made by Canada-based aerospace company Bombardier unless the firm agreed to manufacture products in the U.S.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and Washington Examiner both state that Canada’s tariffs target about $20 billion worth of U.S. products, while Washington Examiner specifies the amount as $27.6 billion. BBC reports the counter-tariffs will apply to nearly C$28bn ($20bn; £15bn) worth of American products. Fox Business and Straight Arrow News both mention Trump’s threat to ban Bombardier sales in the U.S. unless it builds in the U.S., but Fox Business adds that Trump claimed more than half of Bombardier's revenue comes from U.S. customers. Christian Science Monitor and Washington Examiner both note that Bombardier’s jets comply with the USMCA trade deal, while ABC News and BBC do not mention this. ABC News and Washington Examiner both mention that Canada omitted seafood items from its tariffs due to pushback from its seafood industry, while BBC and Fox Business do not mention this omission.

Sources ABC News · BBC · Straight Arrow News · Christian Science Monitor · Washington Examiner · Fox Business

Mayor Mamdani faces backlash after giving symbolic 9/11 pen to former al Qaeda defense lawyer

Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed executive orders Friday designating September 11 as a citywide day of remembrance and service and strengthening efforts to prevent future terrorist threats. After signing the final order, Mamdani handed a ceremonial pen to NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch, who passed it to chief counsel Ramzi Kassem. Kassem previously represented Ahmed al-Darbi, a Guantánamo Bay detainee who pleaded guilty to war-crime charges tied to al Qaeda plots against commercial ships. Al-Darbi’s sister was married to Khalid al-Mihdhar, one of the hijackers aboard American Airlines Flight 77, which crashed into the Pentagon, killing 184 people. The handoff drew criticism on social media, with users arguing it distracted from an event meant to honor those killed in the attacks and the first responders who died trying to save them.

Mamdani is expected to become the first Muslim mayor to attend the city's official 9/11 remembrance ceremony. Critics, including former Mayor Rudy Giuliani and former Gov. George Pataki, have called for him to stay away, citing his association with Kassem and other concerns about his political views. Giuliani claimed Mamdani does not believe the Sept. 11 attacks were acts of terrorism, a claim some have described as baseless. Mamdani has defended his commitment to honoring the victims of the attacks, stating he wants the focus to be on the families and first responders.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News states that Kassem attended the ceremony because he and his team drafted both executive orders as part of his duties as chief counsel. ABC News and The Daily Caller do not mention this justification, though they both note Kassem’s prior representation of al-Darbi. The Daily Wire and Just The News describe Kassem as a lawyer who previously represented an al-Qaeda operative and relative of a hijacker, but do not specify that he drafted the executive orders. The Daily Caller and Just The News mention that relatives of 9/11 victims condemned the gesture, while FOX News and ABC News also note similar reactions. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller include details about Mamdani’s visit to a Brooklyn mosque led by Imam Siraj Wahhaj, an unindicted co-conspirator in the 1993 World Trade Center bombing case, which the other accounts do not mention.

Sources FOX News · ABC News · Washington Examiner · Just The News · The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller

Lindsay Clancy’s Trial Ends in Mistrial as Defense Attorney Faces Criticism

Lindsay Clancy’s high-profile murder trial ended in a mistrial after a jury could not reach a unanimous verdict. The jury, which deliberated for seven days, reached an 11-1 decision, with one juror refusing to convict Clancy. Following the mistrial, defense attorney Kevin Reddington criticized the holdout juror, accusing him of stealing seven weeks of the other jurors’ time and calling him a “one man” who “robbed” the jury. Reddington also claimed that the holdout juror was biased against people with disabilities or mental illnesses. Meanwhile, Corie Walsh, an Illinois woman accused of killing her 2-year-old son, was reported to be following Clancy’s trial closely, texting friends about it just hours before her son’s death. Prosecutors stated that Walsh had become “very invested” in Clancy’s case and was discussing it with friends before the incident.

The mistrial has sparked debate over the fairness of the trial and the potential impact of media coverage on future proceedings. Legal experts and commentators have criticized Reddington’s comments, with some calling for his disbarment. Others have raised concerns about the difficulty of finding an impartial jury due to the extensive media attention and social media discussions surrounding the case.

Where the accounts differ

King Charles III clarifies status of Prince Harry and his wife, Meghan

King Charles III has issued a letter confirming that Prince Harry and Meghan, the Duke and Duchess of Sussex, remain non-working members of the royal family following their return to the United Kingdom. The letter, sent by the Lord Chamberlain on behalf of the king, states that the couple will continue to refrain from using their royal titles and that any charity work they undertake will be done in a private capacity. The letter was distributed to senior government officials, military leaders, and royal representatives. The decision to release the letter publicly reflects concerns within the palace that the couple's status could lead to a parallel royal court, potentially competing with working members of the royal family.

The letter also addresses security arrangements, noting that operational security decisions are the responsibility of relevant police agencies. The couple's request for publicly funded police protection, which was canceled when they stepped back from royal duties in 2020, remains under consideration by a government panel known as RAVEC. The letter emphasizes that the arrangement established in 2020, referred to as the Sandring, remains fully in effect, preventing any "half-in, half-out" status that would allow the couple to draw on royal status for commercial ventures.

Where the accounts differ

NPR states the letter was sent to senior officials in the government and military, as well as lord lieutenants, and that the security question is sensitive due to Harry's long-standing battle for police protection. The Daily Wire adds that the letter was distributed to Harry’s team and mentions a private meeting between the King, Queen Camilla, and the Sussexes at Highgrove House. CBS News notes that the letter was released to "help avoid doubt or confusion" and mentions the couple's return to the UK in late August. BBC reports that a Home Office security committee is meeting to consider taxpayer-funded security for the couple. The Daily Wire and CBS News both mention the couple's children, Archie and Lilibet, while NPR and BBC do not specify their names.

Sources NPR · The Daily Wire · BBC · CBS News

Hawaii declares state of emergency as Hurricane Lowell approaches islands

Hawaii has declared a state of emergency as Hurricane Lowell approaches, with impacts expected as soon as Monday. The storm is projected to reach the islands as a Category 2 hurricane, with its center potentially passing 130 miles west of Ni’ihau or 170 miles west of Kauai between Monday evening and Tuesday morning. Schools and government offices across parts of Hawaii have been closed due to the storm’s threat. The National Hurricane Center (NHC) has issued a hurricane warning for Kauai County and a tropical storm warning for Oahu. Heavy rain, high surf, and dangerous coastal conditions are expected, with forecasters warning of significant flash flooding and mudslides.

Hurricane Lowell is the third storm in just over three weeks to threaten Hawaii, following Hurricanes Karina and Marie. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) has warned of a 69% chance that this El Niño will be the strongest in 70 years. The storm’s projected offshore path is expected to keep the most dangerous winds away from the islands, though heavy rainfall and storm surge remain concerns. Officials have urged residents to stay indoors, avoid unnecessary travel, and monitor official forecasts as the storm moves past the islands.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that Lowell is expected to remain a Category 2 hurricane, while Newsweek states it could be a Category 1 or 2. Straight Arrow News mentions that Lowell increased in forward speed and became a Category 5 hurricane earlier in the week, a detail not included in The Guardian or CBS News. The Guardian and CBS News both mention the projected offshore path, but CBS News adds that Lowell is expected to pass just west of the main Hawaiian Islands on Monday night. Newsweek provides specific rainfall estimates for different islands, including up to 14 inches in isolated areas of Kauai and Niihau, which is not mentioned in The Guardian or CBS News. Straight Arrow News also notes that Governor Josh Green signed an emergency proclamation for Lowell, along with Hurricane Marie and Tropical Storm Karina, a detail not included in The Guardian or CBS News.

Sources The Guardian · Newsweek · Straight Arrow News · CBS News

More than 1,500 pounds of pork recalled over Listeria contamination risk

The U.S. Department of Agriculture’s Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) announced a recall of more than 1,500 pounds of imported ready-to-eat pork products due to concerns about possible Listeria monocytogenes contamination. Two U.S.-based companies, Prime Line Distributors, Inc. and Ferrarini USA, Inc., are issuing the recall, which is classified as a Class I recall, the most serious level. The products, known as guanciale, were produced in Italy on May 21 and imported into the U.S. in July. The contamination was detected during routine import reinspection testing. The affected products have a best if used by date of May 16, 2027, and a lot number of 263311US. The recall includes products distributed to food service and retail locations in multiple states.

Consumers are advised not to eat the recalled products and to either discard them or return them to the place of purchase. FSIS warned that some products may still be in refrigerators or freezers at food service establishments and retail locations. No illnesses have been reported in connection with the consumption of these products, though FSIS urged those in high-risk groups experiencing flu-like symptoms within two months of eating the products to seek medical attention.

Where the accounts differ

Fox Business and New York Post both mention the recall of 1,513 pounds of pork, while Newsweek states the recall is for about 1,500 pounds. Fox Business and New York Post specify the companies involved as Prime Line Distributors, Inc. and Ferrarini USA, Inc., while Newsweek also names these companies. Fox Business and New York Post list the states affected as California, Florida, Idaho, Illinois, Michigan, New Jersey, New York, and Texas, while Newsweek includes the same states. New York Post adds that the recalled meat was shipped to New York, New Jersey, and six other states, while Fox Business and Newsweek list the same states. New York Post mentions the "Dok Guanciale" label on some packaging, which is not mentioned in the other accounts. Fox Business and New York Post both state that no illnesses have been reported, while Newsweek also notes this but adds context about the broader foodborne illness trends.

Sources Fox Business · Newsweek · New York Post
