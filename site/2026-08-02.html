Migrant crisis pushes Italy to restore border checks with Spain, testing Europe's open-border system

Spain faced a massive surge of migrants from Morocco, with over 60,000 crossing into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta in a single day. The crisis led to the deaths of at least 57 people, many from drowning or being trampled. In response, Italy temporarily restored border controls on travelers arriving from Spain, citing national security concerns. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni announced the measure would last one month and be coordinated with France to strengthen controls along the Franco-Italian border. The situation has raised questions about the sustainability of the Schengen Area’s open-border policy, as European leaders debate whether migration pressures could lead to a retreat from free movement.

The crisis has also drawn attention from U.S. President Donald Trump, who warned during a Cabinet meeting that a similar situation could unfold in the United States if Democrats win the upcoming midterm elections. Trump criticized Spain’s migration policies, calling them “weak law, bad management,” and linked the events to the Biden administration’s border policies. Meanwhile, French President Emmanuel Macron deployed military forces and drones to strengthen border controls with Spain, citing the need to protect France’s borders.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News reports that Spain’s Interior Ministry said 48,300 migrants voluntarily returned to Morocco, leaving fewer than 2,000 in Ceuta, while Straight Arrow News states that Spanish officials estimated about 60,000 entries in 24 hours, a figure briefly posted online before being deleted. The Free Press mentions 60,000 migrants crossing into Ceuta, aligning with Straight Arrow’s estimate. The New York Post claims 60,000 Moroccan immigrants entered Ceuta, while The Daily Wire and BBC also report similar numbers. The New York Post and FOX News both mention the death toll rising to 57, while Straight Arrow News reports 67 deaths. The Daily Wire and BBC note that most migrants returned to Morocco, while FOX News and The Free Press state that the majority of those who entered Ceuta returned to Morocco.

Sources FOX News · New York Post · The Daily Wire · Straight Arrow News · The Free Press · BBC

US stands on the brink of a regional war

The U.S. war with Iran is flaring up across the region, threatening to pull U.S. allies into a fight that Washington and Israel started five months ago. A drone sparked a fire on two gas vessels in the port of Damietta, Egypt, on Wednesday, with no one claiming responsibility. The incident has fueled concerns about a widening war. The U.S. launched retaliatory strikes on Iran for launching missiles on U.S. forces in Jordan earlier this week. Saudi Arabia teamed up with the U.S. on Tuesday night to launch joint strikes on Iranian proxy militias in Iraq, in response to drone strikes on the Saudi Aramco oil processing facility in Abqaiq. Iran pinned the blame for the tanker strike on Israel, while praising Egypt as an “important friend and partner” in the region. Military experts were skeptical of Iran’s ability to keep up such attacks, questioning the strategic prudence of pulling Egypt into the war. Trump during a Cabinet meeting on Friday at Camp David said he was “losing faith” in the country’s ability to make a deal with Iran because “they do lie and they do misrepresent.” The escalations come as Trump faces growing political pressure to end the war.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and Al Jazeera both report that Trump said he would cancel attacks on Iran if a deal is reached, while Associated Press and FOX News describe Trump as saying he would halt attacks pending a deal. The Hill and FOX News mention the drone strike on the Egyptian port of Damietta, while Al Jazeera and Associated Press do not. The Hill attributes the claim that Iran blamed Israel for the tanker strike to Iranian Foreign Minister Abass Araghchi, while Al Jazeera and Associated Press do not attribute the claim. The Hill and Associated Press report that Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman raised concerns about U.S. escalating the conflict with Iran, while FOX News and Al Jazeera do not mention this. The Hill and Associated Press report that Trump said he was “losing faith” in the country’s ability to make a deal with Iran, while FOX News and Al Jazeera do not mention this.

Sources The Hill · Associated Press · Newsweek · BBC · FOX News · Al Jazeera

Three people killed and several injured in shooting at In-N-Out in Twin Falls, Idaho: Officials

A shooting occurred at an In-N-Out Burger restaurant in Twin Falls, Idaho, on Saturday afternoon, resulting in three deaths and several injuries. Local authorities confirmed the shooter was among the dead. Twin Falls Police Chief Matthew Hicks stated that the threat to the community was over, though the exact number of fatalities and injuries was still under investigation. Multiple people were taken to St. Luke’s Magic Valley Medical Center for treatment. Witnesses described a chaotic scene with people fleeing the area as gunshots rang out.

The incident took place near a busy shopping area, with hundreds of people present at the time. A witness, Lane Koehn, reported seeing a person with an AR-style rifle exit the drive-thru, followed by a man with a pistol firing at the shooter. Another witness, Nayeli Rodriguez, described an older man trying to protect her and her daughters as they fled the restaurant. The In-N-Out restaurant, which opened on July 24, was part of the company’s recent expansion to Idaho.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and CBS News both reported that the shooter was among the dead, while Al Jazeera initially stated the gunman killed two, though later accounts from other outlets corrected this. The Guardian, NPR, and ABC News all reported three deaths, with the Guardian and NPR providing more detail on the victims and the timeline. The Washington Examiner and CBS News mentioned the shooter’s identity was not yet confirmed, while the Guardian and NPR included quotes from witnesses describing the events. The Guardian and NPR also provided more specific details about the victims and the timeline of the shooting, which were not included in the Washington Examiner or CBS News accounts.

Trump administration to dismiss Reflecting Pool charges against David Hearn

The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia, led by Jeanine Pirro, has moved to drop criminal charges against former Olympic athlete David Hearn, who was accused of vandalizing the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. In court filings, Pirro acknowledged that the damage to the pool was the result of a botched installation by the contractor, not intentional vandalism. The motion to dismiss was filed after Hearn had pleaded not guilty to one felony count of destruction of property. The administration cited newly provided documents from the Department of the Interior that revealed the damage was due to flawed installation rather than vandalism.

The Reflecting Pool, a historic landmark in Washington, D.C., had undergone a renovation project championed by President Trump. The project, which included resurfacing the pool with “American Flag Blue” paint, was completed before the July 4, 2026, Independence Day celebrations. However, the renovation was plagued by issues such as leaks, algae growth, and peeling sealant. The Department of the Interior initially attributed the damage to vandalism, but later provided evidence showing the problems stemmed from rushed and poorly executed work by the contractor.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and The Hill both state that Pirro’s office received additional documents from the Department of the Interior after the indictment was returned, which revealed the damage was due to flawed installation. The Hill adds that the National Park Service observed Hearn “violently” tug at the lining before being asked to stop, while UPI and Washington Examiner note that Hearn was accused of tearing the lining but the peeling had already begun before the incident. The Hill and Washington Examiner also mention that the pool was drained for repairs after the July 4 celebrations, a detail not included in Al Jazeera or UPI. The New York Post and Washington Examiner both cite the contractor as Atlantic Industrial Coatings, while The Hill and BBC refer to the contractor as a Virginia-based company.

Sources Al Jazeera · New York Post · The Hill · BBC · UPI · Washington Examiner

Pima County Sheriff Releases Ransom Notes in Nancy Guthrie Investigation

The Pima County Sheriff’s Office released two ransom notes in the ongoing investigation into the disappearance of Nancy Guthrie, an 84-year-old grandmother and mother of NBC’s Savannah Guthrie. The notes, sent to local TV station KOLD and the Guthrie family, describe details of her home and include threats of harm if ransom demands are not met. The second note, sent on February 6, claimed Nancy Guthrie had died shortly after being abducted, attributing her death to heart-related issues. Authorities have not confirmed her death, and the investigation remains active. Savannah Guthrie has publicly appealed for her mother’s return, urging the public to help locate her.

The Daily Wire and Washington Examiner describe the second note as claiming Nancy Guthrie had died, while the BBC and Straight Arrow News include the same claim. Trump blames Minnesota governor, not Iran, for cyberattacks on state's water systems

President Donald Trump on Friday blamed Minnesota's Democratic governor for cyberattacks on the state's water systems, despite U.S. officials previously indicating they are investigating whether Iran or hackers associated with the country could be behind them. Trump made the comments during a Cabinet meeting at Camp David. Minnesota Governor Tim Walz responded by criticizing Trump's remarks, stating that the president "knows exactly who is responsible for this attack" and that "other states were hit too." Walz also attributed the cyberattacks to federal cuts by the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), which weakened cybersecurity. Over 30 water systems in Minnesota were targeted in a cyberattack over the weekend, according to Minnesota IT Services (MNIT). The attackers targeted systems to remotely monitor and control equipment, including programmable logic controllers (PLCs). The FBI and Environmental Protection Agency issued a nationwide warning about the cyberattacks, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) warned that cyber threat actors are targeting PLCs and modifying passwords to "lock out operators" from U.S. water systems.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and Straight Arrow News both report that Trump blamed Minnesota and its governor for the cyberattacks, while FOX News, The Daily Caller, and Al Jazeera also mention Trump's comments. However, ABC News and Straight Arrow News note that federal officials are investigating whether Iran or hackers associated with Iran could be behind the attacks, while FOX News and The Daily Caller report that U.S. and state officials told The New York Times that Iranian hackers were likely behind the cyberattack. Al Jazeera and Washington Examiner state that no culprit has been identified, and that the FBI is investigating. ABC News and Straight Arrow News also note that Minnesota officials said there was no indication that any water supply was unsafe to drink, while The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner mention that some localities requested residents to conserve water during the attack.

Sources ABC News · Straight Arrow News · FOX News · The Daily Caller · Al Jazeera · Washington Examiner

Town Declared ‘Abandoned’ After Every Official Resigns Over Water Crisis, Fraud Scandal

The Town of Hartman, Colorado, was declared abandoned by the state after its last elected officials resigned, leaving the community without a functioning government. Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold made the declaration in response to a resident’s petition seeking state intervention. The petition cited a lack of chlorine in the water for over a month, making it non-potable and smelling bad. Residents have been under a boil-water advisory since February. The town previously received $1 million in grants for water repairs, but the funds were unused due to the absence of authorized officials. The Prowers County Board of County Commissioners will now assume responsibility for the town’s roads and water.

The declaration followed years of political infighting, financial mismanagement, and a deteriorating water system. Former Mayor Catherine Ferenandez described the town’s internal conflicts as a “complete mess” due to family rivalries. Residents accused leaders of obstructing elections and neglecting infrastructure. A physical altercation during a board meeting in January led to the resignation of the remaining three trustees, leaving the town without governance. Hartman has about 30 residents, with over 93% living below the poverty line.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire mentions a resident’s petition that included a quote from the New York Post about the water conditions, while The Guardian cites public documents and a letter from resident Jessie Simmons to the secretary of state’s office. The Daily Wire states the petition was submitted to the New York Post, whereas The Guardian attributes the same information to public documents and Simmons’ letter. The Daily Wire also notes that the town had no board of trustees or town clerk, a detail The Guardian confirms. The Guardian adds that Hartman had a population of fewer than 60 at the 2020 census, while The Daily Wire does not specify a population figure. The Guardian includes a quote from resident Shawna Casey objecting to the abandonment, which The Daily Wire does not mention.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Guardian
