Charlie Kirk was shot and killed at Utah Valley University on 10 September 2025

Charlie Kirk, founder of Turning Point USA, was shot and killed while speaking at a campus event at Utah Valley University on 10 September 2025. The incident occurred during a public appearance where Kirk was addressing an audience of 3,000 people. According to The Guardian, the attacker was a young man who had hidden on a roof at the university. The Guardian describes the attack as a sudden and violent event that left Kirk critically injured. The incident led to a chaotic scene with the crowd screaming and fleeing, and Kirk’s friends and bodyguards rushing him to a hospital. The Free Press notes that the shooting was a pivotal moment that sparked a range of reactions, including calls for political unity and the emergence of conspiracy theories. Newsweek reports that Tyler Robinson, then 22, was arrested and faces the death penalty for the shooting.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and The Free Press differ in their descriptions of the immediate aftermath of the shooting. The Guardian states that Kirk’s chief of staff, Mikey McCoy, called Erika Kirk to inform her of the shooting, while The Free Press does not mention this detail. The Guardian also provides more specific information about the number of people in the audience and the nature of the attack, whereas The Free Press focuses more on the political implications and the role of Spencer Cox. The Daily Wire and Newsweek both mention Erika Kirk receiving advice from Usha Vance, but the Daily Wire provides a more detailed account of the conversation and its impact on Kirk’s grieving process. The Federalist and Newsweek differ in their portrayal of Kirk’s legacy and the political factions that have emerged in the wake of his death, with The Federalist emphasizing the loss of a key leader in the MAGA movement and Newsweek highlighting the split into two camps with differing views on Kirk’s legacy.

Sources The Guardian · The Free Press · The Daily Wire · The Federalist · NPR · Newsweek

RNC unveils midterm convention speaking order headlined by Trump and Vance

The Republican National Committee (RNC) announced the full list of speakers for its first-ever midterm convention in Dallas, set for Wednesday and Thursday. The event will feature President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance as keynote speakers. Each day will last six hours, with each hour dedicated to a different topic in Trump’s vision for America, intended to contrast Republican candidates with their Democratic competitors. The convention is expected to serve as a platform to rally GOP voters ahead of the November midterms.

The convention has been dubbed "Trumpapalooza" by NPR, with Trump set to headline both nights. Vance will speak on the first night, Wednesday. Organizers say the event will focus on key midterm issues such as lowering costs, boosting incomes, securing the border, and protecting American values. Fewer than half of the Republican candidates in competitive House and Senate races are on the speaker list, reflecting the complexity of the midterm elections.

AXIOS reported that Pennsylvania Sen. John Fetterman made a bombshell appearance at the GOP convention, praising Trump and Republican Sen. Dave McCormick in a video. Fetterman's appearance is seen as a dramatic escalation in his growing estrangement from the Democratic Party, with implications for his future political prospects.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and FOX News both mention Trump and Vance as keynote speakers, but FOX News adds that Trump made a "stunning first-of-its-kind cash promise" to issue a $5,000 dividend to every adult citizen if Republicans win Congress. The Examiner does not mention this promise. Additionally, CBS News reports that some key GOP candidates are skipping the convention, while the Examiner and FOX News do not mention this. The Examiner and FOX News describe the event as a convention, while CBS News notes it will resemble a political rally more than a typical convention.

Sources Washington Examiner · NPR · Axios · FOX News · The Daily Wire · CBS News

Anthropic Researcher Warns AI Could Kill All Humans by End of Decade

AI researcher Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic and warned that the development of increasingly powerful AI systems could pose an existential threat to humanity. Coxon, who previously worked at OpenAI, accused both companies of racing toward self-improving superintelligence without adequately addressing the risks. He claimed that many executives and researchers privately believe AI could kill all humans by the end of the decade. His warning was echoed by Evan Hubinger, an Anthropic employee, who stated that he personally believes there is a greater than 10% chance AI could cause human extinction within the next decade. Coxon argued that current AI models, while not yet capable of causing humanity to lose control, are moving toward systems that could act autonomously and maliciously.

The Free Press reported that Coxon’s warning was supported by former OpenAI researcher Daniel Kokotajlo, who warned that the competition between nations could lead to unsafe AI development. Kokotajlo suggested that sufficiently capable AI systems might acquire enough power that they no longer need to comply with human instructions, potentially leading to catastrophic outcomes. The Daily Caller and The Daily Wire also covered Coxon’s resignation, highlighting his concerns about the civilizational stakes involved in AI development and the risks of companies racing to develop superintelligent systems.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Coxon’s warning was supported by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, who commented on the rapid development of AI models. This is not mentioned in Newsweek, The Free Press, The Daily Wire, The Daily Caller, or BBC. Newsweek and The Daily Wire mention Daniel Kokotajlo’s warning, while Axios and BBC do not. The Free Press attributes Coxon’s warning to a former OpenAI researcher, while other accounts do not specify his prior employment. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller describe Coxon’s post as having 86 million views, a detail not included in Axios, Newsweek, or BBC. BBC notes that Coxon’s warning was in response to a post by Jacob Coxon, while other accounts do not mention this context.

Rhode Island governor projected to lose Democratic primary to former CVS executive

Rhode Island Governor Dan McKee faced a primary defeat to Helena Foulkes, a former CVS executive, in the state's Democratic gubernatorial race. The Associated Press projected Foulkes as the winner with 62.3% of the vote compared to McKee's 37.7%. The race was a rematch of the 2022 primary, where McKee narrowly won. Foulkes, who previously ran for governor in 2022, has campaigned on issues like housing affordability and healthcare access. McKee, who became governor in 2021 after Gina Raimondo left for the Biden administration, has faced challenges including the closure of the Washington Bridge, which was shut down in December 2023 due to structural issues. The bridge's reconstruction, initially expected to be completed by 2024, is now scheduled for November 2028.

The Washington Bridge has been a major issue in the race, with Foulkes criticizing McKee's handling of the project and McKee accusing Foulkes of being out-of-touch. The bridge, which carries about 100,000 vehicles daily, has been a point of contention in the primary, with both candidates blaming each other for delays and cost overruns. The primary result has implications for the November general election, where Foulkes is expected to be the Democratic nominee against either Aaron Guckian or Elaine Pelino, both Republicans.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News and The Guardian both report that McKee is facing a primary loss, but FOX News emphasizes the political implications of the loss, including McKee's potential to become the first sitting governor to lose renomination in a dozen years. The Guardian highlights the bridge issue as the main reason for McKee's defeat. CBS News and Just The News provide similar accounts of the primary results, with CBS News giving specific vote percentages and Just The News citing CBS News for the projection. The Nation focuses on the David Morales mayoral race in Providence, which is unrelated to the gubernatorial primary, and does not mention the bridge issue or McKee's loss. The New York Post states McKee is the first elected governor to lose a primary since 2014, a claim not made by other outlets.

Sources FOX News · CBS News · Just The News · The Guardian · The Nation · New York Post

Lindsay Clancy juror had reasonable doubt but would not agree to acquit, foreperson says

A juror in the trial of Lindsay Clancy, accused of murdering her three children, expressed reasonable doubt about her guilt but refused to acquit her, according to the jury’s foreperson. The foreperson, Roni Carlson, told NBC 10 Boston that the holdout juror admitted to having reasonable doubt but would not agree to a verdict of not guilty by reason of insanity. The jury deadlocked, leading to a mistrial. Clancy, who strangled her children in January 2023, remains in a psychiatric hospital. Prosecutors have not yet announced if they will retry her.

The trial centered on whether Clancy, who admitted to killing her children, was criminally responsible. Prosecutors argued she intentionally killed them, while the defense claimed she suffered from postpartum psychosis and could not be held accountable. Jurors described the evidence as showing Clancy loved her children, suggesting she had a breakdown. The holdout juror, who has not spoken publicly, was criticized by some jurors for not following the court’s instructions on reasonable doubt.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and BBC both report that the holdout juror admitted to having reasonable doubt but refused to acquit on insanity grounds. CBS News and the New York Post describe the holdout juror as someone who did not provide any evidence to support his position, with CBS News noting the juror was called “arrogant” by others. The Daily Wire characterizes the jurors as disregarding the law due to sympathy for Clancy, a claim not made by other outlets. The New York Post states the holdout juror did not provide a single piece of evidence, while the Guardian and BBC do not mention this detail. The Daily Wire also claims the jurors ignored the law due to sympathy, a characterization not present in other accounts.

Sources The Guardian · CBS News · BBC · FOX News · The Daily Wire · New York Post

Trump gave $45,000 to Natalie Harp and other White House aides as holiday gifts

According to financial disclosures released by the Trump administration, the president gave $45,000 in cash gifts to four White House aides, including Natalie Harp, Margo Martin, and Chamberlain Harris. Walt Nauta, director of Oval Office operations, received a $20,000 gift. The gifts were noted in the staffers’ annual financial disclosure forms. The White House stated the payments were personal holiday gifts and not related to their official duties, making them permissible under legal and ethical standards. The disclosures were first reported by The Washington Post.

The White House defended the gifts as a longstanding practice of Trump, who has given Christmas gifts to people in his orbit, including employees and aides, both in government and the private sector. The administration emphasized that the gifts were not tied to any official duties and were therefore legal. The Guardian and Al Jazeera include details about Harp’s role in printing favorable media coverage for Trump, which is not mentioned in the other accounts.

Trump praises far-right German AfD party after election win: ‘THEY ARE ON THE RISE’

President Donald Trump celebrated the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party Tuesday after it scored a landslide victory in a regional election, praising the party’s rise and tying its success to voter frustration over immigration. Trump posted on Truth Social, calling the AfD’s victory a “really big night” and claiming the party’s success was due to voters being “tired of the absolutely horrible Immigration rules and regulations which have hurt Germany so badly.” The AfD won 44% of the vote in Saxony-Anhalt, making it the first far-right party to potentially lead a state government since World War II.

The AfD’s victory has shocked Germany’s political establishment, with Chancellor Friedrich Merz calling the result a “severe defeat” for his center-right Christian Democratic Union. The party’s rise has been attributed to its anti-immigration stance and its challenge to Germany’s postwar culture of Holocaust remembrance. AfD leaders have been accused of making anti-Muslim comments and comparing the Nazi era to a “speck of bird poop” in German history.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and Axios both report Trump praised the AfD’s victory and linked it to voter frustration over immigration. The Washington Examiner does not mention Trump’s use of the acronym “MGGA” or the historical context of the term. UK airport disruption: technical failure causes widespread flight delays and cancellations

A technical failure in the UK's air traffic control system caused significant disruption at major airports including Manchester, Birmingham, and all London airports on Tuesday. The issue, attributed to a problem in the flight processing system of Nats, led to flight delays and cancellations. While Nats fixed the problem within hours, the fallout continued into Wednesday, with over 2,000 cancellations reported. Airlines are advising passengers to check with their carriers for the latest flight information and offering rebooking options or refunds. Heathrow and Gatwick airports confirmed that flights had resumed but warned of ongoing disruptions.

The disruption affected hundreds of thousands of passengers, with at least 1,000 flights cancelled by Tuesday evening and 177 more scheduled for Wednesday. The incident is the third major stoppage in under three years, with Nats stating it was a different issue from previous outages. Airlines such as Ryanair and easyJet reported significant delays and cancellations, with some flights held at foreign airports. British Airways noted the severity of the issue would have a significant knock-on impact for customers and staff.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that the total number of cancellations has passed 2,000 after hundreds more flights were axed, while the Associated Press states that over 1,750 flights were canceled. The Guardian also mentions that the disruption is the third major stoppage in just over three years, whereas the Associated Press refers to it as the second major outage in three years. The Guardian notes that Nats claims the fault was not the same as those causing outages in 2023 and 2025, a detail not included in the Associated Press account. The Guardian also includes a quote from Rory Boland, editor of Which? Travel, which is absent in the Associated Press report.

Sources The Guardian · Associated Press
