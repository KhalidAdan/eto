Bernie Sanders Is Leaving It All on the Field in the Final Days of the Midterms

Bernie Sanders is actively campaigning in Wisconsin to support Democratic congressional candidate Rebecca Cooke, emphasizing the importance of holding President Donald Trump accountable. Sanders has been making a nationwide effort to influence the midterm elections, which he views as a critical opportunity to transform American politics. The senator has been traveling across the country, engaging with voters and activists, and has been a consistent presence in the campaign trail, focusing on issues such as economic inequality and opposition to wealthy elites.

The midterms are shaping up to be a pivotal moment for both parties, with Democrats leading in national polls but facing tight races in key Senate contests. Polls suggest Democrats have a slight edge, but several Senate races remain closely contested, with outcomes potentially determining control of the Senate. The campaigns are centered on economic issues, with Democrats highlighting affordability concerns and Republicans struggling to present a compelling message.

Where the accounts differ

The Nation reports Sanders is focusing on holding Trump accountable and supporting Rebecca Cooke, while Al Jazeera and Newsweek provide broader context on national polling and the potential outcomes of the midterms. The Hill highlights concerns from Senator Ted Cruz about the GOP's chances, noting the closeness of several Senate races. The Washington Examiner and Mother Jones focus on the Michigan Senate race, with the latter detailing the internal Democratic conflict involving Haley Stevens and Abdul El-Sayed. The Nation and Al Jazeera differ in their emphasis, with The Nation focusing on Sanders' campaign and Al Jazeera on national polling and the impact of the war on Iran.

North Korea linked to $6.3B Binance hack

Blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis have linked the $6.3 billion Binance hack to a North Korean hacking unit known as TraderTraitor. The attack, which occurred on October 29, 2026, saw approximately $6.36 billion in assets moved out of Binance wallets across multiple blockchain networks. Binance suspended all deposits and withdrawals, and industry partners froze about $3 billion in assets. The company has reserved over $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the losses. Binance co-CEO Richard Teng stated the company is working with global law enforcement to seek justice and assured users that their funds would be covered.

The FBI has confirmed that North Korea was behind the theft, identifying the group as TraderTraitor. The bureau noted that the stolen assets were primarily converted into Ethereum and warned of further laundering through DeFi and instant swap services. The FBI also released the internet addresses of those involved in the alleged laundering and urged the crypto industry to block their transactions.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and NPR both state that TRM Labs and Chainalysis linked the hack to TraderTraitor, while The Guardian and BBC attribute the hack to North Korea without naming the specific unit. The Guardian adds context about North Korea's use of crypto hacking to fund its nuclear program, which is not mentioned in the other accounts. The BBC notes that the FBI is working with Binance and the US Secret Service, while The Guardian and NPR do not mention the FBI's involvement. Fox Business and The Hill mention that Binance suspended withdrawals, while The Guardian and BBC state that deposits remained unaffected. The Guardian also includes details about North Korea's nuclear program and its history of crypto hacking, which are absent in the other accounts.

Sources The Hill · BBC · Just The News · Fox Business · The Guardian · NPR

Judge tosses Trump lawsuit against WSJ, Murdoch over Epstein story

A federal judge in Florida dismissed President Trump's defamation lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal and Rupert Murdoch over a 2003 story about a letter allegedly written by Trump for Jeffrey Epstein's birthday. The ruling, issued by Judge Darrin Gayles, found that Trump failed to meet the legal standard of "actual malice" required for a defamation claim against a public figure. The judge cited the Supreme Court's 1964 decision in New York Times Co. v. Sullivan, which established that public figures must prove media outlets acted with knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for the truth. Trump had previously filed a similar lawsuit in April 2026, which was also dismissed, but allowed him to refile. The current case was dismissed with prejudice, meaning it cannot be refiled.

The lawsuit centered on a WSJ article that described a sexually suggestive letter bearing Trump's name, included in a 2003 birthday album compiled for Epstein. Trump denied writing the letter and called the story "fake," seeking $20 billion in damages. The judge noted that the article included Trump's denials and described the paper's efforts to verify the claims, including outreach to Trump, officials, and others involved in the album.

Where the accounts differ

Axios and CBS News both state that Trump sought $20 billion in damages, while NPR and Newsweek mention $10 billion. BBC and Straight Arrow News report $10 billion. Axios and CBS News attribute the claim that the article included Trump's denials to the judge's ruling, while NPR and BBC attribute it to the article itself. Newsweek and Straight Arrow News mention the letter was part of a birthday album compiled by Ghislaine Maxwell, which is not explicitly stated in Axios, CBS News, or BBC. NPR and BBC describe the letter's content in more detail, including the drawing of a naked woman, which is not included in the other accounts.

Sources Axios · CBS News · NPR · Newsweek · BBC · Straight Arrow News

Judge opens inquiry into Trump administration’s handling of East Potomac golf course case

A federal judge, Ana Reyes, has launched an investigation into whether Trump administration officials misled the court and violated professional ethics rules during the legal battle over the East Potomac Golf Links. The inquiry follows the administration’s admission that it plans to close the golf course for renovations in November. The judge is examining whether Interior Department officials, government attorneys, and Justice Department lawyers acted in bad faith. The case involves a lawsuit filed by the DC Preservation League and others to prevent the closure of the historic golf course.

The legal challenges to Trump’s proposed memorial arch are intensifying, with the District of Columbia and Virginia filing a lawsuit to block construction. The arch, which would be 250 feet tall, is located near a major traffic artery and could disrupt emergency services. The lawsuit argues that the project would cause traffic snarls, pollution, and other issues. A separate federal judge, Tanya Chutkan, is considering whether to halt the project until the original lawsuit is resolved.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and BBC both report that Judge Ana Reyes is investigating whether the Trump administration misled the court about the golf course renovations. The Washington Examiner states that the inquiry is into whether officials violated professional ethics rules, while the BBC adds that Reyes is looking into whether the administration gave late notice to the court about the renovation plans in bad faith. The Guardian and Axios report that D.C. and Virginia have sued to block the construction of the arch, citing traffic and environmental concerns. The Guardian provides specific details about the arch’s height and its potential impact on emergency services, which are not included in the other accounts. The NPR and ABC News accounts describe the judge’s questioning of government lawyers and her demand for transparency regarding the renovation plans, but differ in the specifics of the legal arguments and the timeline of events.

Police clash with masked rioters after largely peaceful protests across France

Tens of thousands of demonstrators marched across France on Saturday in a third wave of protests over the cost of living and deteriorating public services. The interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, reported that about 150 planned demonstrations passed off peacefully, though police encountered an “extraordinary” level of violence from some rioters as the rallies ended. In Paris, a riot police officer was hit by a molotov cocktail, described as an “attempted murder.” About 320,000 people took part in rallies nationwide, including 70,000 in Paris, which marched along boulevards on the south-eastern outskirts, avoiding the city centre. As evening approached, police in several cities fired teargas and stun grenades to disperse crowds joined by groups of masked rioters. The interior ministry said late Saturday that police had arrested 565 people across the country, with four police officers and one gendarme injured in Paris.

Protesters, including students, trade unions, and gilets jaune (yellow vests) protesters, united in anger at the cost of living, widening inequalities, and Emmanuel Macron’s government. Some protesters wore Halloween costumes, with slogans like “No need for teargas to make our future look bleak” and “Disguised as an angry citizen for Halloween.” In Lyon, protesters threw projectiles at police, prompting officers to fire teargas, and in Toulouse, police used water cannon against an unauthorized protest. The marches brought together school students, who have been calling for better conditions for a month, trade unions, and gilets jaune protesters.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that about 320,000 people took part in rallies nationwide, including 70,000 in Paris, while CBS News states that about 70,000 people demonstrated in Paris alone and France's interior ministry said about 320,000 people rallied nationwide. The Guardian also mentions that about 565 people were arrested nationwide, while CBS News reports more than 560 arrests. The Guardian notes that a riot police officer was hit by a molotov cocktail in Paris, while FOX News reports that a Molotov cocktail was thrown at a group of riot police in Paris. The Guardian and CBS News both mention that in Lyon, protesters threw projectiles at police, prompting officers to fire teargas, while NPR adds that clashes broke out near Porte de Charenton, where the march ended, with protesters hurling glass bottles and fireworks at police.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · BBC · FOX News · CBS News

1 dead, 6 injured in shooting at California Halloween store, police say

A shooting occurred at a Spirit Halloween store inside the Solano Town Center in Fairfield, California, on Friday night. Police confirmed that one person was killed and six others were injured. The shooting is believed to have stemmed from a targeted incident involving one suspect inside the store. Seven people were found with gunshot wounds, with one person dying from their injuries. The Fairfield Police Department responded to the shooting around 8:30 p.m. local time. Constance Hages, a 31-year-old female, was identified as the victim who died, and family members told police she was pregnant at the time of her death. Several victims remain in critical condition, including a 16-year-old male, a 19-year-old male, a 32-year-old male, and a 23-year-old male. Two additional victims, a 19-year-old male and a 43-year-old male, were treated for gunshot wounds and later released from the hospital.

Police are reviewing surveillance footage and other evidence to identify the suspect. The Fairfield Police Department encouraged the public to remain vigilant and report any suspicious activity during Halloween celebrations. Chief of Police Dan Marshall urged the community to provide information to aid the investigation, noting that the mall has cameras that may help.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and The Daily Wire both state that Constance Hages was pregnant at the time of her death, as confirmed by family members. FOX News and The Daily Wire note that the pregnancy has not been confirmed by police, with the Solano County Coroner expected to determine this. Newsweek and The Daily Wire mention that the shooting is believed to have stemmed from a targeted incident, while FOX News and The Daily Wire also state that the motive has not been revealed. New York Post and Straight Arrow News do not mention the pregnancy or the motive, and New York Post does not specify the ages of the victims beyond critical condition. The Daily Wire and ABC News mention that the suspect is still at large, while New York Post and Straight Arrow News do not confirm the number of suspects.

US tech stocks see sharp falls as AI spending nears $1tn

Tech stocks tumbled for the second day in a row as concerns rise over the hundreds of billions of dollars firms are investing in artificial intelligence (AI). Shares in Intel plummeted by 28% on Friday, while Google owner Alphabet saw its stock price continue to fall another 1.4% after a 12% plunge on Thursday. Other tech stocks, including Reddit and Amazon also fell, while the Nasdaq index of the largest tech firms listed in the US ended October with its worst monthly slide since March 2025. While tech firms are spending big on AI, their earnings are not always meeting analysts' expectations, spooking investors and creating fears of an AI bubble. Major tech companies have ramped up artificial intelligence spending, with Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, Amazon, and Microsoft committing to spending $800bn between them in 2026. Alphabet's stock market value fell sharply on Thursday, taking more than $500bn off its market value after it revealed the AI summaries it now provides as the top answer to search queries were affecting its core search advertising revenue. At the same time, Alphabet expects to spend up to $205bn this year on investments, almost entirely related to AI. Amazon on Thursday revealed it had also spent more cash than it was making, and that spending had increased six-fold over the span of three months. The company has committed to spending $220bn this year, putting the money towards investments such as AI data centres, network equipment, and servers.

Where the accounts differ

Axios does not mention this statement. BBC also states that Reddit stock plunged by 29% after the platform revealed weaker-than-expected daily user figures and warned that Google's growing AI-driven search was hurting referral traffic, while Straight Arrow News does not mention Reddit's stock performance. Axios reports that Amazon's net income fell despite soaring operating profits, as it did not repeat the pretax gain from its Anthropic investment that boosted earnings a year earlier, while BBC does not mention this detail. Straight Arrow News does not report on stock price movements or financial figures, instead focusing on public sentiment and fear around AI.

Sources BBC · Axios · Straight Arrow News

Pope Leo XIV becomes first pontiff to vote in a US election

Pope Leo XIV, an American citizen, submitted a mail-in ballot in Illinois for the 2026 midterm elections, becoming the first pope to vote in a US election. The Vatican confirmed the action, stating that the pontiff has exercised his right to vote as a United States citizen whenever eligible. The US midterm elections, held on 3 November, will determine which party controls Congress and have significant implications for the final years of Donald Trump's presidency. Pope Leo, born in Chicago, has lived in Peru and has consistently participated in US elections when eligible, according to the Vatican.

The pope's ballot includes several key races, including the reelection of Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, the race to succeed Senator Dick Durbin, and a House contest between Democratic Rep. Jonathan Jackson and Republican Christian Maxwell. The Vatican also noted that Pope Leo has voted in both Democratic and Republican primaries over the years, with his party affiliation currently undeclared. He requested his mail-in ballot from the Will County Clerk's Office on 3 October, just days before undergoing surgery to remove a lower lobe of his right lung due to lung cancer.

Ebola death toll in DR Congo passes 5,000

The death toll from Ebola in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has surpassed 5,000, according to government data. The outbreak, which began in May, has infected more than 10,000 people and spread to eight of the DRC’s 26 provinces. The Bundibugyo strain of the virus, which has no approved treatments or vaccines, is responsible for the outbreak. The virus has also spread to neighboring countries, including Kenya, Uganda, and Nigeria.

The outbreak, now the deadliest in DRC’s history, has a case fatality rate of 48.6%. Public health officials have identified the Ituri province as the epicenter, a conflict zone that complicates containment efforts. The World Health Organization (WHO) has called for increased testing, treatment, and community outreach to control the outbreak. The UN has noted that this outbreak is the second-largest in history, following the 2014-2016 epidemic in West Africa.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that Nigeria became the fourth African country to report an Ebola case, extending the outbreak’s geographic spread to West Africa. BBC states that two suspected cases were identified in Nigeria on Saturday, with individuals isolated and undergoing testing. Al Jazeera mentions that Saudi Arabia suspended travel to and entry from Kenya, while BBC notes that travel restrictions remain in place for those crossing into Kenya from the DRC. Al Jazeera does not mention the case fatality rate or the UN’s warning about the outbreak’s speed, which BBC includes.

Sources Al Jazeera · BBC
