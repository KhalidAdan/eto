Macron says violent rioters will be ‘prosecuted and punished’ as buildings burn in France

French President Emmanuel Macron denounced violent rioters who attacked law enforcement and damaged national landmarks during escalating protests. The unrest, which began as student-led demonstrations over education policies, has expanded to include public sector workers and unions. Protests across France saw clashes between protesters and police, with some demonstrations turning violent as buildings were set ablaze and shops looted. The violence has drawn condemnation from government officials, who have called for accountability.

The protests, which began last month at schools in Paris, have grown into a nationwide movement against inequality, weak public spending, and rising living costs. Demonstrators have called for higher wages, better pensions, and lower prices. While most marches were peaceful, violent groups targeted public buildings, luxury boutiques, and supermarkets, attacking police vehicles and officers. The government has reported hundreds of arrests and injuries to police officers.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and BBC both report that Macron condemned violent rioters and called for prosecution, but the Washington Examiner omits the specific details about the scale of the violence and the number of arrests. The Guardian and CBS News provide more specific numbers, with the Guardian reporting 1,200 arrests initially and later 1,800, while CBS News states 855 arrests. The Guardian also mentions the damage to the Limoges town hall, including the collapse of the roof, which CBS News and BBC also report. The Guardian and BBC both mention the involvement of the gilets jaunes movement, while the Washington Examiner does not. The FOX News account attributes the violence to "radical far-left groups" and mentions Macron's comments about political exploitation, which are not included in the other accounts. The NPR account includes quotes from protesters and union members, which are absent in the other accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · The Guardian · NPR · CBS News · FOX News · BBC

GOP Senate candidate Mike Rogers breaks with Trump over Russia sanctions — as retiring lawmakers lash president

Republican Michigan Senate hopeful Mike Rogers criticized President Trump over his decision to pause sanctions on Russia, arguing that the administration should enforce the bipartisan sanctions legislation. Rogers, who has generally aligned with Trump on policy, expressed skepticism that waiving the sanctions would lower diesel prices in the U.S. He also rejected claims that Trump is siding with Russia in its war against Ukraine, instead framing the move as an effort to address energy costs and confront Iran. Other Republicans, including retiring Senators, have also publicly challenged Trump’s decision to waive the Russia sanctions.

President Trump announced Friday that he was waiving sanctions under the Graham Act, a bipartisan measure aimed at punishing Russia and countries that buy its oil and gas. The legislation, named after the late Sen. Lindsey Graham, authorized tariffs of up to 100% on major importers of Russian petroleum products. Trump cited “national interests of the United States” as the rationale for the waiver, which drew criticism from lawmakers in both parties who accused the administration of giving Moscow a break.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and Axios both report that Trump waived sanctions under the Graham Act, while The Hill clarifies that no sanctions were lifted, only the diesel embargo. The New York Post and BBC describe Trump’s rationale as focusing on energy prices and negotiations with Russia, whereas Axios and The Hill note that Trump’s aides offered conflicting explanations for the move. The BBC and NPR report that Zelenskyy criticized the waiver as helping Russia in its war against Ukraine, while the New York Post and Straight Arrow News attribute similar statements to Zelenskyy. The Straight Arrow News and NPR also report that Zelenskyy described the attack on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure as one of the country’s largest, while the BBC and Axios provide more detail on the nature of the attack.

Sources New York Post · NPR · Straight Arrow News · BBC · Axios · The Hill

Trump Plows Forward With Plan to Build the Golf Course of His Dreams in DC

President Donald Trump announced plans to shut down the East Potomac Golf Links in Washington, DC, for 15 months to renovate it into a facility capable of hosting international golf tournaments. The project, which he described as “one of the Best and Most Spectacular Golf Courses anywhere in the World,” would begin on November 15. Trump did not specify the cost or funding for the project. The National Park Service had previously started cutting down hundreds of trees at the course, citing a 2019 Cultural Landscape Report to restore the site. However, a federal judge, Ana C. Reyes, issued a temporary restraining order halting further tree removal, citing the government’s late notice and what she called “raced to undertake” actions due to Trump’s demands for haste. The Justice Department has requested that Reyes be removed from the case, accusing her of “bias.”

The renovation has faced legal challenges, with plaintiffs arguing that the project would alter the character of the space and make it less affordable to the public. Democracy Forward, representing the plaintiffs, stated that the course is part of a national park and recreation facility meant for everyone. The Trump administration has also faced criticism for removing over 400 trees from the site, which was part of a 2019 plan predating Trump’s ambitions for the course. The next hearing in the case is scheduled for Monday.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Guardian both note that the National Park Service began cutting trees in accordance with a 2019 Cultural Landscape Report, while FOX News and the New York Post describe the tree removal as part of a 2019 plan that predates Trump’s ambitions. The Guardian and NPR mention that the Trump administration asked Judge Reyes to dismiss the litigation, stating the project was at an early conceptual stage, while the New York Post and Washington Examiner do not mention this request. The Guardian and FOX News report that Trump did not acknowledge the legal fight in his post, while Mother Jones and NPR note that Trump made no mention of the legal battle in his Truth Social post. The New York Post and FOX News include details about the number of trees cut down (430) and the timing of the tree removal (just before Columbus Day weekend), which are not mentioned in the other accounts.

Sources Mother Jones · The Guardian · NPR · Washington Examiner · FOX News · New York Post

Kansas driver who allegedly killed two and hurt 10 at soccer game is released from custody

A woman arrested after driving a truck through a youth soccer game in Wichita, Kansas, has been released from custody without charges. The incident occurred on Saturday afternoon at the Stryker Sports Complex, where the driver struck and killed a 6-year-old boy and a 35-year-old man. Ten others were injured, with five of them being children. The driver, identified as a 34-year-old woman from Garden City, Kansas, was in police custody but later released pending toxicology and mechanical examination results. Authorities have not confirmed whether the crash was intentional or due to any medical condition or impairment.

The driver had two of her children in the truck at the time of the incident, and a third child was playing soccer elsewhere at the complex. Emergency responders arrived at the scene shortly after 3:25 p.m. CT, where bystanders had already begun providing aid to the injured. The victims included several children and adults, with injuries described as traumatic but not life-threatening. The McPherson City FC youth soccer club expressed deep sorrow over the deaths of two of its members, calling the incident an "unimaginable tragedy."

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and FOX News both mention that the driver had two children in the truck, while Straight Arrow News and CBS News refer to them as "juveniles." The Guardian and FOX News state that the driver was released pending toxicology and mechanical examination results, while The Daily Wire and ABC News mention that the driver was released after consulting with the district attorney’s office. The Guardian and FOX News provide specific names of the victims, Cole Adrian and Nicholas Schafer, while The Daily Wire and ABC News also identify them by name. The Guardian and FOX News note that the driver had a valid license and no outstanding warrants, while The Daily Wire and ABC News do not mention this detail.

Sources The Guardian · ABC News · CBS News · Straight Arrow News · FOX News · The Daily Wire

Massachusetts GOP candidate Shawn Oliver arrested, suspends campaign

Shawn Oliver, a New Bedford City Council member and Republican candidate for Massachusetts lieutenant governor, was arrested and suspended his campaign following a late-night crash. The incident occurred around 11:40 p.m. on Dartmouth Street in New Bedford. Police reported that Oliver's SUV flipped over and collided with multiple parked cars, involving five vehicles. Two of these vehicles sustained extensive damage. Oliver was taken to St. Luke’s Hospital for treatment and later arrested on charges including operating under the influence of liquor, negligent operation of a motor vehicle, marked lanes violation, and speeding. Oliver suspended his campaign, stating the election is too important to be overshadowed by personal matters. Republican gubernatorial candidate Mike Minogue called for Oliver to suspend his campaign, expressing disappointment and emphasizing the need for accountability.

Zelensky says Musk-owned satellite system still restricted in Ukraine

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky stated that Ukraine's use of the Elon Musk-owned SpaceX satellite system remains partially restricted, limiting the military's ability to use drones for intercepting Russian attack drones and sending supplies to front-line soldiers. Zelensky noted that the restrictions appear to apply only within Ukraine, as the system functions normally in European Union countries. He expressed skepticism about SpaceX's claim that the speed limit results from technical problems, suggesting instead that the restrictions may stem from a political decision by the Trump administration. Zelensky also mentioned ongoing discussions with U.S. officials, including Jared Kushner, to resolve the issue.

U.S. officials, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio and President Trump, have denied any involvement in restricting Ukraine's access to the Starshield system, which is controlled by the U.S. government. Trump claimed the service is fully operational, while Rubio stated that Ukraine has not had access to Starshield and that the system is a military product not available to Ukraine. Ukrainian officials, however, report that the system's speed limit has been reduced to 161 kilometers per hour, affecting drone operations within Ukraine.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that Ukrainian officials are still determining whether the restrictions result from a political decision by the Trump administration or Musk, with conflicting claims from both sides. The New York Post notes that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent criticized Zelensky for publicly accusing the Trump administration of throttling Starshield, while Axios and CBS News describe Bessent's comments. The Hill and CBS News both mention Rep. Mike Turner calling for an investigation into the loss of Starlink access, though The Hill specifies that the speed limit was 100 mph, while Axios and CBS News report 161 km/h. The Daily Caller and The Hill both mention Turner's call for an investigation, but The Daily Caller adds that Turner cited reports from the Institute for the Study of War and Zelensky as evidence of spotty service.

Trump endorses Netanyahu ahead of Israel’s elections, warns US will have to act alone if Israel fails

President Donald Trump publicly endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Sunday, urging Israeli voters to support him in the Oct. 27 Knesset elections. Trump called Netanyahu a "Great Warrior Prime Minister" and a "Partner for Peace" in the fight against Iran, warning that if Israel selects a leader other than Netanyahu, the U.S. would have to "finish the job" against Iran alone. Netanyahu responded by thanking Trump for his support and stating that "Together, we will complete the mission."

The endorsement comes amid a closely contested election where Netanyahu’s Likud Party is projected to secure between 18–22 seats, while a Netanyahu-aligned right-religious bloc is expected to win around 49–51 seats — short of a majority. The Israeli elections will determine the next prime minister through a proportional representation system, with the Knesset’s 120 seats distributed among parties that clear the 3.25% threshold. Trump’s endorsement is seen as a significant boost for Netanyahu, who faces challenges from within Israel, including his ongoing corruption trial and public backlash over the handling of the Oct. 7, 2023 attack.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News and The Daily Wire both report that Trump called Netanyahu a "Great Warrior Prime Minister" and a "Partner for Peace," and that Netanyahu responded by thanking Trump for his support. However, FOX News includes a quote from Netanyahu stating, "Thank you, President Trump, for the tremendous support and the joint work," while The Daily Wire attributes the same statement to Netanyahu in a Hebrew post on X. The Daily Wire also includes a quote from Netanyahu saying, "Together we will complete the mission," which is not present in FOX News.

The New York Post and Axios both mention that Trump’s endorsement was made nine days before the election, but the New York Post adds that Netanyahu’s Likud Party is running behind Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar Party in several polls, while Axios notes that neither party is expected to secure the 61 seats needed for a majority. The New York Post also includes details about Netanyahu’s declining popularity in Israel due to the conflicts in Gaza, Lebanon, and Iran, as well as his corruption trial and backlash over the Oct. 7 attack, which are not mentioned in Axios.

World Food Programme cuts aid to Gaza due to funding shortages

The World Food Programme (WFP) has announced it is forced to drastically reduce lifesaving assistance to Gaza due to funding shortages. The number of people helped by the UN agency will drop by about a third, from 1.6 million to 1.1 million. The cuts will hit as winter approaches, when many families in Gaza are already struggling to feed themselves. The number of hot meals provided by community kitchens will be cut from 250,000 to 150,000 each day. Families in hard-to-reach areas will receive a meal every other day instead of daily. Nearly all of Gaza's 2.1 million population remains displaced, with two-thirds relying on food aid to survive.

The WFP acting executive director, Carl Skau, described life in Gaza as "absolutely brutal," noting that basic services are lacking, there is massive destruction, and insecurity continues. He reported that the WFP has been reaching almost every person in Gaza for the past few months, but progress is fragile and could be reversed. Skau also mentioned that the WFP is cutting assistance because funding is no longer there, not because needs have decreased or access is limited.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that the WFP is cutting assistance due to funding shortages, and that the number of people helped will drop from 1.6 million to 1.1 million. It also mentions that the WFP is cutting almost half a million people off assistance in the next few weeks. BBC states that the number of people helped will drop by about a third, from 1.6 million to 1.1 million, and that the number of hot meals provided will be cut from 250,000 to 150,000 each day. NPR does not mention the reduction in hot meals, while BBC does. NPR also states that the WFP is cutting assistance because funding is no longer there, not because needs have decreased or access is limited. BBC states that the cuts are being made because of finances, not because the needs are coming down or because the WFP doesn't have access.

Sources NPR · BBC
