Chris Van Hollen to challenge Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader

Democratic Senator Chris Van Hollen announced on Thursday that he will challenge Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader. Van Hollen argued that the American people called for change from the status quo that isn't working for working people. Schumer responded by stating he has the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader. The leadership race is set for a secret ballot in December, with Van Hollen's bid highlighting growing divisions within the Democratic Party.

Van Hollen's challenge comes amid a broader push for change within the party, with progressive Democrats arguing that Schumer's leadership has failed to address key issues like affordability and war. The party's midterm election success has led to calls for new leadership, with some senators-elect, including Abdul El-Sayed, publicly rejecting Schumer's leadership. The race is expected to be closely contested, with Van Hollen claiming he has more than 10 senators on his side.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that Democrats need to change leadership to deliver on an agenda focused on affordability and change, and that Schumer should step aside for younger members. It attributes calls for change to voters and criticizes the party's ties to donors and groups like AIPAC. The article does not mention Van Hollen's challenge or any leadership election.

The Guardian and Washington Examiner both report that Van Hollen announced his bid to challenge Schumer for Senate majority leader, with the Guardian noting that Schumer responded by stating he has the votes to continue as leader. The Guardian also mentions that the race will be the first contested election for Democratic leader in decades, while the Washington Examiner emphasizes Van Hollen's call for new leadership to address an economy rigged against working people.

Brooklyn building collapse kills at least two children and six adults, injuring 18

A three-storey residential building in Brooklyn, New York, collapsed into an adjacent construction site on Wednesday, killing at least eight people and injuring 18. Rescue efforts continued through Thursday as crews searched for survivors. The FDNY reported that six people were found dead at the scene, with two more dying later from their injuries. Among the deceased were two children and four construction workers from a nearby site. The collapse occurred around 4pm on Flatbush Avenue, and the search-and-rescue operation involved over 250 first responders. Officials described the conditions as extremely dangerous, with heavy rain expected to complicate efforts. The building, which housed apartments, was partially destroyed, exposing interior spaces like bedrooms and wardrobes. The New York City mayor, Zohran Mamdani, expressed heartbreak over the tragedy and urged the public to stay away from the site.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and CBS News both report that the death toll rose to eight, while FOX News and Just The News state at least two people were killed initially, with the final toll not confirmed until Thursday. The Guardian and CBS News mention that two children were among the deceased, while FOX News and Just The News do not specify the ages of the victims. The Guardian and CBS News report that six people were found dead at the scene, with two more dying later, while FOX News and Just The News do not provide this detail. The Guardian and CBS News state that four firefighters were injured, while FOX News and Just The News report that one firefighter was injured. The Guardian and CBS News mention that the building housed a number of apartments, while FOX News and Just The News do not specify the building's use. The Guardian and CBS News report that the FDNY completed its search by 3:37pm on Thursday, while FOX News and Just The News do not provide this time.

Pete Hegseth’s Crew Got Chewed Out at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and his entourage were reprimanded by a military sentinel at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier during a Veterans Day ceremony at Arlington National Cemetery. The incident occurred after Hegseth and his staff, including his wife Jennifer and several aides, posed for photos in front of the tomb. Loud laughter and chatter from the group prompted the sentinel to issue a sharp rebuke, shouting, “All visitors will maintain an atmosphere of silence and respect at all times.” Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell confirmed the event, stating the department honors the sacrifices of military personnel. The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, a sacred war memorial, has been guarded by the 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment, known as the Old Guard, since 1948. The sentinel’s role includes maintaining decorum and preventing disrespectful behavior at the site.

Hegseth later awarded a medal to the soldier who reprimanded his group. According to CBS News and the New York Post, Hegseth posted photos of himself pinning a Joint Service Commendation Medal to Specialist Thomas Iglesias, a member of the Old Guard. The Pentagon confirmed the award, noting Iglesias was recognized for his duties at the tomb. The incident has drawn attention to Hegseth’s recent survival of impeachment proceedings and his controversial policies regarding military personnel.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and The Hill report that Hegseth was accompanied by his wife and senior staff, while FOX News and the New York Post specify the names of some aides present, including Ricky Buria, Patrick Weaver, and Tami Radabaugh. CBS News and the New York Post mention that Hegseth awarded a medal to Iglesias, while Mother Jones and The Hill do not mention any award. The Hill and FOX News include details about Trump’s presence and his reaction to the incident, which are omitted in Mother Jones and CBS News. The New York Post and CBS News attribute the award to Hegseth, while Mother Jones and The Hill do not specify who awarded the medal.

US doctor kills building manager after long-running dispute over mould, police say

A doctor who had been in an ongoing dispute over mould and other issues at his Washington DC apartment fatally shot his building's manager and fired at four others before killing himself, police say. Building manager James Stuart, 46, was pronounced dead at the scene, while four others were rushed to local hospitals for treatment. The shooting began on Thursday morning local time inside a gym in an apartment building in the Logan Circle neighbourhood, which is about a mile (1.6km) north of the White House. Police said the suspect, identified as 55-year-old doctor Matt Wade Lambert, then barricaded himself in an apartment, and later died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound. While the suspect was barricaded, "he did fire through the door at officers at least on two occasions", Jefferey Carroll, the interim chief of the Metropolitan Police Department, said at a news conference.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and Axios do not mention the lawsuit. FOX News also states that Lambert severed a natural gas line to a fireplace and sparked a fire, which was contained by the building’s sprinkler system, while BBC and The Daily Wire do not mention this. The Washington Examiner and New York Post both identify the victim as James Stuart, the building manager, but the New York Post adds that Lambert had a feud over black mold in his $1.1 million apartment. The Daily Wire and Axios do not specify the nature of the dispute beyond a "conflict" with the homeowners association.

White House Warns Democrats of Criminal Investigations for Investigating Trump

The White House has issued a warning to incoming House Oversight Committee Chair Rep. Robert Garcia (D-Calif.) that discussing investigations into the Trump administration could be considered criminal. White House Counsel Will Scharf wrote to Garcia, stating that Congress must have a legitimate legislative purpose for its investigative acts, or it constitutes an abuse of power. Scharf cited social media posts from lawmakers, including Rep. Analilia Mejia, who tweeted, “We’re going to toss out subpoenas like Mardi Gras beads.” The letter echoes President Trump’s claims that Democrats are engaging in “extortion at the highest level” and should be held accountable. In response, Garcia stated that Congress’ authority comes from Article I of the Constitution, not from the permission of the White House.

Attorney General Todd Blanche clarified that the Justice Department has no active criminal investigations into Democratic lawmakers’ remarks about their plans to scrutinize the Trump administration. Blanche said he has not seen any statements that crossed a criminal line. Meanwhile, Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer warned that focusing too much on corruption could distract lawmakers from delivering on what Americans voted for.

Where the accounts differ

However, Mother Jones includes specific quotes from Garcia and Mejia, while NPR attributes the quotes to social media posts and does not include the exact wording. The New York Post and The Daily Wire also report that Sen. Richard Blumenthal is targeting donors to the White House ballroom, a detail not included in the other accounts.

Microsoft Outage Disrupts Email, Workplace Apps

A Microsoft 365 outage disrupted access to email and workplace applications Thursday as the company worked to restore service. Outage tracker Downdetector recorded nearly 130,000 reports of Outlook problems Thursday afternoon. Thousands of reports also involved Teams, Copilot, and other Microsoft 365 services. Microsoft was still working to restore affected servers just after 4:30 p.m. ET, when it said it was also considering reversing recent configuration updates to speed recovery. The company acknowledged problems accessing Exchange Online, its cloud email service, shortly before 1 p.m. ET.

The outage impacted a wide range of services, including Outlook, Teams, and Copilot, with reports from users in 74 countries. The disruption continued into Friday, with some services still experiencing issues. Microsoft said it was working to improve server stability and traffic flow, but service health remained variable. The company also mentioned that it was extending the "email timeout window" to 48 hours to help protect delayed messages during recovery.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Guardian both mention Microsoft's initial explanation of elevated load on part of its infrastructure, while Al Jazeera and CBS News note that the elevated load was not caused by an increase in traffic. The Guardian and Al Jazeera report that Microsoft determined some servers had become unexpectedly unavailable, while The Daily Wire and ABC News state that Microsoft paused deployments and redirected traffic as part of its recovery efforts. Newsweek provides additional context on the impact of the outage, including disruptions to government services, airlines, and visa processing, which are not detailed in the other accounts. The Guardian and Al Jazeera mention the outage affecting more than 430 million Microsoft 365 users, while The Daily Wire and CBS News cite 130,000 reports of Outlook problems.

Sources The Daily Wire · Newsweek · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · ABC News · CBS News

GOP holdouts complicate Trump’s final chances to reshape US courts

President Donald Trump is facing pushback from his own party as Republicans race to confirm as many of his judicial nominees as possible before Democrats take control of the Senate in January. The midterm losses left Republicans only weeks to fill lifetime seats on the federal bench while they still have the votes. Making matters worse for the president’s nominations agenda, Sens. Thom Tillis (R-NC) and John Cornyn (R-TX) helped force Trump to withdraw an Arkansas appeals court nominee Tuesday, and Tillis says he’ll vote against other nominees if a Republican senator from their home state objects.

Senate Republicans are racing to confirm President Donald Trump’s judicial nominees before Democrats take control of the chamber in January, seeking to fill as many federal court vacancies as possible while they still have the votes. Republicans have already confirmed 56 Article III judges during Trump’s second term, according to the Senate Republican Conference, which highlighted the confirmations Thursday. GOP leaders hope to confirm as many as 25 more nominees before the new Congress convenes, including four appeals court nominees and 21 district court picks.
