Brazilians head to the polls on Sunday to elect their next president

Brazilians will vote for their next president on Sunday after neither the incumbent left-wing candidate, Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, nor his right-wing opponent, Flavio Bolsonaro, garnered a majority of the votes in the election’s first round earlier this month. Bolsonaro, who has the support of President Donald Trump, is the slight favorite heading into Sunday’s runoff, with him leading by roughly one percentage point in the most recent polls released on Saturday, according to Reuters. The two candidates finalized the arguments of their campaigns on Saturday, as Lula marched along Rio de Janeiro’s Copacabana beach, while Bolsonaro campaigned in a key battleground state.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News reports that Lula won the election with 51.77% of the vote to Bolsonaro’s 48.23%, securing a fourth term, while FOX News states that Lula won 51.8% of the vote, defeating Bolsonaro by nearly 4.4 million votes. The Washington Examiner and Newsweek both mention that Bolsonaro led by about one percentage point in the most recent polls, but Newsweek provides specific poll numbers showing a dead heat. The Guardian and Mother Jones do not mention the final vote totals, instead focusing on the campaign and public sentiment. The Guardian also notes that Lula’s campaign organized a large rally on Copacabana beach, while the Washington Examiner and FOX News describe Lula’s campaign activities without mentioning the rally. Mother Jones includes details about the Kennedy family’s wedding and an inflatable missile rally, which are not mentioned in other accounts.

Sources Washington Examiner · ABC News · Mother Jones · FOX News · The Guardian · Newsweek

Trump, Justice Department escalate fight over Alaska Senate ballot

The Trump administration filed a lawsuit seeking to block Alaska from certifying the results of its U.S. Senate race if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan loses to Democratic challenger Mary Peltola. The lawsuit argues that a second candidate, Dan J. Sullivan, whose name closely resembles that of the incumbent, could confuse voters and siphon support from Sullivan. Attorney General Todd Blanche defended the lawsuit, stating it aims to preserve ballots and allow state officials to assess potential voter confusion. The case has drawn criticism from election experts and advocacy groups, who argue the legal claim is weak and represents an unprecedented federal intervention.

The Alaska Supreme Court previously ruled that Dan J. Sullivan should remain on the ballot, and state election officials have not removed him despite concerns about voter confusion. A recent poll cited in the source material placed Peltola at 49% and Sullivan at 46%, though the methodology and margin of error were not provided. Trump claimed during a news conference that the challenger had received about 2.5 to 3 percentage points in the primary and could receive a similar share in the general election. He also accused Democrats of using the candidate to interfere in the election without offering evidence.

Where the accounts differ

Straight Arrow News reports that Trump recommended the Justice Department examine Dan J. Sullivan’s candidacy, while The Guardian and The Hill state that Trump claimed to have directed the DOJ to file the lawsuit. The Free Press and Axios describe the second candidate as having donated to Peltola and working with a Democratic strategist, a detail not included in Straight Arrow News. The Guardian and The Hill note that legal experts have criticized the lawsuit as legally weak, while Straight Arrow News and The Free Press mention Common Cause and the ACLU opposing the suit. The Guardian and The Hill also include quotes from law professors Derek Mueller and Richard H Pildes, which are absent from other accounts.

Sources Straight Arrow News · The Guardian · The Free Press · Axios · Newsweek · The Hill

Venezuela opposition leader María Corina Machado returns to country

Venezuela opposition leader María Corina Machado returned to her home country after nearly a year in exile, according to multiple accounts. She announced her arrival on social media, writing, “I am here. With you.” Her return was marked by enthusiastic support from Venezuelans, with supporters gathering to greet her at a church in Barquisimeto, west of Caracas. Machado, who had been barred from exiting Venezuela by the Maduro government, had previously attempted to return multiple times but was denied entry. She had fled the country in December 2025 under the cover of darkness to accept the 2025 Nobel Peace Prize in Norway. Her return was described by her senior adviser, David Smoalnsky, as “a major step toward a democratic transition and free, fair, and verifiable presidential elections.”

Machado’s return coincided with the ongoing leadership of interim President Delcy Rodríguez, a Maduro ally who was backed by the Trump administration after the U.S. military deposed Maduro in January 2026. Rodríguez has remained in office beyond her authorized term, with no election scheduled. Machado’s return was also noted by U.S. President Donald Trump, who said, “I don’t know anything about it, but I think she’s a very nice woman. She gave me her Nobel Prize. So how can I say anything else? And I hope she’s safe. She’s a very nice woman. But we’re doing really well in Venezuela.” Secretary of State Marco Rubio claimed Machado’s return was “the direct result of the U.S. supported reconciliation talks.”

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that the Trump administration had reportedly opposed Machado’s return, believing her presence would complicate the relationship with interim president Delcy Rodríguez. This claim is not present in the other accounts. The BBC and Straight Arrow News mention that Machado’s return was contingent on approval from the Trump administration, while CBS News and NPR do not specify this condition. The Guardian also states that Machado took eight hours to reach Caracas due to massive crowds, a detail not included in other accounts. The Christian Science Monitor notes that Machado was briefly detained in January 2025, a detail not mentioned in the BBC or NPR accounts.

Sources CBS News · Christian Science Monitor · BBC · Straight Arrow News · The Guardian · NPR

Obama rips Trump for tariffs and White House renovations as he touts El-Sayed in Detroit

Former President Barack Obama criticized President Donald Trump during a Detroit rally on Saturday, focusing on Trump’s trade policies, White House renovations, and alleged corruption. Obama mocked Trump’s focus on redecorating the White House, suggesting he should be on HGTV, and accused the administration of a “culture that’s set at the top” of corruption. He also took aim at Trump’s trade war with Canada, calling it a conflict with “that historical adversary of ours.” Obama endorsed Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, framing the race as an “inflection point” for change in Michigan. El-Sayed, who has a history of criticizing Obama, praised the former president’s work on healthcare and called their shared experiences as sons of African immigrants a source of inspiration.

Obama’s remarks were part of a broader campaign push for El-Sayed, who faces Republican Mike Rogers, a Trump-endorsed candidate. The rally coincided with Trump’s planned visit to Michigan, where he would campaign for Rogers. El-Sayed, a doctor and former Wayne County health official, won his party’s primary after defeating moderate congresswoman Haley Stevens. Obama’s support for El-Sayed marked his first public endorsement of the candidate during the midterms.

Serbian Nationalist Leader Who Courted MAGA Just Lost in a Landslide

Serbian nationalist leader Aleksandar Vucic conceded defeat in parliamentary elections on Sunday, marking a significant political shift. The student-led opposition movement, known as the Student List, emerged from mass protests following the 2024 collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad that killed 16 people. The movement, which avoided taking a firm stance on Kosovo, focused on anti-corruption and institutional reform. Vucic, who has cultivated ties to Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, faced accusations of downplaying Serbia's 1990s war crimes and using state resources for his own benefit. The election results, which saw high voter turnout, signaled a broad rejection of Vucic's rule, with the Student List projected to win between 135 and 140 seats in the 250-seat parliament.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and Axios both report that Vucic's coalition won 105 seats, while the Student List won 135-140. Axios and The Guardian mention that Vucic projected the Student List would win 135-140 seats, while CBS News states Vucic said the Students' List took around 2.2 million votes compared to his 1.7 million. Al Jazeera and The Guardian note that Vucic conceded defeat, while CBS News reports he blamed "independent media" and "European countries" for the loss. Mother Jones and Axios describe Vucic's support for Jared Kushner's hotel project, which Al Jazeera and CBS News also mention, but Al Jazeera adds that Vucic's political mentor, Aleksandar Seselj, was on his electoral list. The Guardian and CBS News mention the high voter turnout, while Mother Jones and Axios provide more detail on the election's implications for U.S.-Serbia relations.

Sources Mother Jones · Axios · NPR · Al Jazeera · The Guardian · CBS News

Peltola to reschedule Fairbanks, Bethel campaign stops after car crash

Mary Peltola, the Democratic Senate candidate in Alaska, announced she would reschedule her campaign events in Fairbanks and Bethel following a car crash that resulted in minor injuries. The accident occurred on Saturday, and Peltola’s campaign stated that she and her family sustained minor injuries but are in good spirits. The Fairbanks and Bethel events, originally scheduled for Sunday and Monday, will be rescheduled for later in the week. Peltola’s campaign manager, Cheryl Bruce, noted that Peltola is disappointed about the delay but remains eager to resume campaigning. The Anchorage Police Department reported a traffic collision involving Peltola’s vehicle, with the circumstances under investigation. The incident comes as Peltola faces off against Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan in a closely contested race.

Peltola’s campaign said the crash occurred while she was traveling to the Alaska Federation of Natives Annual Banquet. The crash involved another vehicle and resulted in a multi-vehicle collision. The Anchorage Police Department confirmed the crash happened around 6:55 PM on Saturday. Peltola’s family and another vehicle’s occupant were taken to a hospital with non-life-threatening injuries, while a third occupant refused medical transport. The campaign stated that Peltola will resume her campaign activities on Tuesday with a stop in Homer, Alaska.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The New York Post refer to the third candidate as “Decoy Dan,” while The Guardian and The Hill describe him as Daniel J. Sullivan. The Daily Wire and The New York Post note that the Justice Department filed a lawsuit to block certification if Sullivan loses, while The Guardian and The Hill mention this as well but do not name the third candidate. The Straight Arrow News omits details about the third candidate and the lawsuit.

California leader calls attack on alleged noncitizen voting ‘weaponizing the law’

California’s attorney general, Rob Bonta, criticized the Trump administration’s efforts to challenge alleged noncitizen voting, calling it “politicizing and weaponizing the law.” He accused the Justice Department of acting at Donald Trump’s direction. Less than two weeks before the midterms, federal prosecutors asked California election officials to verify within 48 hours a list of about 20,000 ballots belonging to suspected noncitizens. The Justice Department also charged 28 foreign nationals recently, accusing them of fraudulently registering to vote. California officials raised concerns that the directive could disenfranchise citizens, noting that a “high percentage” of the individuals on the list were born in the United States.

A federal appeals court expressed skepticism about the Trump administration’s lawsuit against California, questioning whether the Justice Department’s request to segregate ballots was consistent with the Elections Clause, which gives states authority over election administration. Circuit Judge Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee, raised concerns about the reliability of the administration’s list of suspected noncitizens and whether the court had jurisdiction to intervene. The case is part of a broader effort by the Trump administration to crack down on alleged noncitizen voting ahead of the midterms.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that California’s attorney general, Rob Bonta, accused the Justice Department of acting at Trump’s direction and called the effort “weaponizing the law.” Al Jazeera and The Hill do not mention Bonta’s direct accusation of Trump’s direction, though all three outlets report that the Justice Department sought to segregate ballots. The Guardian and Straight Arrow News both mention that the Justice Department charged 28 foreign nationals, while Al Jazeera and The Hill do not. The Guardian and The Hill describe the list as containing 20,000 names, while FOX News and Straight Arrow News refer to it as “roughly 20,000.” The Guardian and Straight Arrow News attribute the claim that a “high percentage” of the list were born in the U.S. to California officials, while Al Jazeera and The Hill do not.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · The Hill · FOX News · Straight Arrow News

Archdiocese of New York agrees to $800 million settlement over sexual abuse claims

The Archdiocese of New York has agreed to pay $800 million to settle claims brought by approximately 1,300 individuals who allege they were sexually abused by clergy members and lay workers. Jeff Anderson, a lawyer representing over 250 of the plaintiffs, stated the settlement was finalized and that all plaintiffs had agreed to it. Archbishop Ronald Hicks, in an email shared with NPR, noted the deal had not yet been signed but that an agreement had been reached with plaintiffs and their attorneys. The agreement was first reported by The New York Times.

The settlement is the second-largest in the Catholic Church related to sexual abuse claims, following the $880 million deal reached by the Archdiocese of Los Angeles in 2024. The Archdiocese of New York had previously agreed to mediation in December 2025 to settle the 1,300 sexual abuse claims and planned to raise $300 million through staff layoffs and property sales. According to Al Jazeera, each of the approximately 1,300 survivors will receive at least $250,000 from the Archdiocese of New York, with potential for more based on the severity of the abuse.

Where the accounts differ

NPR states that the settlement was finalized and that all plaintiffs had agreed to it, while Al Jazeera reports that the settlement will be formally signed on November 6. NPR mentions that the agreement was first reported by The New York Times, whereas Al Jazeera attributes the story to The New York Times as well but adds that each survivor will receive at least $250,000. Al Jazeera also includes a statement from Jeff Anderson about the survivors' "unanimous agreement" and their role in bringing about a "reckoning and a meaningful step toward accountability," which NPR does not mention.

Sources NPR · Al Jazeera
