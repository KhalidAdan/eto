Trump says US to ‘substantially reduce’ military drills with South Korea

US President Donald Trump has ordered the Pentagon to scale back joint military exercises with South Korea, citing his “very good relationship” with North Korea’s Kim Jong Un. The drills, scheduled to start on Monday, are described by Trump as costly and sending a “totally inappropriate and hostile” signal to North Korea. South Korea’s Ministry of Defence stated the exercises would proceed as previously scheduled. The annual Ulchi Freedom Shield drills involve 18,000 South Korean soldiers and are designed to prepare for defense against North Korea. North Korea has consistently condemned the exercises as provocative, with its foreign ministry calling them a “rehearsal for an aggressive war.”

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and ABC News both report that Trump cited South Korea’s refusal to join the US in denuclearizing Iran as a reason for reducing the drills. CBS News also mentions this point but adds that Trump said the decision to reduce the drills is “somewhat unrelated” to the Iran issue. ABC News and BBC note that South Korea’s Ministry of Defence stated the drills would proceed as scheduled, while CBS News reports that South Korea’s presidential office expressed hope for “meaningful dialogue.” Al Jazeera and BBC mention that North Korea has accelerated its nuclear and missile programs since 2019, while ABC News and CBS News provide more detail on North Korea’s recent missile tests and the context of its military posturing.

Sources Al Jazeera · ABC News · CBS News · Axios · Associated Press · BBC

Luigi Mangione's New York trial delayed

Luigi Mangione's New York state trial has been delayed following his guilty plea to federal charges in the 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson. The defense team filed a motion to dismiss the state case, citing double jeopardy law, arguing that the charges stem from the same incident. The judge set the state trial for December 10, allowing time for both sides to litigate the motion. Mangione faces second-degree murder, weapons, and forgery charges in the state case, while he was charged with federal stalking and cyberstalking resulting in death. Legal experts note that the double jeopardy argument may be complex due to differing charges.

The New York state trial was delayed after Mangione pleaded guilty to federal charges, which the defense claims are based on the same conduct as the state murder charges. The judge ordered prosecutors to respond by October 9, with the defense given until October 30 to reply. The Manhattan DA's office stated it was prepared to litigate the motion to dismiss, emphasizing the potential for further delays if the case proceeds.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and Just The News both report that Mangione admitted to shooting Brian Thompson in Manhattan, while FOX News includes a quote from Briana Rose Lee, a staffer for Ilhan Omar, who downplayed the killing. CBS News and Just The News do not mention Lee's comments. FOX News also includes quotes from Briahna Joy Gray and Rep. Ritchie Torres, which are absent from the other accounts. The Daily Wire and BBC do not mention Mangione's trial or the federal charges, instead focusing on Jason Arday's death and related media coverage. The Daily Caller discusses Arday's death and the claim that racism contributed to his suicide, which is not covered in the other accounts.

Sources CBS News · Just The News · FOX News · The Daily Wire · BBC · The Daily Caller

Hayden Panettiere's life of struggles alongside screen success

Hayden Panettiere, who died at the age of 36, began her career as a child star, appearing in commercials at 11 months old and later in TV soaps like "One Life to Live" and "Guiding Light." She gained wider recognition for her role as Claire Bennet on the TV series "Heroes" and later as Juliette Barnes on "Nashville." Her career included roles in films such as "Remember the Titans," "Raising Helen," and "Ice Princess." Panettiere also faced personal struggles, including addiction, postpartum depression, and domestic abuse, which she detailed in her memoir "This Is Me: A Reckoning." Her death was announced late Sunday night, with her father confirming it in a statement.

Panettiere's death was confirmed by her father, who described her as an "incredible light and a force of nature." Law enforcement stated there was "no sign of foul play," though an autopsy was pending. Her final Instagram post was shared by a photographer, and colleagues and friends expressed their grief, remembering her for her talent and resilience. Her brother, Jansen, had died in 2023 from cardiomegaly and aortic valve complications.

Where the accounts differ

BBC mentions Panettiere's back injury from filming "Racing Stripes" and her struggles with body dysmorphia due to media attention, while The Daily Wire reports on her postpartum depression, addiction, and domestic abuse by Brian Hickerson. Newsweek and ABC News both mention her brother Jansen's death in 2023, but Newsweek states he died in February 2023, while ABC News and CBS News do not specify the month. The Daily Wire and CBS News include details about her memoir and its release in May 2026, while BBC and New York Post do not mention the exact release date. The Daily Wire also includes a 911 call report and details about a sexual harassment incident with an "Oscar-winning actor and director," which other accounts omit.

Jared Kushner meets with Hamas ahead of talks with Netanyahu on Gaza

Jared Kushner, Donald Trump’s son-in-law and Middle East envoy, held a meeting with Hamas’s political chief, Khalil al-Hayya, in Egypt on Sunday. The meeting was part of efforts to advance the stalled 15-point U.S.-backed road map for a Gaza ceasefire. The plan requires Hamas to disarm and Israel to withdraw from the territory. Kushner is set to meet with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday to discuss next steps. The talks aim to salvage the plan, which has been stalled over Hamas’s disarmament commitments. Several regional powers, including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan, and Pakistan, issued a joint statement condemning Israel’s rejection of the road map.

The meeting in Egypt included officials from mediating countries Egypt, Qatar, and Turkey. Hamas officials confirmed the meeting, stating the group is committed to the road map and awaiting mediators to start a 14-day negotiation period to finalize a timetable. Hamas also demanded that Israel halt attacks on Gaza and withdraw to the so-called “yellow line” before implementation. Israeli forces currently control about 60% of Gaza, with plans to expand control to 70%. Israel has killed over 1,200 Palestinians in Gaza since the ceasefire on October 10, 2025, according to Gaza’s health ministry.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and NPR both report that Kushner met with Hamas and that the meeting was confirmed by regional and Hamas officials. Axios adds that Kushner met with Netanyahu on Monday and that the meeting included a U.S. official who described the meeting as helping clarify misunderstandings but also highlighting remaining differences. UPI and Washington Examiner mention that Kushner met with Egyptian President Abdel Fatah al-Sisi and that the meeting was the first at this level since the ceasefire agreement in October 2025. Just The News reports that Hamas told Kushner they intend to demilitarize Gaza, a claim not explicitly made by other outlets. The Guardian and NPR note that the yellow line was never precisely defined, while Axios and Washington Examiner provide more detail on the political context and Netanyahu’s skepticism.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · Axios · UPI · Washington Examiner · Just The News

Trump administration pauses border construction in Big Bend national park

The Trump administration announced on Monday that it is temporarily pausing construction of a border wall segment in Big Bend National Park. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Commissioner Rodney Scott stated the pause would continue while he conducts an on-the-ground evaluation. The project, part of a $46.5 billion border wall expansion approved by Congress in 2025, has faced significant opposition from environmental groups, local officials, and residents. Scott’s announcement followed footage of bulldozers operating in the park, which had not been publicly disclosed earlier in the month.

Environmental advocates and local stakeholders have raised concerns about the impact of the construction on the park’s ecosystem, wildlife, and scenic value. Senator John Cornyn, a Texas Republican, expressed support for the pause, noting that the park’s natural terrain already serves as a deterrent against illegal crossings. Critics argue that the construction would damage the park’s landscape, threaten Native American sites, and disrupt wildlife migration.

The pause has been welcomed by some activists and local officials, who hope it will lead to modifications in the project. However, the administration has not yet provided details on what changes might be made or when construction could resume.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that Scott expects the first layer of the wall to be built by the end of next year, while the New York Post and ABC News do not mention this timeline. The New York Post and Straight Arrow News both include details about the bipartisan opposition and the specific concerns raised by local residents, including the impact on tourism and the economy, which are not explicitly mentioned in The Guardian. The ABC News account includes information about former Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem waiving certain laws to allow construction, a detail not present in the other accounts. The Straight Arrow News provides more specific details about the project’s components, including the inclusion of a new road, lights, and fiber-optic cable, which are not detailed in the other accounts.

Sources The Guardian · New York Post · ABC News · Straight Arrow News

Duane "Keffe D" Davis faces trial for 1996 murder of Tupac Shakur nearly three decades later

Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, is on trial for his alleged role in the 1996 murder of rapper Tupac Shakur. Prosecutors claim Davis planned the drive-by shooting that killed Shakur, though he is not accused of pulling the trigger. Davis allegedly detailed the attack in a 2008 interview with detectives and in a 2019 book. The trial began in Las Vegas, with prosecutors presenting evidence that Davis was present at both the shooting of Shakur and the subsequent killing of Biggie Smalls six months later. The defense argues that Davis embellished and fictionalized claims in his book and that the prosecution relies on unreliable sources.

Prosecutors allege that Davis, a former leader of the South Side Compton Crips, orchestrated the attack in retaliation for his nephew Orlando Anderson being beaten by Shakur's entourage at a boxing match in Las Vegas. Davis is charged with murder with a deadly weapon with the intent to promote, further or assist a criminal gang. His defense attorney claims the prosecution's narrative is "fiction" and that there are no facts to support the charges.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News states that Davis was a confidential informant for the LAPD during the 2008 interview and that the judge allowed his statements to be used in court due to the public nature of his book. BBC reports that Davis was a confidential informant but does not mention the judge's ruling on the admissibility of his book. NPR and CBS News both mention that Davis admitted to being present at the party where Biggie Smalls was killed, but CBS News adds that Davis was briefly suspected in that shooting. UPI does not mention the party or the suspicion in Biggie Smalls' murder. BBC also notes that Mopreme Shakur, Tupac's stepbrother, expressed that the trial would not bring justice.

Sources FOX News · BBC · NPR · CBS News · UPI

House Ethics panel probing Dem Rep. Jimmy Gomez after he admitted to cheating on his wife following Post report on staffer makeout

The House Ethics Committee is investigating Rep. Jimmy Gomez (D-Calif.) for "inappropriate sexual contact with a House staffer" after he admitted to cheating on his wife following a Post exposé that revealed Gomez kissed a much younger aide three years ago. The probe, announced by the bipartisan panel Monday, threatens to drag in prominent Democrats as witnesses, including Gomez’s former roommate Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-Ariz.). Gomez, who has been married since 2011, acknowledged in June that he had engaged in extramarital conduct but insisted that his actions were consensual and did not violate the law or House ethics rules. He stated he would cooperate with the Ethics Committee inquiry.

The New York Post reported in April that Gomez was seen kissing a much younger congressional aide in the backyard of then-Rep. Eric Swalwell’s Washington residence in August 2023. The Post also noted that Gomez initially denied sharing a passionate moment with the woman, then-Swalwell aide Yardena Wolf, against a car outside her boss’ home. The House of Representatives banned members from having relationships with their own staffers in 2018 at the height of the #MeToo movement, but relationships between lawmakers and staffers for their colleagues are technically allowed, though they can still run afoul of congressional rules if there are additional aggravating factors.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and The Daily Wire both report that the alleged incident occurred in August 2023 at the residence of then-Rep. Eric Swalwell, while The Daily Caller and Axios refer to it as occurring in the summer of 2023. The Daily Wire and The New York Post mention that the aide was two decades younger than Gomez, who is now 51, but The Daily Caller and Axios note that the aide was 48 at the time of the alleged incident. The Daily Wire and The New York Post state that the Ethics Committee is reviewing allegations of "inappropriate sexual contact with a House staffer," while The Daily Caller and Axios refer to the allegations as "sexual misconduct." The Daily Wire and The New York Post also note that the Ethics Committee has not determined that Gomez violated House rules, while The Daily Caller and Axios state that the committee is investigating the allegations.

Supreme Court rejects final Trump appeal of $5 million E. Jean Carroll verdict

The U.S. Supreme Court on Monday declined a request from President Donald Trump to reconsider its rejection of his appeal in the 2023 E. Jean Carroll case. The decision marks the end of Trump's bid to overturn a $5 million jury award to Carroll, which her representatives confirmed was paid in July. The Court did not provide an explanation for its decision or note any dissents. Trump, who denies any wrongdoing, is continuing to appeal a separate $83.3 million defamation judgment from 2024, which remains pending with the Supreme Court.

In 2023, a jury found Trump liable for sexually abusing Carroll in a Manhattan department store in the 1990s and for defaming her in a 2022 social media post where he called her allegations "a Hoax and a lie" and said "This woman is not my type!" In 2024, a separate trial found Trump liable for defaming Carroll in 2019 when he denied her allegations of sexual abuse. Trump's legal team argues that his 2019 comments were "official" statements as president and therefore immune from use in a legal case against him.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and UPI both state that Trump paid Carroll the $5 million in July, while the New York Post says he paid $5.63 million — the judgment amount plus interest — soon after the court initially declined to hear his appeal in June. BBC and Axios mention that Carroll received the $5 million earlier this year, with Axios noting she would place it in an interest-bearing account until the petition was denied. UPI and the New York Post both mention that Trump is also appealing the $83.3 million defamation judgment, while ABC News and Axios note that the $83.3 million case remains pending with the Supreme Court. BBC and the New York Post attribute the claim about presidential immunity to Trump's legal team, while UPI and Axios attribute it to Trump's lawyers.

Sources ABC News · BBC · UPI · Axios · New York Post
