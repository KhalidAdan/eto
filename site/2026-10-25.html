Pentagon drafts Iran attack plans as Tehran threatens aggressive retaliation

The U.S. military has prepared options for potential strikes against Iran following the midterm elections, according to a source familiar with the planning. The U.S. Central Command (Centcom) has developed a range of strike options, which are being presented to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. President Trump has not ruled out military action against Iran, with some analysts suggesting a likely return to strikes after the November midterms. The Trump administration is under pressure to secure concessions from Iran amid stalled diplomatic talks, with some officials expressing frustration over the lack of progress. The military is also preparing for potential involvement from Israeli forces, which could shift the focus of strikes toward civilian and energy infrastructure.

The intelligence community has warned that Iran has increased its efforts to target U.S. personnel abroad, with plots reaching “advanced stages” in the Middle East and other countries. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton informed Secretary of State Marco Rubio of the threat in an unclassified memo, which was obtained by multiple outlets. The memo did not specify targets or countries outside the Middle East, nor did it indicate whether attacks were imminent. The warning comes as the State Department reduces staffing at several embassies in the region, citing threats from Iran and its Houthi allies in Yemen. The administration is also considering renewed military strikes against Iran, with some officials suggesting the possibility of another operation similar to the previous campaign.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill reports that the Pentagon has drafted attack plans and that Centcom has strike options ready for presentation to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, while The Daily Wire, New York Post, CBS News, Axios, and FOX News focus on the intelligence warning about Iranian plots against U.S. personnel. The Hill attributes the planning to Centcom and mentions the involvement of Gregory Brew and Harrison Mann, while the other outlets do not mention these individuals. The Daily Wire and New York Post note that the memo did not identify specific targets or countries outside the Middle East, whereas The Hill does not mention this detail. The Hill also states that the Pentagon has not responded to its request for comment, while the other outlets do not mention this.

Sources The Hill · The Daily Wire · New York Post · CBS News · Axios · FOX News

Paris Shops Damaged, Protests Escalate Amid Calls for Government Action

French authorities reported that over 3,000 shops across the country were damaged or looted during last weekend’s protests, with about 450 businesses in Paris specifically affected. The unrest, which began with student-led demonstrations over school conditions and living costs, spread to include broader anti-government sentiment. In Paris, at least 110,000 people marched, though union organizers estimated the number to be higher. The protests turned violent in some areas, with shops smashed, museums closed, and police using tear gas and water cannons to control crowds. The French Interior Minister, Laurent Nuñez, announced plans to deploy 70,000 officers and other security measures to prevent further unrest. Meanwhile, a bakery owner, Jhemima Sheilha, described how her shop was destroyed by looters, while influencer Lola-Fleur Whittaker mocked shopkeepers on a television program. The protests have also drawn international attention, with U.S. billionaire Ken Griffin warning that the chaos in France serves as a warning for American youth about the dangers of socialist policies.

Where the accounts differ

The Free Press reports that about 450 businesses in Paris were damaged, with half looted, while CBS News states that 3,000 shops across France were damaged or looted. The Free Press attributes the destruction of Sheilha’s bakery to looters, while FOX News mentions that nearly 1,000 people were arrested during the protests. The Guardian and BBC describe the protests as largely peaceful at first, with violence emerging at the end, while The Free Press and CBS News focus more on the destruction and looting. The Guardian and BBC also note that the government estimated 400,000 participants, while CBS News reports union organizers estimated 800,000.

Sources The Free Press · CBS News · Fox Business · FOX News · The Guardian · BBC

Ukraine's Starlink service restored after speed restrictions disrupted military operations

Ukraine's Ministry of Defense announced on Saturday that full access to Starlink and Starshield satellite services has been restored, with stable connectivity at speeds exceeding 250 mph. The restoration followed a week of restrictions that limited the Ukrainian military's ability to use drones for intercepting Russian attack drones and for logistics. President Volodymyr Zelensky stated that the service was restored overnight, improving front-line operations and defense against Russian drones. Elon Musk, CEO of SpaceX, admitted that his company had throttled Starlink speeds, citing Ukraine's use of the service for offensive drone strikes against people as a violation of terms of service. Musk maintained that the U.S. government controlled Starshield, the military-grade version of Starlink used for Ukraine's drone operations and communications.

The restrictions on Starlink and Starshield were at the center of tensions between Ukraine and the Trump administration. Ukrainian officials accused both President Trump and Musk of being responsible for the slowdown, while Trump and Musk denied involvement. The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump had called Musk to address the issue, contradicting Musk's claim that the U.S. government controlled Starshield. The Ukrainian military reported that the restrictions affected drones traveling at speeds of 100 mph or higher, limiting their operational effectiveness.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that Musk admitted to throttling Starlink speeds and that Zelensky stated the service was restored to pre-restriction levels. Axios states that the Ukrainian defense ministry confirmed the restoration of speeds exceeding 400 km/h, attributing the lifting of restrictions to "direct dialogue" and Zelensky's diplomatic efforts. FOX News claims that a source familiar with the situation said Ukraine received no advance warning of the restrictions, while The Hill notes that the Wall Street Journal contradicted Musk's account, stating that Musk was behind the restrictions. CBS News and Fox Business both mention that Trump denied involvement, while Axios and The Hill note that Trump and Musk denied responsibility, with Axios adding that Trump claimed he would speak to Musk if the service was still restricted.

Sources BBC · CBS News · Axios · The Hill · FOX News · Fox Business

Seven killed and 40 injured after gunmen open fire at church funeral in Jordan

Gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak, Jordan, killing at least seven people and injuring 40 others. The attack occurred on Friday, according to multiple outlets. Witnesses described scenes of panic as people tried to seek cover during the shooting. Jordan’s health minister confirmed 40 people were injured, while the prime minister condemned the attack as a result of "terrorist ideology and blind extremism." King Abdullah II vowed to retaliate "with an iron fist" against those responsible. Security forces are searching for the attackers, and the incident has prompted increased security measures across Jordan.

The attack took place at St George’s Greek Orthodox church in Hamoud, in Karak governorate. Footage from Jordanian state television showed three attackers, two with handguns and one with a semiautomatic rifle, approaching the church and firing. Security camera footage verified by Reuters showed the attackers running toward the church’s entrance. Witnesses reported the gunfire lasted several minutes, with some describing the attackers as hiding behind trees and a low wall before fleeing. The attack has been described as a rare incident in Jordan, which is generally stable and has seen previous foiled attacks by Islamist militants.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and The Daily Wire report that the attack resulted in seven deaths, while the BBC initially reported seven killed but later revised the toll to seven. The Guardian and NPR state that 40 people were injured, while the Daily Wire and Just The News report 40 injured. The BBC and The Daily Wire mention that the attack lasted several minutes, while the Guardian and NPR describe the duration as 15 minutes. The Guardian and The Daily Wire attribute the claim about the number of injured to Jordan’s health minister, while the BBC and NPR do not specify the source of that number. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention that one of the attackers had a weapon jam, while the BBC and NPR do not. The Guardian and The Daily Wire report that the attackers fled in a pickup truck, while the BBC and NPR do not specify the method of escape.

Sources The Guardian · BBC · FOX News · The Daily Wire · NPR · Just The News

Brazilians head to the polls on Sunday to elect their next president

Brazilians will vote for their next president on Sunday after neither the incumbent left-wing candidate, Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, nor his right-wing opponent, Flavio Bolsonaro, garnered a majority of the votes in the election’s first round earlier this month. Bolsonaro, who has the support of President Donald Trump, is the slight favorite heading into Sunday’s runoff, with him leading by roughly one percentage point in the most recent polls released on Saturday, according to Reuters. The two candidates finalized the arguments of their campaigns on Saturday, as Lula marched along Rio de Janeiro’s Copacabana beach, while Bolsonaro campaigned in a key battleground state.

More than one million supporters of Brazil’s leftwing president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, have streamed on to Rio’s most famous beach for a giant protest they hope will propel him towards a historic election fightback against his far-right rival, Flavio Bolsonaro. Nearly 159m Brazilians will vote in the election’s runoff on Sunday, with the pair tied in the polls after Bolsonaro won the first round on 4 October by 47% to 45%. On Saturday, under a fierce sun, red-clad crowds packed the 2.5-mile Copacabana beach in an attempt to help Lula clinch a dramatic and unprecedented comeback.
