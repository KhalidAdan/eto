Trump-backed group announces $10m ad spend for midterm Senate race in Texas

A political group associated with United States President Donald Trump has announced it will spend $10m on television and digital advertisements in the state of Texas ahead of the upcoming midterm elections. The Saturday filing with the Federal Election Commission (FEC) represents the first major spend in a general election by MAGA, Inc, a super PAC named for Trump’s “Make America Great Again” movement. The advertisements are expected to heavily target Democratic candidate James Talarico, a 37-year-old state representative running for the US Senate against Trump-backed Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton. Speculation abounds that Democrats may have a chance of flipping one of Texas’s Senate seats for the first time since 1993. Paxton has weathered a series of scandals, even facing a state impeachment trial. Still, Democrats have repeatedly faced withering defeats in Texas, having failed to win a statewide office there in more than three decades.

Russian attacks kill 4 in Ukraine as Moscow and Kyiv prepare for visit by US envoys

Russian attacks killed four people in Ukraine overnight, as Moscow and Kyiv await the arrival of U.S. envoys to begin a new push for peace in the Kremlin’s 4 1/2-year-old invasion. The planned visit by Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, who is also U.S. President Donald Trump’s son-in-law, is hoped to revive peace talks amid escalating aerial attacks by both sides. The air war between Russia and Ukraine has intensified as troops have struggled to make progress along the 1,250-kilometer (775-mile) front line. Russia has recently been using fast-flying, jet-powered drones, and the recurring wail of air-raid sirens has become part of daily life in Kyiv. The strikes come despite a message from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy Friday saying that Ukraine would halt air attacks during Witkoff and Kushner’s visit and urging Moscow to do the same.

Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed he will meet the envoys on Sunday after they visit Moscow on Saturday to hold talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin. Witkoff and Kushner have led U.S. President Donald Trump’s efforts to broker a peace deal, but a gulf remains between the two warring sides. Zelenskyy said Ukraine is ready to observe a ceasefire and have a “substantive conversation” with Witkoff and Kushner. “From now through the end of Saturday, as well as on Sunday and Monday, Ukraine is ready to refrain from strikes on Moscow, and we expect the Russians to do the same regarding Kyiv,” he said.

Ceiling collapses inside Kennedy Center amid strong storm

A section of the ceiling inside the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts collapsed during severe storms on Friday evening. The incident occurred in the Grand Foyer, a main hallway connecting the center’s three primary performance spaces. Photos showed a large hole in the ceiling and debris scattered across the red carpet. No one was injured, according to Kennedy Center officials. The main building was closed Friday night, while the Grand Foyer is expected to remain restricted through the weekend as crews assess and repair the damage.

Kennedy Center officials, including Vice President of Public Relations Roma Daravi, stated the incident highlights the urgent need for renovations, which are supported by President Donald Trump and the center’s board. The collapse is attributed to decades of deferred maintenance, according to Daravi, who emphasized the necessity of closing the building for renovations.

Judge lets Pentagon move forward with firing Stars and Stripes journalists

A federal judge allowed the Pentagon to proceed with firing three employees of the Stars and Stripes military newspaper. The judge ruled that the government likely did not violate their First Amendment rights by terminating publisher Max Lederer, editor-in-chief Erik Slavin, and reporter Lara Korte. The firings occurred after the newspaper published an article about deteriorating conditions on the USS Abraham Lincoln aircraft carrier. The journalists were placed on administrative leave and later fired over allegations of insubordination. The judge noted that the journalists' interviews with CBS News for a "CBS Sunday Morning" segment were part of their official duties, and thus their speech was not protected under the First Amendment.

The lawsuit argued that the Pentagon unlawfully terminated the journalists for speaking out about the need for editorial independence. The judge, however, found that the journalists were acting within their official roles when they gave interviews to CBS News. The ruling came after a court hearing where attorneys for the journalists claimed the interviews were conducted as sources, not as reporters, and were on matters of public concern. The judge dismissed these claims, stating that the journalists' speech was part of their job duties and thus not protected.

Far-right AfD predicted to win big in crucial German state election

The Alternative für Deutschland (AfD), a far-right, anti-immigration party, is expected to win a significant victory in the regional election in Saxony-Anhalt. Polls indicate the AfD is leading with around 40-41% of the vote, ahead of the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) at 23% and the Social Democratic Party (SPD) at 8.5%. The AfD's top candidate, Ulrich Siegmund, has predicted gaining an absolute majority, which would allow the party to form a government without needing to cooperate with mainstream parties. The election is seen as a potential turning point for the AfD, which could mark the first time a far-right party holds state-level power in Germany since World War II.

The AfD's platform includes policies such as a "remigration and deportation offensive," a ban on LGBTQ rainbow flags at public schools, and an end to new windfarms. The party has also tapped into nostalgia for communist East Germany and resentment over economic disparities with the west. The prospect of an AfD-led government has raised concerns among mainstream parties and minority groups, with some fearing the party's pro-Russia stance could lead to leaks of sensitive information to Moscow. The German Economic Institute warned that the AfD's immigration policies could be "ruinous" for the state, which has an aging and shrinking population reliant on foreign-born workers.
