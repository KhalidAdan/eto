Flydubai Flight Diverted to Saudi Arabia After Pilot Stabbing Incident

A Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv was diverted to Saudi Arabia after a violent altercation between the pilots, according to multiple reports. The incident involved a co-pilot allegedly stabbing his captain, leading to an emergency landing in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. All passengers are reported safe, though the pilot was seriously injured. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu praised the pilot's actions, calling them "extraordinary bravery." Passengers and crew reportedly intervened to subdue the attacker, with some describing the plane's steep descent during the confrontation. Flight tracking data showed a rapid loss of altitude, with the plane dropping over 17,000 feet in under two minutes before landing.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Hill both mention that the pilot, Smit Machchhar, was stabbed and fought back, enabling passengers to subdue the attacker. The Daily Wire adds that the relief pilots were European, with one being British, while The Hill states that the relief pilots were on board to take over for the next flight. The Guardian and Al Jazeera do not specify the nationality of the relief pilots. The Washington Examiner and The Hill describe a passenger, Yaniv Hayoun, as a plumber who took control of the plane, a detail not included in The Daily Wire or Al Jazeera. The Guardian notes that the motive for the attack remains unclear, while The Hill and The Daily Wire attribute the attack to an Omani national. The Daily Wire and The Hill both mention Netanyahu's praise for Machchhar, while The Guardian and Al Jazeera do not provide specific details about the pilot's actions beyond the stabbing.

Cornell Rape Allegations Fuel Title IX Debate

A criminal investigation into allegations that a Cornell University student was gang-raped by seven fraternity members in 2024 is fueling a push by House Democratic women to strengthen federal protections against sexual violence on college campuses. The Democratic Women's Caucus is calling on congressional Republicans and the Trump administration to strengthen Title IX protections, arguing that the administration's changes to the civil rights law could leave students more vulnerable. The case has also prompted New York Gov. Kathy Hochul to call for an independent review of Cornell's response to the allegations.

New York prosecutors are reopening the 2024 case, which was initially handled by campus police. A woman identified as Jane Doe alleges she was drugged and raped at a Chi Phi fraternity house in October 2024. Cornell conducted a Title IX investigation, resulting in two students being expelled and lesser disciplinary action for others. No criminal charges were initially filed, but the DA has reopened the case after a lawsuit was filed earlier this month. Cornell has defended its handling of the case, stating it followed university policies.

U.S. Forces Withdraw From Iraq After 23 Years

U.S. forces completed their withdrawal from Iraq on Wednesday, ending a 23-year military presence since the 2003 invasion. The departure from Erbil Air Base marked the end of Operation Inherent Resolve, a mission launched in 2014 to combat ISIS. President Trump praised the withdrawal as a victory for both the U.S. and Iraq, calling it the end of a “quagmire.” Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi was also commended for his leadership. The U.S. military has been involved in Iraq multiple times, with a previous withdrawal in 2011 under President Obama, followed by a return in 2014 after ISIS took control of parts of the country.

The withdrawal is part of a planned transition toward a bilateral U.S.-Iraq defense relationship. While American forces are no longer stationed in Iraq as part of Operation Inherent Resolve, the Combined Joint Task Force-Operation Inherent Resolve (CJTF-OIR) remains operational and is now headquartered in Jordan. U.S. forces will continue the counter-ISIS mission in Syria alongside coalition partners and the Syrian government. The mission has pushed ISIS from controlling any significant territory, but it does not mean the group ceases to exist.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and The Daily Wire both report that Trump called the withdrawal a “victory for the United States, it’s a victory for Iraq, and a decisive victory over ISIS,” and that he described the original invasion of Iraq under George W. Bush as a “quagmire.” The Daily Wire attributes this statement to Trump, while the New York Post attributes it to a social media post by Trump. The Mother Jones article does not mention Trump’s comments on the invasion, instead focusing on the cost and consequences of the war. The BBC and CBS News both report that Trump praised the withdrawal as a victory but do not attribute the specific quote about the invasion to a particular source. The ABC News account does not mention Trump’s comments at all, focusing instead on the strategic and political context of the withdrawal.

The incident, which involved three suspicious vehicles and masked men, was reported to authorities by a local farmer who saw the group near the base. Iran has denied any involvement, with its foreign minister calling Burnham’s claims “barking up the wrong tree.” The base has been used by U.S. forces for operations against Iran, including strikes on Iranian missile capabilities. The U.S. State Department and officials like Secretary of State Marco Rubio have expressed concern over the incident, with Rubio stating it “clearly involves the hands of a foreign actor.”

Burnham emphasized that the investigation is ongoing and complex, with no explosives found in the vehicles, though petrol was recovered. The suspects remain under investigation, and authorities have imposed strict bail conditions. U.S. President Donald Trump criticized the decision to grant bail, while British officials defended it as part of a strategic approach. Senate blocks data center bill, citing it as 'toothless' and politically motivated

The Senate voted to block the Ratepayer Protection Act, a bill aimed at addressing rising electricity costs linked to data center construction. The legislation, which passed the House 417-3, would have required states to consider making data centers cover the costs of grid upgrades. However, the Senate failed to reach the 60-vote threshold needed to proceed, with a 57-43 vote. Senate Democrats, led by Chuck Schumer, criticized the bill as a "toothless messaging bill" that lacks enforcement mechanisms. The bill also included provisions on voter ID, which some Democrats argued made it ineffective. Meanwhile, the Stop Insider Trading Act, which passed the House with 13 Democratic votes, was also blocked by Senate Democrats, with Schumer accusing Republicans of adding a "poison pill" to prevent its passage. Republicans, including Sen. Jon Husted, argued the bills address key voter concerns about affordability and corruption.

Spain announces ban on evictions after protests over 87-year-old woman's removal from flat

An 87-year-old woman, María del Carmen Abascal, known as "Maricarmen," will return home after a deal with her apartment's owner, her lawyer confirmed. The case sparked widespread protests in Spain, leading the government to propose new housing measures. The government's decree extends eviction protections until 2030, regulates room rentals, imposes a new tax on seasonal rentals, and bans speculative real estate purchases by vulture funds until 2028. The measures also include tax breaks for landlords renting to low-income tenants and interest-free loans for first-time homebuyers.

The protests, centered around an encampment in Madrid's Puerta del Sol, demanded stronger tenant protections, measures against fraud, and a ban on evictions when alternative housing is unavailable. The government aims to expedite the approval process, calling for an extraordinary plenary session on housing. The Madrid Tenants' Union, which organized the protests, remains cautious about the details of the decree and may continue protests in the coming days.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that Maricarmen accepted a deal limiting her rent to 30% of her income, meaning she would not pay more than 500 euros per month. BBC does not mention this specific agreement. NPR states that the government's coalition needs support from smaller parties to pass bills, while BBC notes that the government lacks a majority in parliament and has struggled to get right-leaning parties to approve previous decrees. NPR attributes the characterization of the government's measures as "a historic standoff" to the Madrid Tenants' Union, while BBC does not attribute this characterization.
