Secret Service increases security for rescheduled White House Correspondents' Association dinner

The rescheduled White House Correspondents' Association dinner took place on July 24, 2026, at the Waldorf Astoria hotel in Washington D.C. The event was originally scheduled for April 25 but was postponed after an armed assailant attempted to assassinate President Donald Trump and other officials during the first dinner. Secret Service Director Sean Curran stated that the agency has seen a surge in threats against its protectees, with approximately 10,000 protective intelligence cases opened since January.

The rescheduled event featured enhanced security measures, including airport-style magnetometer screening for all ticketed guests, a visible police presence around the hotel, and traffic closures due to proximity to major thoroughfares. The Secret Service also implemented a separate entrance for guests rather than using the main lobby.

During the dinner, President Trump delivered a speech that included jokes at the expense of the press and his political opponents. He praised the Secret Service and law enforcement officers who responded to the April shooting, awarding Officer Victor Gonzales with the WHCA President's Award for Exceptional Service.

  • BBC notes that the event was considerably scaled down from the first correspondents' dinner, with about 700 people in attendance compared to over 2,000 in April.

Iran-backed Houthis claim missile attack on Saudi Arabia as regional tensions escalate

The Iranian-backed Houthi rebels claimed a missile attack on the southern Saudi city of Jizan on Saturday, after vowing retaliation against the kingdom for attacks this week. The US military did not announce fresh strikes on Iran on Saturday, having carried out 13 consecutive nights of attacks on the country. During this time, Iran has also launched drone strikes on US military facilities and bases across the Middle East.

The Houthis said the attack was a "dangerous escalation" in response to Saudi airstrikes on Houthi targets in Yemen. The group had vowed retaliation after Riyadh launched air strikes on Hodeida and Kamaran island on Friday. The Saudi civil defence also issued warnings for the port city of Jizan, close to the border with Yemen.

The ongoing conflict between the US and Iran has led to increased tensions in the region, with oil prices surging to $100 per barrel after the Houthis attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea.

At least 1 dead, several injured after van rams into crowd at Pride festival in Berlin

A white van rammed into a crowd at the Christopher Street Day festival in Berlin on Saturday evening, killing one person and injuring at least 16 others. The incident occurred during the annual LGBTQ+ Pride event, which was attended by hundreds of thousands of people. Police were quick to respond, canceling the event and urging everyone to leave the area.

According to police, a suspect has been identified as a member of Berlin's "Islamist scene," but they have not yet been arrested. The motive behind the attack is still unknown. Emergency services treated the injured at the scene, with some victims having life-threatening injuries.

Berlin Mayor Kai Wegner described the incident as an "attack on our free and cosmopolitan society." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz vowed to punish those responsible, saying he and Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt would "push to make sure this horrific act is fully investigated and punished."

Where the accounts differ

Maine Democrats formally nominate Troy Jackson as their new candidate for U.S. Senate

Troy Jackson was formally nominated by the Maine Democratic Party to run against Republican Senator Susan Collins in the upcoming election. Jackson, a former state Senate President and fifth-generation logger, won the nomination with 566 votes out of 571 delegates at the party's convention in Bangor. He replaces Graham Platner, who dropped out of the race after allegations of sexual assault surfaced.

Jackson has been endorsed by progressive champion Bernie Sanders and has run on an economic populist message, emphasizing issues like Medicare for All and raising taxes on the ultra-wealthy. However, his nomination has also raised concerns about his past behavior, including reports of a short temper and alleged mortgage improprieties.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News notes that some Democrats have expressed concern about Jackson's vetting process, with one strategist saying "it's very clear that Democrats should not be looking to Maine to flip the Senate." In contrast, NPR quotes Democratic state Sen. Stacy Brenner as saying that Jackson has evolved on issues like reproductive rights and is a strong candidate.

The Guardian notes that Jackson's nomination caps a chaotic search for a replacement after Platner's withdrawal, and highlights his unusual political biography as a champion of rural workers. CBS News emphasizes the abbreviated timeline for finding a new nominee, with only two weeks to select a candidate before the July 27 deadline.

New York Post reports on the swift coronation of Jackson, noting that he was essentially guaranteed the nomination after most delegates pledged their support. The Daily Wire criticizes the process as undemocratic, pointing out that not a single Mainer voted for Jackson as the nominee.

US imposes new tariffs on 60 trading partners as temporary duties expire

The US has imposed new tariffs on 60 trading partners, including the UK, China, and the European Union, in a move that replaces a temporary global tariff that expired on Friday. The new levies range from 10% to 12.5% and are intended to be more legally durable than their predecessors. The tariffs are based on claims that key economic partners have failed to properly tackle forced labor.

The new duties apply to the top 60 US trade partners, covering 99.4% of US imports, according to the Office of the US Trade Representative. Trading partners that have taken steps to ban forced labor face tariffs of 10%, while those countries that do not effectively prohibit the practice have a rate of 12.5%.

The move has been met with criticism from some quarters, with experts warning that it could lead to higher costs for businesses and consumers.

Where the accounts differ

  • NPR notes that the new tariffs are not significantly higher than their predecessors, but still higher than when Trump's second term started.
  • Fox Business highlights the fact that many products will be exempt from the tariffs, including oil and gas, fertilizer, and certain food products.

Trump orders signs outside Smithsonian warning of "inaccurate" information about American history

The order is part of a broader effort by the Trump administration to push back against what it sees as a politicized and biased approach to American history.

The Smithsonian Institution has defended its approach to American history, with Director Anthea Hartig stating that the museum aims to tell the truth about the country's past, including its failures to live up to its founding ideals.

More than 250,000 people flee wildfires in France and Spain

Devastating wildfires in Spain and France have forced more than 250,000 people to evacuate. In France, over 197,000 people were evacuated, with the interior minister describing it as "probably" the biggest peacetime evacuation the country has seen. The fires have also affected the Tour de France, with the route for the final stage shortened due to security and police being reassigned to assist with the fires.

In Spain, a national state of emergency was declared, and at least one person died in a fire in Manises, Valencia. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said that his government is working to provide shelter for those who evacuated or were confined because of the fires. The fires have burned down over 130,000 hectares of forest this year in Spain, which is about a third higher than the country's annual average.

Where the accounts differ

NPR reports that more than 167,000 people had been evacuated from France as of Saturday night, while The Guardian states that 197,000 people fled in France. BBC reports that over 300,000 people have fled fires in France and Spain, which is higher than the numbers reported by NPR and The Guardian.

India's education minister resigns after student protests over exam leaks and testing irregularities

India's Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on Saturday, meeting one of the key demands of students who have been protesting for weeks over allegations of failing to prevent leaks of medical school entrance exams. The protests, led by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), a youth movement that challenges Prime Minister Narendra Modi's handling of youth unemployment and educational opportunities, began in May after it was alleged that entrance exams had been leaked and sold to certain students.

The CJP called for an end to the weekslong effort to oust Pradhan after his announcement. The protests stemmed from allegations that entrance exams to highly competitive medical schools had been leaked and sold to certain students, forcing millions to retake the tests. Abhijeet Dipke, a Boston University graduate and the party's founder, rallied tens of thousands of students and supporters in New Delhi beginning in May to force Pradhan's resignation and demand compensation for the families of students who committed suicide after being affected by the entrance exam scandal.

Where the accounts differ
