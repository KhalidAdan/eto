Mullin spurns martyrdom concerns from livestreamed execution: ‘No one has to watch’

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin rejected the idea that livestreaming the execution of the Fort Hood mass shooter Nidal Malik Hasan could make him a martyr for his cause, saying those against the idea do not have to watch. Mullin told CBS News’s Face the Nation on Sunday that he sees no “elevated risk” of radicalization in the United States brought on by livestreaming the firing squad execution in December.

FOX News reported that the U.S. government intends to take Nidal Hasan to a firing range at Fort Hood and publicly execute him by firing squad in December. The outlet also noted that Americans will be able to watch the execution on live stream, which has transformed the conclusion of one of the darkest chapters in the Army's history into a more uncomfortable question about how a nation should reckon with the gravity of taking a life, even when that life belongs to a mass murderer.

Axios highlighted that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's announcement to livestream the execution reflects a shift toward putting raw, real-world violence on display for unlimited audiences. The outlet noted that this would be the first known public broadcast of a lawful execution, according to death penalty experts, and that tech companies like TikTok and Kick have said livestreaming the event would violate their policies.

Where the accounts differ

Washington Examiner omits any mention of the planned livestream and focuses solely on Mullin’s comments about the execution not making Hasan a martyr. FOX News includes details about the planned livestream and quotes Fort Hood victim Alonzo Lunsford, who survived being shot seven times by Hasan, about his views on the execution. Axios provides context about the broader trend of making violent events public and the potential implications for tech companies and free speech.

Sources Washington Examiner · FOX News · Axios · New York Post · The Guardian · NPR

Trump calls for Ukraine to replace Zelensky after criticizing diesel deal with Russia

President Donald Trump said on Saturday that it is "time for Ukraine to get a new president" after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky criticized a U.S.-Russia diesel deal. Trump accused Zelensky of failing to reach a peace agreement with Russia and of continuing to authorize attacks on Russian refineries. The deal, announced earlier in the week, allows Russia to resume diesel exports to the U.S. and global markets, with an initial 300,000 tonnes to be released by early April. Zelensky warned that the deal would provide Moscow with additional funds to continue its war in Ukraine.

Zelensky accused Russia of using the deal to justify continued attacks on Ukrainian cities, including a deadly strike on Zaporizhzhia that killed at least 20 people. He argued that the deal would allow Russia to fund its military operations, including the production of weapons and recruitment of mercenaries. The U.S. administration confirmed the agreement, with Trump stating it was part of an effort to lower fuel prices ahead of the midterm elections.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that Trump suggested Ukraine should "get a new leader who can make a deal" and that Zelensky accused Russia of using the deal to continue its war. Straight Arrow News adds that Zelensky directly linked the attack in Zaporizhzhia to the diesel deal, stating Russia "said thank you for the lifting of sanctions on diesel exports by dropping five glide bombs on residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia." The Hill and New York Post include details about the attack, including the number of casualties and the timing relative to the diesel deal announcement. Washington Examiner and Axios note Republican backlash against the deal, with Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick introducing legislation to block Russian oil purchases. The Hill also mentions Rep. Michael McCaul’s criticism of the deal, while Axios attributes the same concern to McCaul and adds that Fitzpatrick is using a discharge petition to force a vote.

Sources BBC · Straight Arrow News · Washington Examiner · Axios · The Hill · New York Post

Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia’s Riyadh airport kills 12, injures over 300

A missile strike on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport killed at least 12 people and injured more than 300, according to Saudi civil aviation authorities. The attack, which occurred on Saturday, targeted a complex housing terminals 3 and 4 for domestic flights. The victims included four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, and one person each from the United States, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan, and Egypt. The Saudi government suspended airport operations and warned citizens to avoid the facility. The attack is part of a series of strikes on Saudi infrastructure, with the Houthis claiming responsibility for several recent attacks. The United States condemned the attack, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio calling it a “reprehensible attack” and expressing support for Saudi Arabia. President Donald Trump said he was evaluating whether the U.S. should join Saudi strikes against the Houthis.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and CBS News both report that the attack killed 12 people, including one American, and injured 300. The Guardian and NPR report the same numbers but note that the attack was the deadliest in any Gulf Arab country since the start of the Iran war. The Washington Examiner and New York Post both mention the American victim but do not specify the exact number of casualties. The Guardian and NPR report that the attack was the deadliest in any Gulf Arab country since the start of the Iran war, while Al Jazeera and CBS News do not make this claim. The New York Post and Washington Examiner attribute the attack to the Houthis, while the Guardian and NPR do not explicitly name the attackers, though they imply the Houthis were responsible.

Four dead and thousands without power after Hurricane Isaias lashes US

Hurricane Isaias made landfall in Florida on Friday as a Category 2 storm with maximum sustained winds of 105mph (168km/h), causing widespread damage, power outages, and at least four deaths. The storm weakened to a post-tropical cyclone by Saturday, with officials reporting two deaths each in Alabama and Florida. In Florida, a woman died when a tree fell on her home, and another person died from carbon monoxide poisoning after running a generator in a garage. In Alabama, two deaths were reported, though details were not provided. The storm caused extensive damage, including roof destruction, flooded roads, and downed trees. Power outages affected hundreds of thousands of people across Florida, Alabama, and Georgia, with some areas experiencing over 900,000 outages. Officials warned of ongoing flood risks and potential tornadoes as the storm moved inland.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and NPR report at least two deaths, with the Daily Wire specifying one in Florida and one in Alabama, while NPR confirms four deaths but does not specify the states. The Guardian and BBC provide more details on the causes of death, including carbon monoxide poisoning and tree-related incidents. The Daily Caller focuses on the impact of the storm on fuel supply and does not mention the number of deaths. The Guardian and BBC mention specific locations for deaths, while NPR and The Daily Wire do not. The Guardian and BBC attribute the deaths in Alabama to trees falling on homes, while NPR and The Daily Wire do not specify the cause of the Alabama deaths beyond a tree falling on a house.

Pope Leo XIV to undergo lung surgery this week

Pope Leo XIV announced Sunday that he will have surgery this week to remove a "localized nodule" in his lung. The procedure is expected to take place at Rome's Gemelli Hospital, where popes typically receive medical attention. The Vatican spokesperson, Matteo Bruni, stated the surgery is scheduled for the first days of the week, with a recovery time of about one week. The Pope also mentioned his upcoming international trip to Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru in November, though it is unclear if the surgery will affect his itinerary.

The Pope described the nodule as found at a "very early stage" and emphasized that doctors recommended the surgery. He thanked the faithful for their spiritual support and entrusted himself to their prayers. The American Cancer Society defines a lung nodule as a small abnormal area, less than or equal to 3 cm, found during a CT scan. Dr. Panagis Galiatsatos, a pulmonary physician, noted that most lung nodules are benign and that surgery is considered when there is significant concern for cancer.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and CBS News both report that the Pope described the nodule as "localized" and found at an "early stage," but CBS News adds that the nodule was "circumscribed," a term not used by NPR. FOX News refers to the nodule as a "tumor," a term not used by the other outlets. The Daily Caller mentions the nodule was "contained within a single area of the lung," a detail not included in the other accounts. Straight Arrow News includes a reference to the Pope's statement on the death penalty, which is not mentioned in the other accounts. BBC notes that the Pope's predecessor, Pope Francis, had a history of lung issues, a detail not included in the other accounts.

Judge orders the Trump administration to cancel taxpayer-funded promotional TV ads

A federal judge in New York ordered the Trump administration to cancel any remaining taxpayer-funded television commercials promoting his political message. The order, issued by U.S. District Judge Jennifer L. Rochon, came after a coalition including Common Cause and Democracy Forward challenged the ads as an illegal misuse of funds. The judge granted a temporary restraining order, instructing the administration to stop any further development, creation, or airing of the ads for two weeks. The Department of Homeland Security allocated $20 million in taxpayer money for the campaign, which began in late September and includes ads glorifying Trump and echoing his political messaging.

The White House defended the ads as public service announcements, similar to past efforts by previous administrations. However, critics argue the recent ads differ from typical public service announcements because they do not aim to help the public benefit from specific government programs. The use of public funds for the ads drew criticism from some Republicans, including Senate Majority Leader John Thune, who stated, "It shouldn’t be paid for with taxpayer dollars." The Democratic National Committee has also sued the administration over the ads.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and CBS News both report that the judge ordered the administration to cancel the ads and that the Department of Homeland Security allocated $20 million for the campaign. CBS News adds that the funds were allocated for a "National Media Campaign" starting on Sept. 20 and that the White House Office of Management and Budget granted DHS's Customs and Border Protection $20 million for "OBBB Commemorative Events," according to a nongovernment database. ABC News does not mention these specific allocations. Axios reports that the ruling stems from a suit filed by Common Cause, Democracy Forward, and others in the Southern District of New York, and that a similar lawsuit was filed by the Democratic National Committee in D.C. federal court. CBS News and ABC News do not mention the Democratic National Committee's lawsuit.

Sources ABC News · CBS News · Axios

Donald Trump Says He Would 'Love' to Have John Fetterman as a Republican

President Donald Trump said Saturday he would "love" to see Democratic Senator John Fetterman of Pennsylvania become a Republican, renewing speculation about the senator's future. Trump described Fetterman as a "very solid person" with "great common sense" and "magnificent parents." The comments were among Trump's clearest public efforts yet to encourage Fetterman to join the GOP at a time when control of the Senate remains in play ahead of next month's midterm elections. Fetterman has consistently said he does not intend to become a Republican, though he has frequently frustrated fellow Democrats by breaking with the party on several issues, including immigration and support for Israel.

Fetterman has repeatedly rejected the notion of becoming a Republican, writing in The Washington Post in May that he would be a "terrible Republican who still votes overwhelmingly with Democrats." Despite this, he has made appearances at Republican events and recorded a video for a Republican midterm convention praising fellow Pennsylvania Sen. Dave McCormick. Polling shows Fetterman has stronger support among Republican voters than among Democrats, with 73% of Pennsylvania Republicans viewing him favorably compared to 22% of Democrats.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News and the Washington Examiner also include this line, though FOX News adds that Trump said "it’s going to be hard for him to get the nomination as a Democrat." At least 139 people dead after boat capsizes off Djibouti, U.N. Says (massive loss of life impacting families and highlighting unsafe migration conditions)

Children among 139 dead after boat capsizes off Djibouti, UN says

A boat carrying 305 migrants from Djibouti to Yemen capsized near Obock on Thursday night, resulting in at least 139 deaths and dozens missing, according to the UN's migration office. The International Organization for Migration (IOM) reported that around 82 people were rescued. All survivors, identified as Ethiopian nationals, received medical care and psychological support. The IOM revised its death toll from 109 to 139 after recovering 30 more bodies. The incident highlights the dangers faced by migrants seeking work in Gulf countries, with tens of thousands making the journey annually.

The IOM noted that the boat sank due to overcrowding, with the incident occurring shortly after departure on the evening of October 8. Survivors were taken to an IOM rescue center in Obock. The IOM's Djibouti mission chief, Alexandre Coissac, described the tragedy as a "stark reminder" of the risks migrants face. The IOM also mentioned that the crossing from the Horn of Africa to the Arabian Peninsula is a common but dangerous route, with more than 900 migrants dying or going missing along the route last year.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that the boat capsized near Obock on Thursday night, while FOX News states the capsizing occurred "Thursday night or early Friday" near Guhere, north of Obock. BBC mentions that the IOM revised its toll from 109 to 139 after recovering 30 more bodies, whereas FOX News notes that the confirmed death toll includes only victims buried by authorities and may rise as additional bodies are verified. FOX News also includes a quote from IOM Chief of Mission Alexandre Coissac stating that some victims were buried without names in a common grave, a detail not mentioned in BBC.

Sources BBC · FOX News
