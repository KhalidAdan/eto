FAA Ground Stop Blamed on Router Overload and Copper Wire Replacement

On November 13, 2026, a nationwide FAA ground stop stranded hundreds of thousands of airline travelers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy attributed the incident to a router overload caused by the replacement of copper wire with fiber-optic cable in El Dorado, Arkansas. He stated that the router in question was broadcasting data into the national airspace network, leading to system instability. The ground stop lasted over four hours, with flights resuming by 8:15 p.m. Friday. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford confirmed that the system was stable post-ground stop and that no lives were at risk. Duffy also called for additional congressional funding to complete the FAA modernization, which includes replacing all copper wiring by the end of 2028.

Duffy emphasized that the issue was not a cyberattack but rather a technical problem with the aging air traffic control system. He noted that the FAA had spent approximately 38% of the $12.5 billion allocated for modernization, with an additional $10.1 billion needed to complete the project. During a press conference, Duffy mentioned that the router in question was being investigated at an L3Harris lab in Florida, and that the issue may have originated from either user error or a faulty installation.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones reports that Duffy blamed previous administrations for a lack of investment, citing 30 years of underfunding. ABC News states that Duffy attributed the issue to a router in Arkansas and that the problem was being investigated with the FBI. CBS News, however, focuses on unrelated bank outages and does not mention the FAA incident. The Hill highlights Duffy’s $50 billion funding request and mentions that Democrats are skeptical of the request. The Christian Science Monitor details the number of canceled flights and the impact on travelers. Washington Examiner notes that Mullin cited an unnamed expert blaming AI for the outages, a claim not mentioned by other outlets.

Sources Mother Jones · ABC News · CBS News · The Hill · Christian Science Monitor · Washington Examiner

Bessent: Nvidia should ‘go outside the company’ for next CEO

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged Nvidia’s board to seek a new CEO from outside the company, citing the need for a dramatic shake-up following former CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation. Bessent made the remarks during an interview with Larry Kudlow, emphasizing that while Huang is a “wonderful innovator,” the company must prioritize compliance. Huang stepped down after admitting to an affair with an employee and being accused of espionage.

President Donald Trump is set to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping during a high-stakes Asia trip, bringing along two Cabinet officials with differing views on China’s access to U.S. AI technology. Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick are accompanying Trump, despite reports of a dispute over whether to blacklist a company suspected of aiding China in obtaining restricted Nvidia chips.

Russia downs over 1,400 Ukrainian drones as Kyiv hits a key bridge in Crimea

Ukraine launched a major drone attack on Russia, with Russian air defenses reporting the downing of over 1,400 drones during the night. The attack coincided with Ukraine targeting a key bridge in Crimea, the Kerch Bridge, which connects the peninsula to mainland Russia. Russian officials confirmed that the bridge was struck again, leading to the suspension of traffic. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the strike as an "unprecedented operation" in response to Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure. The attack also included the use of cruise missiles, according to Zelenskyy.

The Kerch Bridge, a critical supply route for Russian military and civilian traffic, was hit for the second consecutive day. Russian-installed authorities in Crimea reported damage to the bridge, though the extent was unclear. Ukrainian officials claimed the attack caused significant damage, including the collapse of one of the bridge's arches. The strike is seen as a symbolic blow to Russian President Vladimir Putin, especially ahead of an upcoming trilateral summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and U.S. President Donald Trump.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and Al Jazeera both report that Russia downed 1,441 drones on Tuesday, while ABC News and The Daily Wire cite Russia's Defense Ministry as reporting 1,441 drones on Tuesday and 1,484 drones from Sunday night through Monday. FOX News reports Russia intercepted 1,336 drones on Monday, which is lower than the numbers reported by other outlets. Al Jazeera and ABC News mention that the Kerch Bridge was hit for the second consecutive day, while FOX News and The Daily Wire state that the bridge was struck for the second time in 18 months and that the damage was described as minor by the Kremlin. Newsweek and ABC News describe the bridge as partially collapsing, while NPR and Al Jazeera do not specify the extent of the damage.

Lindsay Clancy Is Headed Back To Court As Judge Sets Retrial Date

Lindsay Clancy, 36, will face a second murder trial for the deaths of her three children, Cora (5), Dawson (3), and Callan (8 months), following a mistrial in her first trial. A Massachusetts judge, William Sullivan, set jury selection for March 29, 2027, with pre-trial hearings scheduled for late December and February 18, 2027. Prosecutors from Plymouth County District Attorney Timothy Cruz’s office confirmed they would proceed with the case, though they did not explicitly state the charges. Clancy’s defense attorney, Kevin Reddington, interpreted the prosecution’s push for a jury trial as an indication that first-degree murder charges would be pursued again.

The first trial ended in a mistrial on September 4, 2026, after a jury deadlocked 11-1. The defense had previously sought to have the holdout juror removed and to dismiss the case on double jeopardy grounds, but those motions were denied. Clancy’s attorneys argued she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis at the time of the killings and should not be held criminally responsible.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and BBC note that the district attorney has not officially confirmed the charges, while FOX News and The Daily Caller state that prosecutors did not specifically address whether they intended to seek the same charges. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller mention that Sullivan pushed for a mid-March trial date, while FOX News and NPR note that prosecutors had initially wanted a May trial date.

Robert Pattinson Announced as New James Bond

The James Bond franchise has officially announced Robert Pattinson as its next 007. Pattinson, known for roles in "The Batman," "Twilight," and "Harry Potter," will take over from Daniel Craig, who retired after "No Time to Die" in 2021. The new film will be directed by Denis Villeneuve, who previously worked with Pattinson on the "Dune" franchise. The film will be the 26th Bond movie and the first under Amazon MGM's creative control since they acquired the franchise in 2025.

Pattinson expressed his excitement about the role, calling it "a dream come true." He stated that the franchise has "the timeless soul of 007" and that he is "overjoyed" to join the project. The British royal family responded to the news on social media, welcoming Pattinson to "His Majesty’s Secret Service, Mr. Bond." The announcement ended months of speculation about who would succeed Craig, with fans debating potential candidates like Callum Turner, Josh O’Connor, and Nicholas Hoult.

Where the accounts differ

The Free Press and The Daily Wire both describe the selection of Pattinson as the new Bond, but The Free Press emphasizes the cultural significance of the Bond character across different eras, while The Daily Wire focuses on the literary roots of the Bond character and criticizes recent film adaptations for straying from Ian Fleming's original novels. CBS News and The Daily Caller provide more detailed information about the casting process, including the screen tests and the involvement of Steven Knight as the screenwriter. The Daily Wire also mentions Barbara Broccoli's comments on the gender of the next Bond, which is not included in the other accounts.

Sources The Free Press · CBS News · The Daily Wire · Slate · The Daily Caller

White House Counsel Will Scharf warned Democratic lawmakers against using congressional oversight powers for purposes other than legitimate legislative tasks. In a letter to Rep. Illegal migrant accused of attempted murder at large after Rikers release despite ICE detainer: DHS

A Guyanese citizen living in the United States illegally remains at large after being released from custody in New York City earlier this month, despite facing multiple violent felony charges and an active federal immigration detainer, according to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). Delon Raymond Jaundoo was arrested in Brooklyn on charges including attempted murder, first- and second-degree assault, criminal use of a firearm, and multiple counts of criminal possession of a loaded weapon. His prior criminal record also includes an arrest for third-degree assault. U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) lodged an immigration detainer for Jaundoo on April 29, which the New York City Department of Correction declined to honor, allowing him to be released from Rikers Island.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin criticized New York’s sanctuary policies following the release, arguing that state and local laws prevent cooperation with federal immigration authorities even in cases involving violent offenses. Jaundoo lawfully entered the country during the Obama administration but overstayed his authorized stay, which expired in January 2015, according to DHS. Jaundoo’s release comes amid ongoing restrictions on local cooperation with federal immigration enforcement. Upon taking office, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed an executive order limiting city coordination with ICE, and a state law signed by Gov. Kathy Hochul took effect in August barring local authorities from cooperating with federal immigration officials.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post states that DHS officials stated Jaundoo lawfully entered the country during the Obama administration but overstayed his authorized stay, which expired in January 2015. FOX News does not include this detail about Jaundoo’s entry and overstaying. The New York Post also mentions that a spokesperson for Hochul told Fox News Digital that “Governor Hochul has been clear: New York State is not and never will be a sanctuary for violent criminals,” while FOX News includes this quote but does not attribute it to a spokesperson for Hochul. The New York Post notes that DHS stated several other illegal immigrants arrested for or convicted of violent crimes have similarly been released from custody under the state’s current policies, while FOX News does not include this statement from DHS.

Sources New York Post · FOX News
