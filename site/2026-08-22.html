New details about Trump's plans for Kennedy Center and White House ballroom

President Trump's plans for the John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts and the White House ballroom are moving forward despite ongoing legal challenges. Court filings reveal that the Kennedy Center's board, dominated by Trump allies, has approved a plan to rename the building in his honor and include his name on its facade. The board also intends to rename the physical site "President Donald J. Trump Plaza." Meanwhile, the White House ballroom project, which involves millions of pounds of steel and rebar, is proceeding with a crew of over 200 workers. The Supreme Court has temporarily allowed construction to continue, though the legality of the project remains in question.

The Kennedy Center's board has faced legal challenges after a judge ruled their previous decision to name the building after Trump was unlawful. The board has now voted to add Trump's name to the building's facade, with plans to update the inscription once a $100 million fund is reached. The Justice Department stated that the tarp covering the facade is due to water testing and structural repairs, though the exact duration of the tarp's presence remains unclear.

Where the accounts differ

The Nation claims the tarp was used to remove Trump's name from the building and that the marble may have been damaged. The New York Post states that the Supreme Court allowed the ballroom construction to continue, while The Daily Wire and Axios focus on unrelated stories about a Cambridge professor and a scholar's suspension. Charter plane crash kills 8 near remote radar site in western Alaska, military says

A charter aircraft carrying eight people crashed at a remote radar site in western Alaska on Thursday, killing all on board, the US military said. The crash occurred near Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site Airport, approximately 450 miles west of Anchorage, Alaska. The plane, a Cessna 441, had taken off from Anchorage and was believed to have crashed early Thursday afternoon on approach to Cape Newenham. The Federal Aviation Administration confirmed the crash happened about 12:15 p.m. Thursday west of Cape Newenham. The National Transportation Safety Board’s Alaska region chief, Clint Johnson, stated there were two pilots and six passengers on board.

The crash site is managed by the Pacific Air Forces Regional Support Center and is part of a network of remote radar sites that tracks aircraft operating in Alaska’s airspace and along its borders. The US military described those on board as “dedicated professionals carrying out a vital mission in a demanding environment.” The US Army Corps of Engineers confirmed two of its employees were among those killed, and the NTSB and FAA are investigating the crash.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and NPR report the crash occurred near Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site Airport, while the Daily Wire and CBS News specify it was near the same location. The Daily Caller reports a midair collision between a civilian plane and a police helicopter in Pennsylvania, which is a different incident. The Daily Wire and CBS News mention the plane was carrying Army engineers, while the New York Post and NPR do not specify the identities of the victims. The Daily Wire and CBS News attribute the cause of the crash to a military mission, while the New York Post and NPR do not provide details on the specific work the individuals were doing. The Daily Wire and CBS News state that two Army Corps of Engineers employees were among the victims, while the New York Post and NPR do not specify the identities of the victims.

Sources New York Post · The Daily Caller · The Daily Wire · NPR · CBS News · UPI

Trump's 50% tariffs take effect after US, Canada fail to reach trade deal

The U.S. and Canada failed to reach a trade deal, leading to the imposition of 50% tariffs on Canadian goods. The tariffs, targeting a range of products including food items and alcoholic beverages, took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on Saturday. Both sides blamed each other for the breakdown in negotiations. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer stated that Canada declined to finalize the trade deal under the terms agreed earlier in the week, while Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney suspended trade talks, citing "last-minute changes" to U.S. proposed terms as unfair and uneconomic. Where the accounts differ

IndyCars set to race on streets of nation's capital. Here's what to know

IndyCars will race on the streets of Washington, D.C., for the first time during the Freedom 250 Grand Prix, a 250-mile event celebrating the country's 250th birthday. The race will take place on Sunday, with practice and qualifying runs on Saturday. The 1.66-mile track will include Pennsylvania Avenue and Independence Avenue, with speeds expected to exceed 180 mph. The event is expected to draw about 140,000 spectators each day, with free tickets distributed through a lottery. President Donald Trump is expected to attend, and the race is supported by the White House and IndyCar, which is owned by Penske Entertainment Corp. Organizers have resurfaced roadways and welded over 200 manhole covers shut to prevent them from flying off during the race. The event is part of a series of celebrations for the 250th anniversary of America's independence.

Organizers have also constructed risers for spectators and bridges to allow people to navigate the closed streets. The event is being run by the Freedom 250 Grand Prix organization, which is supported by key partners including IndyCar's promotional alliance with Monumental Sports & Entertainment. The race is not without controversy, as some preservationists have raised concerns about potential damage to historic buildings and art due to vibrations from the race. The U.S. Capitol and other historic sites are located near the race course.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News reports that the race will be broadcast live on Fox, while Just The News states that the race will be covered by The Center Square. Christian Science Monitor mentions that the event is part of Trump's Freedom 250 celebrations, which include mixed martial arts events, a detail not included in ABC News. Straight Arrow News includes quotes from Karen Jarussi and Colton Snedecor, which are not present in other accounts. New York Post and Straight Arrow News both mention Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's comments about the event bringing America together, but only New York Post provides specific details about Duffy's interview. Just The News states that the event is part of over $90 million in taxpayer funds spent on America's 250th anniversary celebrations, a figure not mentioned in other accounts. Christian Science Monitor and Straight Arrow News both mention concerns about the race's impact on historic sites, but only Christian Science Monitor includes quotes from political strategist Michael Caputo and political commentator Chris Cillizza.

World Bank projects war-hit Lebanon’s economy to contract by 6.4 percent

The World Bank reported that Lebanon’s economy is projected to shrink by 6.4 percent in 2026 due to the ongoing conflict with Israel, which has disrupted the country’s fragile recovery. The report noted that Lebanon entered 2026 on stronger footing after a 4.2 percent GDP growth in 2025, the highest since the 2019 financial crisis. The conflict, which escalated in March 2026, has damaged housing and infrastructure, displaced communities, and disrupted supply chains, tourism, and domestic demand. Inflation is expected to rise to 17.5 percent, driven by supply disruptions, higher shipping costs, and volatile fuel prices.

The report highlights that Lebanon’s economic recovery has been sharply interrupted by the renewed conflict, exacerbating an already severe social and economic crisis. The World Bank emphasized the importance of advancing reforms, particularly in banking sector restructuring and fiscal management, to restore confidence and support reconstruction. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has endorsed legislative progress on financial sector reforms and plans to resume technical meetings in Beirut to evaluate further structural measures.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that the conflict escalation occurred in March 2026 and that 1.2 million people were displaced, while UPI states that the conflict began on March 2 and that 4,348 people have been killed and 12,703 injured since March 2. UPI also provides casualty figures and mentions the Lebanese Health Ministry’s latest count, which Al Jazeera does not include. UPI notes that Lebanon’s government recorded a 3.9% GDP surplus in 2025, a detail absent in Al Jazeera’s account. Al Jazeera mentions the IMF’s endorsement of legislative progress and its planned technical meetings, while UPI includes additional context about the financial crisis and the impact of the conflict on public finances.

Sources Al Jazeera · UPI
