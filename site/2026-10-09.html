Trump Condemns Fox News Coverage of El-Sayed, GOP Frustrated

President Trump criticized Fox News for its coverage of Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, accusing the network of promoting "Dumocrats" and losing its way. The White House reportedly reached out to Fox to complain about an interview with El-Sayed, which Trump called "softball" on his social media platform. Republicans have been concerned that Fox's coverage is helping El-Sayed, a Muslim candidate and critic of Israel's government, appear more moderate than he is. El-Sayed, who is running against former Rep. Mike Rogers (R), is seen as a potential first Muslim senator.

Fox News has played a significant role in El-Sayed's campaign, with his ads airing 129 times on the network between October 1 and 6, more than twice as often as on ESPN. GOP strategists have expressed frustration that El-Sayed is frequently featured on Fox, including during interviews with hosts like Jesse Watters, who praised El-Sayed's performance in an August interview. Despite this, El-Sayed has shown he can handle tough questioning, as noted by a Michigan-based GOP strategist.

Where the accounts differ

Axios reports that the White House reached out to Fox to complain about an interview with El-Sayed, while Newsweek and The Daily Wire focus on the debate between El-Sayed and Rogers, with Newsweek highlighting the tariff issue and The Daily Wire noting Rogers' shift on trade policy. Mother Jones and Al Jazeera cover El-Sayed's past criticism of Obama, which is not mentioned in Axios or The Daily Wire. Washington Examiner emphasizes the personal attacks and lack of policy agreement during the debate, which is not detailed in the other accounts.

ICE agent shoots man during immigration operation in New York City

An ICE agent shot and injured a man during a targeted immigration enforcement operation in New York City on Thursday, according to the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). The shooting occurred around 4 p.m. in the Marble Hill neighborhood of the Bronx. The man, identified as Oscar Bergdahl, 28, had prior convictions for grand larceny and multiple arrests involving assault, robbery, drug possession, and weapons possession. He was also linked to the Trinitarios gang. The man was taken to a nearby hospital and is reported to be conscious. A 5-year-old boy, believed to be Bergdahl's son, was inside the vehicle during the shooting and was confirmed safe after a welfare check. New York City Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch confirmed that at least one agent discharged seven rounds, striking the driver in the neck below his left ear. Protests erupted in the Bronx in response to the shooting, leading to multiple arrests as demonstrators blocked federal vehicles and smashed car windows.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani condemned the shooting, calling it an "unconscionable and incredibly serious situation," and accused ICE of acting "with impunity." He stated that he had spoken to President Donald Trump for about two hours to demand the illegal immigrant's release, but the efforts were unsuccessful. Mamdani also called for ICE to be abolished and for federal immigration agents to leave New York City.

Mother Jones and Al Jazeera refer to the man as Oscar Belgal, while CBS News and Newsweek also use the name Oscar Belgal. Mother Jones and Al Jazeera mention that the child was unharmed, while FOX News and CBS News confirm the child's safety. Al Jazeera and CBS News describe the shooting as occurring after an ICE agent approached the vehicle, while FOX News and Newsweek state that the man was surrounded before being shot. Mother Jones and Al Jazeera note that the shooting is part of a broader pattern of ICE shootings, which FOX News and CBS News do not mention.

Maduro and wife expected to face charges over alleged torture of Americans

Former Venezuelan dictator Nicolas Maduro and his wife are expected to face new charges related to their alleged role in the torture of U.S. citizens, officials said Wednesday. The Trump administration captured Maduro from Venezuela in a dramatic military operation in January, bringing the Venezuelan dictator and his wife to the United States to stand trial on narco-terrorism charges and counts related to the possession of machine guns. Trump Approves Military Execution of Fort Hood Shooter Nidal Hasan

The U.S. military has approved the execution of Nidal Malik Hasan, the former Army psychiatrist convicted of killing 13 people in the 2009 Fort Hood massacre. Hasan is scheduled to be executed by firing squad on December 3 at Fort Hood, Texas, marking the first U.S. military execution since 1961. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth recommended the execution method, and President Donald Trump approved it, according to the Pentagon. Hasan, who was convicted in 2013, admitted to the shooting and claimed he was acting in defense of Muslims. The attack remains the deadliest mass shooting at a U.S. military base.

The execution will be carried out by an Army firing squad, with the Pentagon confirming that the event will be livestreamed. Defense Secretary Hegseth stated that the public should be able to watch the execution, emphasizing the need for transparency. This would be the first public execution in the U.S. in nearly a century. Hasan is one of four individuals currently on military death row, all convicted of murder. The military has not executed a service member since 1961, when John Bennett was executed for rape and attempted murder in Austria.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and NPR state that Hasan was shot four times during the attack, whereas The Hill and BBC mention he was shot by civilian security officers and became paralyzed. USS Lincoln finally docks in San Diego after 10-month mission

The USS Abraham Lincoln, an aircraft carrier, docked in San Diego after a deployment that lasted over 300 days. The ship had been supporting U.S.-Israel operations against Iran. Thousands of crew members were welcomed home by families and friends, with many expressing relief and joy at the return. The deployment, which included 264 consecutive days at sea without a port call, set a record for modern naval history. The ship’s return was marked by a large gathering at the naval base, with emotional reunions and celebrations.

The deployment was extended due to the U.S. and Israel’s war with Iran, which diverted the Lincoln from its original planned return date in the spring. The ship’s crew, consisting of around 5,000 sailors and marines, faced challenging conditions at sea, including food shortages, poor living conditions, and reports of mental health struggles. The Navy confirmed that eight sailors from the Lincoln and its strike group had attempted suicide during the deployment. Despite these issues, the ship was honored with the Presidential Unit Citation, the highest award for a military unit.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that the Lincoln’s deployment lasted 321 days, including 264 consecutive days at sea, while the Hill and Al Jazeera state it was 322 days, with 265 consecutive days at sea. The New York Post and Al Jazeera mention that the deployment included 265 consecutive days at sea, while the Guardian and NPR cite 264. The Guardian and NPR also note that the Lincoln’s deployment was the second longest in the post-Vietnam War era, while the Hill and Al Jazeera refer to it as the longest in modern naval history. The Hill and New York Post attribute the suicide attempts to the deployment’s length and conditions, while the Guardian and NPR attribute them to the war with Iran and the ship’s extended time at sea.

Hurricane Isaias strengthens into the first hurricane of the Atlantic season

Hurricane Isaias intensified into the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season late Wednesday, according to the National Hurricane Center. The storm is expected to make landfall along the U.S. Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday, bringing heavy rains and potential flash flooding. The National Weather Service reported that Isaias had maximum sustained winds of 100 mph, with the storm's core located about 420 miles south-southwest of the Mississippi River. The hurricane formed late in the season, nearly tying a 121-year-old record for the latest-forming hurricane. Oil production and tourism in the Gulf Coast region have already been affected, with some oil wells paused and events like the National Shrimp Festival canceled.

Maricarmen, the 87-year-old woman whose eviction shook Spain, has died
