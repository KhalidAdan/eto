Trump’s Iran War Is Going Backward

On Saturday, a senior Iranian national security official issued a set of demands that the US must meet before a key waterway for oil shipping can be reopened. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, the secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, called for the US to lift its sanctions and naval blockade on Iran, “completely compensate” Iran for war damages, “unconditionally” release frozen Iranian assets, and end all attacks on Iran’s allies in the region. According to multiple media outlets, the US government did not deliver an immediate response to the demands. Many of them resemble those in the interim ceasefire deal signed by the US and Iran in June. Back then, the Trump administration said that it would only lift sanctions completely if the two countries came to an agreement on Iran’s nuclear development. While there was an initial ceasefire, it did not last. A couple of days later, Iran’s military closed the Strait of Hormuz, a critical oil transit point, stating the US violated their agreement by allowing Israel to continue bombing Lebanon. Combat resumed and even expanded to Saudi Arabia and the Houthis in Yemen.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and Straight Arrow News both report that Zolghadr listed the same conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, including lifting sanctions, ending the naval blockade, and compensating Iran for war damages. However, Straight Arrow News adds that Zolghadr also demanded the US end aggression against Iran, Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen, and Iraq, and withdraw its naval and air forces from Iran. The Washington Examiner and New York Post also mention these additional demands, while The Hill and UPI do not. The Hill reports that Vice President Vance said the US is “in the middle of the game” with Iran, while the New York Post and Straight Arrow News attribute this statement to Vance as well. The Washington Examiner and New York Post also note that Trump claimed the negotiations were going “very well” and a deal could be “soon,” a claim not mentioned in Mother Jones or Straight Arrow News.

Sources Mother Jones · The Hill · Straight Arrow News · Washington Examiner · New York Post · UPI

Netanyahu Rejects Trump’s Gaza Plan: No Withdrawal Until Hamas Gives Up ‘All Weaponry’

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu rejected President Donald Trump’s 15-point plan for Gaza, stating that Israel will not withdraw from the territory until Hamas is fully disarmed. Netanyahu emphasized that the Israeli military will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas surrenders all its weapons, including heavy and light arms. He said the two sides are still discussing the matter, with some ideas acceptable and others unacceptable to Israel. Trump had promoted the plan as a historic agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups in Gaza, with a phased process involving Israeli withdrawal and the establishment of a new Palestinian administration under U.S. supervision.

The plan, negotiated with Arab mediators in Cairo and overseen by Trump’s Board of Peace, outlined a phased process for Hamas to surrender weapons, disclose tunnel locations, and allow a new Palestinian administration to take control. Israeli forces would leave only after areas were verified as demilitarized. Netanyahu, however, insisted that full disarmament must come first, and Israeli troops would not withdraw beyond current lines until Hamas’s weapons are fully decommissioned. The Israeli military has scaled back attacks since a meeting with the Board of Peace’s representative, Nikolay Mladenov, on Monday.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that Netanyahu’s comments were directed at the U.S. and that Israel has continued attacks on Gaza despite the October 2025 ceasefire. The Daily Wire and UPI mention that Israel has scaled back attacks since meeting with Mladenov, while The Daily Caller and Straight Arrow News include statistics on Palestinian casualties and the International Criminal Court’s arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant. The Guardian and The Daily Wire note that Netanyahu is facing elections in October, while The Daily Caller and Straight Arrow News include quotes from Hamas officials about expecting the U.S. to pressure Netanyahu. The Daily Wire and The Guardian attribute the characterization of the plan as “genuine disarmament” to Netanyahu, while The Daily Caller and Straight Arrow News attribute it to Netanyahu as well.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Guardian · NPR · Straight Arrow News · UPI · The Daily Caller

Woman, baby die after vessel overturns in New York Harbor

A vessel carrying 14 people overturned in New York Harbor near Liberty Island, resulting in the deaths of a woman and her 5-month-old baby. Authorities confirmed the boat's captain, Manuel Hernandez, was arrested for reckless endangerment. The New York Police Department responded to the incident around 10:25 p.m. to 10:55 p.m. on Saturday. Twelve people were rescued from the water before police arrived, with the woman and baby later found and taken to NYU Langone Hospital, where they were pronounced dead. Survivors were reported to have minor injuries and were taken to hospitals in stable condition. The Coast Guard is investigating whether the vessel operated illegally as a charter.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and UPI both identified the woman as Sara Sanchez and the baby as Antonella Garcia, while FOX News and The Daily Wire did not release their identities. UPI and the New York Post mentioned that the baby's father, who was Sanchez's boyfriend, was also on the boat, a detail not included in CBS News or FOX News. The New York Post and Straight Arrow News reported the boat was 21 feet or 22 feet long, while CBS News and UPI described it as a 2019 21-foot white Yamaha AR210 runabout. The New York Post and Straight Arrow News noted the boat was dangerously overloaded, a claim not made by CBS News or UPI. FOX News and The Daily Wire reported the boat was a 22-foot bowrider, while CBS News and UPI described it as a 21-foot runabout. The New York Post and Straight Arrow News stated the boat overturned around 10:30 p.m., while CBS News and UPI reported the time as 10:55 p.m. and 10:25 p.m., respectively.

Sources CBS News · FOX News · UPI · Straight Arrow News · New York Post · The Daily Wire

Trump says Will Scharf will replace David Warrington as White House counsel in September

President Donald Trump announced that White House Staff Secretary Will Scharf will replace David Warrington as Assistant to the President and White House Counsel starting September 1. Trump praised Scharf for his legal background, including his work as a federal prosecutor and his representation of Trump in multiple cases, including at the Supreme Court. Scharf, who previously served as chair of the National Capital Planning Commission, oversaw the approval of Trump’s $400 million White House ballroom project. The White House counsel’s role includes advising on legal and policy matters, coordinating with the Justice Department, and managing congressional investigations.

The White House counsel’s office is central to the administration’s legal strategy, handling presidential pardons, judicial appointments, and reviewing legislation. Scharf, who joined Trump’s legal team in October 2023, was involved in the presidential immunity case that led to the Supreme Court’s ruling in favor of Trump. The appointment comes amid preparations for the midterm elections, with Democrats vowing to pursue investigations into Trump and his administration if they gain control of Congress.

Where the accounts differ

The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both mention that Scharf will replace Warrington effective September 1, while The Daily Wire and UPI state the effective date as September 1 as well. The Washington Examiner and ABC News note that Warrington is leaving for the private sector, though the Washington Examiner does not specify his new role, while ABC News and The Guardian describe him as returning to private practice. The Daily Wire and UPI mention that Scharf was part of the legal team that argued for Trump’s immunity in the Supreme Court case, while The Guardian and ABC News attribute this to Scharf’s involvement in the case. The Washington Examiner and The Guardian both mention Scharf’s role in the National Capital Planning Commission, but The Washington Examiner adds that he led the commission, while The Guardian states he chaired it. The Daily Wire and UPI note that Scharf warned against suspending habeas corpus protections for illegal immigrants, a claim not made by The Guardian or ABC News.

Sources Washington Examiner · The Daily Wire · ABC News · UPI · The Guardian · Al Jazeera

Crews battle wildfires in western U.S. and Canada

Wildfires are spreading rapidly across the western U.S. and Canada, with evacuation orders in place for multiple regions. The National Interagency Fire Center reported 183 new fires, including eight large ones, across the U.S. since Saturday, with over 30,000 personnel and 640 crews assigned to the fires. In Utah, the Rocky Canyon Fire has grown to over 2,800 acres and is 0% contained, leading to evacuations in Morgan County. Two pilots died in a helicopter crash while battling the Widemouth 2 Fire in Fishlake National Forest, and the fire has scorched over 118,000 acres, becoming the largest in Utah this year. In Oregon, a bulldozer operator died while fighting the Wrights Spring Fire, which has spread over 33,000 acres. In Canada, British Columbia has seen over 100 wildfires, with the Bald Range wildfire prompting more than 20,000 people to evacuate. A state of emergency was declared in the province, and officials warned of "explosive" fire conditions due to prolonged drought and high temperatures.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and CBS News both report that the Widemouth 2 Fire has scorched over 118,000 acres and is the largest fire in Utah this year, surpassing the Babylon Fire. However, CBS News also mentions that the Bald Range wildfire has scorched more than 53 square miles, while BBC and UPI report it as 13,618 hectares (33,650 acres). The Guardian and CBS News state that an 80-year-old woman died while trying to escape the wildfire, while UPI and Washington Examiner confirm this death and attribute it to the Bald Range wildfire. The Guardian and CBS News also mention that the Bald Range fire was first detected late on Friday evening and grew to more than 40 sq miles within hours, while NPR and Washington Examiner report it as first reported Friday evening and growing to about 50 square kilometers within hours.

Sources NPR · The Guardian · CBS News · BBC · UPI · Washington Examiner

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez announces decision to freeze her eggs

Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, a 36-year-old New York congresswoman, announced she is freezing her eggs and will share the process online. She described the decision as a way to feel more in control of her life. Ocasio-Cortez shared the process through Instagram stories, showing the medication and tools used, including syringes and alcohol wipes. She injected herself in the dressing room before an ABC interview, stating that women can do anything and that it is “badass” to take such shots. She also mentioned the importance of normalizing conversations about women’s health, especially in a political environment where reproductive care is under threat.

Ocasio-Cortez emphasized the lack of information available to women about their bodies and reproductive health. She highlighted the challenges of balancing personal and political life, noting that being in the public eye at a young age means navigating major life decisions in the spotlight. She also mentioned the financial and technological advancements that have made egg freezing more accessible. Ocasio-Cortez hinted at the possibility of running for president in 2028, though she said the focus should remain on the midterm elections.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and The Daily Wire both mention Ocasio-Cortez’s comments about the political “risk” of sharing her decision, while FOX News and The New York Post attribute similar statements to her. The Washington Examiner notes that Ocasio-Cortez has not ruled out a 2028 presidential run, a detail not included in The Guardian or BBC accounts. The Daily Wire and The New York Post describe Ocasio-Cortez as having been engaged to Riley Roberts since 2022, a detail absent from other accounts. The Guardian and The Daily Wire include quotes from Amanda Litman and Xochitl Hinojosa, which are not present in the Washington Examiner, BBC, or FOX News reports.

Sources The Guardian · Washington Examiner · The Daily Wire · BBC · FOX News · New York Post

Shanghai cancels 1,300 flights as China prepares for Typhoon Dolphin

Typhoon Dolphin, which brought heavy rains to northern Taiwan, prompted authorities in China to cancel over 1,300 flights in Shanghai and relocate more than 300,000 people in surrounding regions. The storm’s outer bands hit northern Taiwan, including Taipei, on Saturday. Shanghai’s Hongqiao and Pudong airports canceled about 60% of Sunday’s expected flights. Authorities in Zhejiang province evacuated 390,000 people in Taizhou by Saturday evening and 900,000 in Wenzhou. In Fujian, 98,900 people were evacuated from high-risk areas. Forecasters warned of severe flooding and landslide risks, with some areas expected to receive 200-400 mm of rainfall over the next few days.

Authorities in Shanghai evacuated 30,000 people from riskier areas and suspended all water-based activities, including ferries and cruises. The storm’s maximum sustained winds were estimated at about 162 km/h (101 mph) as of Sunday morning. The National Meteorological Centre warned that the storm could slow over central and southwestern China, prolonging heavy rainfall and increasing flood and landslide risks.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera reports that Typhoon Dolphin made landfall near Yuhuan in Zhejiang province at 5:30pm on Sunday, with maximum sustained winds of 151 km/h (93 mph), equivalent to a Category 1 hurricane. This differs from the Associated Press, which states the storm’s maximum sustained winds were estimated at about 162 km/h (101 mph) as of Sunday morning. The Guardian also reports landfall at 5:30pm with 151 km/h winds, while UPI mentions landfall at Force 13 speeds of 85 mph. The Guardian also notes that Shanghai evacuated 215,600 people, substantially higher than the 30,300 figure cited by Al Jazeera and the BBC. The BBC mentions that Shanghai canceled nearly 1,000 flights, while the Associated Press and the Guardian report 1,300 and 1,500 flights canceled respectively.

Sources Associated Press · Al Jazeera · BBC · The Guardian · UPI · The Guardian

Salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeños could hospitalize thousands, prompting recalls and economic losses for farmers.

Some meat products with jalapeños may be linked to salmonella outbreak, USDA says

Federal officials issued a public health alert for meat and poultry products containing jalapeños that may be contaminated with salmonella, according to the U.S. Department of Agriculture. The alert applies to products regulated by the Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) that may have been affected by a recalled ingredient. The USDA said it learned of the issue when it discovered that multiple FSIS-regulated establishments had received a recalled ingredient. The agency released a list of 18 recalled products, including brands like Taylor Farms, Ready Meals, Meal Simple, Higher Harvest, and Deli Kitchen. The USDA noted that additional downstream products may be identified as the ingredient recall progresses.

The salmonella outbreak was first reported in mid-June and is linked to jalapeño peppers grown in Sinaloa, Mexico, and distributed by Coast Citrus Distributors. As of Aug. 5, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported that at least 345 people in 27 states had been sickened, with 36 hospitalized. Minnesota was particularly hard hit, with at least 110 cases confirmed. Of 191 patients interviewed, 93% reported eating at a Mexican-style restaurant before falling ill, including Chipotle and Qdoba. Both chains removed jalapeños from their menus. Albertsons voluntarily recalled four items containing jalapeños supplied by Taylor Farms.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News states that the USDA said there have been "no confirmed reports of illness" from FSIS-regulated products containing jalapeños sourced from Sinaloa. Straight Arrow News reports that FSIS said there have been "no confirmed illnesses tied specifically to the FSIS-regulated meat and poultry products containing the recalled jalapeños." CBS News also notes that the first illnesses were reported in mid-June and links them to jalapeños from Sinaloa, while Straight Arrow News attributes the outbreak to "fresh jalapeño peppers grown in Sinaloa, Mexico." CBS News mentions that Albertsons recalled four items, while Straight Arrow News states that the public health alert includes products sold under several brands and distributed through major retailers and convenience stores.

Sources CBS News · Straight Arrow News
