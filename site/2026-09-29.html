Senate passes ‘Protect College Sports Act’ with sweeping bipartisan vote

The Senate on Monday passed the “Protect College Sports Act” (PCSA) with a 77-22 vote, marking a major legislative achievement for the NCAA. The bill aims to address concerns about fairness and equity in college sports by granting the NCAA federal antitrust protection, capping agent fees at 5 percent, and limiting school spending on athletes to $50 million annually. It also guarantees scholarships for 10 years after eligibility and allows one free transfer without losing a year of eligibility. The legislation includes provisions on conference realignment and minimum revenue sharing for smaller sports. The bill was co-authored by Sens. Ted Cruz (R-Texas) and Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.) and is expected to move to the House for consideration after the midterm elections.

President Donald Trump praised the bill as a “really big deal,” calling it essential for saving college sports and colleges themselves. The legislation seeks to create a national framework for college athletics, replacing the current patchwork of state laws and court decisions. It would set limits on how much schools can directly pay athletes through revenue sharing and establish federal rules for NIL deals, including disclosure and contract requirements. The bill also grants the NCAA greater authority to enforce its rules while providing additional protections for athletes' scholarships and medical coverage.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and Washington Examiner both report the bill passed 77-22, while The Daily Caller states the vote was 74-24. The Hill and Axios note the bill includes a provision limiting increases to ticket prices schools can charge for major sporting events, which The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner omit. The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner mention the bill grants the NCAA limited immunity from federal antitrust laws, while The Hill and Axios describe it as granting sweeping federal antitrust protection. The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner include details about the bill’s provisions on athletic department revenue caps and roster protections, which are not mentioned in The Hill or Axios. The Hill and Washington Examiner report the bill includes a five-year eligibility rule, while The Daily Caller and Axios describe it as allowing one free transfer without losing a year of eligibility.

Prosecutors Reopen Investigation Into Alleged Gang Rape at Cornell Fraternity

A former Cornell University student has filed a civil lawsuit alleging she was drugged and gang-raped by seven Chi Phi fraternity members in October 2024. The lawsuit claims the university failed to protect her and imposed only minimal sanctions on the accused. The Tompkins County District Attorney's Office has announced it will reopen the criminal investigation, citing pressure from the community and new evidence. The DA said the woman's sworn statement from November 2024 differed from her civil lawsuit allegations, and her attorney claims investigators never followed up on her claims.

Cornell University conducted an internal Title IX investigation and imposed sanctions on the accused students, including expulsions and suspensions. The university closed the Chi Phi fraternity chapter in 2024 and barred it from campus. The lawsuit accuses Cornell and the fraternity of breach of contract, negligence, and violations of state law, seeking monetary damages. The DA said the woman's initial statement to police did not allege she was drugged without her knowledge or that she did not consent to sexual activities.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and The Guardian both state that the woman's initial statement to police did not allege she was drugged without her knowledge or that she did not consent to sexual activities, while ABC News and NPR also mention this. However, The Guardian and CBS News include more specific details about the initial statement, including the woman's description of her participation as voluntary and consensual. Mother Jones and UPI do not provide as detailed a summary of the initial statement. The Guardian and CBS News both mention that the woman's attorney claims investigators never followed up on her allegations, while ABC News and NPR also note this. UPI and Mother Jones do not explicitly state this point. The Guardian and CBS News both mention that the woman's attorney claims none of the accused were arrested, while ABC News and NPR also note this. Mother Jones and UPI do not explicitly state this point.

Trump floated US weapons sales to Xi, ambassador says

U.S. Ambassador to China David Perdue told Fox News Sunday that President Trump proposed selling weapons to China during recent meetings with President Xi Jinping. Perdue stated that Trump repeatedly said, “Hey, we sell arms to other people around the world,” and at one point asked Xi if he would like to buy some. The comments come amid ongoing tensions between the U.S. and China, including competition in artificial intelligence and disputes over Taiwan. Trump confirmed plans to meet Xi twice more before the year ends, including at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meeting in November and the G20 Summit in Miami.

The U.S. has maintained a strict weapons embargo on China since 1989, following Beijing’s crackdown on pro-democracy protesters. In contrast, the U.S. has sold weapons to Taiwan, a self-governing island claimed by China, to help it defend against potential invasion. The Trump administration has paused a $14 billion arms sale to Taiwan amid trade negotiations and pressure on U.S. stockpiles. Perdue defended Trump, claiming he has sold more weapons to Taiwan than any other president since 1979 and that Trump has made it clear the U.S. supports Taiwan without endorsing independence.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and ABC News report that Trump denied the claim, stating he “never heard of that one” and suggested Perdue may have been referring to Taiwan. The Daily Wire also notes that a White House official confirmed the U.S. has no plans to sell weapons to China, while ABC News quotes a State Department spokesperson making the same point. The Hill and UPI do not mention Trump’s denial, while The Daily Wire and ABC News include his statement. The BBC does not mention the denial but notes that the State Department later clarified U.S. law prohibits arms sales to China.

FBI Deputy Director Andrew Bailey Announces Resignation to Return to Missouri

Andrew Bailey, the FBI’s deputy director, announced his resignation on Monday, stating he would return to Missouri to be with his family. Bailey cited pride in the FBI’s accomplishments under Director Kash Patel and expressed confidence in Patel’s continued leadership. His resignation is expected to take effect on October 2. Bailey, a former Missouri attorney general, was one of two deputies under Patel, alongside Christopher Raia, following the departure of Dan Bongino. The FBI has not yet announced whether Raia will remain the sole deputy director.

Bailey’s tenure at the FBI included overseeing some of the agency’s election-related investigations, including a raid on the Fulton County Election Hub in Georgia in January. He also led efforts targeting violent crime, such as Operation VIPER in the St. Louis region, which resulted in 91 arrests and the seizure of firearms and drugs. In Missouri, Bailey previously secured a $24 billion judgment against the Chinese Communist Party in a 2020 lawsuit.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and The Guardian both state that Bailey was overseeing some of Trump’s election-related investigations, including the Georgia raid. Axios and UPI, however, note that a senior administration official claimed Bailey had "nothing to do" with election security or integrity. The Daily Wire and Straight Arrow News mention Bailey’s role in the 2025 lawsuit against the Chinese Communist Party, while Mother Jones and Axios highlight Bailey’s anti-abortion rhetoric and legal actions against the FDA. The Guardian and Axios report that Bailey and Patel had a strained relationship, while The Daily Wire and Straight Arrow News attribute this to a "contentious relationship" with Patel.

Holdout juror in Lindsay Clancy case had ‘a mountain of evidence,’ attorney says

The attorney for the holdout juror in the Lindsay Clancy case said on Sunday his client had “a mountain of evidence” to support his belief that Clancy was criminally responsible for her children’s deaths. Juror Michael Desronvil, whose vote resulted in a mistrial earlier in the month, had “no doubt” Clancy was responsible for the deaths of 5-year-old Cora, 3-year-old Dawson, and 8-month-old Callan. Clancy admitted to killing them in her family’s Doxbury, Massachusetts, home in January 2023, but her defense argued she wasn’t criminally responsible because she was suffering from postpartum psychosis.

In a separate account, a writer described their personal experience with a brother who killed their mother, highlighting the challenges of understanding and treating serious mental illness. The writer noted that while the Clancy case has drawn significant public attention, serious mental illness rarely makes news unless it involves a crime. The writer also mentioned discovering their mother’s collection of books on mental illness after her death, which provided insight into the complexities of the condition.

Republican senator criticizes White House for taxpayer-funded Trump ads

The White House has expanded a taxpayer-funded advertising campaign to promote Donald Trump’s political agenda ahead of the November midterm elections. US Senator John Kennedy criticized the spending on these ads, suggesting such use of public funds may be illegal. During an interview with CBS News, Kennedy acknowledged that the White House’s approach differs from his own stance on using public money for political promotion. The ads, which include a 30-second spot featuring Trump, are labeled as “public service announcements” and include disclaimers stating they are “paid for by the US government.”

The ads, which aired during college football games and Fox News Sunday, feature Trump using strong language against political opponents, including references to “communists, Marxists and fascists.” The latest ad, which includes a disclaimer, is nearly identical to an online ad Trump posted during his 2024 campaign. Critics, including some Republican lawmakers, have raised concerns about the legality and intent of the ads, with some comparing them to authoritarian propaganda.

Trump announces new Iowa steel plant project

President Donald Trump announced Monday that Mesabi Metallics will build a major steel plant in Iowa, which he called the largest in American history. The project is expected to create up to 6,000 construction jobs and nearly 2,000 manufacturing and mining jobs, with permanent employment at 1,750. The plant will use iron ore from a mine in Minnesota, which Mesabi Metallics recently invested $2.5 billion in. The facility is projected to begin production in 2030. The White House claims the plant will create a fully integrated American supply chain from mine to mill.

The announcement was made during an Oval Office event attended by officials including Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Energy Secretary Chris Wright, and Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds. Trump emphasized the project's role in boosting domestic manufacturing and national security, while also highlighting its economic benefits for Iowa. The plant is part of the administration's focus on domestic production ahead of the November midterms.

Where the accounts differ

FOX News states the steel plant will be the largest in American history and will produce 7.5 million tons annually in its first phase, eventually reaching 10 million tons per year. ABC News and The Guardian report the plant will produce 10 million tons annually. The Guardian also notes that the plant is expected to cost about $15 billion, while FOX News mentions a $15 billion investment. The Guardian reports that the plant will be built by Mesabi Metallics, which is owned by the Essar Group, an Indian multinational conglomerate. Just The News and ABC News mention the mine in Minnesota will produce 7.5 million tons of iron ore annually. The Guardian and New York Post note that the plant will use iron ore from the Minnesota mine, which accounted for roughly 60% of iron ore production in the US in the 20th century.

New York Times exec dies in California shooting; elderly in-laws accused of murder

A 40-year-old New York Times executive, Jonathan McKinsey, was found dead with multiple gunshot wounds in the parking lot of the Dublin Sports Grounds in Dublin, California, on Saturday afternoon. Police identified the suspects as his 77-year-old in-laws, Shouyong Zhang and Shili Chen, both of Dublin. The couple was arrested on suspicion of murder and booked into Santa Rita Jail. McKinsey, who worked as head of engineering at New York Times Games, had been employed by the company since January 2023. Witnesses reported that both suspects were wearing masks and took turns shooting McKinsey. The circumstances leading to the shooting are under investigation.

McKinsey was involved in several legal proceedings at the time of his death, including a child abuse case filed in October 2025. Court records show that McKinsey and his wife, Candace Jang, had filed restraining orders against each other in 2025. McKinsey also filed for divorce in December 2025. The couple was set to appear in court over custody issues prior to the shooting. Police have not disclosed a motive for the killing, stating there are no ongoing threats to public safety.

South Africa experiences multiple mass shootings killing at least 38 people in a week

A series of mass shootings in South Africa over the past week resulted in at least 38 deaths, according to multiple reports. On Saturday night, a tavern in Johannesburg saw 17 people killed when gunmen entered and opened fire, while a shisanyama near Cape Town had 10 fatalities. Earlier in the week, a house party in Durban was attacked, killing 11 people, including a pregnant woman. Police have not identified motives for these attacks, though some are linked to illegal gold mining and gang violence. South Africa's high murder rate, averaging around 60 per day, has led to increased calls for action from President Cyril Ramaphosa, who has deployed the army in affected areas.

South Africa's police have launched investigations into the shootings, with some outlets reporting that the attacks may be tied to turf wars among illegal gold miners known as "zama-zamas." The violence has drawn attention from international figures, including President Trump, who has focused on murders of white people on farms, though official statistics show the majority of victims are poor Black individuals, often women. Protests have erupted over a spate of femicides, with a tenth woman's body discovered recently in Johannesburg.

The Daily Wire adds that the two shootings occurred roughly four hours apart, while NPR does not specify timing.
