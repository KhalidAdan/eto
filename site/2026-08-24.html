Nevada Hawk Fire explodes in size as it surges towards Reno, devours over 15K acres

A fast-moving wildfire grew nearly 50% in a single day as it bore down on Reno, Nevada, sending evacuation zones sprawling toward the city while flames burned closer. The Hawk Fire has devoured 15,040 acres by Sunday afternoon, according to the New York Post. With the fire still at 0% containment, the danger crept closer as evacuation zones expanded south and east and residents were urged to evacuate. Strong, gusty winds, low humidity and dry vegetation helped fuel the flames, officials said. The Washoe County sheriff said the blaze was human-caused and encouraged 90,000 people to leave the area, the Associated Press reported. The fire was zero percent contained as of Sunday afternoon. About 1,000 firefighters and other first responders are battling the fire, officials said. A preliminary investigation has found that the Hawk Fire was human-caused, authorities said at a news conference on Sunday. They did not immediately release details on how the fire ignited. About 90,000 people have been affected by the wildfire, including 42,000 who have been forced to evacuate their homes and another 45,000 people who have been warned to be prepared to evacuate, officials said.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and the Guardian both report the fire had grown to 15,040 acres by Sunday afternoon, while the Guardian also states the fire had reached the edge of the University of Nevada campus. The Guardian and the UPI report the fire had grown to 15,000 acres, while the New York Post and the ABC News report it had grown to 13,000 acres. The ABC News and the Guardian report that 42,000 residents were under strict evacuation orders, while the New York Post and the UPI report 90,000 people were affected, including 42,000 under evacuation orders. The Guardian and the UPI report that 14,000 homes were evacuated, while the New York Post and the ABC News report 90,000 people were affected, including 42,000 under evacuation orders. The Guardian and the UPI report that 10,000 homes and businesses were without power, while the New York Post and the ABC News report nearly 10,000 homes and businesses were without power. The Guardian and the UPI report that the fire had reached the edge of the University of Nevada campus, while the New York Post and the ABC News do not mention this detail.

Sources New York Post · ABC News · Newsweek · The Guardian · CBS News · UPI

Trump will kick off the IndyCar race in the Beast before cars zoom through Washington’s streets

President Donald Trump and first lady Melania Trump are scheduled to watch Sunday’s Freedom 250 Grand Prix as the cars whip through the city’s downtown, including a stretch along Pennsylvania Avenue not too far from the White House. Trump will lead a traditional prerace kickoff in his presidential limo, U.S. Ambassador Monica Crowley, Trump’s liaison to the organizing group America250, said in an interview on Fox Business. The open-wheel race, with cars whizzing past Washington’s monuments at speeds approaching 200 mph, is the latest of Trump’s celebrations this year to mark America’s 250th birthday. The event will also include a prerace show and flyovers of military aircraft, which swooped and boomed over the city during the July Fourth celebrations.

The Freedom 250 Grand Prix is the first major auto race in the capital in more than 24 years. The race will be 250 miles long, or 147 laps, on a 1.7-mile track that includes bumpy DC roads with the Capitol Building, museums, department buildings, and monuments in the background. The cars are capable of hitting speeds of 185 mph on the tight course. Twenty-five racers completed 147 laps around the circuit. President Trump and first lady Melania Trump greeted drivers on the paddock before getting in “the Beast” presidential limo for the ceremonial lap. The president then waived the green flag to kick off the race.

Where the accounts differ

The Associated Press reports that Trump and Melania Trump will lead a traditional prerace kickoff in his presidential limo, as stated by U.S. Ambassador Monica Crowley. The New York Post states that Trump took the first lap around the 1.7-mile circuit in “the Beast” before drivers raced past the speed limit on the National Mall. The BBC notes that the maximum speed limit in Washington DC is 55mph, but some of the world’s fastest drivers zoomed past the capital’s famous monuments and museums at speeds of up to 190mph. The Washington Examiner mentions that House Speaker Mike Johnson wants to make the event an annual one if Republicans retain control of the lower chamber.

Sources Associated Press · New York Post · NPR · CBS News · BBC · Washington Examiner

Dems turning more GOP Senate strongholds into midterm battlegrounds

Democrats are focusing on traditionally Republican Senate seats in key states like Iowa, Alaska, Texas, and Ohio as midterms approach. The race in Texas, where Democrat James Talarico leads Republican Ken Paxton, has shifted to a toss-up according to Cook Political Report. In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola faces Republican Senator Dan Sullivan, with a second candidate also named Dan Sullivan potentially complicating the race. Sean Grayson, convicted in killing of Sonya Massey, dies in prison, attorney says

Sean Grayson, a former Sangamon County Sheriff's deputy convicted of second-degree murder in the 2024 killing of Sonya Massey, died in prison at age 32, according to his attorney. Grayson was serving a 20-year sentence for the shooting, which occurred in July 2024 when he responded to a 911 call about a possible prowler at Massey's home. Body camera footage showed Grayson confronting Massey, who was holding a pot of hot water, and shooting her after she apologized and ducked. Grayson's attorney, Daniel Fultz, confirmed the death but provided no further details. Grayson had been diagnosed with Stage 4 colon cancer before the shooting, which had spread to his liver and lungs by the time of his sentencing in January 2025. His request for medical release was denied by the Illinois Prisoner Review Board in July 2026. Massey's family attorneys stated that Grayson's death does not bring Sonya back, emphasizing the ongoing grief for her loved ones.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and Newsweek both mention that Grayson's cancer had spread to his liver and lungs, while the New York Post and ABC News refer to the same condition without specifying the spread. The New York Post and Newsweek include details about the Illinois Prisoner Review Board's denial of Grayson's medical release request in July 2026, while CBS News and ABC News mention the denial but do not specify the month. The New York Post and Newsweek also include quotes from Massey's family attorney, Ben Crump, about Grayson's death, while CBS News and ABC News attribute the same statement to the attorneys for Massey's family. The Just The News provides additional context about the family's reaction and the $10 million settlement, which is not included in the other accounts.

Tropical Storm Moke poised to slam into Hawaii just days after Hurricane Lala

Tropical Storm Moke is expected to bring heavy rain and strong winds to Hawaii, with the Big Island facing the brunt of the storm. Forecasters predict 5-10 inches of rain, with isolated areas seeing up to 15 inches, particularly on the windward and southeast sides of the Big Island. The National Weather Service warns of potential flooding and mudslides, especially in areas already saturated from Hurricane Lala. Hawaii Governor Josh Green urged residents to prepare by restocking essentials and staying informed about the storm’s progress. The storm is expected to pass south of the islands, with impacts likely to be less severe than Hurricane Lala, though still dangerous.

The National Hurricane Center projects that Moke will bring 130mm to 250mm (5 to 10 inches) of rainfall over the next two days on the southeast and windward sides of the Big Island, with some isolated areas seeing up to 380mm (15 inches). Eastern Maui is expected to receive 76mm to 150mm (3 to 6 inches), while the rest of the island chain will see smaller amounts. Governor Green warned that the storm will be followed by another storm front in about a week, emphasizing the ongoing challenges for recovery efforts.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian and Al Jazeera both mention that at least one person died in Hurricane Lala, while CBS News and UPI do not specify a death, though CBS News notes that "at least one person died" in the storm. Al Jazeera reports that Lala caused more than 1,000mm (39 inches) of rain in some locations, while The Guardian and CBS News cite 15 inches (38cm) in isolated areas. UPI and NPR mention that Lala dropped more than 30 inches of rain in some parts, which is a significant discrepancy. Al Jazeera and CBS News attribute the $43m in infrastructure damage and $4.6m in home damage to Hurricane Lala, while The Guardian and UPI mention these figures without specifying the storm. NPR and CBS News report that more than 8,000 people were without power after Lala, while The Guardian and UPI do not mention this number.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · CBS News · UPI · NPR

Two Paralyzed After Hospital Injected Patients With Same Drug Used In Executions

At least two women remain paralyzed and two others were injured after a pharmacy error led to all four being injected with the wrong drug prior to planned surgeries. Kristina Dorton described her mother-in-law, Glenda Dorton, as paralyzed and having no feeling from her chest down after the injection. The family reported the knee replacement surgery was successful, but the complications from the wrong medication are having a lasting impact. The hospital’s CEO, Dr. Shubhada Jagasia, issued a statement expressing regret and confirming an investigation was launched.

The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation confirmed it is investigating the incident after receiving information from the Tennessee Healthcare Facilities Commission. The hospital acknowledged a "medication error" in its pharmacy that led to the patients being injected with potassium phosphate instead of a regular anesthetic and experiencing "adverse health reactions." The hospital has not provided details about the conditions of the three other patients affected, citing patient privacy concerns.
