Record Turnout, Suspected Fraud: Israel’s Election Day

Israel held its 26th Knesset elections on Tuesday, with historic voter turnout reported. The Central Elections Committee noted that by 2:00 p.m., turnout had reached 44.9%, the highest in the initial seven hours since 1969. Turnout among Israeli Defense Forces members reached 48% by 1:00 p.m. The election was marked by a high-profile voter fraud investigation in the Negev, where three residents of Bedouin communities were arrested on suspicion of planning to buy votes for Ra’am (The United Arab List party). Police seized envelopes containing tens of thousands of shekels in cash, along with notes and ballot slips, and the suspects were set to appear in court on Tuesday.

Benjamin Netanyahu, Israel’s longest-serving prime minister, faced a serious challenge from former Israel Defense Forces Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot, whose newly formed Yashar Party surged in recent polls. Exit polls suggested that the opposition bloc led by Eisenkot would win most seats in the Knesset by a slight margin, edging out Netanyahu’s right and far-right coalition. However, the final results remain uncertain, with neither bloc securing a majority in the 120-seat parliament. The election is seen as a referendum on Netanyahu’s leadership, particularly in light of the October 7 intelligence failures and ongoing conflicts in Gaza, Lebanon, and Syria.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire reports that police seized envelopes containing Ra’am ballots, with cash found in some of them, while The Guardian and NPR do not mention this detail. The Guardian and NPR describe exit polls suggesting the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot would win most seats, while The Daily Wire and Axios note that Netanyahu’s Likud Party emerged as the largest party. The Guardian and CBS News mention that Netanyahu’s Likud Party has 20.8% of the vote with 85% of votes counted, while The Daily Wire and Axios report different seat projections. The Guardian and CBS News also note that Arab parties are projected to win 19 seats, a record, while The Daily Wire and Axios do not specify the number of seats for Arab parties. The Daily Wire and Axios mention that the final parliamentary distributions will hinge on late-tally ballots cast by security personnel and citizens unable to access standard stations, while The Guardian and CBS News do not include this detail.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Guardian · NPR · Al Jazeera · CBS News · Axios

Trump accuses Democrats of election fraud in Alaska Senate race

President Donald Trump accused Democrats and Mary Peltola's campaign of fraud and perjury after the New York Times reported that Peltola aides helped recruit another candidate named Dan Sullivan. The report described a "ballot composition project" involving Peltola campaign staff, Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC) officials, political consultants, and an operative who traveled through Alaska using a false name to recruit potential candidates. The DOJ is seeking to delay certification of the election results if Sen. Dan Sullivan loses, citing possible voter confusion between two candidates with nearly identical names. Peltola's campaign denied involvement in the second Sullivan's candidacy, stating they have no connection to either Sullivan campaign.

The Department of Justice is facing a legal battle in Alaska, where it is trying to convince a federal court that confusion between two Senate candidates with nearly identical names could be enough to delay certification of the election results. The DOJ's complaint stems from a controversy in Alaska where two individuals by the name of Dan Sullivan, one a retired schoolteacher and the other the state’s incumbent senator, appear alongside each other on the state’s ballots. The New York Times reported that Peltola campaign aides worked with a veteran Democratic operative named Jerid Kurtz to recruit another Dan Sullivan to run for Alaska’s Senate seat, hoping voter confusion would draw support from the incumbent Republican senator.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News reports that Trump is organizing a cross-country campaign blitz and has spent $277 million since Sept. 1 to help Republicans, while The Hill notes that more than 50,000 ballots have already been cast in the state. The Daily Wire and Axios both describe Trump's accusations of perjury and fraud, but only Axios mentions that Trump offered no evidence to support his claims. FOX News and The Hill both note that the DOJ is seeking to delay certification of the election results, but only FOX News states that the DOJ is conducting an "aggressive criminal investigation" into how the second Sullivan secured a spot on Alaska's ballot.

Sources ABC News · Washington Examiner · The Daily Wire · FOX News · Axios · The Hill

Trump’s Visa Crackdown Has an Exception: His Friends

Flavio Bolsonaro, the defeated right-wing Brazilian presidential candidate and Donald Trump ally, and his family received U.S. visas to enter the country. The Trump administration continues to implement widespread visa restrictions while suspending and revoking existing ones. Bolsonaro and his family arrived in the U.S. on Wednesday, having entered Argentina from Brazil and then flown to Ecuador before landing in Miami. They were shielded from photographers by umbrellas as they left the airport. Bolsonaro, who lost the presidential election to President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, has refused to concede and claims there was election fraud. The State Department confirmed it would issue visas to Bolsonaro and his family, though the administration has not commented further on the matter.

Bolsonaro faces multiple investigations in Brazil, including allegations of money laundering and tax evasion. Brazilian authorities ordered him to surrender his passports by Tuesday, but he reportedly left the country before complying. The Trump administration has supported Bolsonaro and his family, with Trump calling the election results "rigged" and expressing concern over Brazil’s judicial system. Bolsonaro’s brother, Eduardo, has been living in the U.S. since early 2025, acting as a key link between the Brazilian far right and Trump’s circle.

Where the accounts differ

Mother Jones and NPR both state that Bolsonaro and his family entered Argentina before flying to Ecuador and then to Miami, while ABC News and FOX News report that Bolsonaro arrived directly in Miami. NPR and Mother Jones mention that Bolsonaro’s family was escorted by uniformed officers with umbrellas, a detail not included in ABC News or FOX News. Mother Jones and NPR note that Bolsonaro claimed the election was fraudulent and that he was not fleeing but leaving a "state of exception," a claim not explicitly made by ABC News or FOX News. FOX News reports that Bolsonaro’s departure was confirmed by an ally, while Mother Jones and NPR do not attribute this claim to a specific source. ABC News states that Brazilian authorities were unaware of Bolsonaro’s flight, while Mother Jones and NPR do not mention this.

Dolores Huerta, who co-founded United Farm Workers, dies at 96

Dolores Huerta, who co-founded the United Farm Workers, has died at 96, according to her family. The New York Times reported that the cause of death was cancer. Huerta, along with Cesar Chavez, founded the National Farm Workers Association in 1962, which later became the United Farm Workers. The union helped secure labor contracts and better conditions for agricultural workers, including the right to unionize in California. Huerta was a key organizer, coordinating boycotts and negotiations. She also popularized the slogan “Sí, se puede,” which later became a rallying cry for Democrats. In March, she publicly spoke of being raped by Chavez in the 1960s, which resulted in two pregnancies. She said she kept the secret to protect the movement. Huerta continued to work as an activist, lobbying for immigration reform and supporting civil rights causes.

Where the accounts differ

Straight Arrow News and The Daily Wire both report that Huerta said Chavez raped her in 1966, but The Daily Wire adds that she had two pregnancies and that the children were raised by others. Straight Arrow News does not mention the pregnancies or the children. The Guardian and Mother Jones do not mention the allegations against Chavez, focusing instead on Huerta’s activism and legacy. The Daily Wire states that Huerta kept her account of abuse secret for six decades, while Straight Arrow News says she first spoke of the allegations in March. The Guardian and Mother Jones do not mention the allegations against Chavez, focusing instead on Huerta’s activism and legacy. The Daily Wire and Straight Arrow News both mention that Huerta said she wasn’t aware of Chavez abusing underage girls, but The Daily Wire adds that the Times investigation indicated other victims existed.

Sources Straight Arrow News · The Daily Wire · The Guardian · Mother Jones · ABC News · Axios

Two high-speed trains collide in Spain, killing at least 64

Two high-speed trains collided in Spain on Wednesday, resulting in at least 64 deaths and 80 injuries. The crash occurred near Toledo, with witnesses reporting passengers being thrown into the air. The trains were traveling from Madrid to Seville when one struck the rear of a stationary train. Spain’s transport minister attributed the crash to an “inexplicable” decision by a train controller, while the prime minister vowed to investigate and hold those responsible accountable. The incident marks the second major high-speed rail disaster in Spain this year, following a similar crash in January that killed over 40 people.

Spain’s high-speed rail system, once considered the most advanced in Europe, has faced renewed scrutiny following the collision. The crash occurred on a section of track where a computer malfunction caused a signal fault, leading to the stopping of multiple trains. According to officials, an order was given to resume traffic despite an active stop signal, contributing to the collision. Rescue efforts continued through the night, with teams searching for survivors and recovering bodies from the wreckage.

Where the accounts differ

Straight Arrow News and The Guardian both report the death toll as at least 64, while The Daily Caller and The Hill state at least 68. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention the January crash killed 45 people, while Straight Arrow News and The Hill report 40. The Daily Caller and The Guardian attribute the crash to a signal fault, while The Daily Wire and The Hill cite a computer malfunction. The Daily Caller and The Guardian report the collision occurred near Toledo, while The Hill and The Guardian specify the location as between Urda and Los Yebenes. The Daily Wire and The Guardian mention the transport minister called the crash “inexplicable,” while Straight Arrow News and The Hill attribute the crash to an “inexplicable” decision by a train controller.

Judge denies the Justice Department's bid to stop processing California ballots

A federal judge has denied the Department of Justice's request to segregate thousands of California ballots in the midterm elections. The DOJ sought to stop processing ballots for 20,000 individuals it claims are "likely" noncitizens. California officials argued that the DOJ's list is unreliable and that segregating ballots days before Election Day would be unlawful and disenfranchise voters. The judge emphasized that once ballots are segregated, lasting harm occurs and that the DOJ's actions could threaten democracy.

The judge ruled that the DOJ failed to demonstrate a likelihood of success on its claims and that the requested relief could disrupt the election. He also noted that the DOJ has filed numerous lawsuits seeking voter rolls without success. The ruling cited concerns about inaccuracies in citizenship databases and the potential for false positives. The judge warned that changing election procedures close to an election could create confusion and disenfranchise lawful voters.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and Newsweek both state that the DOJ requested a temporary restraining order to segregate ballots, while Washington Examiner and ABC News mention the judge's intention to appoint a special master to probe the DOJ's process. Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News report that the DOJ's request was part of a broader effort across multiple states, including Washington. NPR and Newsweek note that the DOJ has charged dozens of people for illegally voting as noncitizens, while ABC News and Al Jazeera mention the Trump administration's broader subpoena efforts. Straight Arrow News adds that the DOJ's request was part of a larger pattern of subpoenas in 13 states, which other accounts do not mention.

UN holds annual vote on condemning US embargo on Cuba as tensions spill into public

The United Nations is set to vote Wednesday on a resolution condemning the decades-long U.S. economic embargo on Cuba as tensions between the two nations escalate. The vote, which has occurred annually for 34 years, is expected to pass with overwhelming support from the 193 member states. The resolution reflects global opinion on the U.S. policy, though it is not legally binding. The vote comes amid Cuba's deepening financial crisis and the Trump administration's intensified campaign against the island, including threats of military intervention. On Tuesday, U.S. Ambassador Mike Waltz and Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodríguez engaged in a shouting match on the floor of the General Assembly. Rodríguez accused Waltz of lying, prompting Waltz to demand he "back up" and reminded him he was in the U.N., not the White House. The confrontation followed a similar incident the previous week when Waltz and Cuban delegate Ernesto Soberón Guzmán clashed outside a U.S.-hosted panel.

Where the accounts differ

The New York Post and The Daily Wire describe Waltz showing images of Fidel Castro wearing Rolex watches and displaying photos of Cuban elites' luxury properties, which are not mentioned in ABC News or BBC. US central bank holds interest rates steady ahead of midterm elections – here's what that means

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady at its final monetary policy meeting before the midterm elections, with 10 of 12 committee members voting to keep the federal funds rate at 3.75%-4%. The decision followed a September rate hike and came amid elevated inflation. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh stated the central bank aims to gather more information before making further decisions. The Fed uses interest rate changes to manage inflation and stabilize the job market. Leaving rates unchanged means borrowing costs for mortgages, business loans, and savings remain stable.

The Fed acknowledged that inflation remains high but noted signs of economic resilience. Policymakers emphasized the need for more data before adjusting rates again. The next meeting is scheduled for December 8-9. While the decision was not unanimous, two members—Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and Fed Governor Lisa Cook—voted to increase rates to 4%-4.25%. The Fed's next meeting will be held over 8-9 December.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that the Fed's decision was not unanimous, with 10 of 12 members voting to keep rates unchanged, while Cook and Hammack voted to increase rates. The Daily Caller states that the Fed raised rates by 25 basis points in September, which was the first increase since 2023, and that the CPI increased 0.6% in September and 3.7% over the preceding 12 months. Fox Business notes that the Fed raised rates by 25 basis points in September, which was the first increase since July 2023. The Daily Wire mentions that the Fed had raised rates for the first time in three years in September and that the probability of another rate hike was 7.2% according to CME Group’s FedWatch. ABC News states that the Fed raised rates a quarter of a percentage point last month, marking the first rate increase in three years, and that prices increased 3.7% in September compared to a year earlier. Straight Arrow News reports that the Fed's benchmark interest rate was unchanged at 3.75% to 4%, following a quarter-point increase in September, which was the Fed’s first rate hike in three years.

Sources BBC · The Daily Wire · The Daily Caller · Fox Business · ABC News · Straight Arrow News

Emergency declared at South Carolina nuclear site after lab fire
