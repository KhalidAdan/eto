Three killed after LA news helicopter crashes while covering deadly bus collision

A news helicopter crashed in suburban Los Angeles, killing three people and injuring others. The crash occurred near the site of a bus collision that had happened earlier in the day. The helicopter, operated by NBC Los Angeles and Telemundo 52, was reporting on the bus crash when it went down. Firefighters confirmed that the crash happened just before 7 p.m. local time, between two commercial buildings in Chatsworth. The aircraft burst into flames, damaging several vehicles and storage containers. At least one person was confirmed dead on the ground, while the other two victims were on board the helicopter. Two others were taken to the hospital, though their conditions were not immediately clear. The National Transportation Safety Board and the Federal Aviation Administration are investigating the cause of the crash.

Where the accounts differ

BBC and CBS News both state that the helicopter crashed just before 7 p.m., while The Guardian and Newsweek mention the time as just before 19:00 local time (01:00 BST). NPR and CBS News report that the helicopter crashed near a storage facility, while Al Jazeera and The Guardian describe it as crashing between two commercial buildings. The Guardian and CBS News mention that one person was confirmed dead on the ground, while BBC and Newsweek state that the third victim was on the ground. NPR and CBS News identify the victims on the helicopter as Eliana Moreno and George Marciniw, while BBC and Newsweek also name them, but BBC does not name the third victim. Al Jazeera does not name any of the victims.

Sources BBC · The Guardian · NPR · Al Jazeera · CBS News · Newsweek

US House votes to end Iran war, 7 Republicans join in favour

The US House of Representatives voted to end the war in Iran for the third time, passing a war powers resolution that would halt President Donald Trump’s ability to continue military action without congressional approval. The resolution passed 220-204, with seven House Republicans supporting the measure, including three voting in favour for the first time. The vote comes as the war has dragged on for nearly seven months, with on-again, off-again deadly missile strikes exchanged between the US and Iran. The resolution is likely to be the last House vote on the Iran war before the midterm elections, where the conflict is expected to play a significant role in political campaigns.

The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) reported that the war has cost at least $38bn, with the cost expected to rise by $3bn per month. The report also found that the war has driven up inflation by 0.5 percentage points in the first three months of 2027. The Pentagon’s inspector general confirmed that the war has led to "shortfalls" in munitions and a bottleneck in resupply, contradicting Trump’s claims that the US has "virtually unlimited" ammunition. The report noted that the US spent $22.3bn on munitions alone through June, with total spending at $33.4bn.

Where the accounts differ

Al Jazeera and The Guardian both report that the war has cost at least $38bn, while BBC and CBS News report the cost at $33.4bn. Al Jazeera and The Guardian attribute the cost increase to inflation, while BBC and CBS News attribute it to munitions and equipment expenditures. FOX News and Washington Examiner do not mention the CBO report or the inflation impact. The Guardian and Al Jazeera mention that the war has damaged or destroyed hundreds of buildings at US bases, while BBC and CBS News do not specify which bases were affected. The Guardian and Al Jazeera state that rebuilding missile defenses would take at least five years, while BBC and CBS News do not mention the time frame for rebuilding.

Sources Al Jazeera · BBC · Washington Examiner · FOX News · CBS News · The Guardian

Houthis Advance Along Red Sea Coast, Saudi Pipeline Shuts Down

The Houthis have seized the Red Sea port of Mocha and advanced along the coast toward the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, according to multiple outlets. The group also captured the islands of Greater and Lesser Hanish and Mayyun, bringing them within 20 miles of the U.S. military base in Djibouti. These advances have intensified attacks on Saudi Arabia, including a drone strike that damaged the East-West Pipeline, forcing it to shut down. The pipeline, which transports up to 7 million barrels of oil per day, has become a critical alternative to the Strait of Hormuz, which remains disrupted due to the U.S.-Iran war. Oil prices have risen as a result of the attacks and pipeline closure.

Saudi Arabia has sought U.S. assistance to counter the Houthi advances, with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman meeting with U.S. Central Command head Adm. Brad Cooper. The Houthis have also claimed responsibility for attacks on Saudi airbases and have vowed to continue their campaign against Saudi Arabia until it ends what they call a “siege” of northern Yemen.

Where the accounts differ

The Free Press and The Daily Wire both mention the capture of Mocha and the East-West Pipeline attack, but The Daily Wire attributes the pipeline shutdown to a drone attack from Iraq, while The Free Press does not specify the origin of the attack. The Guardian and Straight Arrow News report that the Houthis claimed responsibility for attacks on King Khalid Air Base, while Al Jazeera and The Daily Wire attribute the attacks to the Houthis, with Al Jazeera noting that Saudi Arabia has not commented on the Houthi claims. The Guardian and The Daily Wire also mention the rise in oil prices, while The Free Press and Straight Arrow News provide specific price figures.

Sources The Free Press · The Daily Wire · Al Jazeera · Straight Arrow News · Washington Examiner · The Guardian

Blanche says investigation unlikely into Russian oligarch's wedding gift to Trump Jr.

A top law enforcement official said Tuesday that an investigation into a Russian oligarch's financial involvement in Donald Trump Jr.'s wedding is unlikely. Attorney General Todd Blanche told reporters that the president doesn't know who the oligarch is and that other members of the family also don't know him. The official added that he has no information about the person. Donald Trump Jr. and his wife, Bettina Anderson, confirmed that the oligarch, Umar Kremlev, paid for parts of their wedding celebration, calling it an "extraordinarily generous" gift. The couple has since acknowledged that Kremlev gave them the gift, though it's unclear why he did so. The White House and Trump family members have denied knowledge of Kremlev's identity, with the president stating he has never heard of him and that the Russian's contribution to his son's wedding was "Not a big deal."

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and The Guardian both report that Attorney General Todd Blanche said the president doesn't know who Kremlev is, but ABC News includes a direct quote from Blanche saying "I don't know this person either," which is not found in any other account. The Guardian and BBC both report that the president called the Russian's contribution to his son's wedding "Not a big deal," but this quote is not found in any other account. The New York Post and The Guardian both mention that Kremlev attended part of the wedding, but the New York Post states that Kremlev was one of about 50 guests, while The Guardian says the guest list was intimate with only about 50 people attending, most of whom were immediate family, close friends, and business partners of the couple.

The House must vote on the articles by Thursday, with the potential for a Senate trial if passed. Crypto Bill Fails to Pass as Senate Votes 49-50 Against Motion to Proceed

The Senate failed to advance the Clarity Act, a cryptocurrency bill that would have established a federal regulatory framework for digital assets, after a 49-50 vote against a motion to proceed. The bill, backed by President Donald Trump and most Republicans, aimed to create clearer rules for digital assets and divide regulatory authority between the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). Democrats, including Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand, D-N.Y., joined all other Democrats in voting against the motion, while a handful of Republicans also opposed it. The vote fell short of the 60 votes needed to move the bill forward. The bill would have allowed state attorneys general to initiate enforcement actions against the president for ethics violations, but this provision was reportedly weakened in the final version.

Sen. Tim Scott, R-S.C., warned that Democratic opposition to the bill could jeopardize the U.S.’s ability to lead in financial innovation, arguing that the legislation would provide clearer rules for consumers and developers. He noted that the bill had undergone extensive negotiations and that Republicans had made numerous concessions to Democrats. However, Democrats, including Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., argued that the bill would effectively allow Trump to continue profiting from his crypto ventures.

Where the accounts differ

The Intercept reports that Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand privately urged her colleagues to support the bill before ultimately voting against it, while Just The News and The Daily Caller do not mention this private lobbying. The Intercept also states that the bill’s provision allowing state attorneys general to initiate enforcement actions against the president was essentially a dead letter due to political officials’ ability to issue legal opinions blocking such actions, a detail not included in other accounts. The Daily Caller notes that Sen. Thom Tillis switched his vote to “no” to invoke a motion to reconsider, a point not mentioned in The Intercept or Fox Business. Fox News and The Daily Caller both mention that the bill’s ethics provisions were not sufficient to address Trump’s crypto profits, while The Intercept and Just The News do not specify the exact nature of the shortcomings.

Sources The Intercept · Just The News · Fox Business · FOX News · The Daily Caller · Straight Arrow News
