Student protests erupt at Cornell over mishandling of gang-rape allegations

Protests erupted at Cornell University on Monday as students criticized officials for their handling of gang-rape allegations, demanding the president’s resignation and painting “SMASH THE PATRIARCHY” on an administrative building. The demonstrations were sparked by a high-profile case involving a former student, known as Jane Doe, who alleged she was drugged and raped at a fraternity house in 2024. Protesters called for the banning of fraternities and criticized the university for failing to protect students. The vandalism at Day Hall, the building housing the president’s office, included messages like “AT CORNELL RAPISTS ARE PROTECTED PROTESTORS ARE EXPELLED.” The university condemned the vandalism, stating it was not the answer to expressing concerns.

The case has drawn international attention, with critics accusing Cornell of being too lenient with the accused men and failing the victim. According to lawyers for Jane Doe, only two members of the Chi Phi fraternity were expelled after the incident, while others faced suspensions or required to write reflection papers. The university has faced backlash for its handling of the case, with students and faculty demanding stronger action against sexual violence on campus.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that four student demonstrators were suspended in 2024, while CBS News and The New York Post do not mention any suspensions. The Guardian attributes the painted messages to students protesting the treatment of pro-Palestine protesters, while FOX News and The Daily Wire do not mention this context. The Daily Wire claims Jane Doe consented to the sexual activity and later retroactively claimed she was assaulted, a claim not mentioned in other accounts. The New York Post and The Guardian describe the alleged incident as a gang rape, while CBS News and The Daily Wire do not specify the nature of the alleged assault beyond the presence of ketamine.

Sources The Guardian · CBS News · FOX News · The Daily Wire · Straight Arrow News · New York Post

US death row inmate Christa Pike awake and speaking after failed execution, lawyers say

Christa Pike, a 50-year-old death row inmate in Tennessee, survived a failed execution attempt last week, according to her lawyers. She was administered two doses of pentobarbital, a barbiturate used in lethal injections, but did not die. Pike, who was convicted in 1996 for the 1995 murder of Colleen Slemmer, remains in critical condition in a Nashville hospital, intubated and on a ventilator. Her lawyers reported that she is "conscious and speaking" and has suffered "severe damage" to her arms. The Tennessee Department of Correction has halted all executions in the state and ordered an independent investigation following the botched attempt.

Pike’s legal team had previously warned that her medical history, including a blood-clotting condition and small veins, made lethal injection a risky and potentially painful method. They had sought alternative execution methods, such as hanging or a firing squad, but the Supreme Court overruled a stay of execution. The failed attempt has raised questions about the reliability and humanity of lethal injection as a method of execution.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that Pike is "conscious and speaking" and that experts and her legal team say her recovery is "unprecedented." Mother Jones states that Pike "remains intubated, on a ventilator" and that her arms are "swollen, burned and blistered." The Nation says Pike "has not spoken since the failed execution." These accounts differ on Pike’s current state of consciousness and physical condition.

Sources BBC · Mother Jones · The Daily Wire · The Dispatch · Slate · The Nation

Supreme Court Begins New Term With Major Cases On Immigration, Gun Rights, Climate Lawsuits

The Supreme Court opened its new term Monday with a series of high-profile cases that include challenges to President Trump’s immigration policies, gun control measures, and climate-related lawsuits. The justices are also facing political pressure as Trump criticizes his appointees and Democrats consider changes to the Court. The term includes cases on Arizona’s voter registration requirements, the legality of deporting migrants to third countries, and the constitutionality of AR-15 bans. The Court also began its term with a major climate case, Suncor Energy v. County Commissioners of Boulder County, where Boulder, Colorado, seeks to hold energy companies liable for climate damages.

The Court’s emergency docket has become increasingly important as the justices consider urgent issues related to elections and immigration. The administration’s immigration policies face two major tests, with the Court set to hear cases on mandatory detention for certain immigrants and the legality of deporting migrants to countries where they are not citizens. The Court has allowed deportations to continue while the litigation proceeds. The term also includes a case on the constitutionality of bans on AR-15-style weapons and a climate case that could affect dozens of similar lawsuits nationwide.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and Axios both mention the Court’s consideration of Arizona’s voter registration requirements and the administration’s immigration policies, but only Axios explicitly states that the Court allowed the administration to restart third-country deportations. The Daily Wire does not mention this specific action. The Daily Caller and Slate provide more detailed accounts of the climate case, including the arguments and the involvement of specific justices, while FOX News and The Daily Wire focus more on the broader implications and legal arguments without the same level of detail on the case specifics.

U.S. officials monitor suspected plague case in Russia after researcher dies

A Russian lab technician died after contracting pneumonia, prompting U.S. officials to closely monitor the situation. The Anti-Plague Research Institute of Siberia and the Far East in Irkutsk is under scrutiny as the cause of death remains unclear. Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated the administration is watching the situation but emphasized there is no cause for alarm. The U.S. government is assessing options and has its most serious travel warning in place for Russia due to the war with Ukraine. The Russian health watchdog, Rospotrebnadzor, said no microorganisms linked to the woman's work were found in her biological samples. Disgraced Former House Speaker Dead At 84

Dennis Hastert, the longest-serving Republican House Speaker, has died at 84, according to a local coroner. He died at his home over the weekend. Hastert, who served as House Speaker from 1999 to 2007, was known for his role in shaping President George W. Bush’s legislative agenda. He later admitted to sexually abusing boys during his time as a high school wrestling coach and was sentenced to prison for financial crimes related to hush money payments. He was released from federal prison in 2017.

Hastert was born in 1942 and worked as a teacher and wrestling coach at Yorkville High School before entering politics. He was elected to the Illinois House of Representatives and then to the U.S. House in 1986. He became House Speaker after Newt Gingrich stepped down in 1998 and Bob Livingston resigned due to extramarital affairs. He led the House until Republicans lost their majority in 2006. After resigning from Congress in 2007, he worked as a lobbyist until his indictment in 2015 for financial crimes.

Where the accounts differ

The Daily Wire and CBS News both state that Hastert was the highest-ranking elected U.S. official to serve a prison sentence, while FOX News and Straight Arrow News do not mention this claim. The Daily Wire and CBS News attribute the claim about the prison sentence to the U.S. attorney, Zachary Fardon, while FOX News and Straight Arrow News do not attribute the claim to any specific source. ABC News and Axios do not mention the claim about being the highest-ranking official to serve prison time.

Trump says 'threat' led US to pull bombers from RAF Fairford

The US military removed its B-1 bombers from RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, England, following a threat to the base, according to President Donald Trump. The Pentagon confirmed the bombers, thought to be about a dozen, were moved back to their home stations in the US. Trump stated the decision was made due to intelligence suggesting a threat, though he did not specify its nature. The move came after a major incident on 27 September when police were alerted to three suspicious vehicles near the airbase. The exact nature of the threat remains unclear, but sources in Washington speculated it might have involved sabotage or an attack on the bombers in the event of resumed hostilities with Iran.

The incident at RAF Fairford led to the arrest of five men and a sixth British-Iranian national, all released on bail. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham suggested there were "strong indications" Iran was involved, while Iran denied any role. The US still maintains the ability to strike Iran with its remaining forces in the Middle East. Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the movement of the bombers as a routine rotation, while Vice President JD Vance linked the decision to an "abundance of caution" against Iran.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that Trump refused to be drawn on the nature of the threat, while ABC News states Trump said "we know who made the threat" but did not confirm the identity. Straight Arrow News claims the withdrawal was connected to existing threat reporting, not a new threat, whereas The New York Post and The Daily Wire describe the move as a response to new intelligence about an Iran-backed plot. The Guardian does not specify the nature of the threat or the exact number of bombers moved, while The Daily Wire mentions that some aircraft left while aerial refueling was still in progress.

Sources BBC · ABC News · Straight Arrow News · New York Post · The Daily Wire · The Guardian
