Democrats blast Stevens over Michigan 'betrayal'

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-Mich.) endorsed Republican Mike Rogers for the Michigan Senate race on Sunday, abandoning her previous support for Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed. The move sparked outrage among Democrats, who viewed it as a betrayal. Stevens and El-Sayed had been at odds over U.S. support for Israel amid the war in Gaza, with Stevens accusing El-Sayed of anti-Semitism for his criticism of Israeli actions. El-Sayed, in turn, criticized Stevens for her campaign tactics and for not adequately addressing Jewish community concerns. The endorsement came just days before the election, with polls showing El-Sayed leading Rogers by a narrow margin. Democratic leaders, including Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, condemned the decision, calling it a "despicable betrayal." El-Sayed dismissed the reversal, stating he does not think about Stevens, and focused instead on policy issues like healthcare and education.

Where the accounts differ

The Hill and Axios both report that Stevens’ former megadonor John Morgan criticized her decision, calling her a "washed up battle axe" and "radioactive." Mother Jones and Straight Arrow News mention that around 15 of Stevens’ staff resigned, including her chief of staff, Justin German, who called the decision "shocked and heartbroken." The Hill and Axios note that Stevens’ fiancé, Jason Turkish, had previously stated he would vote for Rogers, citing concerns about Jewish community issues, while Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News describe the planned joint appearance with El-Sayed and Rep. Jim Clyburn being canceled due to tensions. The Hill and Axios also mention that Stevens downplayed her reversal, saying she was "not turning my back on Democrats," while Al Jazeera and Straight Arrow News highlight that El-Sayed focused on policy issues rather than the endorsement.

Sources The Hill · Washington Examiner · Mother Jones · Al Jazeera · Axios · Straight Arrow News

Justice Department seeks to isolate Alaska ballots amid criminal probe over Senate candidate

The U.S. Department of Justice filed a lawsuit seeking to isolate ballots ranking Dan J. Sullivan to investigate potential voter confusion, while a criminal probe into the Senate race continues. The case involves two candidates with the same name, Republican incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan and independent candidate Dan J. Sullivan, who shares the same name. The Justice Department argues that the presence of both candidates could mislead voters and compromise the integrity of the election. Alaska state officials and election law experts have criticized the lawsuit, arguing it lacks a feasible plan to segregate ballots without violating voter privacy. A federal judge dismissed the lawsuit on procedural grounds, ruling that the government did not provide a workable method to investigate the ballots while preserving voter secrecy.

The Alaska Senate race is considered one of the most closely watched in the midterm elections, with control of the Senate at stake. The race includes three candidates: Sen. Dan Sullivan, Democratic former Rep. Mary Peltola, and independent candidate Dan J. Sullivan. The Justice Department's lawsuit was filed less than two weeks before the election, as early voting was underway. The department also filed a criminal probe into the race, which has led to subpoenas targeting Democratic officials allegedly involved in recruiting Dan J. Sullivan. President Donald Trump has publicly supported the lawsuit, claiming that Dan J. Sullivan was a "fake" candidate intended to confuse voters.

Where the accounts differ

NPR and CBS News both report that the Justice Department filed a lawsuit seeking to isolate ballots ranking Dan J. Sullivan to investigate voter confusion. However, NPR mentions that the lawsuit was part of a civil case seeking to block certification of the Senate race if the incumbent loses, while CBS News states that the lawsuit was dismissed on procedural grounds. The Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire both note that the lawsuit was dismissed by a federal judge, but the Washington Examiner reports that the state government called the case "meritless," while The Daily Wire attributes the dismissal to a judge appointed by former President Barack Obama. The New York Post and BBC both mention that the judge ruled the government did not provide a feasible plan to segregate ballots, but the New York Post adds that the judge also noted the case was too late as many voters had already cast ballots.

Sources NPR · Washington Examiner · BBC · New York Post · The Daily Wire · CBS News

Nine killed as small jet crashes on Florida street

A private jet crashed in a neighborhood in Florida on Sunday, killing all nine people on board, according to U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy. The crash occurred near Cresthaven, approximately nine miles from President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago estate in Palm Beach County, around 12:50 EST. The aircraft, a Learjet 60, had departed from Austin, Texas, and was headed to the Bahamas. The crash site was on Military Trail, a six-lane road that runs through a residential area. The impact caused significant damage to local infrastructure, including water lines, and the road is expected to remain closed for at least 30 days. Thunderstorms and heavy rain were reported in the area at the time of the crash.

Dashcam footage captured the moment of the crash, showing the jet nosediving into the ground almost vertically and exploding into a large fireball. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis described the crash as "cataclysmic," comparing it to a missile descending. Emergency crews evacuated about 600 residents from a nearby trailer-park community. The National Transportation Safety Board and the Federal Aviation Administration are investigating the incident.

Where the accounts differ

BBC reports that no one on the ground was hurt, while New York Post and Straight Arrow News state that there is no evidence of anyone on the ground being injured or killed. The Daily Wire and FOX News mention that the jet hit trees and power lines as it crashed, a detail not included in BBC or Just The News. The New York Post and The Daily Wire describe the crash time as 1:25 p.m., while BBC and Straight Arrow News cite 12:50 p.m. The Daily Wire and The New York Post attribute the final transmission to a pilot saying "Holy f*ck, we’re out of time," while BBC and FOX News report the last remark as "we're out of time."

Sources BBC · Just The News · FOX News · The Daily Wire · New York Post · Straight Arrow News

Outspoken Dem Senator Suffers Stroke At Son’s Hockey Game

Democratic Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT) was hospitalized after suffering a stroke while attending his son’s hockey game on Saturday. According to his chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, Murphy began experiencing numbness in his arm during the game and was taken to a local hospital, where a stroke was confirmed. He was subsequently transferred to Johns Hopkins for further monitoring and is responding well to treatment. The doctors are encouraged by his progress, and he is awake and alert, with optimism for a full recovery. Murphy had been campaigning for Democrats ahead of the November 3 midterm elections and was scheduled to attend campaign events in New Hampshire on Sunday.

Murphy, 53, has been viewed as a potential 2028 presidential contender and has been traveling across the country to support Democratic candidates. His office noted that he had been actively involved in midterm campaign efforts, including recent trips to Michigan and Florida. The incident has prompted updates from multiple outlets, with some emphasizing the importance of recognizing stroke symptoms, such as arm weakness, as part of the BE FAST acronym.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News and Straight Arrow News provide similar details, though Straight Arrow News notes that Murphy is not running in the midterms, a claim not made by The Daily Wire or The Guardian. FOX News and NPR also report the same incident, but NPR focuses on the BE FAST acronym and stroke awareness, omitting details about Murphy’s political activities. The Guardian includes a quote from Connecticut Lt. Gov. Susan Bysiewicz, which is not mentioned in other accounts. The Daily Wire and CBS News mention that Murphy was scheduled to attend campaign events in New Hampshire on Sunday, a detail not included in The Guardian or Straight Arrow News.

US midterms: Democrats face potential blue wave as Trump warns of 'Mr. Veto' role

The US midterm elections are approaching, with Democrats leading in national polls and facing a potential "blue wave" that could shift control of Congress. Opinion polls suggest a Democratic advantage, with some showing a 10- to 11-point lead. The cost of living, exacerbated by inflation and the war in Iran, has become a central issue. Trump, who is running for re-election, has been campaigning aggressively, but his approval rating is at its lowest since Truman's time. The Republican Party is struggling to maintain its slim majority in the House and Senate, with several key races in states like Kansas, Iowa, and Texas being closely contested. Trump has warned that if Democrats take control, he will become "Mr. Veto," indicating he would block their agenda.

The midterms are also shaping up to be a test of the Republican Party's ability to hold together, with internal divisions and financial challenges affecting their campaign efforts. Early voting has already seen a surge in Democratic participation, particularly in critical battlegrounds. The outcome of these elections could have significant implications for the future of the Trump administration and the direction of US policy.

Where the accounts differ

The Guardian reports that Trump's campaign rhetoric and actions have nationalized the races, leading to a potential loss of seats for Republicans. Al Jazeera highlights the economic issues and the impact of Trump's policies on the cost of living, while also noting the challenges in polling accuracy due to underrepresentation of Republicans. FOX News emphasizes the importance of key districts and the potential for a Democratic wave, while the New York Post focuses on specific Senate races and the threat to Republican incumbents. The Washington Examiner adds Trump's personal threat of becoming "Mr. Veto" if Democrats win, which is not mentioned in the other accounts.

Sources The Guardian · Al Jazeera · Axios · FOX News · New York Post · Washington Examiner

4 Americans among dozens killed in train crash in Spain, officials say

A high-speed train collision in central Spain killed at least 69 people, including four U.S. citizens and one each from Canada, Japan, Argentina, Mexico, Tunisia, and the Czech Republic, according to officials. The crash occurred on October 28 near Urda, in the province of Toledo, when a Renfe AVE train struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train, derailing two cars. Officials reported 58 people were injured, with more than 540 passengers on board both trains. Spain's transport minister, Óscar Puente, blamed a traffic controller's poor judgment, while railway unions cited deteriorating working conditions as a contributing factor. The incident marks Spain's second major rail crash this year, following a January accident that killed 46 people.

The crash has prompted national mourning in Spain, with three days of mourning observed. The Spanish prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, called the incident a "tragedy" and pledged an investigation. Among the victims was Dr. Marta Shahbazi, a University of Cambridge scientist who was traveling to collect a scientific excellence award. She was described by colleagues as an "inspiring, talented and passionate scientist" whose research focused on human embryo development and failed pregnancies.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that two citizens of Australia and one Japanese citizen remain missing, while The Guardian and BBC do not mention missing persons beyond the initial list. The Guardian and BBC attribute the crash to a state-operated Renfe train colliding with a stationary Iryo train, while CBS News also notes the crash occurred on the Madrid-Seville line. The Guardian and BBC mention the crash as one of Europe's deadliest rail accidents this century, a detail not included in CBS News. The Guardian and BBC provide details about Dr. Marta Shahbazi's professional and personal life, which CBS News does not mention.

Sources CBS News · The Guardian · BBC

1 dead, 8 injured in Alabama shooting after football game, police say

A shooting occurred after a football game in Birmingham, Alabama, on Saturday, resulting in one fatality and eight injuries. Birmingham police confirmed the incident took place near Legion Field, which hosted the Magic City Classic college football game between Alabama A&M and Alabama State universities. Officers responded to the 400 block of Grayman Avenue West shortly after 7:50 p.m. and found multiple gunshot victims. At least one person was detained and transported to police headquarters for questioning. Multiple firearms were recovered from the scene. Birmingham Mayor Randall Woodfin condemned the violence, calling it "senseless" and emphasizing the community's commitment to justice.

The shooting happened in the area surrounding the stadium, with police noting it occurred after the game but mostly off the property. Birmingham Police Chief Michael Pickett described the incident as a "senseless act of violence" and praised the officers for their response. The mayor highlighted the significance of the Magic City Classic as an event rooted in HBCU traditions, emphasizing family and unity in the face of tragedy.

Where the accounts differ

ABC News and Newsweek both report that one person was killed and eight were injured, while the New York Post and FOX News state that one person was killed and eight were wounded. ABC News and Newsweek mention that nine people were hit by gunfire, with one confirmed deceased and another in critical condition, while the New York Post and FOX News report that eight were wounded, with one in critical condition. Newsweek and FOX News include additional details about other shootings reported across the U.S. during Halloween weekend, which the other accounts omit. The New York Post and FOX News do not mention the mayor's statement about the event being a "night of celebration," which is included in ABC News and Newsweek. The New York Post and FOX News do not specify that the shooting occurred after the game, while ABC News and Newsweek do.

Residents evacuated from Russia-occupied city back in Ukraine

Residents evacuated from the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky crossed into Ukrainian-held territory on Saturday as part of a humanitarian mission during a U.S.-brokered temporary ceasefire. The civilians traveled by bus and plane through Russia and Belarus, following a deal brokered by U.S. first lady Melania Trump. Oleshky, located in Ukraine's southeastern Kherson region, has been under Russian occupation since the start of the Ukraine-Russia war and is described as one of the most dire humanitarian crises of the conflict. The ceasefire began at 3 a.m. Tuesday and is expected to continue through Sunday evening. Those who chose to return to Ukraine were taken to the Russian city of Anapa, then flown to Homel in Belarus, from where they proceeded back home. Ukrainian authorities imposed restrictions on disclosing the number of evacuees or the border crossing location to safeguard those who chose to remain in Oleshky and protect future humanitarian missions. Relatives and volunteers, including organizations like the Red Cross and World Central Kitchen, were present at the Ukrainian-Belarusian border to assist the evacuees.

Residents evacuated from the Russian-controlled city of Oleshky during a humanitarian mission disembarked from a bus upon arriving at an undisclosed location in Ukraine on Oct. 31, 2026. Liza Paliy, 19, who last saw her father over 2½ years ago, was among those waiting at the border. Daria Pavlyn also waited at the border in hopes of reuniting with her father, Mykhailo, whom she had not heard from in about a month. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy stated that the evacuees crossed the border in the northern Chernihiv region and would receive assistance from government agencies and local authorities. He thanked the United States and first lady for their mediation, as well as Ukraine’s Defense Forces and human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets, who helped coordinate the mission.
