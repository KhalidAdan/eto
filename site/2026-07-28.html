US and Iran appear to pause hostilities as Trump gives "diplomacy a chance"

The United States and Iran seem to have paused their military actions against each other, with President Trump indicating that he is giving diplomacy a chance. This comes after nearly two weeks of intense fighting between the two countries, which has resulted in significant losses on both sides.

According to reports, the US has not launched any strikes against Iranian targets since Thursday, and Iran has also refrained from attacking US interests. The pause in hostilities has been attributed to efforts by Pakistani and Qatari mediators to bring the two sides back to the negotiating table.

The US has reportedly made significant progress in its talks with Oman, which is working on a mechanism to manage vessels' passage through the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's foreign ministry spokesman confirmed that talks with Oman were continuing, focused on creating necessary arrangements regarding the strait.

Trump has stated that he wants to give diplomacy a chance and that "friendly talks are underway" with Iran. However, he also warned that the US could still "cripple Iran with full force" if negotiations fail.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that Trump said the US has plenty of ammunition, contradicting earlier reports of stockpile concerns. The Hill notes that Netanyahu's meeting with Trump will be his first in Washington since the breakout of the Iran war in February. Straight Arrow News highlights the Pentagon's change in how it counts casualties, separating public totals from the Iran war across two parts of its database.

Sources CBS News · The Hill · Straight Arrow News

Sen. Rand Paul releases over 1,000 pages of Dr. Anthony Fauci's diary entries from COVID-19 pandemic.

Dr. Anthony Fauci's diary entries, released by Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), provide a glimpse into the former Chief Medical Advisor to the President's thoughts and interactions during the COVID-19 pandemic. The entries span from December 2019 to December 2022 and cover various topics, including Fauci's relationship with President Trump, his media appearances, and his views on the origins of the virus.

Fauci's diary entries reveal a complex and often tumultuous relationship with President Trump. In one entry, Fauci wrote that Trump "wanted to chat about the coronavirus situation in general" during a surprise late-night call on February 27, 2020. However, Fauci noted that Trump underplayed the seriousness of the situation the next day.

Fauci also expressed frustration with Trump's fixation on media attention and his desire for Fauci to appear on TV more frequently. In one entry, Fauci wrote that he told Trump that he was being blocked from TV appearances because he wanted to avoid being pitted against the President or Vice President.

The diary entries also show Fauci's growing disillusionment with Trump as the pandemic progressed. By March 2021, Fauci had written that Trump was "truly an obnoxious adolescent."

Where the accounts differ

  • The Daily Wire and Just The News report on Fauci's diary entries in more detail than other outlets, including his interactions with President Trump.
  • NPR focuses on the disagreement between Sen. Rand Paul and Dr. Anthony Fauci over the origins of the COVID-19 virus, while CBS News and The Hill provide a broader overview of the diary entries.
  • The Guardian highlights Fauci's criticism of Trump's handling of the pandemic, including his description of Trump as "rambling," "crazy," and "incompetent."
  • Just The News reports that Fauci considered some of the media attention during the pandemic to be "flattering," while The Hill notes that Fauci was aware of the negative impacts of the attention on him and his family.

Sources The Daily Wire · NPR · CBS News · The Guardian · Just The News · The Hill

Three people killed and four others wounded in shooting at Seattle food festival.

A shooting occurred on Sunday evening during the Bite of Seattle food festival, an annual event that attracts 350,000 people over three days. According to authorities, two suspects were involved in a shootout, with one suspect surrendering at the scene and the other remaining at large. Three people were killed, including two men aged 19 and 44, and a 56-year-old woman. Four others were wounded, including a toddler who is currently in stable condition.

The shooting happened around 6:00 p.m. local time, with police arriving on the scene shortly after. One suspect was taken into custody "within seconds," according to Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson. The investigation is ongoing, and authorities are reviewing security footage and interviewing witnesses.

Where the accounts differ

  • The Daily Wire reports that a 15-year-old and a 19-year-old were identified as suspects, while other outlets only mention two suspects.
  • FOX News reports that a third suspect may have been involved in the shooting, citing court documents. However, this is not mentioned by other outlets.
  • The Guardian mentions that the Gun Violence Archive has reported over 270 mass shootings in the US so far this year, but this information is not present in other accounts.
  • Al Jazeera and BBC report that Assistant Seattle Police Chief Tyrone Davis stated there was no ongoing threat to the public, while The Daily Wire does not mention this statement.

Sources The Daily Wire · The Guardian · Al Jazeera · BBC · FOX News · UPI

Mitch McConnell's office shares new photo and update on senator's recovery from June fall.

Mitch McConnell, the 84-year-old Republican senator from Kentucky, has been recovering at a rehabilitation facility after a medical episode at his Washington, D.C., home last month. His office released a statement and a new photo of him with his wife, Elaine Chao, on Monday, saying he is "still working hard" to resume his full schedule in the Senate and in Kentucky. McConnell has been absent from the Senate since June 14, when he was hospitalized after taking a fall.

McConnell's office confirmed that he will miss an annual political picnic in his home state while he recovers. In a statement, McConnell said he is "looking forward to getting back to the Senate and to Kentucky soon." His attending physician also confirmed that he is not yet medically cleared to leave the rehab facility and return to the office.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts from The Hill and Newsweek report that Sen. Shelley Moore Capito (R-W.Va.) said a person who recently visited McConnell described him as "clear-minded." However, this claim is not mentioned in any other outlet.

The Daily Wire reports that McConnell's office releases a new photo plus a statement saying he is undergoing "intense physical therapy" and not ready to get back to work in person. This detail is also present in Newsweek but absent from The Hill.

Just The News does not mention the claim about McConnell being described as "clear-minded."

The Washington Examiner reports that Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) recently called on McConnell to resign if he cannot be more publicly transparent about his health, which is not mentioned in any other outlet.

Sources The Hill · New York Post · The Daily Wire · Newsweek · Just The News · Washington Examiner

Sen. Lee pressures GOP leaders to cancel or delay August recess until election-security bill passes.

Sen. Mike Lee (R-Utah) is pressuring Republican leaders to cancel or delay the upcoming August recess until the SAVE America Act is passed. The measure would require voters to prove their U.S. citizenship and provide a valid photo ID when registering, and it would restrict voting by mail. Lee has posted on X, requesting that Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.) cancel or delay the recess and calling for a roll call vote.

Thune has indicated that he may consider delaying or canceling the recess if there is a likelihood of passing the SAVE Act. However, he also stated that if there's no clear path for passage, there's no reason to keep lawmakers in Washington. Thune noted that vulnerable GOP incumbents need to get back to their home states to campaign ahead of the November election.

Several other Senate Republicans have expressed support for canceling the recess and focusing on passing the SAVE America Act. Sens. Rick Scott (R-Fla.), Ashley Moody (R-Fla.), and Jim Banks (R-Ind.) have all announced their intent to object to Thune calling for recess until the bill is passed.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts conflict over the number of senators who support canceling the August recess. Just The News reports that at least six other Senate Republicans support Lee's call, while Washington Examiner states that "several Republican senators and leading proponents of the bill are vowing to oppose a vote to leave for recess." New York Post also mentions that several Republican senators have expressed support for canceling the recess.

The accounts also differ in their characterization of Thune's position. Just The News reports that Thune told reporters he wouldn't rule out delaying or canceling the recess, while Washington Examiner states that Thune "warned against wasting time on a bill that has already failed multiple times on the Senate floor." New York Post quotes Thune as saying he wants to prioritize avoiding a government shutdown.

Sources Just The News · Washington Examiner · New York Post

Wildfires rage across southwestern France and Spain as heatwaves return.

Wildfires have been burning out of control in southwestern France and Spain for days, forcing mass evacuations and destroying homes. The blazes have been fueled by hot, dry weather conditions, with temperatures soaring above 40°C (104°F) in some areas. In France, the Gironde fire has burned over 100,000 hectares of land, about four times the size of Paris, and destroyed more than 240 homes. Spanish authorities have declared a national emergency as crews fight fires on two fronts - one west of Madrid and another near Valencia.

Where the accounts differ

The accounts from different outlets conflict in several areas. The Guardian reports that UN climate chief Simon Stiell warned that "the climate alarm is blaring, from every direction" and that the costs of the climate crisis are reaching national emergency levels (Guardian). However, NPR does not mention this warning and instead focuses on the immediate response to the wildfires, including evacuations and firefighting efforts. Al Jazeera reports that over 150,000 hectares have burned across Spain since January, while Straight Arrow News states that authorities evacuated 79,000 people in Spain.

Sources The Guardian · NPR · Al Jazeera · BBC · Straight Arrow News

ICE to target Haitians as Temporary Protected Status is set to end, sources say.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement has made plans to ramp up operations to arrest and deport Haitian migrants, as hundreds of thousands of Haitians brace for the termination of their legal status, according to two agency sources and federal documents obtained by CBS News. The plans include arresting migrants in Ohio — where large Haitian communities in cities like Springfield have been targeted by President Trump and conservative politicians and commentators — and quickly putting them on deportation flights to crisis-stricken Haiti.

The Supreme Court last month sided with the Trump administration in its efforts to end the TPS programs for Haitians and Syrians. That ruling's binding mandate is set to be issued to lower courts on Monday. Since that ruling, fear and uncertainty have swept throughout Haitian communities across the U.S. Immigrants whose TPS lapses immediately lose their ability to work in the country legally, and become eligible to be arrested and processed for deportation by ICE.

The operations targeting Haitian migrants could start as early as this week, the documents suggest, but more details about the scale of the plans were not immediately clear. The ICE sources who described the preparations requested anonymity to describe internal plans that have not been publicly disclosed and are fluid.

Where the accounts differ

CBS News reports that President Trump amplified baseless claims that Haitians in Springfield were eating cats and dogs during the 2024 presidential campaign, while FOX News does not mention this. The Washington Examiner mentions it but attributes it to "President Donald Trump's past comments". CBS News also states that ICE leadership has been notified of the operational plans, which is not mentioned by other outlets.

Sources CBS News · FOX News · The Daily Wire · Washington Examiner

Johnson & Johnson proposes $5.5 billion talc settlement to end marathon legal fight

Johnson & Johnson has agreed to pay $5.5 billion to settle remaining lawsuits claiming its talc products caused ovarian cancer. The company, which has been fighting talc-related lawsuits for over a decade, said the settlement is conditioned on at least 95% of remaining claimants participating. A U.S. bankruptcy court judge previously denied a $9 billion settlement proposed by Johnson & Johnson subsidiary Red River Talc last year.

The proposed settlement comes after a federal judge ordered plaintiffs to show why their claims should not be dismissed for failing to provide direct evidence linking the company's product to cancer. Johnson & Johnson has long denied that its talc products caused cancer, saying they were safe and did not contain asbestos. The company stopped selling talc-based baby powder in the US in 2020.

Johnson & Johnson expects to pay $3 billion next year as part of the settlement, with no additional payments due until 2028. The company's vice president of litigation, Erik Haas, said the resolution allows the company to put the matter behind it and remain focused on its mission to develop medicines and devices that save lives.

Where the accounts differ

Associated Press reports that Johnson & Johnson previously settled about 95% of filed lawsuits for mesothelioma, a cancer in the tissue surrounding organs like the lungs and heart. The Guardian does not mention this settlement. UPI mentions that Johnson & Johnson paid $2.5 billion to women whose claims were consolidated in Missouri in 2021, but Associated Press and The Guardian do not report this.

Sources Associated Press · The Guardian · UPI
