### Trump faces growing backlash over plans to import diesel from Russia

President Donald Trump announced a deal with Russia to supply diesel to the U.S. and global markets, including 300,000 tonnes immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November, and 1 million tonnes afterward. The agreement comes amid rising fuel prices and ahead of the midterm elections. The deal has also drawn criticism from a former NATO commander, who warned it could provide Putin with funds to continue the war in Ukraine. Analysts question whether Russia can deliver the promised quantities of diesel and whether it will significantly lower prices for American consumers.

### Hundreds of French supermarkets hit by cyberattack causing fridge temperatures to rise to 50°C

A cyberattack on refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets caused fridges and freezers to heat up to as high as 50°C (122F), spoiling food and causing fires at several stores. The attack targeted software provided by Danish engineering firm Danfoss, which allows retailers to manage their cold units remotely. Affected retailers include Intermarché, Cooperative U, Carrefour, Monoprix, and E Leclerc. The Paris prosecutor’s office described the incident as a “large-scale” cyberattack affecting “connected refrigerators in several major food retailers across France.” The attack led to food losses and prompted Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu to order an investigation by France’s National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI). The attack also prompted calls for greater cybersecurity measures in the food supply chain.

The attack disrupted refrigeration systems, leading to the destruction of stocks of fresh and frozen products. Intermarché CEO Thierry Cotillard estimated the losses at “tens of millions of euros nationwide in a single day.” Some stores had to throw away all affected products, while others were working to restore normal operations. The attack also caused issues for staff arriving at stores, with some encountering hot air from freezers and even small fires. The full scale of the attack remains unclear, but estimates suggest around 400 stores were affected out of 15,000 in France.

**Where the accounts differ**

Al Jazeera reports that Intermarché said at least 300 shops of its Netto brand were targeted, along with more than 100 Cooperative U outlets, while BBC states that Intermarché and its Netto brand affected 180 stores, with another 100 urgently disconnected from the internet. Al Jazeera also mentions that Carrefour said 80 supermarkets were affected, while BBC reports Carrefour said fridges in 80 of its 6,000 stores were hacked. Al Jazeera notes that Monoprix said about 5 percent of its roughly 490 stores were hit, while BBC states Monoprix said 5% of its 400-plus stores were affected. Al Jazeera reports that E Leclerc said several of its stores were targeted, while BBC does not specify the number of E Leclerc stores affected. BBC mentions that Danfoss did not confirm how warm the fridges and freezers could become, while Al Jazeera states that the fridges warmed to 50°C. BBC also reports that two supermarkets in the Alsace region reported losses of €270,000, which Al Jazeera does not mention.
