## Trump signs executive order to cut childhood vaccines recommendations, drawing medical concern

President Donald Trump signed an executive order Monday to reduce the number of vaccines recommended for children, sparking widespread concern among the medical community. The order, which calls for limiting vaccines for other diseases to high-risk populations, still recommends childhood vaccines for 11 diseases, including measles and polio. Trump claimed without evidence that childhood vaccines are linked to an increase in autism diagnoses. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who was present for the signing, has maintained this stance as well. The order also advises breaking up the MMR vaccine for measles, mumps and rubella into three separate vaccines. Vaccine advocates and researchers have warned that reducing childhood vaccine recommendations will make children more vulnerable to disease.

Major U.S. medical associations and American doctors were quick to rebuke the executive order as a rejection of science at a time when the United States is experiencing its largest measles resurgence in more than three decades. The National Foundation for Infectious Diseases said in a statement that the nation should be strengthening its ability to protect children, not putting that progress at risk. The American Medical Association criticized the executive order, stating that decisions about how and when vaccines are used should be driven by rigorous scientific evidence, patient safety and independent medical and public health expertise.

CBS News and Axios do not specify the exact number of diseases but both mention the MMR vaccine being split into three separate shots. UPI and The Guardian note that the MMR vaccine is not deadly, while Trump claimed it could be "quite lethal." FOX News and The Daily Wire attribute the claim about the MMR vaccine being "quite lethal" to Trump, while UPI and The Guardian attribute it to Trump as well.
