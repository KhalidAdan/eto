## US Senate confirms Jay Clayton as Director of National Intelligence

The US Senate has confirmed Jay Clayton as the next Director of National Intelligence (DNI) in a 51-47 vote, replacing acting DNI Bill Pulte. Clayton, who was serving as the US Attorney for the Southern District of New York, will oversee the country's 18 intelligence agencies. The confirmation comes after President Donald Trump's original choice, Tulsi Gabbard, resigned last month and was temporarily replaced by Pulte.

Clayton has a background in law and finance, having previously served as chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission. He was nominated to replace Pulte, who faced criticism for his lack of national security experience. Clayton's confirmation is seen as a victory for Trump, who had been pushing for his appointment despite opposition from Democrats.

The confirmation vote comes amid a heightened threat environment, with some lawmakers expressing concerns about the impact on national security. Senate Majority Leader John Thune praised Clayton's qualifications, saying he has "significant national security experience" and will be a "proven leader in this role."

## A 6.8-magnitude earthquake strikes Kumamoto, Japan, causing widespread damage and at least 13 deaths.

A powerful earthquake struck Kumamoto, Japan on Tuesday, leaving a trail of destruction and at least 13 dead. The quake, which measured 6.8 magnitude according to the US Geological Survey, caused widespread damage and power outages in the region. Rescue efforts are underway, with emergency services working to reach trapped individuals.

The earthquake also triggered a tsunami advisory, although it was later lifted within two hours. Aeon Mall Kumamoto, a shopping center that had recently reopened after years of reconstruction following a 2016 earthquake, suffered significant damage and an explosion occurred shortly after the quake. At least 50 people were injured in the blast, with many more feared trapped inside.

Rescue operations are ongoing, with hundreds of first responders from neighboring prefectures arriving on Tuesday evening to assist. The Japanese military has mobilized 3,600 personnel for disaster relief operations, and 20 aircraft have been sent to assess damage from the air.

## Canadian tourism numbers decline in US amid trade tensions and tariffs.

The Canadian government released a report detailing declining tourism numbers to the US, with Canadians making 29.1 million trips to the US in 2025, down from 39 million the year before. The report blames President Donald Trump's administration for the decline, citing his recent tariffs and other tensions between the countries as contributing factors.

However, other factors may also be at play. In years prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, there were fewer Canadian trips to the US than in 2025, with just 25 million trips in 2019. The lifting of COVID-19 restrictions in late 2022 may have contributed to higher numbers in 2023 and 2024.

The decline in numbers could also be due to costs associated with Trump's tariffs, which may be deterring Canadian travelers who are not feeling the same effects.

**Where the accounts differ**

Straight Arrow News reports that Canadians made 29.1 million trips to the US in 2025, while The Guardian states that Canadians spent $3.3bn less on travel to the US in 2025 than in the previous year. CBS News also reports a decline of roughly 25% in visits by Canadians to the US in 2025.

The Free Press provides context on the trade tensions and tariffs between Canada and the US, citing the White House's use of Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 as a provision aimed directly at Canada. Straight Arrow News does not mention this specific provision.

Straight Arrow News notes that Canadian tourism has seen a steady month-to-month increase so far in 2026, while The Guardian reports that return trips to Canada from the US are at similar levels seen at the end of 2025.

## Trump administration asks Supreme Court to allow enforcement of mail-in voting executive order.

The Trump administration has asked the Supreme Court to lift a lower court's block on an executive order aimed at tightening rules on mail-in voting ahead of the midterm elections in November. The executive order, issued in March, directs federal agencies to create state-by-state lists of eligible mail voters and would prevent the U.S. Postal Service from delivering ballots to people not included on those lists.

The administration argues that the lower court's injunction was premature because the agencies have not yet implemented any policy changes. Solicitor General D. John Sauer wrote in a petition to the Supreme Court that "the Order merely sets forth general policy guidance, leaving substantial discretion to agencies about how to implement the Order while admonishing them that they may do so only consistent with applicable law."

The lower court's block on the executive order was upheld by an appeals court, which agreed that the president lacks the authority to unilaterally change how federal elections are administered. The states challenging the order argue that the Constitution gives states and Congress, not the president, the authority to set election rules.

## White phosphorus leak reported at US military base in South Korea

South Korean authorities ordered civilians to evacuate after a suspected white phosphorus leak at the Osan Air Base near Seoul. The alert was lifted several hours later following decontamination work, according to text messages sent out by the city of Pyeongtaek. The U.S. 51st Fighter Wing said it established a cordon around the base "out of an abundance of caution" after experts identified white phosphorus crystallization inside a munitions crate.

The incident occurred at Osan Air Base, which is home to the U.S. Air Force's 51st Fighter Wing. The base commander, Col. Ryan Ley, said that the safety of service members and their families was the top priority. White phosphorus is a chemical substance used in incendiary munitions and smoke shells.

The cordon around the base was lifted after emergency response personnel mitigated the situation, allowing normal operations to resume. There were "no injuries resulting from this situation and no risk to personnel on or off installation," according to the U.S. 51st Fighter Wing.
