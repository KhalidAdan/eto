## Trump says US to ‘substantially reduce’ military drills with South Korea

US President Donald Trump has ordered the Pentagon to scale back joint military exercises with South Korea, citing his “very good relationship” with North Korea’s Kim Jong Un. The drills, scheduled to start on Monday, are described by Trump as costly and sending a “totally inappropriate and hostile” signal to North Korea. South Korea’s Ministry of Defence stated the exercises would proceed as previously scheduled. The annual Ulchi Freedom Shield drills involve 18,000 South Korean soldiers and are designed to prepare for defense against North Korea. North Korea has consistently condemned the exercises as provocative, with its foreign ministry calling them a “rehearsal for an aggressive war.”

## Jared Kushner meets with Hamas ahead of talks with Netanyahu on Gaza

Jared Kushner, Donald Trump’s son-in-law and Middle East envoy, held a meeting with Hamas’s political chief, Khalil al-Hayya, in Egypt on Sunday. The meeting was part of efforts to advance the stalled 15-point U.S.-backed road map for a Gaza ceasefire. The plan requires Hamas to disarm and Israel to withdraw from the territory. Kushner is set to meet with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday to discuss next steps. The talks aim to salvage the plan, which has been stalled over Hamas’s disarmament commitments. Several regional powers, including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan, and Pakistan, issued a joint statement condemning Israel’s rejection of the road map.

The meeting in Egypt included officials from mediating countries Egypt, Qatar, and Turkey. Hamas officials confirmed the meeting, stating the group is committed to the road map and awaiting mediators to start a 14-day negotiation period to finalize a timetable. Hamas also demanded that Israel halt attacks on Gaza and withdraw to the so-called “yellow line” before implementation. Israeli forces currently control about 60% of Gaza, with plans to expand control to 70%. Israel has killed over 1,200 Palestinians in Gaza since the ceasefire on October 10, 2025, according to Gaza’s health ministry.

## Trump administration pauses border construction in Big Bend national park

The Trump administration announced on Monday that it is temporarily pausing construction of a border wall segment in Big Bend National Park. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Commissioner Rodney Scott stated the pause would continue while he conducts an on-the-ground evaluation. The project, part of a $46.5 billion border wall expansion approved by Congress in 2025, has faced significant opposition from environmental groups, local officials, and residents. Scott’s announcement followed footage of bulldozers operating in the park, which had not been publicly disclosed earlier in the month.

Environmental advocates and local stakeholders have raised concerns about the impact of the construction on the park’s ecosystem, wildlife, and scenic value. Senator John Cornyn, a Texas Republican, expressed support for the pause, noting that the park’s natural terrain already serves as a deterrent against illegal crossings. Critics argue that the construction would damage the park’s landscape, threaten Native American sites, and disrupt wildlife migration.

The pause has been welcomed by some activists and local officials, who hope it will lead to modifications in the project. However, the administration has not yet provided details on what changes might be made or when construction could resume.
