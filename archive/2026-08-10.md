## Trump’s Iran War Is Going Backward

On Saturday, a senior Iranian national security official issued a set of demands that the US must meet before a key waterway for oil shipping can be reopened. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, the secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, called for the US to lift its sanctions and naval blockade on Iran, “completely compensate” Iran for war damages, “unconditionally” release frozen Iranian assets, and end all attacks on Iran’s allies in the region. According to multiple media outlets, the US government did not deliver an immediate response to the demands. Many of them resemble those in the interim ceasefire deal signed by the US and Iran in June. Back then, the Trump administration said that it would only lift sanctions completely if the two countries came to an agreement on Iran’s nuclear development. While there was an initial ceasefire, it did not last. A couple of days later, Iran’s military closed the Strait of Hormuz, a critical oil transit point, stating the US violated their agreement by allowing Israel to continue bombing Lebanon. Combat resumed and even expanded to Saudi Arabia and the Houthis in Yemen.

**Where the accounts differ**

Mother Jones and Straight Arrow News both report that Zolghadr listed the same conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, including lifting sanctions, ending the naval blockade, and compensating Iran for war damages. However, Straight Arrow News adds that Zolghadr also demanded the US end aggression against Iran, Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen, and Iraq, and withdraw its naval and air forces from Iran. The Washington Examiner and New York Post also mention these additional demands, while The Hill and UPI do not. The Hill reports that Vice President Vance said the US is “in the middle of the game” with Iran, while the New York Post and Straight Arrow News attribute this statement to Vance as well. The Washington Examiner and New York Post also note that Trump claimed the negotiations were going “very well” and a deal could be “soon,” a claim not mentioned in Mother Jones or Straight Arrow News.

**Sources**  Mother Jones - The Hill - Straight Arrow News - Washington Examiner - New York Post - UPI

## Shanghai cancels 1,300 flights as China prepares for Typhoon Dolphin

Typhoon Dolphin, which brought heavy rains to northern Taiwan, prompted authorities in China to cancel over 1,300 flights in Shanghai and relocate more than 300,000 people in surrounding regions. The storm’s outer bands hit northern Taiwan, including Taipei, on Saturday. Shanghai’s Hongqiao and Pudong airports canceled about 60% of Sunday’s expected flights. Authorities in Zhejiang province evacuated 390,000 people in Taizhou by Saturday evening and 900,000 in Wenzhou. In Fujian, 98,900 people were evacuated from high-risk areas. Forecasters warned of severe flooding and landslide risks, with some areas expected to receive 200-400 mm of rainfall over the next few days.

Authorities in Shanghai evacuated 30,000 people from riskier areas and suspended all water-based activities, including ferries and cruises. The storm’s maximum sustained winds were estimated at about 162 km/h (101 mph) as of Sunday morning. The National Meteorological Centre warned that the storm could slow over central and southwestern China, prolonging heavy rainfall and increasing flood and landslide risks.

## Some meat products with jalapeños may be linked to salmonella outbreak, USDA says

Federal officials issued a public health alert for meat and poultry products containing jalapeños that may be contaminated with salmonella, according to the U.S. Department of Agriculture. The alert applies to products regulated by the Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) that may have been affected by a recalled ingredient. The USDA said it learned of the issue when it discovered that multiple FSIS-regulated establishments had received a recalled ingredient. The agency released a list of 18 recalled products, including brands like Taylor Farms, Ready Meals, Meal Simple, Higher Harvest, and Deli Kitchen. The USDA noted that additional downstream products may be identified as the ingredient recall progresses.

The salmonella outbreak was first reported in mid-June and is linked to jalapeño peppers grown in Sinaloa, Mexico, and distributed by Coast Citrus Distributors. As of Aug. 5, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported that at least 345 people in 27 states had been sickened, with 36 hospitalized. Minnesota was particularly hard hit, with at least 110 cases confirmed. Of 191 patients interviewed, 93% reported eating at a Mexican-style restaurant before falling ill, including Chipotle and Qdoba. Both chains removed jalapeños from their menus. Albertsons voluntarily recalled four items containing jalapeños supplied by Taylor Farms.

**Where the accounts differ**

CBS News states that the USDA said there have been "no confirmed reports of illness" from FSIS-regulated products containing jalapeños sourced from Sinaloa. Straight Arrow News reports that FSIS said there have been "no confirmed illnesses tied specifically to the FSIS-regulated meat and poultry products containing the recalled jalapeños." CBS News also notes that the first illnesses were reported in mid-June and links them to jalapeños from Sinaloa, while Straight Arrow News attributes the outbreak to "fresh jalapeño peppers grown in Sinaloa, Mexico." CBS News mentions that Albertsons recalled four items, while Straight Arrow News states that the public health alert includes products sold under several brands and distributed through major retailers and convenience stores.

**Sources**  CBS News - Straight Arrow News
