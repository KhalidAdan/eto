## Lala is now a hurricane as it nears Hawaii’s Big Island

The National Hurricane Center said in a 10 a.m. HST position update that Lala had maximum sustained winds of 75 mph, making it a Category 1 hurricane, and was moving west-northwest at 9 mph with its center near the Big Island. Earlier advisories warned that mountainous terrain could produce winds stronger than those measured near the surface. The storm prompted a hurricane warning for Hawaii County and tropical storm warnings for Maui County, Oahu and Kauai County. Forecasters said rainfall could reach double digits across parts of the island chain, with the highest totals capable of causing life-threatening flooding and mudslides in steep terrain. Gov. Josh Green declared a statewide emergency ahead of the storm, saying Lala posed an imminent threat and that state and county agencies needed authority to clear debris, secure infrastructure and mobilize resources. The proclamation activated the Major Disaster Fund and authorized the Hawaii National Guard to assist civilian authorities if needed.

Lala, since downgraded to tropical storm status, battered several of the islands with strong winds and heavy rains without officially making landfall on any of them. Roughly 65% of electric customers on the Big Island were without power Sunday morning, and more than 130,000 households were dark across Hawaii’s three largest islands, according to NPR. A flood watch remains in effect for Oahu, Kauai and Niihau through late Sunday night. Officials warned people not to drive or walk through floodwaters.
