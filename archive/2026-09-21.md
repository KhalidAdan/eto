## Hispanic voters’ support for Trump wanes over economy and immigration

Hispanic voters who helped re-elect Donald Trump in 2024 are discontent with his second-term economic and immigration policies, according to recent polling – indicating they may not be willing to support the president’s party in the midterm elections. More than 65% of Hispanic voters said they are displeased with Trump’s handling of his job, and 78% said they would prefer to see the Democratic party in control of Congress, according to a September poll from the New York Times/Siena University. The economy and cost of living are top concerns for Hispanic voters, polls show. Trump’s message of economic populism helped him get elected in 2024, but voters, including Republicans, have soured on his handling of the economy amid high inflation and rising costs. The Iran war has pushed gas prices past $4 per gallon on average. Inflation has risen over the last year and remained stubborn at 3.4%, elevating grocery costs.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Guardian reports that 63% of Hispanic respondents told a Fox News survey in September they plan to vote for the Democratic congressional candidate in their district, while Al Jazeera and ABC News do not mention this specific poll. The Guardian also states that 54% of Hispanic voters said they would vote for the Democratic House candidate in an UnidosUS poll from April/May, compared with 27% who said they would favor the Republican, while ABC News and CBS News do not include this detail. The Guardian attributes the claim about Trump’s economic policies to the White House spokesperson, while other outlets do not mention this.

**Sources**  The Guardian - Al Jazeera - ABC News - CBS News - Axios - BBC

## ICE agent shoots and wounds man at traffic stop in Austin, Texas, officials say

An Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot and wounded a Venezuelan man during a traffic stop in Austin, Texas, on Sunday, according to multiple local officials. The man, identified as 28-year-old Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, was taken to a hospital in Austin in serious but stable condition before being placed into ICE custody. Austin Police Chief Lisa Davis confirmed that the shooting occurred after a traffic stop and that city police were not involved in the operation or the shooting. The city’s police arrived at the scene after the shooting and provided first aid to the injured man. Austin Mayor Kirk Watson called for a full investigation, emphasizing the city’s right to transparency and safety.

Eyewitness video captured moments after the shooting, showing the man clutching his chest and appearing distressed. A bystander, Nedavia Bolaños, recorded footage of the incident, expressing shock at the presence of federal officers in tactical gear who did not respond to her and her husband’s questions. The man was later seen being handcuffed and placed on the ground by ICE officers, with at least one visible bullet wound between his shoulder blades. His lawyer, Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch, stated that Garcés Pérez had arrived in the U.S. two years ago from Venezuela and was making a DoorDash delivery when he encountered ICE.

**Where the accounts differ**

The New York Post and ABC News both state that the man was in the U.S. illegally and had a final order of removal, a claim not made by The Guardian, The Hill, or Straight Arrow News. The New York Post and ABC News also mention that the shooting occurred during a “small foot pursuit,” a detail absent in other accounts. The Washington Examiner and Straight Arrow News note that the man was shot in the torso, while The Guardian and The Hill do not specify the location of the wound. The New York Post and ABC News claim that the man was not legally in the U.S., while The Guardian and The Hill do not specify his immigration status beyond his arrival from Venezuela.

**Sources**  The Guardian - New York Post - The Hill - ABC News - Straight Arrow News - Washington Examiner

## Trump Wants to Make His Triumphal Arch a Killing Machine

President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that he plans to convert his proposed 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington, D.C. into a "top grade military complex" equipped with snipers and drone storage. The arch, which was first proposed in October 2025 to commemorate America’s 250th anniversary, would be located near the National Mall, between Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial. Trump cited a "strong request" from the U.S. military and national security concerns as the rationale for the change. The project has faced legal challenges, including a lawsuit from Vietnam War veterans who argue it blocks views of Arlington National Cemetery and lacks congressional approval. A federal judge ordered the administration to provide 48 hours’ notice before any work on the project. The National Park Service has also raised concerns about the arch’s potential adverse effects on nearby landmarks.

The arch, which would be one of the tallest structures in Washington, D.C., has drawn criticism for its height and location. The Federal Aviation Administration stated the arch poses no hazard to flights at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. However, Democratic lawmakers and heritage groups have raised concerns about the project’s impact on historic sites and airspace. Trump has previously used similar national security arguments to justify other projects, including a White House ballroom, which the Supreme Court allowed to proceed despite opposition.

**Where the accounts differ**

These are conflicting descriptions of the arch’s height. ## Russia election results show Putin’s party winning; Ukraine launches drone attacks on final day

Russia’s parliamentary elections, held since the start of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, saw President Vladimir Putin’s United Russia party dominate early results, according to state media. The Central Election Commission reported 90.43% of ballots counted, with United Russia securing 57.86% of the vote. Other parties, including the Communist Party and the Liberal Democratic Party, trailed behind but were still projected to win seats. The vote, which included residents of annexed Ukrainian regions and Crimea, was described as tightly controlled with limited opposition to Putin’s policies.

Ukraine launched a major drone attack on the final day of the election, targeting Moscow and its surrounding areas. Russian officials reported shooting down over 1,000 drones, with at least two people killed and 20 injured. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy claimed the strikes hit a Russian oil refinery and logistics facilities, aiming to disrupt the election and pressure Russia’s economy. The attack coincided with Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure, including a monastery and residential areas, which killed additional civilians.
