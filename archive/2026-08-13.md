## ICE plans to equip officers with electric shock gloves as state officials warn of legal risks

Federal authorities are moving forward with plans to equip Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers with electric shock gloves, known as G.L.O.V.E. (Generated Low Output Voltage Emitter), which deliver small electrical pulses to deter combative detainees. The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has allocated up to $20 million for the purchase of these devices, which are manufactured by Compliant Technologies in Kentucky. The gloves are described as a "conductive distraction and de-escalation device" and are intended to be used in situations where officers need to subdue individuals without causing physical harm. ICE has stated that officers will undergo training and re-certification every two years before using the equipment.

New York state officials, including Governor Kathy Hochul and Attorney General Letitia James, have raised legal concerns about the use of the gloves within the state. They argue that the technology could violate New York law and expose ICE officers to legal consequences if used in the state. Hochul and James have indicated that their offices are reviewing the DHS proposal and may pursue legal action if the devices are used in New York and cause harm.

**Where the accounts differ**

FOX News reports that the gloves are described as "electric shock gloves" and that the state officials are warning of legal consequences. The Washington Examiner and The Daily Wire also mention the legal concerns but do not specify the exact nature of the state officials' warnings. The Guardian and NPR do not mention the legal concerns or the specific equipment being discussed. The Washington Examiner provides a range of $10 million to $20 million for the purchase, while FOX News and The Daily Wire specify $20 million. The Daily Wire notes a 1,300% surge in assaults by individuals ICE is trying to arrest since the Trump administration began its deportation campaign, which is not mentioned in other accounts.

## Trump Admin Releases Final Rule Attacking Healthcare for Low-Income Trans Kids

The Trump administration finalized a rule on Tuesday that prohibits Medicaid and the Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP) from covering puberty blockers, hormone therapy, and surgeries for transgender youth. The rule, which takes effect October 13, applies to patients under age 18 for Medicaid and under 19 for CHIP. A six-month tapering period is provided for those already receiving hormone therapy. The administration cites a 2025 HHS report and the UK’s Cass Review as justification for the restrictions, which it claims lack sufficient evidence of clinical benefit and pose serious risks. The rule does not affect mental health services for gender dysphoria.

The final rule is part of a broader effort by the Trump administration to limit access to gender-affirming care for minors. The administration argues that such treatments are experimental and can cause irreversible harm, including infertility, impaired sexual function, and altered brain development. CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. have both defended the policy as following the science and protecting children from unnecessary risks. Advocacy groups, including the Trevor Project, have criticized the rule as a dangerous attempt to restrict access to essential care, citing research linking gender-affirming treatments to lower rates of depression and suicide risk among transgender youth.

**Where the accounts differ**

The service was launched on August 1, 2026, and is part of a broader effort to improve the financial health of Trump Media & Technology Group, which has faced significant stock price declines.

The lawsuit also highlights concerns about insider trading, with Democratic lawmakers and legal experts raising questions about whether the service enables Wall Street firms to profit from the president's announcements on economic policy, the war in Iran, and other topics. The government plans to establish a period for people to surrender illegal firearms, with possible exemption from penalties. Data from 2017 shows Thailand had the highest number of civilian-owned firearms in Southeast Asia, with over 10 million guns, many unregistered. The new school safety plan includes stricter entry screening and increased mental health support. Investigators are still seeking a motive for the shooting, though police noted the boy had watched violent videos online and once brought an air gun to school.
