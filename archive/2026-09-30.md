### Tennessee to execute a woman for first time in 200 years after governor denies clemency

Christa Pike, a woman convicted of murder 30 years ago, is set to be executed in Tennessee on Wednesday after her request for clemency was denied by Governor Bill Lee. Pike, now 50, was 18 when she and her boyfriend killed 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer in 1995. The case gained attention due to the brutality of the killing, the ages of the people involved, and the presence of a satanic symbol carved into the victim’s body. Pike was convicted of first-degree murder and sentenced to death, making her the youngest woman to be put on death row in modern US history. Her attorneys argue that her mental health challenges and history of trauma should mitigate her sentence. The last woman executed in Tennessee was in 1820.

Pike’s attorneys have asked the Supreme Court to stay the execution, citing her mental health and the potential for severe psychological distress during the lethal injection. They claim her childhood included sexual abuse and neglect, which were not fully presented to the jury during her trial. Pike has since been diagnosed with bipolar disorder and post-traumatic stress disorder. She has also requested an all-female execution team, which Tennessee corrections officials have agreed to accommodate as much as possible.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Guardian and The Hill mention that Pike’s boyfriend, Tadaryl Shipp, was 17 at the time and received a life sentence with the possibility of parole, while The New York Post and Straight Arrow News note that Shipp was 17 and received a life sentence with the possibility of parole.
