## El-Sayed holds narrow lead in Michigan Senate primary but race too close to call

Abdul El-Sayed, a progressive former public health official, held a narrow lead over Congresswoman Haley Stevens in Michigan’s Democratic Senate primary on Tuesday, according to the Associated Press. With 95% of votes counted, El-Sayed had 48.7% and Stevens 47.3%. The race remains too close to call as ballot counting continued into Wednesday morning. El-Sayed, who ran on a platform of universal healthcare and campaign finance reform, called for unity against the Republican nominee, former representative Mike Rogers, whom he criticized as a “corporate sellout.” Stevens, who received tens of millions in outside spending, including from AIPAC, cast herself as a pragmatic problem-solver. Both candidates emphasized the importance of uniting the Democratic base ahead of the November general election.

The race has drawn national attention as a proxy battle between the party’s progressive and centrist wings. El-Sayed’s victory, though narrow, is seen as a sign of growing support for progressive populism within the Democratic Party. However, the results also highlight the challenges of winning a swing state like Michigan, where Republicans have won twice in the past. With 13 weeks left before the general election, El-Sayed faces the task of reuniting a divided party and appealing to independent voters.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Guardian and Al Jazeera both report El-Sayed had 48.7% and Stevens 47.3% with 95% of votes counted, while Al Jazeera notes 91% of votes were counted. The Guardian and Mother Jones differ on the margin of victory, with Mother Jones stating El-Sayed led by about 1 point, while The Guardian and Al Jazeera note a smaller margin. The Guardian and CBS News mention El-Sayed’s campaign focused on ending military aid to Israel, while Al Jazeera and Axios highlight the role of Israel policy in the race. Mother Jones and Axios report El-Sayed was outspent 9 to 1 by Stevens and her allies, while The Guardian and Al Jazeera do not specify the exact spending figures. The Guardian and CBS News attribute El-Sayed’s campaign to “Medicare for All,” while Al Jazeera and Axios emphasize his stance on ending military aid to Israel.

## Trump says deal to reopen Strait of Hormuz could come as early as Wednesday

U.S. President Donald Trump stated that a deal on the Strait of Hormuz could be announced as early as Wednesday. The agreement, which would allow oil tankers to freely exit the Persian Gulf, is emerging from talks between Iran and Oman. The Trump administration has previously ruled out any deal that would give Iran control over the strait, which was an open international waterway before the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28. Oil prices eased on hopes of progress for a deal that would allow oil tankers to freely exit the Persian Gulf again and deliver crude around the world.

The U.S. and Iran are nearing an interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with the U.S. aiming for a Wednesday announcement. The deal would set up a 60-day temporary arrangement between Oman and Iran, allowing ships to enter the Gulf through a northern lane via Iranian waters and exit through a southern lane via Omani waters. No tolls or fees would be charged during the 60-day period. The White House, Oman, and regional mediators thought they had a deal with Iran three weeks ago, but Iran resumed attacks on ships, leading to two weeks of fighting and almost an all-out war.

## Senate votes to confirm Dr. Erica Schwartz as CDC director

The Senate voted on Wednesday to confirm Dr. Erica Schwartz as the next director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Schwartz was confirmed in a 51-44 vote that generally fell along party lines. She will replace Dr. Jay Bhattacharya, who took over as acting CDC director in February, and will become the first permanent director in about a year. President Donald Trump nominated Schwartz in April, describing her as "incredibly talented." During her confirmation hearing, Schwartz faced questions from senators on vaccines and running agencies independently. Ranking Member Sen. Bernie Sanders asked if she would remove a webpage on the CDC's website linking autism and vaccines, which she did not commit to removing. Chairman Sen. Bill Cassidy said he spoke with her again and reviewed the transcript of her hearing, stating she "knows what she is doing and will stand against those who do not."

Schwartz's confirmation comes after nearly a year without a full-time director. She succeeds Dr. Susan Monarez, who was fired by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. after less than a month in the role, allegedly due to disagreements over vaccine policy. Public health leaders have expressed mixed reactions, with some concerned about her ability to operate independently from Kennedy. Dr. Richard Besser, former acting director of the CDC, expressed hope that Schwartz would encourage vaccination and address public health issues like the cyclosporiasis outbreak.
