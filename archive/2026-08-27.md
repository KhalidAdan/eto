## 3 Americans among hundreds missing after Nepal flash flooding

A flash flood in Nepal on Wednesday killed at least 72 people and left hundreds missing, including three Americans. The disaster struck the northern Rasuwa district along the border with Tibet around 8:30 a.m. local time. The Nepal Tourism Board reported approximately 384 travelers missing, though many nationalities remain unverified. The U.S. Embassy in Nepal is assisting in locating American citizens, but communication has been disrupted. The flood also affected areas along the Bhote Koshi, Trishuli, and Narayani rivers, with severe flooding reported in several districts.

The flood was triggered by a glacial collapse and debris flow, not an earthquake, as confirmed by the U.S. Geological Survey. The International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development warned of potential secondary flooding due to debris blocking river systems. Search-and-rescue operations are ongoing, with Nepali authorities and international teams working to locate survivors and recover bodies. The U.S. Embassy has advised American tourists in the region to seek higher ground and follow local instructions.

**Where the accounts differ**

Straight Arrow News reports three Americans are missing, while The Daily Wire states more than 50 Americans are among the missing. Associated Press and The Daily Caller both mention 54 Americans missing, but The Daily Wire attributes this number to the Nepal Tourism Board. The Guardian identifies 34 Australians missing, including children as young as 11, which is not mentioned in other accounts. The Daily Caller and Associated Press differ in the number of missing people, with The Daily Caller citing 384 missing and Associated Press reporting over 1,300 missing. The Guardian also notes that nearly 1,500 people are missing, a figure not mentioned elsewhere.

**Sources**  Straight Arrow News - Associated Press - BBC - The Daily Wire - The Daily Caller - The Guardian

## Canada Is Spending Billions to Help Its Workers Survive Our Tariffs

Canadian officials announced a $7.5 billion support package for businesses and workers affected by U.S. tariffs, including $3 million non-repayable loans for small businesses and $2 million interest-free loans. The package also includes $2 billion for medium and large firms, $500 million for retraining, and a one-year extension of temporary employment insurance. The measures come after U.S.-Canada trade negotiations collapsed, with the U.S. imposing 50% tariffs on $20 billion in Canadian imports. Canada retaliated with dollar-for-dollar tariffs, which will take effect on September 8.

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs, which include products like steel, aluminum, and dairy, are designed to match U.S. levies. The Canadian government also emphasized the need for a "right attitude" from the U.S. in future negotiations, with Prime Minister Mark Carney stating Canada will return to talks if the U.S. shows true partnership.

## Trump claims all mines removed from Strait of Hormuz; Iran and Oman near agreement

He warned Iran that any new mine placement would result in immediate destruction. The clearing of mines is part of a low-key operation that has been ongoing for several months, allowing more oil tankers to transit the strait. Iran and Oman have been in talks to establish a temporary shipping route through the strait, with both nations expressing hope for a soon-to-be-announced agreement. The U.S. military has been using the southern lane of the strait to move oil tankers, with over 500 ships transiting through that route in the last 30 days.

Iran and Oman have been discussing a framework to create a temporary joint corridor through the strait and clear mines, while also holding technical talks on a permanent navigation route. The U.S. has been blocking Iranian ships from moving through the strait, and Trump's announcement on mine removal has made the U.S. achieve much of its goal in the strait. However, an Omani-Iranian agreement may not be relevant if the U.S. blockade continues to prevent Iranian ships from passing through.
