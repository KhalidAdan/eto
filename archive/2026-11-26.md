### Iowa braces for controlled bomb blast: ‘We had only five days of warning’

The U.S. Army has scheduled a controlled detonation of 68 barrels of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, on Wednesday. ### Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade postponed for first time in history

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, marking its 100th anniversary, has been postponed for the first time in its history due to severe weather. The event, which typically takes place on Thanksgiving Day, will now occur on Saturday at 8:30 a.m. The decision was made after consultations with city and agency officials, citing safety concerns related to an approaching storm. Macy's stated that the safety of participants and spectators is their top priority. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani acknowledged the inconvenience but emphasized that safety must come first. The National Weather Service has issued high wind warnings, with gusts potentially reaching 60 mph, which could make it unsafe for the balloons to fly.

The parade, which features giant inflatables and live performances, has been a New York tradition since 1924. This year's event was expected to include 100 inflatables, including new designs. The balloons are typically inflated on the Upper West Side, but due to the storm, they will be deflated and stored in New Jersey until Saturday. The NYPD has confirmed that there are no known specific or credible threats to the parade, though an increased police presence will be on hand for security.

**Where the accounts differ**

### US preparing to unveil plan for new tariffs on global trading partners

The US administration has proposed new tariffs on imports from 14 major trading partners, including China, the European Union, and several Asian countries. The tariffs are being introduced under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, following a legal challenge to previous tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. The proposed duties aim to address what the administration calls unfair foreign manufacturing practices that disadvantage American producers. The tariffs are not expected to take effect until next year and will undergo public consultation and hearings before finalization.

The new tariffs are part of a broader trade policy that includes investigations into the trade practices of various countries. The administration argues that foreign governments have supported domestic manufacturing through subsidies and other policies that drive down prices and weaken American industries. The proposed tariffs vary by country, with China facing up to 20% in some cases, which could be stacked with existing tariffs to reach 40%. Other countries, such as Vietnam, face up to 35%, while the EU, Japan, and South Korea face up to 15% or 18%. The administration claims the tariffs are based on trade agreements and investigations, and they are not automatically stacked on top of existing duties.
