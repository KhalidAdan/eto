## Energy Department raises 2027 diesel price outlook by 33 cents amid Iran war

The U.S. Energy Department's statistics arm raised its forecast for 2027 diesel prices by 33 cents to $4.40 a gallon, citing tight global supplies and low domestic inventories. The outlook suggests Americans may face elevated fuel costs through 2027, with President Trump acknowledging that oil prices may not fall until after the midterms. The EIA also raised its 2026 forecast to $5.07 a gallon. The war in Iran and disruptions in the Middle East, Ukraine, and Russia are contributing to supply losses and high prices. The EIA expects distillate fuel oil inventories to drop below 100 million barrels in September and remain low through much of 2027. 

The Iran war has driven up global energy costs, with oil prices reaching $100 per barrel and diesel hitting a record $6.05 a gallon. The conflict has strained shipping routes, with tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz falling to its lowest level since May. The war has also led to attacks on oil infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, with at least 73 people wounded. The economic impact has been significant, with U.S. households spending an estimated $860 more on energy due to higher prices. The war has also affected the U.S. freight and delivery network, with higher fuel costs impacting groceries and packages.
