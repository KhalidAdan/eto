## Missouri Supreme Court to Review Redistricting Map for Third Time

The U.S. Supreme Court has agreed to review a Missouri redistricting case for a third time, following a federal appeals court ruling that ordered the state to use a GOP-friendly map for the November 2026 general election. The case centers on whether Missouri can use different congressional maps for its August primary and November general election. The 8th Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that using the 2022 map for the general election would violate the Constitution, as it would force some voters to choose from candidates not on their primary ballot. The court ordered Missouri to use the 2025 map, which was passed by the legislature in 2025, for the general election. Justice Brett Kavanaugh directed Missouri officials to respond to the appeal by Wednesday afternoon. The dispute has led to confusion for over a million voters, with absentee voting already underway under the 2022 map.

The battle over the map has been ongoing for weeks, with courts issuing conflicting orders. The 2025 map, which Republicans hope will help them win additional House seats, was initially blocked by the Missouri Supreme Court, which ruled it could not take effect without voter approval. The 8th Circuit Court of Appeals later ruled in favor of the 2025 map, contradicting the state court’s order. The Supreme Court has previously blocked efforts to use the 2025 map, but the latest ruling has reignited the legal battle. The outcome could shape the balance of power in Congress as Republicans seek to maintain their slim majority in the House.

**Where the accounts differ**
