### Brazil heads to a runoff election after Bolsonaro and Lula fail to garner a majority of votes

Brazil’s presidential election resulted in neither candidate securing a majority, leading to a runoff between incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Flávio Bolsonaro, the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro. The electoral court confirmed that both candidates will face off in a second round on October 25. Lula, 80, is seeking a fourth non-consecutive term, while Bolsonaro, 45, is running as the leading right-wing candidate. The election comes amid a polarized political climate, with significant implications for Brazil’s future direction and its relationships with global powers.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Washington Examiner and Al Jazeera both state that Bolsonaro received 47% of valid votes and Lula 45.1%, while the Guardian reports Bolsonaro with 47% and Lula with 45.1%. The Guardian also mentions Bolsonaro received 56.1 million votes to Lula’s 53.8 million, a figure not included in the Washington Examiner or Al Jazeera. The BBC and Newsweek do not provide specific vote counts but note that neither candidate reached the 50% threshold. The Guardian and Al Jazeera mention Bolsonaro’s lead over Lula in the first round, while the BBC and Newsweek describe the race as closely contested with polls showing them roughly tied. The Guardian and Al Jazeera also note that Bolsonaro’s father, Jair Bolsonaro, is serving a 27-year sentence for plotting a coup, a detail included in the BBC and Newsweek but not in the Guardian or Al Jazeera.

### Trump praises Israeli hero who thwarted cockpit attack, calls him 'my plumber'

President Donald Trump praised Israeli citizen Yaniv Hayoun, a Flydubai passenger who helped subdue a radicalized Omani co-pilot during a mid-flight attack, calling him “my plumber” and a “Trump fan.” Trump shared a video on his Truth Social account highlighting Hayoun’s actions, which he described as heroic. ### US Coast Guard searches for missing air ambulance off coast of Massachusetts

The US Coast Guard confirmed it found debris from a medical transport aircraft off the shore of Massachusetts’ Nantucket Island and is searching for the six people who were on board. The jet lost contact with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) during a flight from Bermuda to Boston on Saturday morning. The Canadian-registered air ambulance jet had four crew members and two passengers onboard when it departed Bermuda’s LF Wade International Airport overnight for the flight to Massachusetts. The plane was flying at 24,000 feet (7,300 metres) for much of the journey before descending 13,000 feet in about two minutes shortly before flight controllers lost contact. The Massachusetts Port Authority confirmed the aircraft did not arrive at Boston Logan International Airport as scheduled.

The FAA issued a search-and-rescue alert notice for the Gulfstream G-100 at about 1.20am local time Saturday. The plane is operated by Latitude Air Ambulance, based in Ontario, Canada, and provides medical evacuation services. The company’s website states it has run thousands of missions since 2009 with a "flawless safety record." The Coast Guard deployed two search and rescue planes, a helicopter, and a motor lifeboat to continue the search. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) is gathering information but has not provided an update on the situation.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Guardian and CBS News both state the plane departed Bermuda at 12:41am on Saturday, while FOX News and Straight Arrow News report the departure time as 11:41pm on Friday. The Guardian and CBS News mention the plane descended 13,000 feet in about two minutes, while FOX News and Al Jazeera report the plane plummeted from 24,000 feet to 11,000 feet in three minutes. The Guardian and CBS News attribute the statement about the plane descending to flight data, while FOX News and Al Jazeera attribute it to FlightAware data. The Guardian and CBS News note the FAA issued a search-and-rescue alert at 1:20am, while FOX News and Straight Arrow News report the FAA issued the alert at 1:20am as well, but Al Jazeera states the FAA alerted the Coast Guard at about 2am on Saturday. The Guardian and CBS News mention the plane had four crew members and two passengers, while FOX News and Al Jazeera specify the composition as two Canadian pilots, two medical staff, a patient, and a relative.

**Sources**  The Guardian - NPR - CBS News - FOX News - Al Jazeera - Straight Arrow News

### White House Monitors Suspected Plague Case in Russia

The White House is monitoring reports of a suspected plague case in Russia, according to a Trump administration official. A 28-year-old woman, Daria Shipilova, worked at an anti-plague research institute in Irkutsk, Russia, and died after breaking a test tube containing the pneumonic plague pathogen. She was hospitalized on Sept. 29 with severe pneumonia and died between Oct. 1 and 2. Russian authorities imposed quarantines at several medical facilities and placed nearly 200 people under medical observation. Russian health authorities have not confirmed that Shipilova contracted plague, stating she had "pneumonia of unknown etiology" and that tests found no microorganisms linked to her work. The White House referred questions to the State Department, which did not respond to a request for comment.

Russian officials have taken precautionary measures in response to Shipilova's death, including quarantining hospitals and monitoring contacts. The Russian public health agency described the epidemiological situation in the region as stable. Some outlets reported that five hospitals were placed under quarantine, while others cited three. The number of quarantined hospitals and the exact number of people under observation vary across reports.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller state that 200 people were placed under quarantine, while Axios and Straight Arrow News report nearly 200. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller claim that Shipilova broke a test tube containing live Yersinia pestis, a claim not explicitly made by Axios or Straight Arrow News. The Daily Wire and The Daily Caller attribute the claim about the test tube to The Moscow Times, while Axios and Straight Arrow News do not specify the source.
