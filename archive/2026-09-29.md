### Senate passes ‘Protect College Sports Act’ with sweeping bipartisan vote

The Senate on Monday passed the “Protect College Sports Act” (PCSA) with a 77-22 vote, marking a major legislative achievement for the NCAA. The bill aims to address concerns about fairness and equity in college sports by granting the NCAA federal antitrust protection, capping agent fees at 5 percent, and limiting school spending on athletes to $50 million annually. It also guarantees scholarships for 10 years after eligibility and allows one free transfer without losing a year of eligibility. The legislation includes provisions on conference realignment and minimum revenue sharing for smaller sports. The bill was co-authored by Sens. Ted Cruz (R-Texas) and Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.) and is expected to move to the House for consideration after the midterm elections.

President Donald Trump praised the bill as a “really big deal,” calling it essential for saving college sports and colleges themselves. The legislation seeks to create a national framework for college athletics, replacing the current patchwork of state laws and court decisions. It would set limits on how much schools can directly pay athletes through revenue sharing and establish federal rules for NIL deals, including disclosure and contract requirements. The bill also grants the NCAA greater authority to enforce its rules while providing additional protections for athletes' scholarships and medical coverage.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Hill and Washington Examiner both report the bill passed 77-22, while The Daily Caller states the vote was 74-24. The Hill and Axios note the bill includes a provision limiting increases to ticket prices schools can charge for major sporting events, which The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner omit. The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner mention the bill grants the NCAA limited immunity from federal antitrust laws, while The Hill and Axios describe it as granting sweeping federal antitrust protection. The Daily Caller and Washington Examiner include details about the bill’s provisions on athletic department revenue caps and roster protections, which are not mentioned in The Hill or Axios. The Hill and Washington Examiner report the bill includes a five-year eligibility rule, while The Daily Caller and Axios describe it as allowing one free transfer without losing a year of eligibility.
