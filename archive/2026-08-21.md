## Trump orders reduction of U.S.-South Korea drills, citing North Korea concerns

U.S. President Donald Trump announced on Monday that the Pentagon should “substantially reduce” the annual joint military exercises between the United States and South Korea, which were set to begin on Monday. The order came via Trump’s Truth Social platform, with the president describing the drills as sending an “inappropriate and hostile” signal to North Korea. The drills, known as Ulchi Freedom Shield, were initially scheduled to last 11 days, but by Wednesday, the U.S. and South Korea had agreed to cut the exercise to five days and scale back some field training drills. South Korean officials expressed surprise at the lack of prior notice, with Foreign Minister Cho Hyun stating that neither the government nor U.S. officials were informed of Trump’s directive in advance.

The decision has sparked debate in South Korea over the reliability of the U.S. security guarantee, which has been a cornerstone of the alliance since the Korean War. South Korean President Lee Jae Myung called for strengthening the country’s self-reliant defense capabilities while expressing support for the U.S.-South Korea alliance. However, some South Korean lawmakers and citizens have criticized Trump’s approach, with one user on social media stating, “We can’t trust everything Trump says.”

**Where the accounts differ**

Al Jazeera reports that Trump’s order was made public via his Truth Social platform, while ABC News states that the announcement was made via a press availability on Monday. UPI and Washington Examiner both note that Trump cited a “good relationship” with Kim Jong Un as a reason for the reduction, but Al Jazeera and Mother Jones attribute this claim to Trump directly, while UPI and Washington Examiner attribute it to U.S. officials. Mother Jones and Associated Press mention that North Korea launched a missile barrage the day after the drills were scaled back, while Al Jazeera and UPI do not mention the missile launches. Al Jazeera and Mother Jones include quotes from South Korean officials and citizens, while ABC News and UPI focus more on the statements from North Korea and the U.S. government.

**Sources**  Al Jazeera - ABC News - UPI - Washington Examiner - Mother Jones - Associated Press
