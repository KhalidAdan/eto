### FAA Ground Stop Blamed on Router Overload and Copper Wire Replacement

On November 13, 2026, a nationwide FAA ground stop stranded hundreds of thousands of airline travelers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy attributed the incident to a router overload caused by the replacement of copper wire with fiber-optic cable in El Dorado, Arkansas. He stated that the router in question was broadcasting data into the national airspace network, leading to system instability. The ground stop lasted over four hours, with flights resuming by 8:15 p.m. Friday. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford confirmed that the system was stable post-ground stop and that no lives were at risk. Duffy also called for additional congressional funding to complete the FAA modernization, which includes replacing all copper wiring by the end of 2028.

Duffy emphasized that the issue was not a cyberattack but rather a technical problem with the aging air traffic control system. He noted that the FAA had spent approximately 38% of the $12.5 billion allocated for modernization, with an additional $10.1 billion needed to complete the project. During a press conference, Duffy mentioned that the router in question was being investigated at an L3Harris lab in Florida, and that the issue may have originated from either user error or a faulty installation.
