## US strikes Iran for first time in over a month

The United States launched strikes at Iran on Sunday afternoon for the first time in over a month. The U.S. military struck Larak Island after observing Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps forces preparing to deploy sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, according to a statement from U.S. Central Command. The strikes hit two rocket launchers to protect the commercial shipping lane after the military finished clearing the mines throughout the waterway last week. The strikes mark a return to military action after the Trump administration shifted its focus to economic measures, as the U.S. last struck Iranian missile and drone facilities on July 29.

The Mother Jones article focuses on internal U.S. military concerns about the sustainability of the war, which is not covered in the other accounts. The Al Jazeera and Associated Press accounts focus on the economic impact of the conflict, which is not covered in the other accounts.

## Two people dead and nearly 80 rescued after Grand Canyon flash flood

A flash flood struck the Grand Canyon over the weekend, resulting in at least two deaths and the rescue of nearly 80 people. The National Park Service (NPS) confirmed a second death on Monday, with the body of a 46-year-old man recovered near Crystal Rapids along the Colorado River. The flood, which occurred in Bright Angel Canyon and Phantom Ranch, led to the evacuation of dozens of visitors by helicopter. The NPS warned that heavy rain and thunderstorms were expected to continue, increasing the risk of further debris flows and flash floods. The Transcanyon Waterline, a key pipeline for water supply, suffered damage, prompting emergency water restrictions.

The flood caused significant damage to infrastructure, including the destruction of most footbridges spanning Bright Angel Creek. The NPS called on the public to provide information about missing individuals, with some accounts suggesting up to 15 people remain unaccounted for. The National Weather Service (NWS) noted that the region is under flood watches, with additional rain and thunderstorms forecast for the area.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Guardian and CBS News both mention the recovery of a 46-year-old man’s body, but the Guardian adds that at least one other body has been recovered, while Al Jazeera and CBS News do not specify additional bodies. The Guardian and UPI note damage to the Transcanyon Waterline, with the Guardian stating 40% of the pipeline was damaged, while UPI mentions "approximately 40% of the Transcanyon Waterline was damaged or destroyed." The Guardian and CBS News report the evacuation of 62 people, while Al Jazeera and UPI mention similar numbers. The Guardian and BBC include quotes from hikers describing the event, while other outlets do not.

## Jury convicts Duane Davis of murder in 1996 Tupac Shakur killing

A Las Vegas jury convicted Duane “Keffe D” Davis of first-degree murder in the 1996 killing of rapper Tupac Shakur, marking the first time anyone has been held criminally responsible for the rapper’s death. Prosecutors argued Davis orchestrated the drive-by shooting as revenge for an earlier public beating of his nephew, Orlando Anderson, by Shakur and Death Row Records founder Marion “Suge” Knight. Davis, 63, faces life in prison if convicted. The trial, which lasted over a week, centered on Davis’ repeated public admissions of his role in the killing, including in his 2019 memoir “Compton Street Legend.” Prosecutors claimed Davis was the “shot caller” who ordered the attack, while the defense argued his statements were fabrications meant to boost his street credibility and promote his book.

Davis was not charged with firing the shots that killed Shakur, which prosecutors attribute to his nephew Anderson, who was killed in a 1998 shootout. The prosecution emphasized Davis’ gang leadership and his alleged role in organizing the attack, while the defense highlighted the lack of physical evidence placing Davis at the scene. The trial also touched on the broader gang rivalry between the South Side Compton Crips and the Mob Piru Bloods, with Davis’ gang allegedly connected to Death Row Records and Shakur’s entourage linked to the rival Bloods.

**Where the accounts differ**

The New York Post and Al Jazeera both state that Davis was not charged with firing the shots, while The Guardian and The Daily Wire mention that prosecutors never gathered enough evidence to press charges against him for the actual shooting. The Guardian and The Daily Wire also note that Davis was briefly a suspect in the Notorious B.I.G. murder but was later ruled out, a detail omitted by The New York Post and Al Jazeera. The Daily Wire and The Guardian describe Davis as the only living witness from the car that fired shots at Shakur, a claim not made by The New York Post or Al Jazeera. The Guardian and The Daily Wire also mention that Davis’ memoir was used as evidence, while The New York Post and Al Jazeera describe it as a key part of the prosecution’s case but do not explicitly state it was used as evidence.

## US Supreme Court clears way for Trump to keep building White House ballroom

The US Supreme Court ruled on Monday that construction of the White House ballroom can proceed, allowing the project to continue despite legal challenges. The 5-4 decision stated that the National Trust for Historic Preservation likely lacks standing to challenge the project. The court did not address the legality of the project itself, focusing instead on the Trust's legal standing. President Trump praised the ruling, calling it a victory and stating the ballroom will be one of the greatest ever built in Washington, D.C. The project, which includes a large underground bunker, is part of a broader effort to renovate the White House and surrounding areas.

The National Trust for Historic Preservation argued that the construction is causing irreversible damage to a historical landmark and that Congress must approve the project. Alison Hoagland, a member of the Trust, claimed that the ballroom would harm her aesthetic, cultural, and historical interests. Chief Justice John Roberts, in his dissent, said the injuries claimed by Hoagland were sufficient to allow the lawsuit to proceed and that the project was likely unlawful. The Trust expressed disappointment with the ruling but welcomed the dissent.

**Where the accounts differ**

BBC reports that the ballroom is part of a wider effort to transform Washington's cityscape, including the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool and a planned "Arc de Trump." CBS News mentions the ballroom is 90,000 square feet and that the court did not address the legality of the East Wing project. Axios states the project is estimated to cost at least $600 million, up from an initial $400 million. UPI notes the project is $400 million and that the court did not say if the project was legal. Straight Arrow News mentions the project is 65% complete and that the cost has ballooned to $600 million. Just The News states the ballroom is roughly two-thirds complete and part of Trump's efforts to "beautify" the capital.

**Sources**  BBC - CBS News - Axios - UPI - Straight Arrow News - Just The News

## Trump says Venezuelan oil will replenish Strategic Petroleum Reserves

President Donald Trump claimed on Sunday that Venezuelan oil will replenish the United States’s Strategic Petroleum Reserves, calling it a "Gift from Venezuela to the People of the United States." Trump attributed the depletion of the reserves to former President Joe Biden, stating that Biden had "virtually emptied" the reserves. The deal, announced on Friday, involves securing majority U.S. control of more than 65 billion barrels of proven oil reserves in Venezuela. The White House stated that the agreement would help refill the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, which has been drawn down due to global oil supply disruptions, including the conflict in Iran.

The White House provided additional details on Monday, revealing that the deal involves a private joint venture with North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), owned by Venezuelan businessman Alejandro Betancourt. The agreement grants the Pentagon a 35 percent ownership stake in the company and guarantees the U.S. a right to purchase 20 percent of the output at cost. Venezuela’s interim President Delcy Rodriguez granted the company 100-year rights over 17 oil fields with proven reserves of 65 billion barrels, many of which were previously controlled by Russian or Chinese firms.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Washington Examiner and UPI both state that Trump claimed the reserves had been "virtually emptied" by Biden, while CBS News and NPR attribute the depletion to a combination of factors including the release of oil for price stabilization and congressional drawdowns. The Washington Examiner and UPI claim the deal involves "majority U.S. control" of the oil reserves, while the Al Jazeera and NPR accounts describe the agreement as granting the U.S. a 35 percent ownership stake and a right to purchase 20 percent of the output at cost. The Daily Caller provides a detailed timeline of the SPR’s depletion, including specific figures on the number of barrels released and the impact of congressional decisions.

**Sources**  Washington Examiner - Al Jazeera - The Daily Caller - NPR - CBS News - UPI
