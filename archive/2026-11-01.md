### North Korea linked to $6.3B Binance hack

Blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis have linked the $6.3 billion Binance hack to a North Korean hacking unit known as TraderTraitor. The attack, which occurred on October 29, 2026, saw approximately $6.36 billion in assets moved out of Binance wallets across multiple blockchain networks. Binance suspended all deposits and withdrawals, and industry partners froze about $3 billion in assets. The company has reserved over $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the losses. The FBI has confirmed that North Korea was behind the theft, identifying the group as TraderTraitor. ### Judge tosses Trump lawsuit against WSJ, Murdoch over Epstein story

A federal judge in Florida dismissed President Trump's defamation lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal and Rupert Murdoch over a 2003 story about a letter allegedly written by Trump for Jeffrey Epstein's birthday. The ruling, issued by Judge Darrin Gayles, found that Trump failed to meet the legal standard of "actual malice" required for a defamation claim against a public figure. The judge cited the Supreme Court's 1964 decision in New York Times Co. v. Sullivan, which established that public figures must prove media outlets acted with knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard for the truth. Trump had previously filed a similar lawsuit in April 2026, which was also dismissed, but allowed him to refile. The current case was dismissed with prejudice, meaning it cannot be refiled.

The lawsuit centered on a WSJ article that described a sexually suggestive letter bearing Trump's name, included in a 2003 birthday album compiled for Epstein. Trump denied writing the letter and called the story "fake," seeking $20 billion in damages. The judge noted that the article included Trump's denials and described the paper's efforts to verify the claims, including outreach to Trump, officials, and others involved in the album.

**Where the accounts differ**

### Ebola death toll in DR Congo passes 5,000

The death toll from Ebola in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has surpassed 5,000, according to government data. The outbreak, which began in May, has infected more than 10,000 people and spread to eight of the DRC’s 26 provinces. The Bundibugyo strain of the virus, which has no approved treatments or vaccines, is responsible for the outbreak. The virus has also spread to neighboring countries, including Kenya, Uganda, and Nigeria.

The outbreak, now the deadliest in DRC’s history, has a case fatality rate of 48.6%. Public health officials have identified the Ituri province as the epicenter, a conflict zone that complicates containment efforts. The World Health Organization (WHO) has called for increased testing, treatment, and community outreach to control the outbreak. The UN has noted that this outbreak is the second-largest in history, following the 2014-2016 epidemic in West Africa.
