## U.S. and Venezuela Reach Oil Agreement with U.S. Gaining Control of 65 Billion Barrels

The Trump administration announced a deal granting the U.S. majority control over 65 billion barrels of Venezuela’s oil reserves, with the U.S. government acquiring a 35% equity stake in North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), a private oil company led by Venezuelan businessman Alejandro Betancourt López. Under the agreement, the U.S. will have the right to purchase 20% of the oil produced by NABEP at the cost of production, while the remaining 80% can be sold to other buyers. The deal also gives the U.S. government veto power over NABEP’s board of directors, requiring a majority of board members to be U.S. citizens. Venezuela’s interim government granted NABEP 100-year rights over 17 oil fields, and the U.S. plans to use the oil to refill the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, which has reached historically low levels. Venezuela’s president, Delcy Rodríguez, publicly supported the deal, claiming it could generate over $200 billion in tax revenue for the country.

Chevron announced a separate $7 billion investment to expand its oil production in Venezuela, aiming to increase output to 600,000 barrels per day by 2031. The company has been granted access to two additional oil fields in Venezuela’s Orinoco Belt, which contains vast reserves of extra-heavy crude. Chevron’s CEO, Mike Wirth, stated the investment reflects confidence in Venezuela’s resource potential and its ability to attract investment. The deal with NABEP is part of a broader U.S. strategy to boost oil production and lower gas prices, with Trump claiming the agreement would “substantially lower Gas Prices for all Americans, long into the future.”

**Where the accounts differ**

Fox Business reports that the U.S. government received a 35% ownership stake in NABEP’s parent company through the Department of War’s Office of Strategic Capital, while The Guardian and Axios describe the same stake as being held by the Pentagon. The Daily Wire states that Chevron’s investment is part of the broader agreement with NABEP, while CBS News and Axios note that Chevron’s investment is separate from the Trump administration’s deal with NABEP. The Guardian and Axios mention that Betancourt has been investigated by U.S., Swiss, and Spanish authorities over alleged money laundering, while Fox Business and The Daily Wire provide more detail on the nature of the investigations and Betancourt’s denial of wrongdoing. The Daily Wire and Axios report that Betancourt was listed as an unnamed “conspirator” in a 2018 federal indictment, while Fox Business and The Guardian do not mention this specific charge.

## Apple changes name of Lake Ontario to ‘Lake America’ in maps app

Apple has updated the label for Lake Ontario to “Lake America” in its maps app for U.S. users, following an executive order from President Donald Trump. The change occurred on Tuesday, the same day John Ternus became Apple’s new CEO. Apple is now the second U.S.-based tech giant to comply with Trump’s order, following Google, which also changed the name for U.S. users. The U.S. Geographic Names Information System, which standardizes federal naming conventions, was cited by both companies as the basis for the change. Canadian users continue to see “Lake Ontario,” while international users may see both names. MapQuest, another map provider, has refused to change the name, stating it will not comply with Trump’s request.

The renaming of Lake Ontario is part of Trump’s broader effort to assert U.S. influence over naming conventions, including renaming the Gulf of Mexico to the “Gulf of America” and changing the name of Denali to “Mount McKinley.” Trump’s order does not compel private companies to change their naming conventions, but it does update the U.S. government’s official reference guide. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum stated that Trump “reached out to Apple directly” to request the change, though Apple did not immediately comment on the matter.

**Where the accounts differ**

Al Jazeera and The Guardian both state that Trump “reached out to Apple directly” to request the name change, while Axios and Just The News attribute the claim to Interior Secretary Doug Burgum. Al Jazeera and The Guardian mention that Trump’s order does not compel private companies to change their naming conventions, whereas Axios and The Guardian also note that Trump’s order does not compel Canada to change the name. The Guardian and Axios clarify that Canadian officials have stated they will not call Lake Ontario “Lake America,” while Al Jazeera and The Guardian do not explicitly state this.

**Sources**  Al Jazeera - Axios - BBC - Just The News - The Guardian

## NBA suspends Clippers owner Ballmer, fines team $30M, Kawhi Leonard $700K in cap circumvention case

The NBA announced on Wednesday that Los Angeles Clippers owner Steve Ballmer has been suspended for one year and the team fined $30 million for violating salary cap circumvention rules. Kawhi Leonard was fined $700,000 for his role in the case. The league cited the Clippers for improperly funneling off-court endorsement and income opportunities to Leonard through corporate partners, including Aspiration, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, and Lockton Insurance. The investigation, which began in September 2025, was prompted by a report from podcast journalist Pablo Torre about a $28 million endorsement contract between Leonard and Aspiration Fund Adviser LLC, a company that filed for bankruptcy last year. The Clippers had previously denied wrongdoing and stated they would challenge the findings. Leonard, through his new agent, expressed regret for the distraction caused by the situation and accepted responsibility for lapses in judgment by people within his inner circle.

The NBA also suspended President of Basketball Operations Lawrence Frank for six months and team president of business operations Gillian Zucker for one year. The league accused Ballmer of failing to create conditions under which his organization abided by the NBA’s circumvention rules. Leonard’s trade to the Toronto Raptors, which had been on hold pending the investigation, is now expected to proceed, with both teams indicating they are moving forward with the deal.

**Where the accounts differ**

NPR and The Guardian both mention that the Clippers had said they would challenge the findings, while BBC and Al Jazeera include the Clippers’ statement about the investigation being "heavily biased" and "predetermined narrative." The Guardian and Al Jazeera note that the investigation was conducted by Wachtell Lipton, a New York law firm, while NPR and BBC do not specify the law firm.
