### Hackers targeted refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets, raising temperatures to 122°F and causing fires and food spoilage

Hackers remotely accessed refrigeration systems at hundreds of French supermarkets, raising temperatures to as high as 122°F (50°C), which led to food spoilage and fires at several stores. The Paris prosecutor’s office described the attack as “large-scale,” affecting connected refrigerators in several major food retailers across France. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu ordered France’s National Cybersecurity Agency (ANSSI) to investigate, with officials coordinating with European counterparts due to potential cross-border impacts. The attack disrupted the cold chain, leading to the destruction of fresh and frozen products, with affected stores instructed to discard spoiled food.

The attack targeted refrigeration systems from Danish company Danfoss, which manage temperature settings for store freezers and fridges. Danfoss stated that affected units were running outdated firmware and had been advised to update or replace older systems. Cybersecurity firm Claroty had previously published research on vulnerabilities in Danfoss systems that could allow attackers to bypass authentication. French authorities and Danfoss have not yet linked these flaws to the recent attack. Some affected stores have resumed operations, though others continue to assess damage.
