### Cornell rape allegations fuel House Democratic women's Title IX push

A criminal investigation into allegations that a Cornell University student was gang raped by seven fraternity members in 2024 is fueling a new push by House Democratic women to strengthen federal protections against sexual violence on college campuses. The Democratic Women's Caucus is calling on congressional Republicans and the Trump administration to strengthen Title IX protections, arguing that the administration's changes to the civil rights law could leave students more vulnerable. The push comes just two days after the Trump administration rescinded Biden-era sex discrimination Title IX regulations and reinstated rules adopted during President Trump's first term.

A former Cornell University student, referred to as "Jane Doe" in legal documents, is suing the school, seven Chi Phi fraternity members and others over allegations that she was drugged and raped at a fraternity house in October of 2024. Cornell conducted a Title IX investigation after "Jane Doe" reported the incident in 2024 that resulted in two of the students being expelled and lesser disciplinary action for the other students. The DA has said criminal charges were not pursued because Jane Doe's initial police statement did not allege that she was gang-raped or drugged against her will. A criminal investigation was reopened after the lawsuit was filed earlier this month, and the New York attorney general has launched a separate review of the university's handling of the allegations. No criminal charges have been filed.

**Where the accounts differ**

Axios reports that the DA said criminal charges were not pursued because Jane Doe's initial police statement did not allege that she was gang-raped or drugged against her will. The Daily Wire omits this detail and instead focuses on the lawsuit and the lack of criminal charges without specifying the reason. The Washington Examiner also mentions the DA's decision to reopen the case but does not explain why charges were not pursued initially. The Mother Jones article provides additional context by discussing New York's voluntary intoxication loophole and how it may have influenced the DA's initial decision not to pursue charges.

### Christa Pike still alive, ‘snoring’ after 2 lethal injections as Tennessee governor halts all executions

Christa Pike, a death row inmate convicted of murdering her classmate in 1995, was given two doses of pentobarbital during an attempted execution in Tennessee on Wednesday. Witnesses reported that Pike remained alive and audible, snoring and speaking, after both injections. The Tennessee governor, Bill Lee, suspended all executions in the state and ordered an independent investigation following the botched attempt. Pike was taken to a nearby hospital for treatment, though her condition remains unclear. Her legal team claimed the execution process was flawed, citing issues such as difficult vein access, degraded drugs, and lack of emergency medical care.

Pike’s attorneys described the execution as “unnecessary agony” and “cruel and torturous,” arguing that the state failed to follow proper procedures. They noted that the execution team used multiple needles, one of which became bent, and that no life-saving care was provided after the attempt was abandoned. The Tennessee Department of Correction maintained that it followed all established protocols, stating that the lethal injection chemical had consistently been effective and that the protocol did not allow for additional procedures beyond what was carried out.

**Where the accounts differ**

The New York Post and Al Jazeera both report that Pike was taken to a nearby hospital for treatment, but the New York Post specifies that her condition is unclear, while Al Jazeera states that her lawyers had not been told about her condition. The Guardian and CBS News both mention that Pike was in critical condition receiving life-saving care, but the Guardian adds that her attorneys condemned the execution as “cruel and torturous,” a claim not explicitly made by CBS News. The ABC News and Axios accounts differ in the timing of the Supreme Court’s decision to allow the execution to proceed, with ABC News stating it was a 6-3 decision and Axios noting that the court’s majority did not explain its decision.

### Judge permanently dismisses Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool vandalism case against former Olympian

A D.C. Superior Court judge on Thursday permanently dismissed the criminal case against former Olympian David Hearn, who was accused of damaging the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool in June. The ruling, made with prejudice, means federal prosecutors cannot refile the charges against Hearn. The case was initially brought by the Trump administration, which alleged Hearn damaged the pool’s lining. However, prosecutors later dropped the charges after new evidence from the Department of the Interior indicated the damage was caused by a "rushed and botched" installation by a contractor. Hearn, who was arrested on June 19 and later indicted, faced up to 10 years in prison if convicted. His defense team argued the case was a miscarriage of justice and that Trump’s public pressure on prosecutors threatened his rights.

The judge, Todd Edelman, cited political pressure from President Trump as a key reason for the dismissal. He noted that while prosecutors deemed the case meritless, Trump repeatedly criticized U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro and urged her to reconsider the decision. The judge also criticized the speed of the indictment process and the government’s attempt to retain the possibility of recharging Hearn if new evidence emerged.

**Where the accounts differ**

FOX News reports that the judge rejected prosecutors’ request to preserve the possibility of recharging Hearn if new evidence surfaced, finding existing findings sufficient to show the damage stemmed from a flawed renovation. BBC and NPR state that prosecutors admitted the damage was due to flawed installation, not vandalism. New York Post mentions that the National Park Service engineer was alerted to the peeling lining at least 10 days before the incident on June 9, a detail not included in other accounts. FOX News and New York Post both mention that the DOJ cited $1,000 in damage, while BBC and NPR do not specify the amount. FOX News attributes Trump’s disagreement with Pirro’s decision to the White House, while BBC and NPR attribute it to Trump’s social media posts.

### 944 children abused by Catholic clergy, Mass. AG says — but no new charges

The Massachusetts attorney general released a report Wednesday documenting more than 270 Catholic clerics accused of sexually abusing 944 children across three dioceses — but the investigation will result in no new indictments. The report, which the AG called the first full public accounting of the abuse and institutional failures, details how diocesan leaders covered up allegations before 2002 by transferring accused clergy to new congregations without warning parishioners. The report also found that some clergy were sent to psychiatric treatment facilities before being allowed to return to ministry, where they later reoffended. Survivors described church processes that left some feeling pressured to testify to internal church investigators or stay silent, with some reporting that dioceses used NDAs to stop survivors from discussing abuse allegations.

The report was the result of an investigation launched in 2019, examining church responses to abuse allegations over more than six decades. It reviewed more than 300,000 pages of documents and interviewed over 100 people, including survivors, attorneys, and clergy. The AG noted that most of the abuse occurred before 2002, and newly found misconduct fell outside applicable statutes of limitations. Many accused clergy are also dead, leaving survivors with few options for legal recourse. The report also highlights that 12 dioceses across the United States still have not published lists of credibly accused clergy, representing less than 5% of all dioceses and orders.

**Where the accounts differ**

Axios reports that the report sought to provide the first comparable public accounting of what happened in the dioceses of Fall River, Springfield, and Worcester, while the New York Post states that the report provided the first full public accounting of the abuse and institutional failures. Axios mentions that the AG's office made three referrals to local district attorneys during the probe, leading to charges in one case in Worcester, while the New York Post states that the AG did not pursue any prosecutions due to some conduct not being illegal at the time it occurred or because the statute of limitations had expired.
