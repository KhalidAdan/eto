## Energy Secretary Chris Wright expresses cautious optimism about gasoline prices amid ongoing Iran conflict

Energy Secretary Chris Wright told Sunday political shows that gasoline prices are likely to decline, though he stopped short of making firm promises. He cited falling demand after the Labor Day holiday and recent changes to refinery specifications that ease production. Pump prices remain at record highs, averaging $4.15 per gallon, according to AAA data. Wright also mentioned that gasoline futures contracts suggest prices could drop significantly in the coming months. However, he acknowledged that future prices are volatile and not guaranteed. The conflict with Iran has contributed to rising oil prices, with crude oil hitting a six-week high above $96 per barrel.

Wright also discussed the U.S. strategy to secure the Strait of Hormuz, noting that oil transits through the waterway have reached over 9 million barrels a day, with additional flows via bypass pipelines. He emphasized that the U.S. is working with allies to maintain the flow of oil, though he admitted that Iran continues to pose a threat to the region's stability. The situation remains tense, with both sides engaging in periodic attacks and retaliations.

**Where the accounts differ**

The New York Post and The Guardian report that Wright suggested the U.S. may not reach a nuclear deal with Iran and instead focus on degrading Iran’s nuclear capabilities. The Guardian adds that former Defense Secretary Leon Panetta warned the Iran war could last another six months. The Daily Caller and ABC News both mention Wright’s comments about the U.S. military taking out Iranian oil tankers and the ongoing conflict over the Strait of Hormuz. The BBC reports that Iran claimed to have attacked an American remote-controlled vessel in the Strait of Hormuz, which the U.S. denied. The New York Post and ABC News both include Wright’s remarks about Operation Economic Outcast and the economic pressure on Iran, while the Guardian does not mention this.

## More than 1,500 pounds of pork recalled over Listeria contamination risk

The U.S. Department of Agriculture’s Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) announced a recall of more than 1,500 pounds of imported ready-to-eat pork products due to concerns about possible Listeria monocytogenes contamination. Two U.S.-based companies, Prime Line Distributors, Inc. and Ferrarini USA, Inc., are issuing the recall, which is classified as a Class I recall, the most serious level. The products, known as guanciale, were produced in Italy on May 21 and imported into the U.S. in July. The contamination was detected during routine import reinspection testing. The affected products have a best if used by date of May 16, 2027, and a lot number of 263311US. The recall includes products distributed to food service and retail locations in multiple states.

Consumers are advised not to eat the recalled products and to either discard them or return them to the place of purchase. FSIS warned that some products may still be in refrigerators or freezers at food service establishments and retail locations. No illnesses have been reported in connection with the consumption of these products, though FSIS urged those in high-risk groups experiencing flu-like symptoms within two months of eating the products to seek medical attention.

Fox Business and New York Post list the states affected as California, Florida, Idaho, Illinois, Michigan, New Jersey, New York, and Texas, while Newsweek includes the same states.
