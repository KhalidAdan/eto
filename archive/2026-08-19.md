## US, Canada hold last-minute talks to stop 50% U.S. tariffs

President Donald Trump announced a three-day pause on 50% tariffs on Canadian goods hours before they were set to take effect, citing a pending deal. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney acknowledged the postponement but stated that work continued on reaching an agreement. The White House claimed the tariffs were imposed in response to Canada’s “discriminatory treatment” of U.S. commerce, while Canadian officials expressed concerns over U.S. tariffs on steel, aluminum, and softwood lumber. Both sides emphasized the importance of avoiding the tariffs, with U.S. trade officials noting the potential impact on American consumers and Canadian businesses.

The negotiations involved discussions over market access, economic security, and digital trade, with the U.S. seeking greater access to Canadian resources and Canada pushing for relief from existing tariffs. Trump also mentioned the potential revival of the Keystone XL Pipeline as part of the deal. The delay allowed for further talks, though no final agreement had been reached by the deadline.

## Mobile gold miners in eastern Congo complicate health workers’ efforts to contain Ebola

The Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo has reached 5,021 confirmed cases, including 2,378 deaths, according to government data. The Bundibugyo virus, which causes the current outbreak, has no approved vaccines or treatments. Health workers report that the outbreak is spreading faster than any previous Ebola outbreak in history, with cases increasing three times more rapidly than the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak. The outbreak is concentrated in Ituri province, where artisanal gold mining is prevalent. Miners, including many children, work in hard-to-reach areas and often move between villages, complicating efforts to track and contain the virus. Health workers say that measures like handwashing and social distancing are not followed at mining sites, increasing the risk of transmission.

Miners and small-scale traders move frequently between mining areas and population centers, making it difficult to trace infected individuals and monitor the spread of the virus. Health workers have lost track of two miners who were identified as Ebola contacts after they moved to another mining site. The outbreak has also led to attacks on health teams, with residents in Ituri province attacking a health team in the territory of Aru, setting fire to an ambulance and damaging two other vehicles.
