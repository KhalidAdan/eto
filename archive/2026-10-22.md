### Trump Persuades Putin To Free Jailed American Teacher Who Was Taken On Biden’s Watch

President Donald Trump claimed to have persuaded Russian President Vladimir Putin to free Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old American teacher detained in Russia for over four years. Hubbard was released and was waiting at a St. Petersburg airport for his flight back to the U.S. according to U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff. The State Department had previously labeled Hubbard as wrongfully detained. Trump stated that he had made the request to Putin during a phone call last week, and that no prisoner swap was needed. Hubbard was seized by Russian troops in eastern Ukraine in April 2022 and was sentenced to nearly seven years in prison in October 2024 for allegedly serving as a mercenary for Ukraine. His family and legal team denied the charges, claiming he was too old to fight and had never owned a gun.

During a meeting in St. Petersburg, Witkoff thanked Putin for releasing Hubbard, calling him a "humanitarian." This prompted backlash from some U.S. lawmakers, including Senators John Cornyn and Thom Tillis, who criticized Witkoff's comments. Putin also announced his readiness to resume trilateral peace talks with the U.S. and Ukraine, though these talks have been rejected by Ukraine. The meeting included discussions on a potential framework agreement to end the war in Ukraine.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Daily Wire reports that Trump took a swipe at former President Joe Biden, stating that Hubbard was taken on Biden’s watch and that the previous administration’s talks with the Kremlin went nowhere. This is not mentioned in Axios, NPR, or CBS News. The New York Post and BBC include Witkoff’s praise of Putin as a “humanitarian,” which is not mentioned in The Daily Wire or Axios. The Daily Wire and CBS News mention that Trump claimed to have made the request directly to Putin, while Axios and BBC do not specify the method of communication. The Daily Wire and CBS News state that no prisoner swap was needed, while Axios and BBC do not explicitly state this. The Daily Wire and CBS News mention that Hubbard was in poor health and would be flown to Texas for treatment, while Axios and BBC do not specify his health condition.

### Sea mine crisis re-emerges in Strait of Hormuz as Tehran urges Washington to accept deal

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) navy reported that two oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines while crossing the Strait of Hormuz, according to Reuters, citing Iran's official Islamic Republic News Agency (IRNA). Both tankers were carrying about 2 million barrels of crude oil. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) warned that sea mines pose a serious threat to vessels navigating the strategic waterway, with IMO Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez stating that the traffic separation scheme, approved in 1968, is still in place but not guaranteed to be clear of mines. The developments came amid stalled negotiations between Washington and Tehran over ending the conflict.

The U.S. State Department ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks intensified, according to a spokesperson. The order applies only to Saudi Arabia, though reports suggest similar measures may be taken in other Gulf countries. The Houthis have attacked civilian airports and critical infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, leading to civilian casualties and travel disruptions. The U.S. is also considering military action against the Houthis, with President Trump weighing direct strikes.

### Meta hit with $25 billion penalty for misleading Facebook users

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion in penalties after a jury found the company misled users about data privacy. The ruling follows a trial where jurors determined Meta violated New Mexico’s consumer protection laws over 43 million times. The judge reduced the potential penalty from over $219 billion to $25 billion, citing state law. Meta has stated it plans to appeal the decision. The case stems from the Cambridge Analytica scandal, where the firm harvested data from millions of Facebook users without consent. The state accused Meta of failing to inform users about data access and mishandling harmful content.

The case is part of a broader trend of lawsuits against Meta over data privacy and user safety. In August, the company agreed to pay $17 billion to settle similar claims with 47 states and the District of Columbia. New Mexico’s attorney general, Raúl Torrez, called the ruling a significant victory for consumer protection. Meta argued the penalty is excessive and could deter businesses from operating in the state.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Daily Wire reports that the judge mentioned Facebook’s content moderation shortcomings and that the case was broadened to criticize the company’s strategy, including its shift from internal fact-checkers to community notes. This detail is not present in the other accounts. The Daily Wire also claims that Torrez criticized Meta for abandoning its fact-check program after Trump’s 2024 election win, a claim not included in the other reports. The Daily Wire attributes the claim about Torrez meeting with other Democrat attorneys general to the outlet itself, while other accounts do not mention this.

### Supreme Court Blocks Trump Admin Bid to Restrict Transgender Medical Care for Federal Prisoners

The U.S. Supreme Court on Wednesday rejected the Trump administration’s request to immediately enforce new restrictions on transgender medical care for federal prisoners. The court’s order did not explain its reasoning, but it noted that conservative Justices Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito would have granted the administration’s request to pause a lower court’s order blocking the policy. The Justice Department plans to continue defending the policy before the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit. The policy, which limits medical treatments and social accommodations for transgender prisoners, was introduced under an executive order from President Trump. The policy was supported by an executive order from Trump, though the Bureau of Prisons said it adopted the policy independently, citing security concerns and its determination that standards pushed by the World Professional Association for Transgender Health were not reliable. The policy was challenged by three transgender prisoners, represented by the American Civil Liberties Union, who argued it was arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act. U.S. District Judge Royce Lamberth, appointed by President Ronald Reagan, blocked the policy after finding that the prisoners were likely to succeed on their claim that it was arbitrary and capricious. The judge also directed the Bureau of Prisons to continue providing medical treatment for the transgender-identifying prisoners under its prior policy.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Daily Wire and The Guardian differ on the number of transgender prisoners in federal detention, with The Daily Wire stating there are about 1,000 and The Guardian reporting over 2,000. The Federalist and The New York Post mention the Bureau of Prisons adopted the policy independently, while The Guardian and ABC News attribute the policy to the executive order from Trump. The Federalist and The New York Post note that the policy was a successor to a 2025 version, while The Guardian and The Daily Wire do not specify the timeline. The Guardian and The Federalist mention the policy’s extension to “social accommodations,” while The Daily Wire and ABC News describe it as including “social accommodations” but do not specify what they entail.
