### Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO After Board Reversal

Nvidia's board of directors reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO effective immediately following a reversal of its earlier decision to remove him. Huang had resigned earlier this month amid reports of extramarital affairs, including one involving Alice Huang, a woman identified by U.S. intelligence as a possible Chinese government spy. The board cited satisfaction with Huang's candor and leadership ability, and stated it received input from senior U.S. government officials that confirmed no national security risk. Nvidia stock surged over 30% following the announcement, with broader markets also seeing significant gains. The decision has drawn mixed reactions, with some praising it as necessary for the company's future and others expressing concerns about national security implications.

### Russian warship sinks in Benghazi after suspected Ukrainian drone attack

A Russian warship docked in the Libyan port city of Benghazi was struck in the early hours of Sunday in a suspected Ukrainian sea drone attack, causing a dramatic night-time explosion and the sinking of the vessel. Libyan officials from the country’s eastern government confirmed the strike severely damaged the Merkury, a Russian navy corvette that had stopped to refuel and take on supplies. The attack, which took place in the Mediterranean, more than 1,000 miles from south Ukraine, marked a notable geographic expansion of the now near five-year war. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy claimed responsibility for the attack, stating that seven domestically made sea drones were used to sink the ship. The UN mission in Libya expressed “serious concern” over the drone attacks and called for “an immediate, independent and transparent investigation.”

Hours later, a Russia-linked cargo ship said to be carrying military supplies, the Ascalon, was hit by a sea drone off the coast of Benghazi. Video showed the drone heading directly towards one end of the vessel, again causing a huge explosion. Libyan naval forces rescued the vessel’s crew, with some members in stable condition with minor injuries. The number of injured and their nationalities are not known. The eastern-based Libyan authority said port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe and that normal operations would resume once the fire was brought under control and the site secured.

**Where the accounts differ**

### Trump announces federal death penalty directive as he blasts Newsom’s clemency plan

President Donald Trump announced Monday that he would direct the Justice Department to seek the death penalty in federal child murder cases and in cases in which rapists or human traffickers kill their victims. He used the speech at the DOJ headquarters to condemn outgoing Gov. Gavin Newsom’s (D-CA) plan to commute 548 California death sentences. Trump’s directive would tell federal prosecutors to seek the death penalty in child murder cases and in cases involving rape or human trafficking in which the offender kills the victim. The move would have no effect on Newsom’s plan to replace 548 state death sentences with life without parole, which Trump condemned before an audience that included victims’ families.

Trump said he would sign a presidential directive that would also order federal prosecutors to seek the death penalty for rapists and human traffickers who kill their victims. "Once juries have spoken, capital punishment will be swift and certain," he said. Newsom announced Nov. 5 that he intended to replace all 548 death sentences with life in prison without parole, arguing that capital punishment is incompatible with the state’s values. Trump and several speakers described the commutations as already granted, but the plan has yet to take effect. Under California’s Constitution, 382 of the proposed commutations require a recommendation from the state Supreme Court because the inmates were convicted of more than one felony. Newsom’s administration has said it will wait for the court to act before proceeding with any of the commutations.
