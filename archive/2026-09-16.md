## Three killed after LA news helicopter crashes while covering deadly bus collision

A news helicopter crashed in suburban Los Angeles, killing three people and injuring others. The crash occurred near the site of a bus collision that had happened earlier in the day. The helicopter, operated by NBC Los Angeles and Telemundo 52, was reporting on the bus crash when it went down. Firefighters confirmed that the crash happened just before 7 p.m. local time, between two commercial buildings in Chatsworth. The aircraft burst into flames, damaging several vehicles and storage containers. At least one person was confirmed dead on the ground, while the other two victims were on board the helicopter. Two others were taken to the hospital, though their conditions were not immediately clear. The National Transportation Safety Board and the Federal Aviation Administration are investigating the cause of the crash.

The US House of Representatives voted to end the war in Iran for the third time, passing a war powers resolution that would halt President Donald Trump’s ability to continue military action without congressional approval. The resolution passed 220-204, with seven House Republicans supporting the measure, including three voting in favour for the first time. The vote comes as the war has dragged on for nearly seven months, with on-again, off-again deadly missile strikes exchanged between the US and Iran. The resolution is likely to be the last House vote on the Iran war before the midterm elections, where the conflict is expected to play a significant role in political campaigns.

The report also found that the war has driven up inflation by 0.5 percentage points in the first three months of 2027. The Pentagon’s inspector general confirmed that the war has led to "shortfalls" in munitions and a bottleneck in resupply, contradicting Trump’s claims that the US has "virtually unlimited" ammunition. The report noted that the US spent $22.3bn on munitions alone through June, with total spending at $33.4bn.
