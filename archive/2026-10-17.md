### Katie Zacharia’s short tenure as White House press secretary ends amid legal credential scrutiny

### Trump: White House didn't impede satellite system in Ukraine

President Trump claimed Friday that his administration did not disrupt an Elon Musk-owned satellite system used by Ukraine to wage war against Russia. Ukrainian officials remain highly skeptical of Trump's claims and believe someone in the U.S. government participated in throttling the systems. ### Trump waives Russia sanctions he signed into law

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday to waive new sanctions and tariffs on Russia, which he had previously signed into law. The move came two days before the sanctions were set to take effect and a week after announcing a diesel deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin. The law, named after late Senator Lindsey Graham, was passed by Congress and aimed to impose economic pressure on Russia by sanctioning officials, financial institutions, and energy exports. Trump claimed the waiver was in the national interest.

The sanctions law included provisions for tariffs of up to 100 percent on major importers of Russian oil and gas, with exceptions for countries importing less than 15 percent of Russia’s natural gas. It also targeted Russian officials and a shadow fleet of tankers used to circumvent sanctions. Trump’s decision to suspend the sanctions has drawn criticism from both Democrats and Republicans, with some lawmakers accusing him of capitulating to Moscow and undermining support for Ukraine.

Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the waiver “an important and truly historic moment,” while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy expressed disappointment, stating the sanctions could have helped “enormously” in pressuring Russia to end the war. Zelenskyy also highlighted recent Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, which he said were aimed at causing a blackout ahead of winter.

**Where the accounts differ**

Al Jazeera and Newsweek both mention the diesel deal with Putin and the suspension of sanctions, but only Newsweek specifies that the order created and then blocked a 500 percent tariff on Russian goods and a potential 100 percent tariff against major buyers of Russian oil and gas. BBC and Fox Business both note the diesel deal and the waiver of tariffs under the Graham Act, but only Fox Business mentions the specific provision about countries that knowingly bought Russian oil 30 days after the law went into effect. ABC News and Washington Examiner both state that Trump signed the order waiving the sanctions, but only Washington Examiner refers to the law as the “Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act.”
