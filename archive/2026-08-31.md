## U.S. strikes Iran to prevent Hormuz mine threat

The U.S. military struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz to prevent the deployment of sea mines, according to a U.S. official. The strike marked the first known U.S. attack on Iran since late July. Iran retaliated by launching ballistic missiles at a U.S. base in Jordan, though no damage or casualties were reported. The Jordanian military intercepted eight missiles. The U.S. Central Command stated the strike was a "limited, precise action" against "minelaying forces posing an imminent threat." Oil prices surged following the strike, with Brent crude up 2.9% and U.S. crude up 2.7%. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping route, remains closed to commercial traffic, though Iran and Oman agreed on a temporary maritime route.

The conflict has led to significant disruptions in oil trade, with Brent crude rising about 22% since the war began in February. The closure of the strait has affected global oil prices and exposed U.S. energy companies to risks, with some reporting reduced oil and gas supplies from the Gulf region. The U.S. strike is part of a broader military and economic pressure campaign against Iran, which includes sanctions and a naval blockade affecting Iran's oil exports.

A catastrophic flash flood along the Nepal-Tibet border has left at least 900 people dead and more than 3,000 missing, according to conflicting reports from Nepali and Chinese authorities. The disaster, triggered by a glacial collapse, devastated mountainous regions and caused widespread destruction. Rescue operations have been hampered by poor weather, with aerial rescues temporarily halted and ground teams struggling through mud and debris. Over 3,700 people have been rescued in Nepal, with efforts focused on hydropower tunnels where dozens of workers are believed to be trapped. International aid has been pledged, including from the U.S., India, and the EU, to support recovery efforts. Survivors describe losing everything, with many left homeless and without access to clean water or food.

## Google Maps Complies With Trump’s ‘Lake America’ Order

Google began displaying “Lake America” in place of “Lake Ontario” for U.S. users of Google Maps on Saturday, complying with an executive order President Donald Trump signed Thursday amid an escalating trade dispute with Canada. The order directs the Interior Department to update the U.S. Geographic Names Information System (GNIS), the federal database that governs official American map labels. Google stated its maps are updated “to reflect name changes in official government sources,” and that the change follows the company’s “long-standing policy for bodies of water with names that vary from country to country.” Under the new display rules, U.S. users see “Lake America,” Canadian users continue to see “Lake Ontario,” and users elsewhere see both names, with “Lake America” appearing in parentheses.

Ontario Premier Doug Ford responded by erecting a large sign along the Canadian shoreline reading “Lake Ontario — Now and Always,” criticizing Trump’s move. Ford stated the name would endure beyond the political moment, saying, “Long after President Trump is gone, it will still be called Lake Ontario.” Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney also rejected the rebranding, writing that “Canadians also know that naming reality means calling it Lake Ontario — then, now and always.” The lake, the smallest of the Great Lakes, sits on the border between New York and Ontario, with its surface area split roughly 52% Canada and 48% U.S.

**Where the accounts differ**

The Daily Wire reports that MapQuest said it won’t adopt the new name at all, instead rolling out a tool letting users rename the lake to whatever they choose. UPI and CBS News do not mention this detail. Straight Arrow News and Newsweek include information about the Seneca Nation leader criticizing Trump’s decision, while The Daily Wire and BBC attribute the statement to J. Conrad Seneca. The Daily Wire and BBC mention Trump’s AI-generated videos, which other accounts do not.

## US, Venezuela tout ‘biggest oil deal in world history’

President Donald Trump announced a sweeping oil agreement with Venezuela, calling it the "biggest oil deal in world history." According to Trump, the deal would give the U.S. majority control over more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuela’s proven oil reserves. Venezuelan interim President Delcy Rodríguez praised the agreement, calling it a "historic milestone" and stating it would bring "a significant flow of investment" for Venezuela’s hydrocarbons industry. The deal is expected to develop 17 strategic oil fields, with a production target of more than 1.5 million barrels per day over 25 years. Trump claimed the agreement would "substantially lower Gas Prices for all Americans, long into the future."

The agreement, negotiated by Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, is said to involve private business interests. A U.S. official told The Associated Press that the U.S. would have a 55% effective interest in the output of a new private company managing the reserves. The deal comes amid high global oil prices and a depleted U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve, which has fallen below 300 million barrels, its lowest level in over four decades.
